# Chapter 324: Creation of Heaven and Earth — The Eternal Long Road, One

Aside from the two of them and Argan-sama, everyone had gone from old women back to their youthful appearances.

Having aged, having grown old.

Because they had accepted it so naturally, the first three days or so brought an indescribable sense of bewilderment.

When they came face to face with the rejuvenated Selene-sama and the others, there was a strange mutual embarrassment, but they accepted it surprisingly quickly — surely thanks to Argan-sama.

Simply because Argan-sama was there, they settled in right away.

Back into 'the manor' as it had been when they were not yet old.

By now they should have been far older, and yet because Argan-sama was unchanged, treating them as naturally as ever, the distance of all those years had vanished to some faraway place.

They had surely built up something over all that time — there had been at least some growth.

If even someone like her felt that way, all the more so for the others.

And yet, as if they had all been sent back in time — or as if they had spent all those years together — Argan-sama was naturally at the center of the manor.

"……It really has been a terribly long time, hasn't it."

"Yes. Argan-sama, you truly have no memories at all from that day?"

"That's right……It felt rather like a single sleep, I suppose. I simply woke up, and there I was on the bed without a stitch on — fufu, I was a bit startled."

Krische-sama's — or rather, the private room shared by the three of them: Argan-sama, Kreschenta-sama, and Krische-sama.

At one end stood a canopied bed, wardrobes lined the wall, and at the center sat a large desk and sofa.

Argan-sama was seated there, with Krische-sama and Kreschenta-sama resting their heads on either side of her thighs.

Since arriving here, the two of them had spent nearly all their time at Argan-sama's side.

Considering their feelings, that was only natural.

Gazing at their blissful sleeping faces, Argan-sama stroked their heads and wore a gentle — unchanged, beautiful smile.

She wore an apron dress.

Her fair cheeks had good color, and the flesh that had once thinned had filled out plump and healthy.

A girlish face graced with a calm, gentle lady's smile — the light streaming through the window illuminated the three of them with an almost divine radiance.

The bridge of her nose tingled, and she nearly shed tears without meaning to, pressing her hand to her eyes.

Seeing that, Argan-sama smiled wryly and said, "I'm sorry for all the trouble I've caused."

"There have been so few changes in the manor that I don't really feel the reality that decades have passed……No, I suppose it's careless to say such things so lightly."

"……That's not true at all."

"But when I look around closely, I can see the years in the small details of the manor. There are scratches on this desk I don't recognize……"

"Th-that's, well, that was when I……during the big cleaning……"

"Ah, I see."

She traced the scratches on the desk with her finger, giggling fondly.

She had tripped while carrying Krische-sama's gauntlets and slammed them into the desk.

Seeing Argan-sama's delighted smile, that tingle rose in her nose again.

She was supposed to be quite an old woman by now.

She had lived twice as many years as Argan-sama, and by now she should have been the more experienced one — both as a person and as a servant — yet Argan-sama remained flawless, the very image of the servant she had always admired.

Even the simple fact that she was now the older one felt wrong, and she couldn't shake the illusion that all these years had been spent together like this, unchanged.

As if those years without Argan-sama had never been — as if they had been some bad dream — Argan-sama was Argan-sama.

The revered, great servant she so admired.

For her, Argan-sama remained even now a superior to be respected, a great senior.

Even Selene-sama, a Grand Marshal whose name was known across the continent, who commanded respect from every warrior, was still just Ojou-sama.

Despite all the agonizing she had done, once they were face to face, she was immediately bickering happily — and that sight, too, was a continuation of what had always been.

Huffing and puffing, complaining to Argan-sama, repeating her lectures — Selene-sama's manner was so like those days when she had first come to Christand that she could almost believe nothing in this manor had changed at all.

*——To spend eternity with these people.*

The reason she hadn't felt much doubt about such a future was surely that.

After the civil war ended, the world outside the manor had been a whirlwind of events, but inside the manor, nothing had changed in the ten years since.

During the day they went out for work, and in the evening they gathered for meals together, chatting over tea.

She had never questioned those days.

Tending to the manor, caring for Krische-sama and the others, sleeping, and waking in the morning.

Peaceful, comfortable, blissful days — *if only this could go on forever.*

She had surely wished for eternity somewhere in her heart.

Her only fear had been losing that everyday life.

When Krische-sama said she wanted to create an eternity, unlike Selene-sama, she had been bewildered — yet she had accepted it remarkably easily, she thought.

How carefree of her.

Those with greater thoughtfulness — Selene-sama, Elvena-sama, Lila-sama — must have had many things to agonize over, and for that she felt a little guilty.

The manor was unchanged, filled with beloved, familiar faces.

Eternity had begun with the arrival of the guest Lila-sama, and now one week had passed.

The scene before her eyes still reflected the happiness she had imagined, just as she had pictured it.

"Speaking of which……won't Anne-sama have a seat? You still don't seem accustomed to your body, and standing the whole time must be tiring."

"N-no, I need to get used to my body quickly so I don't cause trouble……"

She raised her arms, opened and closed her hands, her eyes wandering.

Everyone except the three of them had been struggling with their new bodies.

If they let their guard down, they felt as if they might topple over — the body didn't feel like their own.

Even washing her own body was still difficult, and she had even had the rare experience of nearly drowning in the bathtub — for the time being, bathing was done together.

In the morning, getting dressed required mutual help with Elvena-sama just to put on the apron dress, so getting used to the body was an urgent priority.

Even Selene-sama, who was accustomed to controlling her body with mana, still seemed to feel some lingering discomfort, and was training in the garden even now with Lila-sama, who was skilled in physical arts.

Knowing of such efforts, a servant like her could hardly afford to laze about.

And it wasn't just Selene-sama — Lila-sama was the same.

In Kreisharana, the handling of mana was drilled into everyone from childhood regardless of gender, and Elvena-sama was skilled with mana from helping Krische-sama — her natural talent was beyond comparison.

The one who would be slowest to adjust to this body was, without question, the servant called Anne Giterns.

"Please don't worry about being a bother — compared to me. Besides, there's plenty of time; you can get used to it at your own pace."

"Ugh……b-but……"

"Rest is important too. You'll naturally adjust as you go about your work in the manor."

Still, refusing a kindness too stubbornly went against the way of a servant.

Accepting Argan-sama's words, she sat down in the single-seat sofa diagonally in front and to the right.

As she settled into the seat, her body threatened to go slack, and she had to consciously straighten her spine.

Argan-sama chuckled softly, then traced her lips with her fingertip.

As always, a subtly sensual gesture — gazing up at the ceiling in thought, Argan-sama spoke.

"However……with no more duties of state, we'll have quite a lot of free time, won't we. The manor work isn't much to speak of."

"That is……indeed true."

She nodded.

Cleaning, laundry, preparing meals.

Even after Argan-sama had been gone for so long, she and Elvena-sama had managed admirably.

Kreschenta-sama had her accompanying on official duties, and Elvena-sama occasionally accompanied Selene-sama, and there were days when manor work was pushed aside — but on those occasions, Krische-sama would single-handedly and effortlessly take care of everything.

As for the vexing financial management of Christand, Kreschenta-sama would occasionally — while complaining — handle it with startling ease (at what felt like 1,200 times Anne's processing speed), so there had never been an issue.

If personal attendance and such juggling were no longer necessary, then four of them would be handling the manor work.

Even so, it was a small number by general standards — but the manor work had always been something that was mostly done once Argan-sama alone was present.

Adding herself, Elvena-sama, and even Krische-sama to that was, honestly, somewhat excessive.

Even if they were more thorough than before, there was a limit to how much work could be added, and most of it would likely be done before noon.

For now, their unfamiliarity with the bodies kept them needlessly busy each day, but Krische-sama had said it would only take a few years at most to adjust.

"Have you thought about what to do with your free time?"

"Not yet……ah, but actually, I've been thinking I might try writing some stories, perhaps……I've had the idea for a while."

"Stories……"

"Nothing grand, really……but I thought I might write them down and make them into books. I remembered how, a long time ago, I made my letters to Father into a book."

Ah, said Argan-sama with a wry smile.

She had never shown the contents, but she recalled Argan-sama making the same expression when she saw the two finished 'letters' — and her cheeks colored.

"Back then I had a bookbinder do it and all sorts of favors, but since we have plenty of time, I thought I might do everything from start to finish myself — write stories and make them into books……"

"Storybooks……fufu, that sounds lovely. How wonderfully extravagant."

"Yes."

Books were rather expensive things.

What was recorded in them was mostly academic, and there were few books made purely for entertainment.

Simple picture tales of famous stories for reading aloud to children were fairly common, but those were fairy tales and fables.

Books created purely as entertainment for adults — even in the royal palace library, which held over ten thousand volumes, you could count them on one hand.

Of course, there might be some elsewhere, but they certainly weren't so common as to line the shelves of a lending library.

Making the books she wanted to read herself — that sounded truly delightful, and it was one of the things she had considered if she were really to spend eternity here.

"What about you, Argan-sama?"

"Let me see. Perhaps expanding the orchard, or building a garden from scratch. Collecting seedlings and seeds from all over, doing my own selective breeding, harvesting delicious vegetables and fruits, and cooking them. I'd always been interested, but it does require so much effort and time that I'd settled for a small orchard."

"I see……"

"Fufu, I'm sure Krische-sama would enjoy it too."

She said so with a soft smile, stroking Krische-sama's head.

Her truly blissful sleeping face was childlike, and seeing it made her think, truly — *how wonderful.*

Since coming here, both of them had been even more radiant, their happiness overflowing from every fiber of their being.

"When that time comes, please do let me help."

"Thank you. Though it will be a little while yet……I'd like to properly begin once we've settled into life here a bit more."

"……Yes."

Gazing at Argan-sama's gentle smile, she nearly let out a sigh of admiration.

Argan-sama was here, Krische-sama and the others were here, and she was here.

The manor she had dreamed of had returned.

The place she had once worked was called the Erune Estate.

One of seven estates built within the great gardens of the royal domain, known as the King's Garden.

Among them, the most frequently used was the Erune Estate — essentially a place for the royal family to receive their vassals.

More of a reception hall in the shape of a manor, really.

There were times when different nobles were received in the morning, noon, and evening, and busy periods were indeed busy — but it was rare for anyone to stay the night.

Few people resided there at all.

She was a servant of the royal domain.

For the first six months or so, she thought she had been satisfied with that title.

Part of the reason was surely that she had been too busy learning the work.

But what she had dreamed of and envisioned was 'a servant who attended someone' — and the daily routine of simply working in an apron dress left an unshakeable sense of something being off.

Welcoming guests, tending to them, seeing them off.

It wasn't that the work lacked all fulfillment, but whenever she watched the servants who followed their masters to the estate, she felt that something was different.

A servant of the royal domain had no master to attend, no lord to pledge loyalty to.

In theory, they served the royal family with loyalty, but the members of the royal family were figures above the clouds — beings with whom one neither conversed nor made eye contact.

She might catch a distant glimpse of Kreschenta-sama or His Majesty taking a stroll, but to call that 'serving' was a stretch given the distance.

An indescribable feeling of being closed in.

*At a loss* — that was the phrase for such a situation.

What on earth was she doing here?

Dreams were dreams after all; reality wasn't made like stories.

Just when such resignation began to show its face, the Christand family visited — and with them, attending, was Argan-sama.

How immense the impact of that moment had been for her.

Argan-sama was the kind of person she was, with devastatingly low self-esteem (a trait that might be said to be common to everyone in this manor).

It still frustrated her that this never seemed to fully get across, but in any case, it was a bolt from the blue.

*Someone like this really exists in this world* — her mind felt as if it had been struck with a blunt instrument.

Having one's ideal appear before one's eyes in living form was, in a way, almost violent.

At a single glance she understood, and *ah, this is it* — her entire heart was captivated.

As if light had suddenly pierced through darkness, illuminating everything around it, a path appearing before her — and a world so radiant that she could no longer imagine one without it.

Everyone surely had an ideal of *what they wanted to become* in childhood.

As one grew up, one acquired a measure of good sense and resigned oneself to *I can never become that* — such dreams.

If one were a man, it might be a general.

But no matter how hard one tried, fulfilling a dream required luck, talent, and circumstance — things not granted to everyone.

Even a centurion was one among a hundred who aspired — children's dreams not coming true was normal.

She thought it was no shame to give that up; it was simply the good sense of an adult.

Had she not met Argan-sama that day, she would probably have been one of those people.

She wouldn't have thought that a bad thing, and she would surely have been happy enough.

She might have returned home, married some gentleman, been blessed with children — and laughed about how she'd once been so foolish as to go to the royal domain, making it a funny story without shame.

She might have worn a knowing face and lectured some child who dreamed foolish dreams just as she once had.

But her ideal had appeared before her, in the form of Argan-sama.

She could no longer laugh it off as a foolish ideal or a child's fairy tale.

To serve the same master at this person's side.

Once the radiance of such a future had taken shape, all that remained was to reach for it and step forward.

Looking back, her entire life came down to that encounter.

Before and after that meeting, everything about her ordinary self had changed.

"Heh, I see……Youth is a force indeed."

"I-I'm sorry. I got carried away……"

After the final battle of the civil war.

Lying in the cot of the tent was Gallen-sama, who had fallen slightly ill after the fighting.

While nursing him and talking, the conversation had turned to how she had come to Christand.

Embarrassed at having become so impassioned, Gallen-sama shook his head with a wry smile.

"No, that's just right. Bery is……well, to put it unkindly, she's a bit difficult, or rather, she has a tendency to be shy around people."

"……Shy?"

Gallen-sama sat up and nodded, reaching for the herbal tea on the box that served as a side table.

"Especially in the old days. It's been some twenty years since we first met, but for the first ten or so, I can hardly recall having a proper conversation with her. I almost never saw her smile. She was an exceedingly quiet girl to begin with……she's become much more open now, but that doesn't mean what was once a weakness has become a strength."

"Is that……so?"

Gallen-sama nodded and brought the herbal tea to his lips.

Shy — that was not the impression she gave at all.

She was gentle and courteous with everyone, never without a smile, the sort of person who was liked and never made enemies.

If anything, she had an approachable air about her, and surely that wasn't limited to her alone.

"Her elder sister Lazura was the polar opposite, a spirited woman of great vigor……she even had Bogan under her thumb, but those two sisters were truly sun and moon. After losing Lazura, Bery pushed herself terribly, but……it was truly a good turn of fate. Meeting Krische seems to have improved things a great deal."

The same could be said of Selene, Gallen-sama continued, and took a breath.

"Krische is, as you can see, a somewhat unusual girl. She can do difficult things almost too well, yet sometimes she doesn't understand the simplest things. When I decided to entrust her to Bogan, I worried whether she'd be accepted, but……perhaps it was precisely because Krische was the way she was that things worked out. There was likely something they had in common."

*Something in common,* she thought, picturing the two of them.

If asked whether they were similar, they were really quite different.

But she did understand how difficult it was to earn the heartfelt respect of someone like Krische-sama, who was so exacting with both herself and others.

A girl who could calmly instruct warriors whose fame echoed across the surrounding world.

A talent that could not be captured by the single word 'genius.'

Even Selene-sama, whom an ordinary person like her could only call a genius, didn't even register as a point of comparison in that regard.

"Being too intelligent, she probably sees too much. That's why she tries to reason through everything, and sometimes gets lost in those dead ends.……Unable to notice things that she'd realize easily if she simply followed her honest feelings."

Gallen-sama's eyes softened, and he looked at her.

"……In that sense, having someone like you come to the manor is something I'm truly grateful for."

"B-but, I'm not anything like——I practically barged in uninvited……I'm still nothing but trouble."

"I doubt anyone thinks you're trouble.……Your simple admiration alone drove you to leave the royal domain — and that way of being is surely something quite dazzling to Bery. Of course, some would call you foolish."

He smiled wryly and tapped his own head.

"But being clever doesn't necessarily lead to happiness. Being able to act on your heart's impulse, as you do — that is the greatest virtue of all."

"Virtue……"

"Your parents' upbringing must have been good, too.……Once things settle down, do write them a letter. They must be terribly worried."

"Y-……yes."

Gallen-sama wore a kind smile, and she grew a little embarrassed, her eyes wandering.

When she had left home, she had a tremendous fight with her father.

At the time she had wondered why he couldn't understand — but of course a father would worry about his daughter.

She was aware she owed him an apology.

"……That's good, being honest. Of course you have your reasons too, but parents worry about their children no matter how old they get. Words that feel nagging are also a form of love.……If it ends as just a quarrel, that's fine — but sometimes things get tangled, and sometimes you lose the chance to apologize forever."

"……Yes."

"I too parted from my father in a quarrel — he was a father I respected, yet he tried to stop me from becoming a soldier, and in a moment of emotion I cursed him……by the time I returned, I could no longer even apologize."

His eyes grew distant as he spoke.

"True foolishness is something like that.……To make sure that doesn't happen, put it into words properly."

She nodded, and he patted her head.

He had seemed stern when she first met him, but he was kind through and through.

His voice was quiet, composed, and surprisingly soft.

"As adults, it's actually harder to follow one's honest heart. Enslaved by petty pride, cause and effect, logic and reason, position and responsibility — I've done nothing but pile up such foolish regrets.……I never gave proper words to the wife I'd promised to make happy, let her depart alone……I only said 'I love you' honestly a handful of times, back when I was young."

He lowered his eyes sadly, closed them.

Then he looked at her and continued.

"So being able to speak your honest heart to others without any pretense, as you do — I believe that is the finest talent of all."

Her encounter with the woman called Bery Argan had been an impact that turned the entire world she had known on its head.

A path to a world as elegant as a picture-book — yet in a landscape where everything shone, even walking felt presumptuous.

In a world where everything shone, she alone felt like a foreign body.

"……Cherish that quality. Simply by doing so, you'll surely walk the right path where many others go astray.……It is a great treasure that surpasses foolish cleverness."

And so, she couldn't say how much courage those words of Gallen-sama had given her.

She was probably a fool to an absurd degree, she thought.

She was used to being told as much, and she felt it was true of herself.

That was precisely why being told *that is something wonderful* — she had never heard anything like it before — and how grateful, how happy those words had made her, Gallen-sama surely had no idea.

"……What's wrong?"

"I-I'm sorry. I was just so happy……"

Gallen-sama's hand patted her head gently, looking troubled and smiling wryly.

Unable to stop the tears spilling forth, she dabbed at her eyes with a handkerchief, head bowed, letting him.

Before her — lost and at a loss — if Argan-sama had been the one who created heaven and earth,

"Good grief, it's nothing to cry about."

Gallen-sama was the one who gave her the courage to walk through that world.

To her, the two of them were something like gods.

Surely both of them would look troubled and deny it, saying the praise was excessive.

*——But that was what she believed.*

# Chapter 325: Creation of Heaven and Earth — The Eternal Long Road, Two

*——Rows of small bottles lined the cupboard, each containing tea leaves.*

In the royal castle, there was a room dedicated to storing tea leaves and the like.

"Karen-sama. It's been a while. We'd seen each other from afar, but……"

She had left the royal domain, the civil war had ended, and now she was back.

The Christand family had moved into one of the estates used for royal lodging.

Naturally, there were many familiar faces, and she occasionally ran into acquaintances.

She had come to fetch tea leaves from the royal castle's storeroom, and there she found Karen Schlotte-sama.

A charming woman with lovely chestnut hair, very personable.

Also from a marquis family, she had come to the royal domain about two years before her.

Karen-sama had been her training supervisor, and since they shared a room, they quickly became friends — during her time in the royal domain, they had often been paired for work.

After becoming a servant of the Christand household and returning to the royal domain, they had caught each other's eye from a distance several times, but it had been quite a while since they had properly spoken.

However, in stark contrast to her own smile,

"Ughhh……"

Karen-sama took one look at her and furrowed her brow, making a distinctly complicated face.

Then she pressed a hand to her forehead and sighed.

"Long time no see.……Haah, I still can't believe that *you* of all people are a servant at the side of Her Majesty the Queen.……The power of initiative is a frightening thing."

"I didn't expect things to turn out like this either when I left the royal domain……"

She answered with a wry smile, leaning against the shelves.

She had left the royal castle simply because she wanted to study at Argan-sama's side — but through some twist of fate, it had been one upheaval after another.

The nation fell into civil war, and on top of that, the princess Kreschenta-sama had come to shelter with Christand — it had been one surprise after another for her as well.

"But, well……everyone seems so astonished, it's a bit ticklish."

"Of course they are. Anyone would be astonished. Clumsy Anne is just casually walking next to Her Majesty the Queen. The first time I saw it, I thought my eyes would pop out. I didn't just double-take — I questioned my eyes at least seven times."

"S-seven times……"

"You said something incomprehensible about meeting your destiny and just up and left the royal domain, and then a little later you come back as Her Majesty's attendant — who on earth is supposed to believe that?"

Karen-sama sighed once more.

She was absolutely right, and there was no denying it.

"S-strictly speaking, I'm not exactly an attendant……"

"You're not?"

"The Christand household only has three servants, so by circumstance I often end up in that role, but……in terms of rank, ability, and Her Majesty's trust, the formal attendant is Argan-sama. I would be her substitute."

In practice, Argan-sama always accompanied the Queen at important ceremonies, festivals, and when receiving foreign dignitaries.

Given Her Majesty's position, that was how it should always be, but Argan-sama was kept busy with Christand household financial management and various other duties.

She and Elvena-sama accompanied the Queen during less important, routine occasions — and since Elvena-sama usually helped Krische-sama, it was simply by process of elimination that she ended up at Her Majesty's side.

It wasn't explicitly decided who the Queen's attendant was, and within the Christand household, the matter was treated rather casually (for such an important role, it was surprisingly lax — often decided in the flow of conversation).

However, between her and Elvena-sama at least, there was a shared understanding that the formal attendant was Argan-sama.

Her Majesty's moods were easy to read, and when Argan-sama was at her side, her spirits were remarkably good.

The better her mood, the more talkative the Queen became; the worse her mood, the more taciturn — and at Argan-sama's side, she would happily complain all day long, voicing demands she would never make to her or Elvena-sama: *this isn't enough, that's missing.*

The sight of her like a child craving attention was so adorable it made one's chest ache.

"Argan-sama single-handedly manages all of Christand's affairs and is extremely busy, so it's difficult for her to leave the manor every day."

"I feel like there's no duty that should take priority over attending Her Majesty, but……no, well, I suppose the situation is the situation. Putting an unknown person at the Queen's side in this situation would be worrying."

"Yes. Given her position."

Her Majesty was adorable beyond measure, but at the same time, she drew a clear line between inside and outside.

She must have been raised in circumstances beyond what someone like her could understand.

The premise that her life was something to be targeted — her vigilance was very strong.

She noticed the most trivial changes.

For instance, when she had stepped out of a room in the castle: *this teacup has been moved slightly, there are traces that this was touched* — even the slightest change would trigger visible wariness.

She always had guards posted at every room she used, and during her time in the castle, she barred even the castle's own servants from entering.

When she had secretly tidied up to be helpful, the Queen spent a long while silently inspecting the entire room.

She had been slow to realize what was happening, and when she explained that she had done it, she was quite scolded for doing something so confusing — a recent memory still fresh.

In daily life, everything the Queen wore was kept stored at the manor since *who knew what might be done to it* — she kept no personal belongings or daily items at the castle at all. In such matters, she was thorough.

The Queen's demeanor at the manor versus outside was subtly different; outside, there was often a slightly taut edge in the air.

Given that she was this way, it would indeed be difficult to increase the number of servants close to her.

Elvena-sama had been saved from slavery by Krische-sama and owed a deep debt to the Christand household.

Her elder sister was also a member of Krische-sama's personal troops, and that was likely the basis of trust.

She herself had come to Christand purely for Argan-sama's sake.

She hadn't come with the presumptuous goal of attending the Queen, and it was mere coincidence.

Perhaps the Queen judged that a woman this clumsy could hardly be an assassin — in any case, she was trusted.

However, given such circumstances, bringing in a servant other than herself or Elvena-sama, who lived under the same roof, would require substantial justification.

The numbers were certainly small.

For ceremonies and festivals the manor had to be left empty, and having one more person like Karen-sama would be reassuring — but she didn't imagine the Queen would place someone near her simply on the grounds that she was a trusted senior servant.

"I've heard there were such incidents when Her Majesty was young……multiple princes dying under suspicious circumstances."

"Yes, politics is a murky world, isn't it……"

It was only recently that the late Prince had been spreading claims that the Queen had killed the siblings.

The world of the royal court was a dangerous place rife with intrigue, and Argan-sama's presence was surely part of why the Queen's mood was so much better when she was near.

By ordinary standards, the Queen's caution would probably be considered neurotic.

She took care to attend to every small detail so as not to trouble Her Majesty, and when Argan-sama accompanied her, the Queen's air was as soft as when she was at the manor.

Trust and confidence — even if the Queen trusted her and Elvena-sama, the only one she truly *relied upon* was probably Argan-sama alone.

Wishing for more people was asking for the impossible — first she should become that kind of person herself, earning the trust of a servant who made the shortage of numbers irrelevant.

"It's an enviable position, or maybe not — that's the sort of place you're in.……Christand is a garden of flowers, there are no gentlemen to be found, and it seems like nothing but mental exhaustion."

"A garden of flowers……"

"Of course, serving Her Majesty the Queen and the Kingdom's Marshal sounds glamorous, but……they're all women. For someone like me, it's nothing but stress and no perks."

"Karen-sama, you're so, well, pragmatic——"

"Oh, hush. That's normal. Most servants are looking for romantic prospects."

Karen-sama said it with a glare.

"From a normal servant's perspective, *you're* the abnormal one. Being happy just being a servant……do you plan to spend your whole life like that? What about marriage?"

"I haven't given it much thought for now……of course, it's not that I have absolutely no desire for a household of my own, but I thought I'd consider such things when the right encounter comes along."

"With that attitude, you'll be left on the shelf before you know it. You really are a shame in so many ways……"

And Karen-sama leaned in close.

# Chapter 328: Creation of Heaven and Earth — The Eternal Long Road, End

The first decade flew by too.

A decade of going out to shop and traveling to see all sorts of towns and cultures.

The heroic epic and continental unification chronicle of the great Queen, Krische-sama, Selene-sama and the others.

Beings who remade the world out of grief over a servant's death.

The days of quaking through such exchanges were long past.

Arguments and scuffles between Argan-sama and Selene-sama were constant in the manor — and how peaceful were the days filled with such arguments and scuffles.

Through civil war and wars and tragedies — and beyond all of that, childish bickering.

A manor of eternity, hidden quietly outside the world.

The words painted a lofty image, but what existed here was not some otherworldly grandeur, only perfectly ordinary daily life — and that was what everyone wanted.

Each time she felt that, she was relieved from the bottom of her heart.

Everyone treasured these trivial days — feeling that, she was reassured.

"It feels strange, somehow, walking through this city."

"Strange……?"

"……I should have lived here for ten years, and yet it's like visiting for the first time. I used to go shopping with Krische-sama every day when we were in Gurgain, and yet……"

Argan-sama had spent almost all her time in the royal domain, inside the manor.

It was inevitable given her position — in terms of time spent outside, Argan-sama had probably the least of anyone.

"Just walking through town like this, shopping, doing the most ordinary things — I never did any of that back then……fufu."

Argan-sama's gaze softened as she looked around at the town.

"……I just remembered something I'd been thinking for a while — that someday I wanted to go out to town with no particular errand, just strolling around, looking at things, shopping."

Then, *another dream come true,* she said, and giggled happily.

Whenever something needed doing, she and Elvena-sama had volunteered.

They'd told themselves they mustn't waste the time of Argan-sama, the most capable of them all — but hearing those words, she froze, wondering if it had actually been the opposite.

In hindsight, Argan-sama was the one who most valued precisely that kind of seemingly wasteful time.

*'Argan-sama, where are you going?'*

*'It seems Ojou-sama forgot her lunch basket, so I thought I'd bring it to her……'*

*'Leave it to me! I'll go instead!'*

*'Um, well, I'm already done with work, and Kreschenta-sama won't be up for a while, so Anne-sama, why don't you take a little bre——'*

*'I'm fine! You rest, Argan-sama……!'*

*'……Y-yes. Well then, please do……'*

There must have been days when she wanted to take a leisurely stroll outside.

Argan-sama would create slack in the schedule, and then spend that time doing nothing in particular, using it extravagantly — she loved that.

*Argan-sama is lazy and wastes time.*

Kreschenta-sama often said so, and in a way it was an accurate assessment.

Argan-sama herself often said she was lazy, and she now understood that it was truly not modesty but heartfelt.

Finishing work at superhuman speed, then spending ages indulging Kreschenta-sama's whims, or having tea, or playing with Gururun — using time elegantly, extravagantly, wasting it away — that was Argan-sama.

She used to wonder: *she's so busy; is she really all right?* But most likely, Argan-sama did her daily work precisely *for* that purpose.

She had simply assumed that because Argan-sama settled into the royal domain so effortlessly, even shaping the environment to suit her, she was someone who could carry on perfectly anywhere.

But surely, compared to life in Gurgain, there had been inconveniences, troubles, hardships — things she endured. One or two at the very least.

Only now, belatedly, did she realize such an obvious thing, and her eyes darted.

"Ehehe, from now on Krische will take you to all sorts of places. It's peaceful now, so places like Arna that we said we wanted to visit together, and all kinds of mountains and seas……"

"That sounds lovely. I'd love to see many different places."

"Yes! Space, and……oh, the moon would be nice too. There's nothing but sand and rock, but the stars are beautiful, and this planet sparkles like a jewel. Krische thinks Bery would like it too!"

"My. Fufu, I can't wait."

Looking at Krische-sama, she thought: *ah.*

Krische-sama probably understood Argan-sama all along.

They had been avoiding this area since people who knew them might still be around, but once they could go out again, Krische-sama had been making an unusual number of travel suggestions.

Under the guise of shopping trips, she had apparently taken Kreschenta-sama all around the world in advance, scouting beautiful scenery.

*It's like this and this, and the view is really beautiful,* Krische-sama would explain to Argan-sama — and most likely, Krische-sama's idea of a 'nice view' or a 'beautiful place' was exactly that.

A view that Argan-sama would enjoy was, to Krische-sama, a beautiful view.

Just walking through town, and Krische-sama's eyes seemed to sparkle.

As if the scenery around her were radiant, its brilliance reflected in those eyes.

Darting happily around Argan-sama.

*This shop, that shop* — beckoning, Krische-sama's delighted figure.

Just watching from behind warmed her chest — and that warmth nearly rose to sting her eyes, and she shook her head.

The great war that accompanied Alberan's fall had been not so long ago.

They had decided not to interfere with this side of things, but they hadn't been indifferent, and she had been somewhat anxious about how things were going.

But Albernaria, visited again after so long, was almost exactly as she remembered it.

And in this place, Krische-sama wore a truly happy smile alongside Argan-sama.

Without a care, without a worry.

She had thought there might be something nagging — some lingering feeling.

All things considered, this was the best course — Krische-sama and Selene-sama had made many arrangements with Aleha-sama and the others on this side, doing what needed to be done, bringing a hundred years of peace to a war-torn continent.

They had built, for a time, a historically remarkable peace — and yet, perhaps there had been a slight sense of guilt.

Depending on how you looked at it, they had abandoned the possibility of even greater peace and found happiness for themselves alone.

It could be seen that way, and if people knew, some would think so.

Because of that, the kingdom had split, war had broken out, and people who might not have died had died. That was a fact.

A lingering guilt left behind.

If the war had dragged on and devolved into a quagmire, that guilt would have grown far stronger — and so she was glad that the war had ended so quickly, that the world was moving toward a new era.

Remembering the boy (whose eyes really were very narrow, matching his nickname) who had gazed at Krische-sama with sparkling admiration — was it too much to think that this peace, too, might have been something offered to Krische-sama?

In any case, this peace and happiness were not theirs alone.

"Over there is Mia and Kalua's dojo. It's a pretty big dojo, and——"

As she took in the nostalgic scenery and savored the small joys of each day, her gratitude for all those people welled up.

Such scenery would not last forever.

Someday it would surely fade — but that was not a sad thing.

It would simply melt and blur into the gentle haze of memory.

In that soft parting, it would remain, never truly disappearing.

*——I won't last much longer.*

One day, drinking tea in Gallen-sama's room, she heard those words.

"These days, I often think about what will happen after I'm gone."

"Gallen-sama, you mustn't say such——"

"It's not a gloomy subject. People die eventually; I'll simply part from Krische and you all a bit earlier than the rest……that's all."

True to his words, there was nothing dark in his voice — the same gentle tone as always.

The same gentle, softly-laughing smile.

"Grace and Gorka……When Krische lost her parents, I lamented how little time I had left. I wanted to stay at that child's side and watch over her, to take their place……but there was nothing to be done about age. After Krische killed the bandits to protect the village, the villagers feared her, and she had no place to belong……I argued with Galla over and over — she'd been looking after Krische in Grace's stead — about what to do."

He brought the black soybean tea to his lips and looked up at the ceiling.

"That's why I entrusted her to Bogan.……If I'd been younger, I might have left the village and lived with her somewhere else, but given my age, I wouldn't last long. She was clever, talented — she'd have no trouble surviving on her own. But precisely because of that, I wanted her to find someone she could rely on."

And he narrowed his eyes.

"I wanted someone who would love her, watch over her."

To her ears, those words sounded like a prayer.

"Back then, I couldn't have imagined how things are now.……That Krische would always be right here, at my side……I truly began to feel she might be with me to the very end."

His voice caught, slightly hoarse, but continued with warmth.

"But as it turned out, I'm here alone. Krische is at the manor, laughing happily, living surrounded by many people……and I've become just another old man who drops in from time to time to dote on his granddaughter."

"Gallen-sama……"

"Feeling a little wistful, yet……strange as it is, truly happy."

Then, with a wry smile, he turned to her.

"Now I'm just one among many who love her, and she loves me as one among many. The people who love her and are loved by her are too many to count, and I'm no longer someone special to Krische. She'll grieve my passing……but that grief will fade amid so much love, and in time I'll become just one of many memories."

She wanted to say *that's not true* — but couldn't.

Gallen-sama was roughly the same age as her father, but her father was still youthful, and Gallen-sama looked far older.

He was neither noble nor mana-wielder; even if he lived several more years, he wouldn't live for decades.

No words would change anything, and he wasn't seeking comfort.

He had already accepted all of it.

"Her Majesty the Queen, despite her youth, is a wise sovereign. Now that the recent war is over, peaceful days will continue……and as long as they do, Krische is the gentlest of girls, one who loves such days. Not everything will go smoothly, of course, but she is surrounded by many kind people — including you — and what happened in that small village won't happen again.……I can't see the future, but that child's future is quite bright……surrounded by much love."

His voice carried an inexpressible tenderness.

He didn't stand at Krische-sama's side the way Argan-sama did — yet surely he held the same depth of feeling, treasured in his chest even across the distance, and she felt that, and lowered her gaze.

"Do you think parting is a sad thing?"

"……At least, right now, yes."

"Of course, sad partings exist……but so do many that aren't. I've commanded many subordinates. There are many I haven't seen since leaving the military, but quite a few are surely living happy, peaceful lives on different paths. Whether they're alive or dead, I may never know."

*Parting is like that,* he said.

"Life is full of such partings. Not just death. I'll never see those people again. But each walked their own path, and parting as a result is not a sad thing. Surely you have friends like that too?"

"That's……yes."

Karen-sama, who had been like an older sister, had returned home and sent a letter saying she was to marry.

Her partner was a lower-ranking noble but a fine young man — the letter was bubbling with excitement.

They would probably never meet again.

She had felt a little wistful, but it wasn't a sad thing.

"That's how it is. If you live long, there will be many more such partings. Death is just one such parting, and if it turns out to be a good one — that's enough."

Gallen-sama looked at her gently.

"I'm glad you're fond of this old man……but don't feel you need to grieve. The same is true for Krische. I feel death drawing near, and yet I can smile, sit here drinking tea with a kind girl like you, and feel happy.……You may not understand yet, being young, but it's really not a bad thing."

At some point the tears had started falling, and Gallen-sama smiled wryly, stood, and gently stroked her head.

"As many partings blur into shining memories, fading and wavering with time into recollections, each time something catches your eye, you'll find yourself smiling without knowing why.……One day, you too will see that kind of scenery. And when that day comes——"

The hand was rough, yet strangely soft.

"If you remember an old man's idle musings over tea and smile — I'd be glad."

*——As one trivial memory among many.*

Few things have been described with as many words as the beauty of the moon.

A waxing crescent turned the sky to deep black; a full moon painted it deep indigo.

It was always the moon that set the color of the starry sky.

Not as dazzling as the sun, its outline clear — perhaps that modest radiance was what made it so.

Gazing at it every day, the moon held an allure that made one lose oneself.

What it was, she understood in some academic terms — but for her, the moon was a distant vista, a kind of painting.

Something one could never touch — a concept, a beauty that existed only in the imagination.

*——To think she was now standing in such a place.*

Stretched before her, an undistorted horizon of perfect clarity.

It nearly threw off her sense of perspective.

Part of it was the sand and rock, and part of it was the scenery being far too visible in the distance.

Though the sun was visible, the sky held the stars of night.

Far away, a blue sphere — the world where they normally lived — floated against a backdrop of stars and void.

Hanging there as naturally as anything, shining like a jewel.

"Is that black soybean tea?"

"Yes, I was in the mood."

"Maybe I'll have some too. I haven't really had any since coming here……"

At Selene-sama's words, she nodded and poured some into a cup.

Having finished packing up the tent and supplies they'd brought, this was a moment's rest before heading back.

Selene-sama sat on the crate beside her, brought the black soybean tea to her lips — and frowned.

"……Isn't this a bit strong?"

"Oh, shall I dilute it?"

"No, it's fine, but……you do like it strong, don't you."

"Black soybean tea, well……Gallen-sama preferred it about this strong, and I got in the habit."

*I see,* Selene-sama chuckled, and cradled the carved-wood cup in both hands, gazing at the blue star.

Argan-sama was strolling with the two excited ones perched on her shoulders. Lila-sama and Elvena-sama were apparently performing some Kreisharana ritual——

With solemn expressions, they bounced about, stacking moon rocks into a mysterious tower.

Trips usually ended in these groupings.

The energetic duo and the ritual-starting Lila-sama were too much energy, and Selene-sama, looking a bit tired, often spent these moments lounging beside her.

"No matter how many times I see it, it's beautiful. We've seen all kinds of beautiful scenery, but……"

"Truly. I never imagined we'd come to a place like this. I got a little carried away, at my age."

"Fufu, they were talking about how Selene-sama didn't come back for dinner."

Strangely, everything was lighter on the moon.

The heavier the celestial body, the stronger the gravity; the lighter, the weaker — and the light bouncing of what seemed like a short walk had led Selene-sama to get lost for half a day.

"It's a bit lonely, though. The sky is beautiful and the floatiness is fun, but there's nothing else but sand and rock……"

"Yes……it's enjoyable as a trip, but if someone told you to live here, it would be rough."

Normally one couldn't even breathe, and there wasn't a single living creature.

Not a blade of grass grew — apart from the starry sky, the scenery could be called bleak.

"I'm pretty satisfied, and I think I'll be fine for the next twenty or thirty years. It's not like the ocean or mountains where you want to go back every year……I just want to relax at the manor for a while. My body's sense of balance is probably off too."

"I bet it'll feel incredibly heavy."

She laughed wryly, and Selene-sama nodded, sipping her tea.

She too savored the bitter taste, and slowly turned her eyes skyward.

As if cutting out the view, sealing it away into memory.

"Black soybean tea while gazing down at the earth from the moon……after a hundred years of such absurdities, time's just flown by. We're using time so extravagantly, I almost feel guilty about it."

"It's true — these are days I couldn't have imagined when we were over there."

"Same here. I'm appalled at how lazy a person I turned out to be. I was supposed to humor that idiot, and before I knew it, I'd become a fellow idiot. I wonder what Aleha and the others would think if they saw us."

She watched Selene-sama sigh, laughed, thought for a moment, and spoke.

"……When I was dying over there, I felt strangely happy."

"……?"

"Happy, old memories kept surfacing, wrapping around me……and I imagined the scenery I hadn't yet seen. I thought: even if I don't wake up, that might be all right. It was that comfortable."

That must have been what Gallen-sama had spoken of.

Simply comfortable, happy — a gentle time, gradual days.

"When Gallen-sama passed, I was terribly sad at the time……but having stood in that same place myself, I understood that Gallen-sama was truly happy. And it probably wasn't just him……when we went to Albernaria the other day, I felt something similar."

"Ah, you did seem pensive during dinner."

"Yes……fufu, only we are here. We drifted to a different place from all those people, and we parted.……But when we taste that nostalgic scenery, that nostalgic food, somehow……"

She gazed at the distant figures of the two, riding happily on Argan-sama's shoulders.

"I think it's because many people tried to leave something behind. In their final moments, perhaps they pictured us as we are now, and departed smiling……I can't help but feel that."

Albernaria remained as it was — the beautiful White City.

From Kalua-sama and the others' swordsmanship academy came the lively voices of children, and at Koza-sama and the others' restaurant, they savored cooking as delicious as ever.

Surely each had their own reasons.

But among those reasons, she thought, there was surely something like that.

"A blessing for a new world's journey……so that the departure might be a little less lonely."

She saw Gallen-sama's kind face reflected in the dark tea.

"……As a benediction for the long road ahead."

She closed her eyes, and a smile came naturally.

Most of those people were now only memories.

Wistfulness came and went, but more than that, a warmth settled in her chest and colored it.

Diluted by the ever-growing happy memories, much would fade and blur and lose its shape.

But such gentle memories would surely go on casting their faint light within her heart forever.

"You've been staring off into space by yourself, but is that what you were thinking about?"

"Well……drinking black soybean tea just reminds me of Gallen-sama, and I get lost in thought……"

"……Please don't burst into tears out of nowhere. I always think something's happened."

"Th-that's……I'm sorry……"

She let her gaze wander, and Selene-sama smiled wryly and gently stroked her head.

"But, well, I agree with you. Being able to enjoy the moon like idiots with those idiots — that's thanks to people like that. Alberan ended about as ideally as it could, and now it's a new era……we made preparations for that, sure, but that alone doesn't make things go smoothly."

"……Yes."

"It's because so many people thought: let that idiot's selfishness be just a little bit happier. Being here now isn't just because she was amazing, or anything like that……it's because all those people were watching over us."

*We should be grateful,* she said with a gentle smile.

"……Not that you of all people would ever forget that."

The palm was harder than a woman's should be — and strangely soft.

She felt Gallen-sama's hand in it, and shook her head.

"No, I wouldn't be so sure. I'm the type who can't even remember what I ate three days ago……since the first century has passed, I thought I'd write a book — a summary of everything from back there through these hundred years, using my diaries."

"……Didn't you write a similar book about fifty years ago? Those absurdly thick three volumes?"

"Those covered only the days over there, so it's a bit different. This time I want to include that gratitude, everything from coming here through the first hundred years."

"I-I see……"

"This time it will likely be five volumes. Actually, I've already decided on the title——"
