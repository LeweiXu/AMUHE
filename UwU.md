You bear a child in search of a reason to exist, and not a single thing you do differs from any other human being.'*

*'……Would you like me to kill you?'*

*'Goodness, no. Just a little instruction from your new tutor, stepping into Benuure-sama's role.'*

Coolly sipping her tea, narrowing her eyes.

Never dropping her composed air, showing neither fear nor timidity.

Nothing but an infuriating woman.

*'We are merely a herd of beasts, in the end. Each one freely pursuing their own interests, as they please. Money, the opposite sex, power……everyone is free to do as they wish. You are the same — by loving Elsreine-sama, you merely seek the return of being loved yourself, using her for that purpose. Even pure altruism exists only because of the return of self-satisfaction. Whether consciously or unconsciously varies from person to person, but……'*

Mockingly, Fiiri continued.

*'This is the world we live in. If you call it worthless, then you should die. There is no human being who can love another without any return at all. ……And you should know this already, and yet you go on living — is it not because you hoped that someone in this worthless world would give you a reason to live?'*

*'…………'*

*'But the world will not reshape itself just to suit you. It is you who must change, Reine-sama. If only you would open those closed eyes, you would find no shortage of people who seek your love, who wish to love you, who wish to give you a reason to live.'*

*——Including me, of course.*

She reached out her hand, touched Reine's cheek, and traced her skin with tender affection.

*'Won't you start by accepting my love? I promise you will not regret it. So that Reine-sama need never close her eyes again……I will grant your every wish.'*

Then she withdrew her hand, and with a soft laugh, covered her mouth and let the laughter spill.

She drained her tea, and stood.

*'It is late, so I shall take my leave, Your Majesty. ……Please think it over at your leisure. Tomorrow morning, I shall come to tidy and clean——'*

Reine seized her hand and threw her onto the bed.

She fell without resistance, and Fiiri stared up at Reine in blank astonishment.

*'……You can make that sort of face, too.'*

*'Well, naturally……I am human, after all.'*

Saying so, her gaze wavered and her cheeks flushed; she let out a sigh laced with a deep breath.

She loosened the tension from her body and gave an embarrassed, wry smile.

*'Am I to take this as accepting my earlier words?'*

*'Hardly. ……I simply felt like seeing just how worthless this love you speak of really is.'*

*'Ah, as expected of Her Majesty. You can even discern whether another's love is true or false through the joining of skin. What a splendid talent——'*

Reine kissed her, pushed her tongue in, and ran it across every surface.

For an instant, Fiiri went rigid, wide eyes staring, but did not resist.

*'……Be quiet.'*

At those words, Fiiri obediently fell silent and nodded, her gaze unsteady.

Most things in this world were little more than tools that happened to speak.

They were different from Reine and her kind — it was merely a whim, using one such tool in that way.

There was no great meaning to it; she had not accepted the unpleasant woman's words, nor changed her mind, nor loved her.

It was just a passing fancy.

That unpleasant woman who smiled and smiled, with those mocking, condescending eyes — they had grown tiresome enough, and she simply wanted a small measure of satisfaction. That was all.

She was curious to see what lay beneath that infuriating mask.

*'……In the end, everything in this world is an illusion no different from a dream, Reine-sama.'*

Yet even after it was over, she pressed her bare body close and spouted lecture-like nonsense.

*'We are creatures with uncertain things called hearts, and we express them in various forms, and receive them. We press our bodies close, touch each other, weave words to make things seem certain — and yet, no matter how far you go, you can never convey or read a heart directly. Each person simply receives things in their own self-serving interpretation, seeing only the reality that suits them.'*

Nothing but grating words.

*'If it can never be known regardless, is it not far more rational to choose the interpretation that makes one happy? At the very least, I, who do not wish to make Elsreine-sama sad, will never kill you or harm you. So to begin with, why not simply interpret it conveniently — that you are loved by me?'*

*'That is merely convenient for you.'*

*'Of course. Ahaha, I have no interest in anything beyond my own happiness, after all.'*

Softly, softly laughing, shoulders shaking, eyes narrowing.

As though expressing that what she held was precious, she peered into Reine's eyes.

*'What I desire is the convenient reality of being loved by the two Reines. To be invited into an Elsren where the two of you spend your days happily together, and to share in those happy days. ……Since you cannot bring yourself to kill me, resistance is futile.'*

*I am persistent, after all*, she said with a delighted laugh, taking Reine's arm and pressing her cheek against it.

*'Even if you say no, I shall attend at your side, whispering love, and every word and gesture of yours I shall receive and interpret entirely to my convenience, and devote my entire life to this. ……No matter how much you doubt me, I will tell you I love you until there is no end to it.'*

When Reine said she should never have had Benuure pick her up, Fiiri laughed.

*It is only after it cannot be undone that we call it regret*, she said, rubbing her cheek against Reine's arm.

*'……May I stay and sleep beside you until morning?'*

*'I'm sleeping alone. Get out.'*

*'I only asked. It is only that I wish to sleep beside you.'*

Softly, softly, softly.

Laughing, she pressed still closer and kissed her.

*'When the other Reine-sama returns from the battlefield……from tomorrow, every night, I shall visit your room. And every night, I shall whisper idle words and sleep beside you.'*

Then, as though speaking of a happy future, she used Reine's arm as a pillow.

*'……When she returns, if we both sleep beside her, she will surely be overjoyed. ……She is always so very happy……such a wonderful person.'*

*——Good night, Reine-sama.*

She said what she wished to say, and closed her eyes.

Reine let out a breath as though expelling her irritation.

She was an insufferable woman — but, surely, that was how things would turn out.

Seeing the reality of being made to watch her sleeping face, she felt a certainty that it would.

She had only felt that it would be so.

That future never came.

"…………"

Perhaps the drug was taking effect — lying alone on the bed, drowsiness slowly crept over her.

The same drug she had used to kill her father — it brought death as though falling asleep.

With her mind growing hazy, she wondered why she had been born.

Had all the things she had done to survive truly held any meaning?

Or had she made some mistake, and that was why she was here?

*——Oh, wonderful. Truly wonderful. This child's name is Reine. We shall call her Reine.*

Later, she learned the meaning of the word — something that shines.

A beautiful, magnificent name, the servants said.

She was wanted, and loved, and so Reine came into the world.

*Is that so*, she thought, and convinced herself it was true.

Wanted, and loved — and that was why she was here.

If love had been given, then it was only natural to return love.

*What is love?* — she opened many books and searched, but could not find the answer, and so she simply tried to return what she could. Enough for her mother, who had died so soon, as well — simply doing whatever was to her father's benefit.

Had it not reached him, or had it not been love after all?

*'I am truly sorry, Reine. I love you……truly, I love you. Forgive this foolish father, who used you out of self-preservation……'*

Had what he directed at her at the very end been love?

When she whispered *I love you*, he answered, *I love you*.

That she had indeed been Reine to her father.

Again and again, repeating the same words like a dying man's delirium, until the very last breath.

None of it mattered.

It was over. The answer would never be found.

This was the end, so it did not matter.

*'——If it can never be known regardless, is it not far more rational to choose the interpretation that makes one happy?'*

By doubting everything, by using everything, she had finally been permitted to live.

But had that life held any meaning?

It might indeed be rational.

If there was no happiness to be found, if there was only suffering, then there was no meaning in living.

*'That is not so. You are by no means foolish, either. ……What the princess sought was not something so calculating — it was, beyond any doubt, love from the late king.'*

That she had lived wanting exactly that was, in the end, the truth.

She had been born, and lived to obtain it, and given up when she realized it was not to be — and after that, she had found Fiiri, and Reine was born.

*'……You look down on this worthless world, insisting you are different, and what you truly are is simply afraid — afraid that if you do not believe yourself special. You use the word "special" as self-justification for your own alienation, nothing more. You bear a child in search of a reason to exist, and not a single thing you do differs from any other human being.'*

Recalling that face, even now it stirred resentment and frustration.

The woman who had grandly lectured her had died so quickly, and with her, the sense of purpose had vanished.

Not a single wish had been granted.

*——This is a worthless world after all, Fiiri.*

Or perhaps it was her own fault, for being unable to convince herself that those wishes had been granted.

If she had accepted things sooner, might there have been a different future?

She felt there might have been, and lowered her eyes, curling her body like an infant.

"——Mother!"

Unlike her daughter, Reine could not accept things with such openness.

She could not believe in anyone, in anything — doubting, fearing, pushing away, using — she had known no other way to survive.

"What are you doing!? Someone call a physician!! Mother, stay with me……open your mouth, please, Mother——"

She had known, really. She had simply been afraid.

She grasped the arm that shook her, stilling its movement.

Drawing the other close, she quietly closed her eyes.

If she could have become like that child, would there have been a happy present?

As though shutting away the blurring world, as though embracing herself, she curled inward.

*——As though surrendering body and soul to a dark, tender darkness.*

Elsren was ruled by the Three Grand Dukes.

Reine appeared at parliament for ceremonial purposes, but she neither opened her mouth nor spoke a word.

Only when occasionally asked for her opinion by Gorzarius and the others did she perform calculations and write them on paper.

It was only that ability they needed; her views were not sought.

Still, she was now given more opportunities to see the nation's finances than before, and she would write proposals for improvement on paper and hand them to Gorzarius.

About half were ignored; the other half were approved in parliament.

At the very least, it was better than doing nothing — if it could even slightly improve the lives of the people, she continued to offer proposals.

All were submitted to parliament as proposals from the Three Grand Dukes, and accepted as such.

She understood she was being used for their credit, and yet even that no longer mattered.

This would not last forever; eventually, they too would die.

Another ten years, twenty, perhaps a little longer — and eventually, people who were not like them might emerge.

She hoped to build a good nation with such people.

For that purpose, she thought only of keeping the country from declining further.

Public order was in disarray, and small-scale revolts occurred in various regions.

She heard that some churches had been burned, and children at orphanages had been caught up in the violence and killed.

Nothing large — just minor disturbances.

The people were apparently told it was Alberan's doing, but that did not seem right.

If her mother were serious, the country would be in far worse shape.

Still, hearing it repeated again and again, she sometimes found herself vaguely thinking, *Perhaps it is so.*

Reine had betrayed her mother, and skirmishes continued along the border.

Her mother was far more clever than Reine.

Perhaps she was carrying out schemes that Reine could not perceive.

Reine understood nothing.

Nothing about the country, nothing about anyone — she simply did not understand.

*What do they mean by this?*

*Why are they doing this?*

Everything had become incomprehensible to Reine.

She was anxious about how her words would be received. Anxious about how her actions would be received.

Even communicating by writing was somewhat frightening.

"Elsreine-sama, it's finished!"

Looking at the orphanage children put her at ease, if only a little.

A girl showed Reine the picture she had drawn, and Reine smiled and patted her head.

After watching for decades, even the pictures she had once found indecipherable had become clear.

The picture showed the orphanage, and the orphans together with Reine.

Everyone wore smiles, looking happy as they stood side by side.

From their little distinguishing features — this one was that child, and this one was this child.

When she pointed them out, the girl laughed delightedly and said *That's right!*, and seeing that made Reine happy, too.

"I wanted to try painting like Elsreine-sama's……"

She patted the girl's head, and the girl laughed, *Ehehe*, and seeing this, the other children came to show their pictures too.

This place never changed, no matter when she visited.

The director here had been an orphan boy who had taken over, and now he was a fine man, teaching the children many things.

They were all good children — they quarreled sometimes, but they were close, and every time she came, they welcomed Reine with happy faces.

Surely, someday, this would become a good country.

She could believe that if children like these grew up, things would change — and each time she felt a little low, she could come here and recover that feeling.

"When I grow up, I want to be like Elsreine-sama——"

"Elsreine-sama."

She turned toward the voice and saw Fenera's joyful face.

She tilted her head, and Fenera smiled. *You have a visitor*, she said.

The man who appeared from beside her — unlike Fenera, who was a mana-bearer, his features had aged considerably, but there was still a trace of the person she remembered.

"……!"

She tried to call out *Neil*, but her voice would not come, and her eyes darted about.

Then she smiled and walked toward him.

He had been a boy at the orphanage at the same time as Fenera.

"……It has been a long time, Elsreine-sama."

She nodded at his words and bowed her head.

When Neil looked puzzled, Fenera spoke.

"The thing is……various things happened, and her voice stopped working."

"……Her voice."

Neil furrowed his brow and let his gaze waver.

Was he worried about her? She shook her head and put on a smile.

She had heard that Neil, after leaving this place, had apprenticed himself to a craftsman.

Yet his build was that of a fighter, and his hands bore the calluses particular to one who wielded a sword.

She found it odd but did not press the matter, and when he suggested they go out to the garden, she followed his lead.

She listened to his conversation with Fenera as they walked.

Fenera asked what he had been doing, and he said *this and that*; Neil in turn asked Fenera, in her apron dress, what had happened in her life.

The conversation on the way to the garden was not particularly long.

"……This'll do."

"This spot……?"

"Yeah. ……I'm sorry, Fenera."

The moment Neil spoke those words, he put his fingers to his lips and whistled.

Then he wrapped his arm around the bewildered Fenera's neck and pressed a hidden knife to her throat.

"Neil! What are you——!?"

"Elsreine-sama, please do not move."

Seeing the bewildered Reine, Neil spoke.

What she heard were screams from the direction of the carriage parked at the entrance.

From the surroundings, too, dozens of men appeared, scrambling over the walls that enclosed the orphanage, overpowering the soldiers through sheer numbers as they broke in.

"……Please, will you die quietly?"

He tightened his grip around Fenera's struggling neck and brought the knife closer.

The men who appeared were grinning. *Good work, Neil*, they said.

Even Reine, confused as she was, could understand the situation.

These men were Neil's companions.

"So this is Elsreine. Well, well……to think she really comes to play at a place this poorly guarded, with only that handful of escorts. Playing the saint……must be a real laugh."

"Letting those rotten lords run wild while you play at charity with the money squeezed from the people."

The men hurled abuse at Reine with bloodshot eyes.

At the very least, what she did must look that way to them.

*Hypocrisy* — perhaps it was.

Reine had accomplished nothing.

She looked at Neil, and his gaze wavered.

"I know……what kind of person you are. And you must have your reasons. But……the state of this country has strayed far from the Elsren you once spoke of. ……The ideal place you described will never come."

"N, iil……Elsreine-sama, please don't worry about me……!"

"The wife I loved refused a noble's advances and was killed for it. Even killing that noble changes nothing — another beast steps in to rule. ……This country can no longer be left in your hands. There is no choice but for you to die."

*Please do not resist*, Neil said.

"If you comply, I swear……we will not lay a hand on Fenera."

She instinctively backed away from the approaching men.

Escaping would have been easy.

But she looked at Fenera, held hostage, and then at Neil.

*'Someday, um……when I'm bigger, I want to go to the battlefield and fight for Elsreine-sama……'*

*——I want to help build the country that Elsreine-sama wishes for.*

Dazed, she remembered the words he had once spoken, and lowered her eyes, and sank to her knees.

"Cor, take her. As promised, the rest of you — hands off. ……I'll do it."

"That's right. Tie her up and take her. With a hostage, they can't touch us."

"Don't be stupid! That's not what I——!?"

"Elsreine-sama!"

Raised voices arguing, a voice calling her name.

Fenera, having broken free of Neil's grasp, forcibly seized Reine's arm and pulled her to her feet, and——

"You bitch!"

"Stop!!"

And shoved her away.

No voice came; and upon Fenera, who wore a smile, a sword was brought down.

Bright red blood sprayed through the air.

——Alone in her room, Reine sat before a white canvas.

Reine's memory, which should have been infallible, was patchy.

She had slaughtered dozens, cutting them down like routine work, and then sat blankly before Fenera's cold body.

Then the soldiers had gathered and escorted her back, along with Fenera, to the castle.

Fenera's body being burned at the funeral — from there, too, her memory was patchy.

She woke, she slept, she felt hunger but did not care. She remembered Gorzarius and the others coming to visit, but not what they said.

Reine sat before the canvas.

A canvas on which nothing was yet painted — had she been about to paint something?

Unable to understand even her own actions, she called up Fenera's face in her mind.

*'Ahaha, what a truly lovely painting. I love Elsreine-sama's paintings……so much.'*

Remembering those words, she mixed the colors.

"…………"

As she mixed, she thought of Fiiri's face.

Fiiri had taught her — what mattered was what you wished to convey.

What did Reine wish to convey?

What kind of painting was she trying to create?

She did not know.

She did not even know why she had picked up the brush.

*'Are you saying you paint without even knowing what the painting is, Fiiri?'*

*'I suppose so……ahaha, I do often just paint from whatever comes to mind.'*

*'Whatever comes to mind……'*

*'Yes. I draw lines at random, and as something vaguely takes shape, I give it form……I paint while thinking about what kind of picture I myself want to see.'*

Thousands of paradises she had painted — she envisioned them and gazed at the canvas.

A shining, small paradise.

Fiiri's Elsren.

From the Elsren that had once been filled with all sorts of people, the adults had vanished without her noticing.

When Fiiri painted people, she would imagine what they were thinking as she drew them, she had said.

Reine had followed her example and done the same.

This person wants bread, and the one beside him gives it freely.

This person is being poured tea, and that one is expressing thanks.

She had painted while imagining such things — but gradually, she could no longer understand what they were thinking, and without realizing it, she had stopped painting them.

Paradise was filled with only children — and yet, she recalled the faces of the children who had watched Reine through the window, looking frightened.

What were they thinking? Had they been frightened of Reine?

*'……This country can no longer be left in your hands. There is no choice but for you to die.'*

*——I want to help build the country that Elsreine-sama wishes for.*

Come to think of it, she did not truly understand what they were thinking, either.

She had only believed she understood — perhaps the truth was different.

Either way, they too would one day become adults, and she would no longer understand them.

She tried to paint something, but the figures had vanished from the Elsren in her mind, and the shining paradise seemed to wither as something dark descended upon it.

As though the swirling dark, a murky mix of many colors, were painting over the radiant world.

*Round and round, round and round* — the dark paint drove the paradise farther and farther away.

The Elsren she had painted just the other day now seemed impossibly distant.

*Round and round, round and round* — as though the dark drove the paradise ever further into the distance.

She paused, wondering what she was doing, and looked at the canvas.

The white canvas was being filled with darkness from the edges; she stared blankly at it, then glanced at the other half-finished canvases.

A shining, small paradise — whenever she finished one, Fenera would always rejoice as though seeing it for the first time.

Lately, that had been her only reason for painting. She moved to finish one, and stopped.

How was she to complete it?

When she tried to envision it, the dark something pushed it away from inside her mind.

As though a dark shadow had fallen — she imagined a paradise with no one in it, and felt something rising from the pit of her stomach, and pressed a hand to her mouth.

"……*kh*……"

She retched for a while in her nausea, then raised her head.

All the half-finished canvases looked the same to her now.

As though a dark shadow were painting them over from the edges — she stared blankly at them.

Her head spun, and she threw the dark-stained brush aside.

She looked right, she looked left, and every painting she saw was being painted over by darkness.

Even the paintings in her memory — every one of them only made her feel ill.

"Fii……ri……"

She called the name, but no one was here.

She was nowhere to be found.

She had been gone for so long.

For decades now, she had been gone.

In the Elsren that Reine painted, no one existed anymore.

There was no shining landscape — only dark colors.

Her vision blurred, and the inside of her chest convulsed as though she were hiccupping.

She had been taught so much — and yet, there was nothing Reine could do.

She had done nothing but obey others and bring suffering to the people.

The country was twisted, the paintings were twisted — what would Fiiri say if she saw her now?

She could no longer even imagine her Elsren.

*——She had only wanted to convey it.*

That it was because Fiiri had been there that this place had been created.

Only that, and nothing more.

She knew she would never see Fiiri again.

And so, at the very least, she had wanted to paint something she could be proud to show her.

She had wanted to imagine that Fiiri would have praised it, to be filled with that happy imagining, and to live that way.

But Fiiri would surely not praise her now.

She could not even imagine it.

Decades had passed, and she had failed at everything — she could no longer even paint the pictures Fiiri had once praised, and she could not even picture Fiiri smiling.

*——In a way, this place right now is my Elsren.*

She wanted to go back to those days.

To when Fiiri was always by Reine's side.

How had things come to this?

*Round and round*, such words repeated in her mind.

If this was how it would end, she should have died the day Fiiri died.

Something caught the corner of her eye — a fruit knife.

She picked it up almost unconsciously and stared at the sharp blade.

Was it truly the case that she would never see Fiiri again?

She recalled the incomprehensible talk of heavenly kingdoms taught at the churches — if such a place existed, would Fiiri be there?

But only good people were welcomed there.

Even if such a place existed, Reine surely could not go.

"…………"

She looked around the room, at the paintings darkened with paint.

Every one had lost its light, murky and sickening.

Either way, she would never see her again by remaining here.

She realized she was no longer even permitted to imagine her — and, trembling, pressed the blade against her throat.

Her neck split open, and she collapsed; she watched the bright red blood spread across the floor.

If she had been more clever, like her mother — would things have been different?

If she had been more clever, like Fiiri — would things have been different?

If she had been — would none of this have happened?

*Round and round*, nothing but regret circled through her mind, and slowly, she closed her eyes.

As though fleeing from thoughts without end.

*——As though casting body and soul into a dark, murky abyss.*

——The first Empress of the Elsren Holy Empire, Elsreine, died of illness.

Several days later, the strong Queen of Alberan — benevolent ruler and tyrant alike — Grabareine, also passed away.

The eldest son who inherited the throne, Seil, told his subjects that the age of terror had ended, rallied the uneasy court, and five years later abdicated in favor of his nephew.

He spent the rest of his life not as king, but as an advisor.

Regarding Grabareine's death, Seil said little, neither confirming nor denying the many rumors.

That he himself had usurped the throne; that he was shielding someone who had killed the Queen; that he was concealing the suicide of the kingdom's supreme authority — many rumors circulated among the people, but given the many enemies the Queen, Grabareine, had made:

It was generally held that some righteous soul had punished her.

Many who knew of Grabareine's rule of terror, while giving due credit to her immense achievements, spoke of the kingdom that had ultimately fallen into disorder and division, saying such a tragedy must never be repeated.

A private compact was drawn up: the beings known as the 'babes who do not cry,' transmitted through the royal line since ancient times, were to be deemed cursed children, and their lives taken.

The most famous of the babes who do not cry were the founding king Bazarishe, and Queen Grabareine, and Princess Elsreine.

——And the great Queen who unified the continent and inscribed the Unified Calendar upon the world, together with her elder sister, were also said to have been such a pair of babes who do not cry.

"……I see. So it seems there will be no war for a while."

"Everyone feared Grabareine after Elsreine-sama's death, and yet……you alone were calm, Grand Duke Rukazan. Did you know this would happen?"

"No, I simply had a feeling. ……Get out."

"……Yes, sir."

Without glancing at the man who left the room, Gorzarius remained on the large sofa, gazing at the paintings above the mantle.

All of them were paradises she had painted.

Images of smiling people.

Their exquisite detail and transcendent — no, aberrant — technique.

Every one of them was an ultimate work of art, unsurpassable until the end of this world.

Swirling a glass, he took a mouthful of strong spirits and savored them.

And the man who gazed upon the paintings in this way held no particular interest in their superficial qualities.

No matter how fine, they were merely well-made paintings.

To him, that was all they were.

The ultimate beauty for which many collectors of wealth would pay fortunes.

Such things were irrelevant to him.

He simply called to mind the one who had painted them, and tried to savor what wishes she had poured into each canvas — that alone was what he gazed upon.

Not the dazzling technique that bedazzled so many, but what the painting was trying to convey — he looked at it sincerely, and tried to receive it.

His gaze traveled from one end to the other.

At last, he stared at the painting at the center, above the mantle.

Compared to the others, it held not a trace of beautiful technique — a painting that resembled a scribble.

Dark pigment of many mixed colors filled the white canvas haphazardly from the edges inward.

The final painting she had left behind.

Officially, it had been titled 'Light in the Dark Night,' he had heard.

From adults to children, and finally to a light amid the darkness.

*This represents rebirth*, the Pope had grandly proclaimed to the people.

It was laughably foolish, but it did not matter.

By now, most things were of no consequence to Gorzarius.

Nothing was worth as much as this painting.

The final work of a single girl who had painted so many splendid paradises, created at the end of being driven to the brink.

Without technique, without anything — a single canvas where emotion had simply bled through.

When he first saw it, for the first time in his life, he felt a tremor from the depths of his being.

Recalling the journey of that girl — broken, yet able to care for others with such purity — to this final point.

Simply looking at it brought the past several decades flooding back.

He remembered a childlike, trusting voice calling *Rukazan.*

"…………?"

He noticed something running down his cheek, wiped it, and looked.

The back of his hand was wet, and — *ah* — he understood.

"……Elsreine-sama."

Right fist to left breast — *I pledge my life to you.*

It was that kind of salute.

The unidentifiable emotion may well have been the most foolish, the most worthless of feelings.

At the very least, this something that squeezed and ached in his chest was surely that.

He had a wife. He had children.

He had done everything that could be called human, and yet he had never loved a person.

He was a broken wretch — gold and power and pleasure, and nothing else to enjoy.

And yet, what he felt now — he was, strangely, certain of it.

"……You were my Reine, after all."

What he had gained at the end of decades was achingly, suffocatingly bittersweet.

And that, beyond all remedy, gave Gorzarius a sense of being alive.

A Reine that could never again be reached — vanishing into the far, far distance.
