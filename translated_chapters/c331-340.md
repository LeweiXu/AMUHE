# Chapter 331: Super Galaxy Queen ☆ Krischeria ~The Beloved Princess With Excessive Destiny-Force Just Goes On Being Happy in a Kindly World~

Thank you for enjoying *A Maiden's Unwanted Heroic Epic*.

Approximately three years have passed since its completion.

Reading back through the work again, I have come to feel that *A Maiden's Unwanted Heroic Epic* is, after all, a piece carrying many problems.

Setting aside typos and unattended-to bad prose, the cruel depictions and structure are conspicuous in places, the protagonists keep grinding through inconclusive inner conflicts every time, and there isn't a shred of pop sensibility to it.

Suffering is precisely where a story's depth and interest reside.

What appeal could there possibly be in a tale of characters with no inner conflict?

It is from such considerations that I deliberately built it as that sort of work, but on review the whole thing has indeed turned out to be an extraordinarily dark, gloomy story.

In the end, this work arrives at an unprecedented and frankly idiotic-seeming conclusion that ought to be called

"Yuri-Yuri ☆ Happy Eternal End ☆,"

in which seven beautiful girls live forever in yuri-yuri bliss — but many readers are thrown off by the misery along the way, and that very ending stands atop a great many sacrifices.

Many people who, with a different first encounter, might never have died and could have cooperated with the protagonists and built good relationships with them, ended up dying.

Just a few buttons done up wrong.

The deaths of those men and women, dying for reasons written into the work itself, are too sad and too cruel.

Add this to the inner conflict above, and what arises is something to be called "(;_;)↓Synergy of Gloom↓(;_;)."

It paints over the work's sunny side, "o(^_^)o↑Mansion Yuri-Yuri Effect↑o(^_^)o," and the gloom wins out.

In this full remake, as far as possible, in order to scoop back up the people who got kicked aside for the sake of those girls' happiness, I plan to drastically improve the encounter aspect in particular — strengthening the "system in which characters appear and meet at exquisite timing and somehow get along nicely."

The aim is a full revision into "*A Maiden's Unwanted Heroic Epic in Which the Buttons Are Absolutely Never Done Up Wrong.*"

Also, the title *A Maiden's Unwanted Heroic Epic*, gloomy-looking from the very letters of it, is being changed to match.

"*Super Galaxy Queen ☆ Krischeria ~The Beloved Princess With Excessive Destiny-Force Just Goes On Being Happy in a Kindly World~*"

Please bear that in mind.

The revision is currently in progress, but some readers may be uneasy about what kind of story it will become.

For now, please enjoy the following digest version of the early portion as a sample of the atmosphere.

(※Important) Notice regarding the full remake of *A Maiden's Unwanted Heroic Epic*.

"Uncle and Niece: 1"

Setting aside many remonstrances, in the end his elder brother Sherbaza had apparently decided to raise the girl, who had been kept under house arrest, as a princess.

Was he so reluctant to lose the child of his concubine? It was an unusual thing for his earnest brother, Gildanstein thought.

The name given to her was Krischeria — a noble moon, waning and tracing an arc.

The names of the royal house must always be impeccable.

On that count, that name was discordant — a name carrying the guilt of breaking with custom.

"……Your Highness, my lord uncle."

"An uncle has come to see his niece, that's all. Don't go on guard. ……What are you doing?"

The servant had stiffened when he entered her chamber, but Gildanstein paid no mind and walked in.

Krischeria, now five, had taken a vase in her hands and was polishing it with a cloth.

Beautiful silver hair, jewel-like violet eyes. A white one-piece dress.

The way she expressionlessly tilted her small head apparently struck many people as unsettling.

Without anyone saying so aloud, those in the royal palace knew she was the despised child who had been kept in confinement; the pressure of those emotionless eyes likely drove the impression.

Eyes that watched every gesture of their interlocutor — like a snake's, if one were to compare them.

Of all the eyes Gildanstein had ever seen, he had never encountered any like them.

"……? Krische is wiping the vase, wipe-wipe."

He spoke, exasperated by the look that seemed to say, *isn't that obvious?*

"That much I can see. I was asking why you, a princess, are doing the cleaning."

"Hahh……"

"……My apologies, Your Highness, that is to say——"

"Enough. I'm used to it by now. ……Tea."

The black-haired servant with the unlucky-looking face bowed deeply and brewed black tea.

While Krischeria continued to polish the vase, Gildanstein propped his cheek on a hand at the seat by the window.

A strange girl — perhaps it was because she had been confined in the tower, or perhaps it had been there from the start.

When she finished wiping the vase, she let a small satisfied smile float across her face, then came pattering over toward him.

"Did you have something to discuss with Krische?"

"I came to see your face just to kill some boredom. Don't shorten your name."

"Krischeria is too long…… Tou-sama said it was fine to use that."

Out of guilt of some sort, his brother was tremendously soft on Krischeria.

"……Enough. Lecturing you is less rewarding than teaching a dog."

"Krische is smarter than a dog."

"So you are. Apparently too smart for me to be your owner."

He patted her head loosely and Krischeria watched, the corners of her mouth softening.

Letters and arithmetic she was said to have absorbed in no time at all; her cleverness was not on the level of an ordinary person. Her younger sister, born after, was the same — at only two years of age she was learning from her elder sister and picking it all up in no time.

"Genius" was an inadequate word for how clever she was.

That was likely one of the reasons people found her uncanny.

Most people felt that way toward beings beyond their understanding.

Some loved the two of them, but most kept the pair at a distance and watched from afar.

He set his lips to the black tea the servant called Nora had set down, and for a while the topic was tutors.

There seemed to be no one capable of properly educating them; two tutors had already, in no time, requested reassignment.

Being too clever was its own problem — they couldn't even hold a proper conversation.

Especially scholars, who had high pride and often delicate temperaments.

The arithmetic instructor, having seen her solve at a glance the proof he had taken on as the lifelong proposition of his career, seemed to have come down with an illness of the heart.

"The problem is rather that worthless scholars were appointed as your tutors. I'll speak to my brother. In their place, read your way through the palace library from end to end. If you go at it earnestly, two or three years will be enough for you."

"Books……"

"I'm not telling you to *understand* it all. The parts you don't understand, just memorize. Ask my brother later. When I have time, I'll teach you what I can. ……Books will serve you better than tutors."

"Hmm, that may indeed be so. Once Krische finishes reading them, is that the end?"

"Of academic learning, yes. For things that can't be learned from books — horsemanship and the like — keep on with the tutors."

Krischeria made an unmistakably reluctant face.

"What is it? If you dislike the tutor I can change them."

"……Riding hurts Krische's bottom. Running seems faster."

"Endure it, at least somewhat. It costs you nothing to know. Over long distances, a horse is convenient."

Krischeria had already mastered physical enhancement perfectly.

Beyond mere intelligence, she was a head and shoulders above in every faculty.

If only she weren't a fool besides, there would be nothing to say, but perhaps no one is perfect.

"After that — self-defense…… If you want to learn the use of a sword and so on, I'll teach you, but only after these other things. You're still too small in body, and my brother would dislike it."

"Should Krische learn it as well?"

"I think so. Born into the royal family, you can never know when your life will be sought. Learning to defend yourself can hardly hurt. You'll pick it up quickly."

"Indeed, that may well be so……"

She tilted her head this way and that, considered, and nodded.

Then, picking up a cookie, she stared fixedly at him.

"……?"

"It's nothing. Cookies — do you not like them?"

"It isn't that I dislike them…… nor am I particularly fond."

He frowned at the meaning of her question and looked at Nora, who bowed her head in fright.

"Today's cookies, Your Highness — that is, Princess Krischeria… Her lessons ended early and she had time, and I thought perhaps if there were anything I could teach her…… My apologies."

"I see. Don't be so frightened. I have no intention of doing anything to you or to her."

"……I am sorry."

A tiresome woman in every detail.

She seemed terribly afraid of Gildanstein, flinching at his every motion.

Krischeria was glancing back and forth between Nora and him; he sighed, picked up a cookie, and put it in his mouth.

The taste of an utterly unremarkable cookie, slightly sweeter than what he usually ate, spread through his mouth.

"Ehehe, was it good?"

"Ordinary. Not bad, but cloyingly sweet."

"Mmh…… Krische felt it had come out terribly well. Kreschenta said it was delicious too."

"Then your sense of taste is the thing that's off."

At his answer, Krischeria again tilted her head, mused alone, and ate a cookie.

Watching her, he decided his business was done and was just rising to leave when, from the bed, there came a rustling — and his niece Kreschenta sat up.

Rubbing her sleepy eyes, she stood, and seeing Gildanstein she made a dubious face — and as a matter of course leapt up onto her elder sister's lap.

She fixed him with a glare, saying nothing — this was always the way of her.

"I came to see my niece's face, that's all."

"…………"

At the words she turned her face pointedly aside and clung tightly, hugging Krischeria.

Still only two — and like Krischeria, a baby who never cried.

He knew she could already speak fluently, but unlike her sister she was inward; with anyone besides Krischeria and Nora she scarcely spoke at all.

In appearance she was a small child, but in temperament she looked somewhat more proper than Krischeria.

"Kreschenta, you must greet him properly."

"……, hello."

"You greet someone with your eyes on them. Until you can greet him properly, no cookies."

At Krischeria's words she pursed her lips and reluctantly, looking away, said *hello.*

Krischeria gave a small *mmh* of disapproval, then took a cookie and gave it to her sister.

"Here, that's fine — but in front of others, take a more befitting, endearing manner. Whether or not the two of you are as you appear is no concern of mine, but there are those who'd dislike it."

Kreschenta glanced sidelong at him and clung once more to her sister.

"……But Krische *is* as she appears."

"Yes, in a sense you are."

"In a sense……"

In intellect and capability she was outstanding, but in temperament she was a docile five-year-old, just as she looked.

She held the question in mind for a moment, then evidently it ceased to matter.

Stroking her sister's head, with a vacant expression as if she'd just remembered something, she asked—

"Ah, that reminds me, there was something Krische wanted to ask."

"Something to ask?"

"Yes. Word is that Uncle takes pleasure in tormenting innocent people——"

"——Lady Krischeria!"

"Mghh?"

In a flustered hurry Nora pressed her hand over Krischeria's mouth.

And bowed her head in fright.

"M-my apologies, Your Highness, that is——"

"Let her go. I don't mind. ……What of it?"

Released, Krischeria looked troubled, glanced at Nora, then back at Gildanstein.

"It's just, Uncle has rather lost his reputation over that, and Krische, who is friendly with him, feels her own reputation is being dragged down by association. Honestly, Krische would very much like you to stop."

"That your head is not on straight is plain fact. It isn't all my fault."

"Mmh…… Even so. Surely the fewer reasons your reputation drops, the better."

Krischeria spoke in displeasure, and asked—

"As far as Krische is concerned, if Uncle has some reason for it, Krische won't insist, but if it isn't strictly necessary it ought to stop. ……Is there some good that comes of doing it?"

"Not particularly. ……At a stretch, one obtains various pieces of information."

"Information……"

"Hurting them makes them speak of various things, and merchants who come selling things know I'm a good customer and pass me their information. Well, it's a side-benefit to the play…… in any case, since around the time you were born, I've grown bored with it. That sort of play is no more frequent than the average."

At first it had simply been torture.

He would lay hands on those who had broken the law and vent his various irritations upon them.

When that ran out, with information-gathering as the pretext, he would buy slaves brought in from other countries and repeat the same.

Before he knew it, the torture itself had become the point; he would buy the slaves who interested him and spend hours absorbed in faces one could not ordinarily see.

Mere criminals. The arrogant, the abject, those merely unfortunate, the strong-of-justice incompetents and fools.

Only at the extremity did people show Gildanstein their true faces.

Those who said *do as you like* and, when their hands and feet were being cut off, begged for mercy.

Those who said *I won't talk* and broke at the third nail.

Or those who cursed Gildanstein as a rotten degenerate and, even driven mad, kept that posture unchanged.

Those who would die for an ideal, holding to a noble's soul.

That, surely, was what he had wanted to see.

Around then the play had become routine.

Just by looking into those eyes and exchanging a few words, he could tell them apart before the play even began.

There is no game so dull as one whose answer one already knows.

"Then, have you stopped now?"

"At most I buy a slave I've taken a fancy to and keep them at the manor. I attend slave duels and shows now and then out of social obligation, but only that much for entertainment…… If you're interested, will you come along next time?"

"Krische will not. Just to be clear, Krische is *talking* about how Uncle's strange games drag down Krische's reputation."

She frowned and glared at him.

"If you say you've stopped, that's well enough. If it's only by way of obligation, perhaps in various respects there's no helping it. But please, at least, do your best not to let strange rumors arise. If your reputation gets any worse and Krische's drops along with it, Krische will sever ties with you."

"What an ungrateful child you are."

"It's because Krische thinks you've been good to her up to now that she's saying this for your sake. Surely, rather than being hated by others, working to be liked is far better…… isn't that right, Nora?"

"E-eh, um……"

Nora, unable to interpose, let her eyes wander in fright; Krischeria tilted her head.

Kreschenta yawned sleepily and watched her sister with an exasperated look.

"Why should it matter to be liked by the rabble?"

"……? Surely there's no comparison to be made between being liked and being disliked. Being disliked comes back to you as harm; being liked comes back as benefit."

Plain and simple, the girl described the world she saw.

In her crystal-clear violet eyes there floated not a doubt.

Without the complicated reasoning and logic of nobility, royalty, or humanity, she divided the world in two with the words *benefit* and *harm.*

*A refreshingly pure fool* — that was what one thought, watching her.

"In Krische's experience this investment is highly profitable. The rate of return is extremely high. Krische is surprised that Uncle didn't already understand it…… Ah, that's right."

*Uncle,* she said, and beckoned with little waves of her hand.

When Gildanstein frowned and approached, she beckoned further.

Before he could ask what she meant, her small hand touched his head and began to move.

Nora was speechless.

"……What is this?"

"A reward. If Uncle does his best, Krische shall reward him with pat-pats. Krische will also do her best to bake cookies you'll find delicious. ……A clear, easy-to-understand reward, surely, leads to motivation."

"Don't lump me in with you and Kreschenta."

Without anger so much as exasperation, he caught Krischeria's hand and pulled it away.

*Mmh,* Krischeria said, considering.

"……Well, I understand your argument. I'll consider it."

"Hmm, just considering won't do — Krische wants you to do your best…… Ah, Krische is also planning to bake a cake next time. She'll let Uncle eat some too."

"I don't want it."

"Uncle and Niece: 2"

Some time after that, he attended a feast of the dregs.

Dregs flattering this way and that, and he himself watching that mad revelry idly.

A tedious time it was.

When boredom came on, he would usually order someone to dispel it for him, but this time even that desire failed to stir.

Without his noticing, his thoughts had turned to Krischeria.

What to teach her next, his thinking ran; thinking that, his thoughts then ran to what he himself wanted to make of her.

What in fact did he himself wish to do?

A question he could think on without arriving anywhere.

Yet the boredom of before was now, somehow, gone.

As far as time-killing went, it was far superior — a play he did not tire of.

* * *

"……What is this, Gil?"

"A roster, as you can read for yourself, brother. What each of them has been doing is summarized too. Dispose of them as you see fit. They're skilled at hiding their tails — if you act, act all at once."

The king's chamber — along the walls, armor and swords and paintings of battlefields were displayed.

Sherbaza Alberan, like Gildanstein, sighed and swept up his golden hair with one hand.

Their faces were said to closely resemble each other.

Their builds were the same; the only difference was a beard, present or absent.

"I am asking what you intend by this."

"I've simply tired of them. I'm weary of dealing with them. Don't worry — pieces useful to me I've kept aside; do as you like with the rest."

He poured the wine he had brought into a glass for his brother; Sherbaza gazed at it.

Without minding, he poured one for himself, took a sip.

A truly fine wine.

"……Surely, Gil, you don't mean that for this——"

"Heh, your weakness is that you're too soft, brother. Do you still trust me even now? ……Drink. It's a label I haven't tried before, but for the price it's not a bad wine."

Sherbaza sighed, then quietly took the wine in his mouth and swallowed.

And said—

"I do not believe you are rotten to the core, a degenerate. ……Had a few things been even slightly different, it would not have come to this."

"There are no *might-have-beens* in the past, brother. All of it was inevitable…… what was meant to happen, happened."

"A choice at the end of doubt does not necessarily turn out the same. I still believe, even now, that you were the one fit to be king. And it was not only I — at the time, many thought so. ……But I hesitated."

He spoke as if mocking himself, and stood.

And took down the king's sword Bezelia from its place on the wall.

"My hesitation arose from weakness. I did not want to acknowledge that I was no vessel for kingship. *It is too soon to give up; if I work at it, I might still——* and so I deferred the single word: that I would yield the right of succession to you."

He drew the great sword that only the king is permitted to wield, and gazed at it.

Sherbaza's face was reflected in the beautiful blade.

"The king's sword Bezelia, the sword consecrated to the founder-king Bazarishe. Its beautiful blade represents the unclouded heart, and at the pommel, the clear violet eye that pursues the ideal. ……I would gaze at our father's sword in secret and look at myself reflected in it. Even now, when I gaze, my face clouds the steel."

Returning the sword to its sheath, Sherbaza returned to his seat and continued.

"……More devoted to the ideal than any, never doubting that to assist our father and me was your duty…… If anyone is the soft-hearted one, Gil, it is *you.* I was not born with as pure a heart as yours."

"It's true I'll not deny that the me of those days was a fool."

He answered with a laugh, and Sherbaza watched the surface his wine traced.

"……It's because you were so devoted to the ideal that I can understand the depth of your disillusionment. By rights, I ought to have spoken the contents of my heart to you before it came to that. My foolish, viscous feelings…… would, in time, have faded before the figure of you become a proper king, and I would not now be sitting here regretting it."

"There's no use speaking of a past that won't return."

"Indeed. ……But the future, perhaps, can still be changed."

Sherbaza emptied his wine and lifted the glass.

Gildanstein poured.

"……The many words you once swore to me, I have not forgotten even now. And neither have you. Even repeating your degenerate amusements, you have fulfilled your duty as king's brother, and you have supported me."

"I'm doing only enough work not to have my head taken off."

"That is not so. ……However much you have despaired of the world, you do what you do for one purpose only — not to soil the words you swore on your name. ……Even so, I am your brother. I know that, whatever happens, you are a man who can never throw away the last pride of a noble."

*——And that is exactly why, even envying, longing for, hating you, I still cannot kill you.*

Sherbaza spoke quietly, and laughed.

"Watching those two, I am reminded of you in your younger days. The resemblance is strong."

"……That's an insult past insult, that one."

"I don't mean it to mock. ……Why a baby who does not cry is called a despised child — watching you and watching them, I came to understand. The world is simply too sullied for those who are too pure to live in."

And, looking up as if at the ceiling, he continued.

"……I'm glad I avoided yet another foolish choice. That you came to see me today — surely the spark for it was those two, was it not? Looking at you, I can tell."

"Interpret it as you like."

He tipped his glass, and into the empty glass Sherbaza poured for him.

And, emptying his own as well, Sherbaza smiled.

"Truly, this is a fine wine. ……Next time, come empty-handed. I'll have my treasured wine brought out."

"……Brother's taste is rather lacking, so I won't get my hopes up."

"That assessment, I'll have you reconsider on the next occasion."

Sherbaza said it with a laugh, and brought the wine to his lips.

"……Those two, I leave to you. Show them what you wish to show, what you have wanted to see. In time, surely, they will show you the scene you sought from the bottom of your heart."

"Don't blame me if the country splits."

"Entrusted to the you of now, surely it will not come to that."

Eyes closed, Sherbaza smiled and said it.

"Uncle and Niece: 3"

Royalty have the duty of attending many ceremonies, and most of them were tedious.

But of them, the victory feast alone was something he did not dislike.

He would read the battlefield records sent in, and picture in his head what kind of battle it had been.

How the soldiers had fought; how the commanders had agonized and reached what decisions.

And how it had come to its conclusion.

While composing the words he would speak at the victory ceremony, he listened to the steel echoes the warriors played, the discord of life and death.

There were also those, like Dagren Garkha, who sent in records too curt to be of any interest, but this time the main theater had been the west.

Aulgorn Hilkintos and Elderant.

In terms of how he fought, Aulgorn — who took no risks — was not to his taste, but the over-ornamented, detail-heavy records were not bad as reading.

The amusing thing was that, taken on their own, they made him look like an undisputed master of war.

As a general entrusted with one of the four cardinal directions, Aulgorn was a man of sufficient talent.

Only — he was thorough about winning what he could be sure to win, avoiding direct confrontation until the chance of losing had been painted over, and he despised any blot on his undefeated record too dearly.

The west, sacrificial to that disease, was always troubled; though by rights it was reasonably rich land, of the four directions it produced the least tax revenue.

He spoke of those sacrifices in such phrases as *the violations inflicted upon the people by an enemy army that launched a contemptible surprise attack,* and *hearing the cries of the pitiful people occupied and oppressed,* he would *launch a desperate counter-offensive* and succeed in *bringing the iron hammer of justice down upon evil barbarian hordes possessed of vast forces*— that was the reliable structure.

This time, Aulgorn had bought time by retreating until Gildanstein arrived.

At the moment Bogan, charging hard from the north, had cut off the enemy's rear, Gildanstein and he together had turned and counter-attacked.

The matter was settled when Gildanstein took the enemy general's head.

That kind of give-and-take he understood; by yielding glory to Gildanstein and to the central generals, Aulgorn arranged for accommodations regarding "sacrifices" — that was the usual way.

This time too, in a tone as if he had been right there at the scene as Gildanstein took the enemy commander's head, with truly inflammatory language he had recorded *the vigorous heroics of His Highness,* in such a vein that even Gildanstein himself, the subject, was left exasperated.

Glory, or the petty self-preservation of small men — neither concerned him.

Being lifted up too far made his skin crawl, and the goggling eyes were not to his liking.

*I'll dispose of him on some pretext like neglecting the protection of the people,* he would think on the road west each time, but once the battlefield play was over, Gildanstein was always in a good mood.

That, in the end, he was inclined to forgive a little unpleasantness, perhaps showed that this man too was, surprisingly, a capable hand.

* * *

"You two — what are you doing?"

"……? Krische is braiding Kreschenta and the beard."

"Hahaha, it seems my beard has greatly taken Their Highnesses' fancy."

The victory ceremony had ended; this was the feast.

Looking for the two of them, he found them in a corner braiding Felwors's long white beard.

Splitting the beard left and right, Krischeria in a black dress and Kreschenta in a white dress were each twisting a side into braids; Felwors was laughing pleasantly.

"Ehehe, Kreschenta, you're good at it."

"Onee-sama is good at teaching."

"As expected of Krische's little sister. Krische shall pat-pat you later."

"Yes……!"

The kingdom's First Princess and Second Princess — clad in elegant dresses, twisting and twisting at the beard, the two of them left Gildanstein exasperated.

Nine and six; for all that they had grown somewhat in appearance, they were as foolish as ever.

Even Kreschenta, who had been inward and seemed to have somewhat more proper sensibilities than Krischeria, was day by day walking the same fool's path as her sister.

"Uncle?"

"……Whatever do you want?"

He took the two by the necks and pulled them away, and told Felwors—

"Don't indulge them. What do you mean to do if they get any more foolish than this?"

"My apologies, Your Highness. It was so endearing somehow that I quite——"

"……Both Onee-sama and I are wiser than Uncle."

"However wise you are, if to outside eyes you are a fool, then a fool you are. Have a little awareness of your station as a princess."

Kreschenta glared at him and puffed her cheeks in displeasure.

"Come. There's someone to greet."

"Understood. ……Kreschenta, you mustn't get angry like that. Uncle is short-tempered, so it can't be helped. We must take that as a counter-example to learn from."

"Mmh…… very well."

"Yes. What a good child you are. You mustn't grow up to be like Uncle."

He pushed the foolish conversation behind him out of his attention.

Like animal cries — there was no use being angry at each utterance.

Pushing through the crowd that way, he reached his target.

"Christand. Krischeria and Kreschenta."

"Well, then…… It is my first honor to meet you, Your Highnesses."

Bogan Christand dropped to one knee, and his wife Lazura, with red hair gathered up and wearing an indigo dress, took the hem of her skirt and gave a standing curtsy.

Even the usually foolish two understood the etiquette to that level, and matched, like a textbook, an ideal curtsy.

At the perfect, unjarring synchrony of the two from start to finish, Bogan briefly knit his brows; Lazura's cat-like eyes went round.

"I am Bogan Christand, and this is my wife Lazura. Though my abilities pale beside those of my great predecessors, I have been entrusted with the protection of the north as one of the kingdom's four-direction generals."

"Ah, well…… Krische knows. The brilliant feats of arms you have accumulated — Krische, as First Princess, is very glad that a warrior such as you has staked life and body in fulfilling your duties for the kingdom. In this latest action, your performance was splendid."

"Words too kind for my station."

Having recited what was half a set phrase, Krischeria glanced sidelong at Gildanstein.

*We've made our greetings, may we go home now?* — he ignored the glance entirely.

"I particularly wanted you to meet her. As you may know, I had her come along recently, and on a trial basis I had her draft the operational plan. You found it easy to move, didn't you?"

"Yes, Your Highness. ……I had heard rumor, but…"

At the figure of a girl who was nothing more than a child, Bogan was perplexed; Gildanstein laughed and told him—

"This is the Reine of our age, Bogan. As you can see, she is a brat, but I have no hesitation in calling her a genius."

"Reine……"

"For today, take it as no more than an introduction. In time I will bring her to your side as well."

"……My lord."

And he told Krischeria—

"This man is the general I trust most, and a warrior. He will be a help to you in the future. Remember him well."

"Hahh…… A pleasure to make your acquaintance. The other day, thanks to General going around behind so quickly, things ended quickly, and Krische would be very glad if you keep up that sort of work."

The moment the set phrases were over, she was as usual.

Gildanstein had given up.

"No, my own performance was thanks to fine subordinates and the supporting attack of the central army…… that strategic maneuver — was that also Princess Krischeria's design?"

"Eh, well…… do you mean when the enemy was on two fronts?"

"Yes."

"Then yes. Thanks to your moving so swiftly, we encircled them cleanly."

The enemy had divided their forces against the Christand army pressing from the north, and the Hilkintos army and central army pressing from the east.

Krischeria had ordered the Hilkintos army to march south, making it appear that a triple envelopment from north, east, and south was forming, and pulled the enemy's attention to the south.

There was not a day he did not vomit.

Neither he nor any of them, at the time, knew Krische-sama's real ability; with too many opportunities to deal with her, they were skeptical of it.

Each time a soldier like the captain bowed and scraped to Krische-sama, it always left a bad taste.

# Chapter 333: The Dawn Crescent II

The next day, the young Christand lady who came to the training-ground forest was the same as ever.

Just as in the rumor that she disliked horses, she came on foot, with a face that suggested no enthusiasm.

The captain crisply gave the salute and we matched him; she gave a perfunctory salute in return.

He had thought she would at least praise the previous day's victory, but of course she did not.

"Right then, let's start at once. Krische doesn't mind doing all of you at once for the time being…… Dagra, what do you think?"

"My lady. If I may ask, please instruct one team at a time. That way it'll be easier for these men to absorb."

"Mh, I see……"

And then Krische-sama for the first time turned her eyes to us, probably because I happened to be the closest.

Looking at me, she beckoned.

"Second team, at the double! The rest, fall back a little!"

Seeing this, Dagra issued the orders, and in the small clearing the five of us faced Krische-sama.

At that moment, I think I was still wondering whether she meant for us to swing the sword for her or something.

"Dagra, a wooden sword."

"My lady."

The captain at once held out the wooden sword he had been carrying in both hands and stepped back.

She twirled the wooden sword skillfully in one hand, and said to me—

"Go ahead."

"……? *Go ahead,* you say?"

"Kouz! Answer like a soldier!"

"H-hah! My apologies, sir Captain! What does *go ahead* signify, sir!?"

Hurriedly correcting myself at the captain's voice, Krische-sama gave a small tilt of her head.

*Whatever does that mean,* her manner suggested; she traced her lip with a fingertip and considered.

We were the ones who wanted to tilt our heads.

That she could mean a perfectly insane order — *come at me, five against one* — none of us imagined; we exchanged looks.

"As I told you yesterday, the Vice-Adjutant of the Corps will personally instruct you. If you understand, take up your swords!"

"S-sir, sir? Erm, five against one, sir?"

"Yes. ……Begging your pardon, Vice-Adjutant. Is that correct?"

"Yes. Krische was vague. ……That's right, so begin whenever you'd like."

Even hearing that, our confusion was strong.

We had heard, certainly, but five against one — and the opponent a child not yet fifteen.

Yesterday she'd said various things, but five-on-one made the spirit hesitate, even so.

"It's five against one, but you don't need to hold back — come at Krische as if to kill. Erm, even if Krische is hurt or killed, Krische swears on her name there'll be no complaint."

She nodded, blithely saying it; we exchanged looks again.

*What do we do?* the team asked me with their eyes; reluctantly I drew the wooden sword from my belt and took stance.

"It would be bad to actually injure her."

I murmured it; *yeah,* they nodded.

"Krische *is* saying you don't need to hold back……"

The distance and the volume should have been beyond hearing — what kind of ears did she have?

Looking back, it must have been lip-reading.

Krische-sama had monstrously good eyes, and could read words from the movement of the lips alone.

"Well, fine. It's the first time anyway, so let's wrap it up briskly, demonstration included."

We were five *ken* apart.

It wasn't that I couldn't see.

I saw the instant Krische-sama lowered her stance, and the instant she stepped in.

I just *saw,* that was all.

That strange sensation is hard to describe.

I knew she'd stepped in, and my head knew I'd better parry.

But my body felt no danger whatsoever, and didn't twitch.

Normally it's the reverse.

When someone suddenly slashes at you, your body moves before your head thinks; reflexively your body backs away.

Even people unaccustomed to violence — even amateurs — at least *flinch* if startled from out of cover.

Whether they freeze or have their legs go from under them differs from person to person, but the body moves before the thought.

That's how humans were made, I believed.

And yet, gripping the wooden sword, I dimly watched as it was knocked aside, was kicked in the gut, and didn't come out of it until I was on the ground.

Writhing in pain, looking up, I found my four teammates kneeling, with only Krische-sama still standing.

"As you can see, Krische is strong, so it's fine. Next team."

"Hey, second team's in the way. Get them off to the side."

And so, before we could recover from the pain and confusion, we were dragged out of the ring.

Thanks to our sacrifice the next team was permitted to attack first, but their wooden swords didn't even graze.

And as one team after another in succession was beaten down without lifting a hand, the men who'd been watching with smirks at us — their faces grew grim too, even so.

Krische-sama's movements left ample reserves.

From the side it looked only slightly fast; setting aside our first team, the watching men ought to have been able to respond — but oddly, no one's reactions came in time.

Without even noticing she had stepped in, they would have their swords flicked aside in a daze, or be struck on the head, or kicked over; men who had crushed *Red* now seemed like training scarecrows.

Kirik, the second-best swordsman in the corps after Kalua, was handled like a child.

The first team, made up of all skilled men aside from his adjutant Mia, was dispatched effortlessly.

In the blink of an eye, forty men, far from being able to clash swords, had not even been touched, and went down on one knee before one girl.

*All of you at once doesn't matter.*

Now, finally, all of us understood the meaning of the words.

Even if everyone in this corps to the last man came at her, beating us back would not even cost her trouble.

That was what the words had meant.

Not a single man laughed now; the air had gone cold.

On a real battlefield, all forty of us, myself included, would have died facing only Krische-sama.

And if she so chose, she could surely have wiped everyone in this place out.

If five surrounding her could not even respond, dozens surrounding her would be the same.

That she had cut down hundreds with a single guard's curved sword, that she was a monster veteran fighters could not stand against — those preposterous rumors we had all laughed at.

For a giant of monstrous strength, it would be one thing — but to say a child not yet five *shaku* tall could do such things, surely, was overdoing it.

Even as the toadies around the Christand young lady had ginned it up to flatter her, it was farcical exaggeration, we'd laughed.

Yet exaggerated though it was, a rumor was a rumor.

*She's at least no weakling,* we'd thought; but her figure was far from the *strong man* I imagined, and at most I'd expected one might lose to her in a one-on-one match.

But reality was so chillingly cold it raised the gooseflesh.

Who could have imagined that the child gnawing on a cookie with a leisurely face was, in fact, a monster who could mow down a hundred men effortlessly?

If this had been a battlefield, every one of our heads would have been struck off in the moment of our inattention.

Before Krische-sama, fighting was not contest but procedure.

The numbers and strength of the opponent were merely a question of how long the chore took.

With her absurd talent, by sheer force, beyond protest, she would pin the opponent down and dispatch the matter.

*Beyond imagination* — that's what an expression like that referred to.

You could imagine a man like Corps Commander Agrand, who plainly looked the part, mowing down a hundred; but you could not imagine a single girl like Krische-sama cutting down a hundred.

If you cannot imagine it, you cannot understand it; if you cannot understand it, you cannot deal with it.

And that being so, there was no road but to be killed without comprehending why.

*The Black Century* — and after it the *Black Flag Special Service,* which came to be called the world's strongest private corps — owed its name precisely to that, I think.

Whether or not you knew that someone like Krische-sama existed in the world.

That, I think, was the great dividing line.

Looking at us individually, myself included, none of us was outstanding.

A *genius* would describe Kalua at most; the others could perform reasonably well as mana-holders.

Even so, by ordinary people's standards we were more than enough — something to be proud of — but Krische-sama's existence permitted us no overconfidence on the battlefield.

Against any skilled opponent, we always attacked many to one.

Never accepting an even fight or near-even fight, never gambling — eye-gouging, throwing rocks, a crushing arm-bar if needed — we did whatever it took to win, hands free of any pretense.

With local numerical advantage, we crushed the enemy without letting them lift hand or foot.

Whether the man before us looked strong or weak was beside the point.

We did not let our guard down; we simply made use of the advantages of the group.

The battlefield was an unjust world.

If your luck tipped poorly, on the battlefield you might run into Krische Christand herself.

Before injustice was visited on you, you had to inflict injustice — or die.

Fortunately we escaped *the worst injustice;* but afterward, beginning with the strong-armed Nakilus and the Black Lion Gildanstein, we saw plenty of monsters who, alone, wielded injustice.

That at the training stage we had laid eyes on the very pinnacle of injustice was, I think, what made us strong.

A child of fourteen could wipe us out to the last man.

Then naturally, more terrifying monsters must exist out in the world, we'd thought at the time; and being so, training only gained in seriousness.

In fact, there was no second person like Krische-sama in the world; it was only over-thinking — but if asked when our beginnings as the Black Century were, surely it was that day.

And by the time the forest training was done, our consciousness had clearly changed.

Of course, asked whether we held goodwill toward Krische-sama, the answer for most was no; but learning the indisputable fact that she was a genius, one could be reconciled to her past attitude to a degree.

*She is not the worst kind of noble's daughter who looks down on commoners; she is a girl genius who simply cannot understand the ordinary,* and considering it that way, our anger eased somewhat.

That's what genius is, we thought; it can't be helped, and resignation came of itself.

Looking at her in that light, Krische-sama was overflowing with a sort of *very-genius-like* quality, which was another reason.

She paid no heed at all to how others saw her, free as she pleased.

Toward Selene-sama and Bery-sama she was just like a child; we had seen her clinging to them in public many a time.

For better and for worse, not a soldier, not even a noble.

And the same was true toward us.

"……Beltz. Why are we being made to practice cooking?"

"I don't know. But if she's said to do it, we have no choice but to do it."

Returned from the forest to the fort, another inspection.

We had thought she would see the fruits of how much we'd reformed and grown since.

That day's training was on encampment, and just at lunchtime.

While we were setting up tents and so on and cooking our own meals, Krische-sama came by, took a sip of soup, and went into a fury.

*"Vulture, what is this?"*

*"Y-yes, ah…… is there some problem——"*

*"There certainly is. I do not get the slightest sense from this soup of any care, of any wish to make a good meal. Will eating this raise the soldiers' morale?"*

*"No, indeed it would not, Vice-Adjutant!"*

*"You have had many opportunities to camp before — why do you not work to improve? I had thought Vulture was no bad centurion, but Krische is disappointed. In enemy territory it might be one thing, but in the present circumstances, with safety secured and time in abundance, this soup is a desecration of the ingredients. What in the world were you thinking to make——"*

The captain, who had now apparently gained the nickname *Vulture,* received the lecture in front of us.

On the whole, Krische-sama's evaluation of the captain was good; she might point out things or weigh in on policy, but never directly contradict him.

Yet this once, she must have been deeply displeased; her fury exceeded anything before.

Truly the picture of full-bodied prostration, Dagra kept apologizing—

*"E-excuse me, sorry. Today's cooking duty is on us, the first team, and the eleventh team……"*

Kalua, unable to bear watching, called to Mia and offered her up as the sacrifice (the cooking detail today *was* the first team), and at last Krische-sama's anger softened.

*If the food had simply turned out badly because of Mia's poor cooking, then it could not be helped,* she seemed to accept that for the time being.

*If you tried earnestly and failed, it can't be helped,* she nodded; but *that doesn't change the matter,* she said, and the training was suspended on the spot, replaced with cooking instruction.

* * *

"Mia, what kind of an adjutant are you? Just like deciding which soldiers to move in what order, you must consider the order of additions for soup ingredients. What good comes of dumping it all in at once?"

"S-sorry……"

"Clumsiness is one thing, but the consciousness of doing things carefully is what matters. *If you think hard about how to make it taste good, the feeling reaches the eater,* Bery said. Krische thinks she is quite right. Cooking, you see, is properly thinking about the eater——"

The cooking lesson going on behind us, we listened with mixed feelings.

After the previous day, surely everyone had been keyed up and full of motivation, but it was a let-down.

Made to make ten servings of soup divided in two-team groups, the men felt the same — *what on earth are we doing on a training ground?*

Soldiers being marched or made to run nearby kept glancing at us with looks that said *what are these guys doing* — that, too, was wearying.

We were the ones who wanted to ask that more than anyone.

When I tried to put cut ingredients into the pot, Beltz held up a hand.

"Wait a moment. If we don't make this seriously, we'll get yelled at."

"You've looked very practiced from earlier. Done this before?"

"My family used to. I left because I hated it."

"Huh, surprising. The kitchen knife doesn't suit you."

"Well, after that I lived a life much like yours. That's what I did longer."

Beltz laughed, ladled some soup into a dish, and tasted it.

"To think it'd come in useful here — for once, thanks to my old man. From the looks, no one here looks like a good cook. If we play this well, we might even take first."

He sounded confident.

As he said himself, his hands had been deft from earlier, the knife handling brisk.

We were one of the three groups that browned the meat for the soup, and the color of the sear was remarkable.

For first place, tomorrow's training was excused; for the bottom team's cooking lesson aside, it was a holiday.

Mixed as our feelings about all this were, if it ended that way, it wasn't bad.

"That'd be appreciated. We're counting on you——h."

"How's it over here? It looks not bad, by the look of things."

With a *nyu,* without a sign of presence, a face popped out from behind us — Krische-sama.

Her appearance was nothing but presence, but how could her presence vanish when she stood behind you?

Surely unconsciously, but it was bad for the heart.

"My lady. It is shaping up to be not a bad soup."

Beltz spoke and ladled some soup into a small dish to hand it over.

With a remarkably serious face she gazed at the small dish, closed her eyes, and smelled it.

She did not put it to her mouth, but a wordless interval continued.

A simple taste-test.

It shouldn't have been any great matter, but she wore an expression more serious than we had ever seen.

Strangely tense, Beltz and I exchanged a glance despite ourselves.

Slowly Krische-sama took her time to put the small dish to her lips, and again the pause stretched.

Like an antiquarian appraising a work of art, the affected manner — Beltz, I saw, swallowed.

Then slowly opening her eyes, Krische-sama glanced at the ingredients waiting on the cutting board, and asked—

"Why are you holding the chorus leaves?"

"I-I'd thought to add them just before finishing, so the aroma wouldn't, ah, escape……"

At Beltz's somewhat stiff reply, the silver-haired girl, knitting her brow, quietly nodded.

"The ingredients are coarsely cut, and shoddy preparation has left a roughness in the flavor."

"Y-yes…… my apolog——"

"But on the other hand, the way you have brought out the umami well by adjusting heat and order of addition is not bad. Whom did you make this soup for?"

"F-for the Vice-Adjutant…… I thought a lighter taste would be preferred."

"I see. Krische had meant for it to be made for soldiers tired from training, so on this point Krische's instruction may have been at fault. Even so, the very effort to consider the eater is to be greatly praised."

With a *yes-yes,* arms folded as if some craftsman, she said—

"Beltz, tomorrow you will instruct Mia's team in cooking. Vulture."

"My lady!"

At her call the captain ran over and straightened.

"Beltz is hereby provisional cooking instructor. From now on, when soldiers' rations are prepared, ensure Beltz tastes them, and instruct as needed."

"My lady, understood."

"Kouz. Learn from Beltz's work; assist him at his side. The other team members likewise."

Having said so much, Krische-sama turned to face Beltz.

He hurriedly straightened.

"As I said, this is hard to call a splendid dish."

"……!"

"But as soldier's rations, it is more than passable. Krische highly commends this soup's care in considering the eater — its thoughtfulness. Do not grow proud, but continue to refine and never lose your inventiveness. Surely you can aim higher still."

*——It was a delicious soup.*

At the words that followed, Beltz's eyes opened wide.

"My lady……! Thank you!"

He returned with a salute that looked truly delighted.

*Yes-yes,* Krische-sama nodded again and turned away.

"Vulture. Krische will return now."

"My lady! For your personal instruction, my deepest thanks."

"Don't bother with thanks. This is part of Krische's work too…… Until next time."

And patter, patter, off she trotted toward the fort, all of us watching her go.

Before the half-finished pot, an indescribably awkward atmosphere drifted.

"For now, finish the soup. We'll have it for dinner. Beltz, by the Vice-Adjutant's personal designation, the tasting of soldier's rations is yours from now on. In case of surprise inspections, teach these men the basics each time."

"My lord. Understood, Captain."

"……Resume work."

Everyone felt indescribable, but the captain probably most of all.

He had told us repeatedly that there would be another inspection by Krische-sama once we returned from the forest, that we should brace ourselves, but it had ended as a cooking lesson in its entirety.

He had wanted to show off the tighter, less complacent corps, and probably had been the most fired up of any of us — and as he walked away, melancholy hung about his back.

He was the chief victim, you might say.

Truly pitiable.

"Ahh…… well, in any event, that turned out well, Beltz——?"

At my voice, I found Beltz staring fixedly at the soup.

With somehow a faraway, nostalgic look.

"What's the matter?"

"It's nothing…… I was remembering old things."

"Old things……?"

He took a small dish of soup, and put it to his lips.

"My old man was particular about taste. From the first time I made a dish, he never once said it was good. ……So I came to hate cooking——"

And then with eyes closed, he nodded.

"——But, having someone say *delicious* about a soup I made for the first time in ages…… I know it's stupid, but I felt a bit happy. It wasn't that I'd come to hate the cooking itself."

"Th-that so…… well, that's good."

Unable to find anything to say to the wistful Beltz, I just patted his shoulder.

If the spark for the world's strongest private corps was the day Krische-sama showed us her sword, then the spark for Beltz and me ending up running an eatery together was, without doubt, that single word *delicious.*

Showing the sword had merely been instruction.

The word *delicious* too had been just an honest impression after drinking the soup.

Krische-sama herself, no doubt, was not thinking deeply about it; she had only done what came to her, said what came to mind.

But for everyone else, generally, that wasn't so.

Krische-sama's *injustice* was not, after all, limited to swords.

Her very existence was, in all likelihood, made of the word *injustice.*

With one casual act or word, easily she would break a person's life or warp it, or change it — and pay it no mind, not even know, gnawing on a cookie with a leisurely face.

The greatest of all natural disasters, surely, is called *Krische Christand.*

That, sometimes, is what I think.

# Chapter 334: The Dawn Crescent III

Krische-sama had any number of dangerous epithets, but the most famous, surely, was *the Head-Hunter.*

Eventually the prefix dropped naturally, but I haven't forgotten the origin.

Or rather — for us, it would be more accurate to say we *cannot* forget.

That battle was the one in which we came to truly know Krische Christand.

*"Our object is to buy time until the friendly armies can be reorganized. To that end, our top priority is delaying actions against the enemy. Combat is to be limited to surprise attacks and ambushes; absent further orders, prepare to withdraw once the dogs are dispatched. Do not engage in unauthorized pursuit."*

Those words were the start of an interminable week.

The struggle for the Dragon's Maw ended in the victory of the king's brother Gildanstein.

Even so, it had not been a complete defeat; in terms of casualty ratio we had overwhelmed the enemy.

With the general slain, defeat was defeat — that logic could be understood — but in every other phase we had taken the win, and to call it a defeat was, at most, a defeat by a hair.

We had burned the enemy together with the mountain and made the retreat cost them several times the bleeding.

The head of a corps commander who led five thousand, taken by a single century.

As if shaving the soft flesh of the throat.

"Mia, the rabbit really isn't doing well, is she?"

Even just closing eyes lightly and lying down made a difference in shedding fatigue.

Just letting consciousness go for a moment cleared my head.

The wake from light sleep came with a scream from up the mountain.

There was no need to imagine who had caused it.

The Christand force here was only us, and only one had gone up the mountain.

"Vulture, water."

"My lady……"

The men watching said not a word, watching the figure of that girl.

The next day, as a matter of course, she led troops in the raid.

Considering our endurance, perhaps, with half the corps resting.

Only the captain was always with her.

As Kalua had said, the limit of her stamina was clear.

Whether a fever had risen, her cheeks were flushed, and she swayed.

She did not look like the same person as the young lady who had idly watched us with a cookie.

Even those who had spoken ill of her now showed concern.

This was no longer a matter of stamina.

However strong, the body was a child's.

The stamina of a girl not five *shaku* tall surely could not exceed ours.

By stamina alone she should long since have been at her limit.

A fatigue that an ordinary person probably could not even stand under was visible — yet so long as one could manipulate mana, one could move; that was a mana-holder.

Literally, Krische-sama was moving her body on willpower alone.

*"If Krische can buy you a quarter-hour, that is one more quarter-hour Selene has to rebuild. While Krische can still fight, if she retreats again, she could not face Selene anymore."*

She would say things like that, or—

*"Selene was angry with the useless Krische. ……When the lord died, she cried so much…… because Krische was useless, Krische, hated by Selene……"*

She would say it dimly and fall asleep.

It was as if she were inflicting some punishment on herself.

*Surely tomorrow she'll retreat,* we said again and again, and on the fifth day we, too, were at our limit.

Honestly, given the chance, we would have wanted to flee.

With a different commander we would have, and depending on the case, we might have killed her, faking an accident, and reported it as honorable death in battle.

But Krische-sama was, for better and for worse, a child.

If shown a child of fourteen fighting desperately, covered in wounds, no full-grown man can leave her and run, nor speak a word of complaint.

Krische-sama was, in many senses, a sly person.

Earnest, dogged, with no concern for how others looked at her.

Put plainly, it was Krische-sama's selfishness.

Made to go along with it, dragged into a life-or-death fight, we couldn't even complain — we offered our lives and followed.

Half by compulsion, you might say.

That was the kind of human being Krische-sama was — naturally setting other people in that frame of mind.

Whether she was running a fever, whether she was at her limit, was beside the point.

Krische-sama worked harder than anyone, killed more than anyone, and helped her comrades more than anyone.

Before such a child, even if you split your mouth open, you could not voice complaint.

With each passing day the situation deteriorated, and Krische-sama's sword grew rougher.

Only when those swordsmanship techniques, which should have been called the height of fluid grace, dulled, did rabble like us at last come to grasp how monstrous they were.

The roughened sword of Krische-sama was, even to us her allies, a demon-blade that froze the blood.

"……Hh."

The small curved sword shaved or struck off heads.

Even an ordinary mass-produced sword found on the ground became a fairy-tale famed blade in the hand of Krische Christand.

Even counting was foolish.

As if clad in red wind; against her silver hair and white cheeks, life's red showed off well.

Her form was that of a girl, but it was a *concept.*

Not a sliver of sentiment, not a hesitation.

Merely *because someone stands before her with a sword in hand,* she kills.

For one standing on the battlefield, that is the most correct way of being.

But it was not the way of a human being.

Compared to one who can kill on the bare ground that someone happens to stand in their way, a pleasure-killer would be a far more human creature.

Krische Christand was a girl who easily crossed that one line.

"U-uwaaaaah!!"

"Tch……!"

While my attention was caught, an impact on my body.

What ran into me was a soldier giving off the smell of urine.

Knocked off balance and pushed down, the back of my helmet struck a tree root.

In the moment I was dazed, he took the short sword in a reverse grip, raising it to drive into me.

"Ngh, Kouz!"

The team noticed my state, but they wouldn't make it.

*This is death,* I gazed at his face dimly.

"Oh — ?"

With a *fwip,* something passed across, and the brat's head shifted askew.

The slip widened, fell from his neck, and a bloom of blood sprang up in its place.

"Y-yes……"

A small girl whom one wondered how she had ever been on a battlefield.

The beautiful blade was in tatters.

With innumerable nicks, it was a wonder it had not snapped.

Its curve, like a worn-out saw, was wavy.

Sharp nicks crushed dull by flesh, the warped trace.

A single girl, the result of having taken hundreds of lives.

"Kouz."

For anyone who had handled a sword to any degree, the abnormality was clear.

That she had used this curved sword without breaking it meant she had intended still to fight.

With a body unable even to stand properly, in order to cut down tomorrow's enemies.

Unless someone forcibly stopped her, she would have done so until death, surely.

That she was mad was beyond doubt.

*Sick in the head,* Kalua laughed, but that was what one believed from the depth of one's heart.

Never giving up on anything, not permitting herself to give up.

The worst kind of noble's daughter, into a girl-genius mad in the head.

The day that she became *our lord,* surely, was on that road home.

After that I saw Krische-sama's battles many times, and saw her achieve unbelievable results many times.

But that genuine, full strength, that was the first and the last.

Not because anyone said anything, but because each of us, surely, thought we should.

*Let Bernaich be the last.*

*——As Krische Christand's sword.*

Such fancy words, embarrassing as they were, we never said aloud, but this much can be said with certainty.

From that day, we fought with the same words in our hearts. With that conviction.

# Chapter 335: The Dawn Crescent IV

We had done what was right; the whole thing was a small, well-meaning rebellion.

But put into words, we'd seized command and retreated on our own.

Probably, *this is bad,* every man thought.

After a battle like that, three days later, when training resumed, no one complained, and everyone faced training in earnest.

Of course, having now learned the harshness of real combat.

The deaths of comrades had something to do with it as well, but above all it was Krische-sama.

Through that battle the human being called Krische-sama had begun to come dimly into view, and at the same time there was no longer room to doubt how dreadful she was.

For Krische-sama, killing people was no more than mowing grass.

Enemies were on the order of grass that talked, and there was neither mercy nor hesitation nor pity.

At a fundamental level, the contents of her head were different.

Most of us, myself included, had piled up a fair amount of experience.

We knew evil men of cold, peerless cruelty, but a person who could take human lives so unmoved as that — I, unfortunately, had never seen.

True, her actions were of good intent.

But whether that young lady would understand it that way was deeply doubtful.

Of course, we had performed well too.

Surely we wouldn't be executed, we thought, but in our hearts we were nonetheless drawing up plans to escape, *just in case it came to that.*

Krische-sama came calling about a week later.

*"——For the time being, Krische had various complaints, but the matter is dropped."*

At those words, we felt relief from the bottom of our hearts.

Not only that — when it was decided we'd be given rewards, the lot of us were overjoyed.

Well, our performance was our performance — by any reckoning we deserved at least that much, but we'd been thinking that, on a bad turn, we might be killed.

That she gave us rewards rather than punishment — there was no way we wouldn't get fired up.

On the talk of what to do for the unit insignia, I joined in too, *no, this way, no, that way.*

About half the corps participated, and we tossed around proposals.

The density of time, you might say.

The experience of sharing a battlefield closes distance fast.

People have their preferences; there will always be one or two with whom things don't click.

But brothers in arms can drink shoulder to shoulder with a man they would never have shared a drink with had they met him in town.

And for better and for worse, the century had every kind of man.

For instance, men like farmers and others who had never gripped a sword grouped with their like, while men like me, the bouncers and those who had eaten by their fists, would group with theirs — that's normal.

Naturally, given that we couldn't talk together.

During training that division was fairly clear, but before and after the battle, things changed dramatically.

The battlefield brings out one's true nature.

A loud-mouthed bully turned out to be a small-souled man, and out of nervous adrenaline tripped charging in; or a thin, weak-spirited brat we'd thought a coward turned out to shield him and save him.

That it was that hard a battle was part of it, but everyone owed his life to someone.

Watching what looked like a bully and a victim sharing a drink with arms around each other's shoulders, *this isn't bad either,* I thought.

I had a strange pride about having walked the world by my fists alone.

Compared to those buffoons, I was rather something — a lone wolf who didn't run with others.

*If I die in some ditch one day, that's me, that's my way, my life.*

*Not a bad life,* I'd say.

But if so, why on earth had a man who could be proud of such a life joined the army at all?

Probably, unconsciously, I had longed for a circle like this — that's what I think now.

The same went for the others.

There were many like me.

Friends made for a single night with drink, love bought for a single night with money, vanishing into foam each time dawn broke.

Yesterday and today were different worlds, yet the same kind of day repeated.

A month ahead lay in darkness; thinking of the future depressed me, and I distracted myself daily with disposable things.

Wanting something under my uncertain feet — that's why we'd gathered here, surely.

Made to undergo nonsensical training, with a commander who was a genius mad-rabbit.

Day after day, cursing while training as if to die, and fighting.

Yet noisy and bustling, even just deciding the unit insignia got us excited.

*Why are we so worked up over such a trifle?* the cool part of me whispered, but oddly the mood wasn't bad.

*— What about the moon?*

I don't remember who said it.

*Krische* meant a moon tracing an arc, the man said.

A learned fellow. Surely it had to be Bilza or someone like that.

I closed my eyes and watched the moon on the inside of my eyelids.

Cold to the point of freezing, but undeniably the queen of night, illuminating the world.

A signpost gently lighting the road ahead through pitch-darkness.

Onto that vanishing crescent the figure of the girl from the other day overlapped, and dimly the figures of us following her back.

*Then the flag color must be black,* someone said.

*Those who march carrying up the moon — the Black Century is the night-darkness lit by it.*

*Not bad,* everyone laughed and nodded.

* * *

It was after the battle with Klare Markelus.

That Kalua brought the princess to a victory celebration we'd thrown without permission.

A bold woman, I knew that — but fearless past the point of sense.

Carried in, Krische-sama looked as if to say *this is a nuisance.*

Set down on Kalua's lap, she silently surveyed the surroundings.

As ever, an expressionlessness that gave no indication of what she was thinking.

A drinking party, with the courtesies suspended.

Krische-sama is beautiful.

Anyone glancing at her thinks so.

*A person's heart shows in their appearance,* they say; that, perhaps, is what it means.

A spiteful man becomes a spiteful face; a timid man, a timid face.

In Krische-sama's case, surely, that purity seeped out from the inside.

Moonlight and a dazzling watchfire.

The soldiers lit by it.

At its center, peeling fruit, she suited the scene as well as anything could.

By day she had cut down the General and several dozen others in the blink of an eye, but it almost seemed that had been a dream.

The peeling work continued for a while, ended at one word from Kalua, and the talk turned to the mystery-shrouded Christand young lady, Krische Christand.

With drink in us and cheeks flushed, our mouths grew loose.

What sort of stories would emerge from her mouth — everyone was waiting expectantly.

The thrill of fearful curiosity, no doubt.

Less than a year since her first battle.

And yet, even at that point, she was a girl who had killed over a thousand by her own hand, taken the same number of lives, and made tens of times that many — including their families and lovers and friends — unhappy.

With a story of Krische-sama, naturally, men were interested.

I was no exception.

*This is war, it can't be helped* — there are plenty who say such things.

I was usually on that side too, but a slight sense of guilt always remained, and an ordinary man would, say, recall it before falling asleep at night.

Weighing the value of the man you killed against your own — *was I really worth taking his life?* — at least that, I think, anyone has thought.

To kill a person is to take a life of a fellow human being.

However you dress it up, you cannot deny that.

For the country, for comrades, for family — *I had to kill.*

There are people who can keep killing simply for themselves, but they're either fools or madmen.

Ordinary humans aren't, and grow tired somewhere along the way.

Just what justification this girl used to take more than a thousand lives.

What logic she used to escape that guilt.

*It probably isn't for herself,* I had sensed.

Anyone who'd shared Bernaich knew that.

You couldn't sacrifice yourself for yourself the way she had.

Mad, certainly, but with some commensurate reason — that I expected.

The thousand she'd killed didn't trouble her, the men she'd killed held no interest for her, and she did not see any value in them.

Truly a modest wish.

Calling Krische-sama a madwoman would anger some, but my view is unchanged from the time.

Without doubt, Krische-sama was abnormal — a person of the kind one calls so.

*A person who can render everything else worthless for the sake of her own beloved daily life* — what is that, if not a madwoman? No one can answer.

*Love, in the end, is discrimination,* I think when I look at Krische-sama.

Making one person happy can lead to making another sad.

A proper human being agonizes each time, weighing it on the scales in his head, but Krische-sama, surely, doesn't trouble herself with such things.

For her, things are far clearer — one or zero.

Simple, extreme, without hesitation.

I have known no one as deeply loving as Krische-sama.

I have known no one as sincere, earnest, and pure as Krische-sama.

That way of being is, from the depth of my heart, something I feel is beautiful.

But proper human beings are dirtier things.

We worry about how others see us, struggle desperately not to be made fools of, get caught up in all sorts of bonds, and live floundering, ungainly.

Even so, struggling and concealing our ugliness, we live earnestly — that's what humans are.

A princess who can stay beautiful by trampling on that effort without even knowing it exists.

If we are told *that's the right way of being,* what on earth are we?

Thinking that, after all, Krische-sama was a madwoman.

# Chapter 336: The Dawn Crescent V

Resolved to stake our lives, using even dirty tricks, leveraging numbers.

The most splendid fellows in the world.

The world is not equal.

The monster Ferizar split in two, the king's brother whom Kalua and the rest could not touch — beheaded with one stroke.

For mere insects, more than enough; with change to spare.

Killing people like insects, lowering her eyes that way.

With fewer people, the party ends.

The ordinary line, blind to the mood.

Without minding why no one else said the words, perfectly serenely.

As if commenting on a neighbor's unforeseen accident.

*And yesterday they were so well, too.*

Our princess, Krische Christand, does not understand the human heart.

We merely ran, holding up the moon.

Krische-sama is unjust, with no charm to spare, and never repeats the same mistake.

For the opponents, it must have been unbearable.

Krische-sama held no interest at all in position, honor, power, and the like.

King's elder sister and Alberinea.

For people who weren't her subordinates, even more so.

Within such relationships, she was so good it made the watcher anxious — yet outside the relationship, she'd render everything worthless instantly.

The biggest reason Krische-sama yearned for the role of servant, I sometimes think, is so as not to have to show that side of herself.

Everything within the relationship, a small world cut off from outside.

In such a place Krische-sama can stay good, and no one will think her mad.

Even the great achievement of unifying the world, probably, was for that reason alone.

Not a princess who loved peace — she only needed peace to hide her insane self.

Some will deny it, but for me, it makes more sense that way.

# Chapter 337: The Dawn Crescent XI

A small inn that served meals.

Talking with someone in the know, in the castle-town especially they were encouraged to raise the flow of people, and the tax was low compared to apartments.

It also fit with our goal of serving meals — being an inn was no bad thing.

We'd been making the rounds saying hello to the wholesalers who'd supply ingredients, doing this and that.

Nothing was finalized yet, and no contracts had been signed.

A short delay was no problem, but honestly, it was a bind.

"By the way, does the work go on forever?"

"Nope. About ten years and we'll be obsolete. You know about the Zenith?"

"Hm? Yeah……"

"Around that time it'll cover this world with mana. When that happens, Krische-sama and Her Majesty the Queen can do anything they want about anything."

"——So this is what we were thinking."

The magical crystal hung at her neck still held Bery-sama, sleeping.

Behind her eyelids she must have pictured that figure.

Opening her eyes, she turned her violet eyes our way.

Like jewels — to those organic-yet-mineral eyes, oddly, the cold of before could no longer be sensed.

"Krische thinks Kouz and Beltz's dream of a shop serving delicious food is a wonderful dream. But if you do it, then not as a side-job — fully, thinking only of the joy of the people who'll eat. ……That's how Krische wants you to work at it."

Krische-sama said it, and smiled.

"The two of you who put so much earnestness into cooking, surely you can build a really fine shop…… Ehehe, Krische supports you from the heart."

*When the shop is open, Krische will come eat,* she said, words that brooked no argument.

That kind of thing from Krische-sama got the surrounding men into a fuss; *use it for shop money,* they all started handing money over.

Few thought of *living a fairly comfortable life;* it was a lavish hand-out for the *future tavern.*

Because they were such a foolish bunch, Beltz and I had no choice but to build an inn lavishly using land in the capital.

Total amateurs, opening as a first shop something none of them had ever run — far too large a shop to make sense.

"Hey — it's started……"

On the street, arms folded, Beltz spoke as if dazed.

On the cleared lot at the very middle of the main street, materials in great quantity were being brought in one after another, and dozens of craftsmen were digging holes for pillars or doing carving.

"It's started, but…… do they intend to build a fortress in the city?"

"At the planning stage I thought it was a bit too large, but seeing it for real, it's overwhelming. Are we all right? We're half amateurs."

"Don't say that, Beltz. We can't back out now."

This had originally been a much lighter affair.

*If we ever retire someday, the two of us could pool our money and make a shop where corps members can come for a casual drink* — beginning from such talk, it was natural.

*"Ehehe, just in time. Actually, the day before yesterday Krische had a chance to talk with a scholar studying architecture, and as we were chatting about it lightly——"*

That kind of thing — Krische-sama brought a famous architect-scholar of the kind royals and great nobles entrust various designs to.

Alberinea had personally brought in a famous architect.

*A chance to make our name,* everyone must have thought.

With our small dream tossed in, what should have been a little shop was transformed into a jewel-box that the capital's leading architect and craftsmen took on in earnest.

Krische-sama was all leisure—

She laughed; out of pure goodwill alone, the worse for it.

Saying *we'll just do it as a hobby and live on it* would have been impossible to say with a straight face, and we couldn't muddy the faces of these men working.

Not young, not old enough to be called an old man.

Middle forties or so, by his look — young as a master.

A house centered on the castle-town that did work for merchants and commoners would understand things better, was the first reason.

What we wanted to build was no manor or inn for nobles.

# Chapter 338: The Dawn Crescent — End

And so the shop was finished, and before opening, we gathered the comrades for a feast.

Some couldn't come for work, but nearly two hundred came; the supposedly spacious shop was full of people.

At first I thought *the shop's too big,* but I came to think it was for the best.

Had we got cold feet and made the shop smaller, we'd never have seen this view.

Preparations from the previous day; cooking and laying things out.

That day was learning of various sorts.

*Lots of variety on the menu,* Beltz and I had thought, but for this large shop, the two of us had too much on our hands.

Cooking each dish on order would mean nothing but making customers wait.

For a feast we set things up ourselves, but with orders coming in, we couldn't manage two hundred.

"Reduce roasted dishes as much as possible. Best to focus on dishes that don't need warming, or dishes that don't take time to plate. Soups and stews — things you can finish work on the day before or in the morning — are appropriate."

At Kalua and Mia's table.

Sitting on Kalua's lap, picking up the menu and gazing at it, was Krische-sama.

At her seriousness, rarely seen, Beltz and I straightened too.

Krische-sama is generally a casual sort about most things.

Even communicating tactics on the battlefield, she's unsettlingly relaxed; but on matters of cooking, she switches like this.

*Isn't it usually the reverse?* I always thought, but that's the human being called Krische Christand.

She cannot be measured by ordinary values.

"Indeed…… having tried with people in actually, Kouz and I were just talking about how this won't work."

"It's not impossible, but supposing Krische were cooking here with Bery, the peak time you faced with this menu was substantial. To balance speed and quality, you must concentrate considerably on the cooking."

Krische-sama set the menu down, folded her arms, and closed her eyes.

"Good things are born from leeway. And the ideal of work is to do it at thirty or forty percent of full effort, is Bery's teaching. Even should some problem arise, the important thing is to be able to deal with it with leeway."

And, as always citing one of Bery-sama's golden lines, Krische-sama nodded.

We were a little exasperated, but the point was sound.

Missed orders, mistakes in serving, troubles are inevitable.

"To be able to handle even all conceivable inconveniences and worst cases with leeway is the basic of work. Improving capacity to make leeway is one approach, but in practice it's more realistic to distribute the work across time."

"I see."

"It's a bit pricey, but adding a freezer would also be good. Pies and so on can be made ahead and frozen, and put straight into the oven without much issue. You might also increase oven dishes in place of roasted ones——"

And so she gave advice on this and that.

Having researched cooking daily for years, the breadth of her knowledge was astonishing.

The Alberinea whose name resounded across the world was more in earnest about cooking than about war.

Krische-sama's tale, having read every literature and tested it, was useful, and while the others were drinking and making merry, Beltz and I were absorbed in cooking lessons.

"For the common folk, working hard with ingredients matched to the price, that's clear, and they're all reasonably tasty, but only reasonably. Stars and supporting roles — where you spend money on ingredients should be considered a bit more."

"Stars and supporting roles……"

"Yes. If each performs the role appropriate to it, that is enough. Not everyone needs to be a star."

Krische-sama said it, and gazed around at the men drinking nearby.

With somehow happy, narrowed eyes.

"Various ingredients gather, and only then is one dish formed."

*Bery used to say,* she added proudly, raising a finger, with that flower-blooming smile of hers.

*A strange thing,* I think.

Krische-sama, honestly, is incomprehensible to me even now.

The fundamental wiring of her thought is different, so that's a given.

Because she says what she thinks, in that sense she's easy to grasp; but with Krische-sama, even when you think you've read what's in her head, words come out from above-or-below at an angle.

*I'll never understand this person in this lifetime,* I gave up.

But just at that moment, what was she thinking, watching them?

That, I understood, and somehow Beltz and I exchanged looks and nodded.

We are supporting roles among supporting roles, men who could never be the leads.

But yes — for instance, in a place where the leading-lady girl, dropping in casually, says—

*"This shop is always delicious."*

A place where she laughs and eats — to be its master and cook is no bad thing.

Even as a supporting role, that's a tasty supporting role.

* * *

"Father, drinking liquor of all things…… honestly, if you die I'll know nothing of it."

"What's wrong with a drink before dying. Don't worry, I'll be dead by tomorrow."

My daughter sighed wearily and set the lamb stew she'd brought on the counter.

Lamb simmered melt-tender from the day before.

A soup base extracted from vegetables, reduced; depth from red wine.

*Speak of the Dawn Crescent and you speak of the lamb stew.*

The signature menu item everyone knows, that all regulars order.

A spoonful — rich, with a refreshing touch of acid.

"Today too, Beltz's flavor."

"Indeed. Delicious."

Tagel answered; I closed my eyes.

Krische-sama showed up at the shop quite often.

Mostly she'd come for lunch with Mia and Kalua and the others.

Krische-sama wasn't terribly fussy about flavor.

If she found something delicious she'd say so, and there was nothing put-on.

Her skill, of course, was extraordinary; with knife in hand she made artwork-like dishes Beltz and I could never match — but she didn't expect that of dishes set before her.

Well, deliciousness past a certain point becomes a question of fine difference.

It's subjective, and a bit lacking is not the same as bad.

Sixty out of a hundred, with hunger, can taste delicious.

At eighty no one will call it bad; above that is taste.

In that sense Krische-sama was rather lenient, an easy customer.

She seldom faulted others' cooking unless it was truly unworthy.

But Beltz and I couldn't accept that.

Looking back I laugh, but probably we wanted praise.

We wanted to make a dish that Krische-sama, from the bottom of her heart, would think *this shop's food is something else,* and would deliberately make her way over for.

Not *because it's a subordinate's shop,* not *along the way.*

After seeing off Krische-sama who would say *that was delicious* and head home, Beltz and I would discuss her reaction, *no, this way, no, that way.*

*"Today's soup, I thought it was the height of taste and aroma…… but it was the same as ever. I'd been confident in it……"*

Beltz would slump and sigh; I always slapped his shoulder.

*"Don't be down. I thought today's soup was extraordinary too, but…… looking at the results we've gotten so far, the direction may be wrong."*

*"Wrong?"*

*"Even you can't beat Krische-sama on technique. If we make food of the kind she normally makes, we can't beat her, naturally. To make Krische-sama feel impressed, shouldn't we compete with food and seasonings she doesn't normally make?"*

Krische-sama learned cooking from Bery-sama.

Bery-sama too is noble-born, the direction is delicate and refined — *noble cooking* combining various ingredients; Krische-sama's cooking is fundamentally in that direction.

*"Common-folk cooking by definition…… food with punch like what's served in taverns. How about basing on that and giving it a nice arrangement?"*

*"……I see. With punch, huh."*

*"Yes, not soup, but stew dishes — that kind of thing."*

The biggest difference from noble cooking is, surely, the ingredients.

Meat, for instance — common folk use strongly-aromatic vegetables and herbs to mask the smell, finishing into delicious food.

Krische-sama, who at the royal estate handles only first-class ingredients, makes artwork-like dishes that draw out the ingredients themselves — meaning the reverse approach, she almost never takes.

*"However good ingredients we line up, with what we can stock, we can't make a star fit to satisfy Krische-sama. Then better to line up bottom-to-bottom rubbish supporting roles and build up something delicious by skill."*

*"……Surprisingly, that may be more interesting to her."*

*"That's what I mean. What do you think?"*

*"……Not bad. Let's go that way, Kouz."*

I'm a complete amateur at cooking.

I don't dislike it, of course, and I've some confidence I cook better than the average man.

But Beltz, unlike me, has knowledge, and thinks about things on a different plane from a *cooking enthusiast.*

A natural craftsman through and through.

Truly, had he gotten on with his old man, he might well have ended up cooking in the royal palace.

He turned my passing thought into a serious dish, working late into the night for half a month.

Basing on a hearty common-folk stew, adjusting the combinations of ingredients and herbs and the simmering, he gave it depth and delicacy.

A lamb stew that would make both dull tongues and gourmets groan.

*This is good,* it gained a small reputation in the neighborhood; *we can do this,* we laughed together.

*"……How is it?"*

Eating it, Krische-sama knit her brows, with a difficult face.

*"What's the matter, Krische-sama?"*

*"Hmm — too rich for the rabbit? I think it's delicious, though."*

*"Yes. I think it's very tasty too……"*

*"……No, certainly it's a bit rich, but not too much so. Good with bread too."*

Usually Krische-sama would chat through a meal, but rare for her, she ate silently.

Finishing the stew, eating three pieces of bread—

*"……Three nights from now, I'll bring Selene and the others. Plan accordingly."*

*"Eh? Y-yes……"*

She said.

* * *

"……A delicious stew. Even after he's dead, the flavor doesn't change; even after I die, this flavor won't change."

I broke up the melt-tender lamb with the spoon, put it in my mouth, and looked up at the ceiling.

Customers' voices echoed from the high ceiling, and a busy clatter from the kitchen.

Beltz had died quite a while back, but he'd looked very satisfied.

Before dying he too sat at this counter and ate the lamb stew.

Probably I will die in the same way.

"Don't you think it's trifling, when you think about it? A lifetime spent and what you leave is one shop, your end as the master of a bakery. We're no great heroes whose names ring through the world, no great men. As children we were going to be more impressive grown-ups, weren't we?"

"He who knows when he has enough has the happiness fitting to him. I don't think I was unhappy; I've lived a happy life."

"Boring, but yes. Trifling, but not bad. A hundred years lived without doing anything notable, yet strangely, somehow. I'm satisfied."

Born to a poor house, learned thieving, and before I knew it the back alley was my place.

Drinking and buying women — eventually I tired of distractions, and even with a wife who wasn't a beauty but laughed often, we lived in places the sun didn't reach.

After we came to serve that mad princess, much changed, but flattered as a hero, I'm a small man you'd find anywhere.

With or without me, in this wide world, my existence didn't really matter.

"I'm a trifling man. But what about the great hero of the realm, Krische-sama? She's enormously satisfied with that very trifling thing. She must be having a grand time over there now."

"……Maybe so."

"Indeed, I think this. *Humans are all about that level. Trifling things.*"

I tipped the cup, emptied it, exhaled along with the alcohol.

Putting the rest of the stew in my mouth, I stood, and slapped Tagel's shoulder.

"Thanks to you, I'm in a good mood. Goodbye, Tagel."

"Yeah. ……Pleasant dreams, Kouz."

"……You too."

At those words I gave a single laugh and headed for the stairs.

Before going up I turned, and gazed at the bustling shop.

*"——Very delicious."*

At the center of the wide shop, with Her Majesty the Queen, Selene-sama, and two servants alongside.

Folding her arms, Krische-sama said it with a solemn face.

*"This lamb stew, in Ber——! Ahem."*

Krische-sama, as she started to speak, glanced at Selene-sama and, deliberately, cleared her throat.

At the gesture, Selene-sama flushed and sighed.

That cryptic exchange caught our notice too, but neither Beltz nor I had the leeway to mind.

*"Mh…… compared to the finest dishes Krische has eaten, this lacks beauty, lacks delicacy. But that does not mean we should fail to value the deliciousness — built up by skillful combination of inferior-quality ingredients."*

*Yes-yes,* she nodded, and said—

*"With Selene's permission. Krische intends to grant the simplified crest of the Christand house to this shop."*

*"……!"*

*"However, do not grow proud and continue to refine. By any chance, should this taste decline, Krische will revoke the simplified crest without mercy."*

At the finger-raised Krische-sama, Beltz, deeply moved, saluted.

*"Yes! Thank you……! We did it, Kouz! Thanks to you!!"*

*"Idiot, it was your effort."*

What a coarse fool, hugging me though we were both men.

With that scene in mind, I trudged my heavy body up to the second floor.

Walked the long corridor, headed for the room, and threw my body onto the bed.

Despite drinking only a little, the drink had gotten round, and the mood was not bad.

The same mood as yesterday, when I'd failed to die.

*Whether or not I wake doesn't matter.*

Out the window the absurd great tree was in full bloom with rainbow flowers.

The faint moon at the height of heaven I'd seen on waking had hazed over and disappeared.

Sun in clear sky, lighting the flowers in fantastic color.

As if dreaming, the world seen from the window looked very beautiful.

The most trifling, mad-headed garden of love in the world.

Thinking they have set off into that ridiculous world, this world may indeed be like a dream.

A dream world the way that princess wishes.

I was a character in a thoughtless dream, a bit-part wherever you go.

Probably in time, casually, as if remembering, she'll drop in.

Laughing with reminiscence, *I once granted this shop a simplified crest.*

How many years it'll last I don't know, but at least for a while it'll be safe.

Beltz's flavor remains and is passed on to grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

"……We'll guarantee the flavor."

*——Patronize us a hundred years from now too.*

I closed my eyes, picturing the smiling figure of the princess.

* * *

The Dawn Crescent is a shop that serves common-folk dishes.

Founded by men of the Black Flag Special Service, in the day even the famed Alberinea and Her Majesty the Queen are said to have come several times in disguise.

But that's a story of long ago.

Customers visibly noble are now hardly ever to be seen.

"Keep the change."

"My, ……thank you very much."

That day, unusually, seven beautiful women came, all together.

Likely they were nobles.

Customers most rare for this shop.

They finished their meal looking satisfied, presented silver coins, in fine spirits.

Relieved that it had suited them, I asked—

"Was the meal to your satisfaction?"

"Yes, very much so."

The girl-like servant in an apron-dress smiled like a flower opening.

"Ehehe — delicious as ever."

- Alignment

Lawful — values rules and discipline, universal concepts; emphasizes ordered society and law.

Chaotic — values the individual and their feelings and thoughts; emphasizes will over social discipline and morality.

Neutral — between the two.

Good — altruistic temperament. Values morality believed in over personal interest, and dies for it.

Evil — egoistic temperament. Values personal interest, and does not balk at trampling others for it.

- How to read kingdom-noble names

Example: 1, Gildanstein = 2, Kalnaros = 3, Vel = 4, Sarcarinea = 5, Alberan

1, Given name: Gildanstein

2, The largest territory among the lands they administer: Kalnaros.

Properly the entire administered region is included, making it very long, and so this is abbreviated.

3, Vel for male royals with right of succession; Vera for female royals.

4, Honorary title: Sarcarinea

5, Surname: Alberan

Properly between 4 and 5 a military rank such as Marshal or General, or a court office such as Chancellor, would be inserted, and after the surname a peerage rank such as Grand Duke would follow.

But the foreign-letter strings are already long and the eye slides off, so within the text we attach the necessary rank or office at the end and write it in kanji for clarity.

Aside from the surname, none of these are necessarily passed to children, but unless there's a particular issue, by custom the legitimate heir most often inherits the lands and the title.

However, only 4, the honorary peerage marking martial achievement, is not inherited.

For someone without a surname who receives a *Nea* — quasi-knight informal investiture, often they take their place of origin as their name.
