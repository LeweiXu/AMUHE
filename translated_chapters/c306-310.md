# Chapter 306: Reine VII

She rode relay horses to the royal castle.

Without eating properly, without sleeping, she made straight for Fiiri's room.

There was no one in Fiiri's room, and a thin layer of dust had gathered on the shelves and paintings.

The room was full of things, but Fiiri had been fastidious about cleanliness.

That sight alone was enough to tell her of Fiiri's absence.

"……Elsreine-sama."

"……Where is Fiiri?"

From the still-open door, the face that peered in belonged to her attendant, Libinia.

She quietly shook her chestnut hair and shook her head.

Her face was dark.

"About two weeks ago, while she was tending the flower beds…… a knife, to the chest."

"The culprit?"

"A servant of the royal estate — Nasel. However, he took his own life…… The Riselnia house, his birth family, was disbanded, and a thorough investigation was conducted, but the reason……"

She knew the face.

She had never spoken to him.

"……Why Fiiri?"

"I don't know. ……Why Fiiri-sama had to be killed…… I just……"

She covered her face to hide her tears and sank to her knees.

Reine could not understand.

Fiiri was a servant, uninvolved in politics.

She was not the kind of person who made enemies, nor was she capable of it.

If it had been incidental, that would be one thing — but from what she had heard from Gorzarius and the woman's manner, this had clearly been targeted.

Reine passed the weeping woman and climbed the stairs.

The servants watched her with expressions of fear, pity, or sorrow.

She knocked on the office door, and a voice said, *Come in.*

Her mother was working at the desk and looked up at Reine as she entered.

"……You were quick."

"Did you kill her, kaa-sama?"

Her mother sighed, looking tired, troubled — then smiled.

She stood and approached.

"Do you think there's anything for me to gain by doing that? I don't want to be hated by you."

"Kaa-sama hated Fiiri."

"That is, admittedly, true."

She stepped away from the hand her mother reached out.

Then she glared at her mother.

Her mother wore a troubled expression, her hand wandering in the air.

"Reine did as you said, kaa-sama. She took care of Rougran-niisama and all the others, put down the rebellion, and has been a good girl all this time. So why did you kill Fiiri?"

"Why do you think it was me?"

"……Who else, and for what reason, would kill Fiiri?"

Her mother sighed again.

*Calm down,* her mother said.

"Someone has put foolish ideas in your head. ……This situation is exactly why — the purpose is to drive a wedge between you and me, wouldn't you say? I certainly tried to distance that girl from you. I considered killing her, but if I were going to, I would have done it long ago, and not so clumsily."

"……The other day, Seil-niisama said he spoke with Fiiri. Kaa-sama must have found that displeasing too."

"……All you do is suspect me."

"Then who would do such a thing?"

*We're going in circles,* she sighed.

"Suppose I did kill her — what will you do? Will you kill me to avenge her?"

"That's……"

"……Rest a while, Reine. You're exhausted and not thinking clearly. You look terrible. Then we can sit down and talk."

The usual kind voice.

Mother was always kind to Reine.

She had never doubted the words that said she loved Reine, and even now, she did not doubt it.

——But she was not that way with others.

Her mother hated everyone except Reine.

Perhaps this, too, was a form of love.

Perhaps she had not wanted to make Reine sad — and yet.

"……Reine doesn't understand you, kaa-sama."

For an instant, her mother's face stiffened.

"Reine has heard that before Reine was born, things were terribly hard. That's why you couldn't trust anyone anymore……"

*It is the King's Sickness,* Gorzarius had said.

The retainers who had offered counsel and been executed had said similar things, and Fiiri too had probably wanted to do something about it.

Whenever Fiiri saw Reine say she wished her mother would change, she always looked happy.

"But there really are good people in the world, like Fiiri, and all Reine wanted was to build that small paradise kaa-sama once talked about, together with people like her…… to live happily with kaa-sama, and Fiiri…… and everyone……"

That was all.

She loved both her mother and Fiiri, so she had only wanted them to live together happily.

If only her mother could notice Fiiri's good qualities — that was all she had wanted to convey.

Her vision warped, and a sob escaped.

Her chest was tight and she could barely breathe.

Why had it not gotten through?

That was the only thing she had wanted to tell her mother.

"Was it…… because Reine thinks that way? Because Reine is so foolish, because she can't think the same way as kaa-sama…… is that why……"

"Reine, no, that's not it — I——"

"It's fine. ……Reine doesn't want to talk to kaa-sama anymore."

"……!"

She ignored the voice calling *Reine,* and left the office.

She ran, mounted a horse, and rode out of the royal estate and beyond the capital.

*Why,* she repeated.

*'——Reine doesn't understand. Why…… why Fiiri?'*

*'……Unfortunately, there is no other candidate but Her Majesty. A murder within the royal estate…… if it were an assassination aimed at Her Majesty, that would at least make sense, but to go out of one's way to target a servant — it is difficult to imagine anyone doing something so pointless and absurd.'*

Round and round, the exchange repeated in her head.

*'From what Your Highness has told me, Her Majesty found the servant called Fiiri tiresome. Every argument between you was about that servant…… perhaps Her Majesty felt threatened by the Fiiri that Your Highness loved.'*

*'Threatened……'*

*'That Your Highness might drift away from her. For Her Majesty, Your Highness is her one and only irreplaceable person — it is only natural to fear losing that.'*

*'Fiiri would never…… and Reine——'*

*'Indeed. But…… Her Majesty is ill. So deeply that she cannot trust anyone but Your Highness. She may even, in the depths of her heart, doubt even you.'*

Before going to the capital, as he prepared a horse for Reine, Gorzarius had spoken with a pained expression, looking reluctant to answer.

*How lamentable,* he sighed, yet to the stunned Reine, he told her many things.

Too plausible to disbelieve, too real to dismiss.

As he said, her mother had disliked Fiiri, and sighed every time she saw Reine being influenced by her.

They had quarreled just the other day — and just as her mother had removed Fiiri from her position as attendant, Reine had always feared her mother might punish Fiiri, and had been careful to prevent it.

But that anxiety had always existed in her heart, and so she could not deny his words.

*'……Could it not be a mistake? That, well, it wasn't Fiiri, but someone else……'*

*'……I too am only relaying what I have heard, not something I have confirmed with my own eyes, but…… please leave this to me. Seeing for yourself would likely be best.'*

And then, kneeling, Gorzarius continued.

*'……I am your servant. I shall wait here. ——Elsreine-sama.'*

Her head spun and her body was heavy.

She wondered what she would do when she returned, yet she simply wanted to get away from the capital.

She rode her horse, changed mounts, and simply headed east.

She slept on horseback, ate meals, and so passed several days.

"Reine……"

"……Seil-niisama."

Appearing on horseback with a small escort was Seil.

His face was stern, brow creased, and he glared at Reine.

"……Where are you going?"

"That is none of Seil-niisama's concern."

"It most certainly is. ……Do you intend to betray mother?"

Reine did not answer and tried to pass him, but the escort cavalry also moved to block her path, their expressions grim.

"……Please move aside."

"Calm down. You're only confused. Did Gorzarius say something to you? Do not trust that man. He defied my orders, treats the army as his personal tool, and is trying to use you for some scheme."

Seil repeated, *Calm down.*

"If you act, the kingdom will be split in two. It will become a quagmire. ……At the very least, this is not what Fiiri would have wanted——"

"……What did Seil-niisama say to Fiiri?"

"……?"

"Benuure did it for Seil-niisama…… Did you fill Fiiri's head with your nonsense too? Is that why this happened?"

Seil froze. Reine leapt from her horse onto Seil's mount.

"Gah, kh……!?"

Grabbed by the collar and slammed onto his back on the ground, the bared tip of a blade was pressed to his throat.

The purple eyes that gazed down at him were ice-cold, filled with hatred.

"……Do not ever appear before Reine again. If you interfere further, Reine will kill every one of you here and now."

"Reine……!"

Having spoken, Reine remounted.

The young girl's eyes were freezing cold, and even the battle-hardened cavalry, trembling like green boys, instinctively cleared the path.

Reine rode off again, and Seil simply slammed his fist into the earth.

* * *

A tyrant who imposed oppression, executed many people of good conscience, and sought to dominate the world through fear alone — Queen Grabareine.

Elsreine, the only one at her side who had tried to correct her deranged heart, was forced into an unwanted war against brothers who had risen for the people's sake, and in the end, even the servant she loved was murdered.

Queen Grabareine's heart was gone.

And so Princess Elsreine rose up and raised the banner of rebellion against Queen Grabareine.

Such was the story that spread like wildfire, and when it reached the ears of those who had been crushed by the princes' great defeat, people flooded into the east from every corner.

The only one who could stand against the ever-victorious Grabareine was her alone.

She had already demonstrated her brilliant talent in the wars against the princes, and she had treated with leniency the soldiers who had participated in the great crime of rebellion.

She had released the many who had been incited to join, declaring that the people's guilt was not heavy, and it was said that her swift assaults on enemy camps and the four duels in which she personally slew her brothers had been for that very reason.

The tax reductions originally planned for after the rebellion's suppression — the humane and lenient measures were the carrot after the stick — but those who followed Elsreine spoke of them as born of the princess's mercy and compassion, spreading the narrative of good versus evil.

In barely a month, even those who had not joined the princes' rebellion began to rise.

"Elsreine-sama, how about it? Shall we go outside for some fresh air?"

"Lukazan, Reine wants to be left alone."

"……That is not possible. The people are gathering. Many in the east have begun to pledge their allegiance. It is time for Elsreine-sama to look forward."

The Alberan rebel army's base — Renaria.

It was the largest city in the east, and the former capital of a kingdom once destroyed by Grabareine.

In a room in that castle, Gorzarius visited Elsreine — who had been sleeping in her bed — nearly every day.

"Look forward, and do what? ……Reine doesn't want to do anything."

"If Elsreine-sama remains like this, Fiiri would be saddened."

"……Fiiri is gone."

"She may not be alive, it is true."

Elsreine frowned and sat up.

Gorzarius spoke while gazing out the window.

"However…… her words live on in your heart, Elsreine-sama. Were the days she lived and spent meaningless? Do you say that everything she was born for, everything she taught you, had no meaning at all?"

"That's……"

"I understand your grief at Fiiri's death. She too must have passed with great regret, her work left unfinished. ……But for you to waste your time like this is tantamount to denying her life."

Reine's eyes widened, and Gorzarius closed his.

"Why do you think we soldiers risk our lives in battle?"

"……Well,"

"Most are not as strong as you. That even I am alive is luck…… a gamble that doesn't even add up — it must look foolish to you."

After thinking for a moment, she nodded.

"Everyone is so fragile compared to you. And yet they choose to fight. ……Merchants, craftsmen, farming the land would be fine — there are plenty of ways to live, yet they risk their lives. Where does that reason lie?"

Gorzarius turned to face her and looked straight into her eyes.

"……It is a wish, Elsreine-sama."

"A wish……"

"Even if they die for it, so long as what they protect can live in peace — that is the wish. ……It is because of that wish that they can sometimes accept becoming a stepping stone, accept even death. Believing that those who survive will surely fulfill their wish."

Then he smiled gently.

"A wish more precious than one's own life…… Fiiri surely had one too. In her final moments, she must surely have thought of it…… Without fulfilling that wish, will you simply spend the rest of your life lamenting Fiiri's death?"

"Reine……"

"To me, at least, that is far sadder. All the many words Fiiri wove for Elsreine-sama would have meant nothing — they would simply vanish."

That was, indeed, a sad thing.

Fiiri would surely not be happy to see Reine like this.

"……What say you, Elsreine-sama? Shall we fulfill her wish together?"

"Together with Lukazan and the others……?"

"A world of peace, without war…… a small paradise, I believe you called it."

"A small…… paradise……"

What came to mind was the painting Fiiri had once made.

A place where everyone smiled and was happy, where unused swords and armor lay discarded.

"Of course, it will be a difficult road. Uniting the hearts of many and creating such a world…… However, I do not believe it is an impossible wish. At the very least, if we continue to wish for it, I believe we can draw closer, little by little."

"…………"

"……In name, we are currently a rebel army against Her Majesty. But Elsreine-sama — you still cannot bring yourself to truly hate Her Majesty, can you?"

She nodded.

Soldiers had gathered in more than sufficient numbers, and conversely, the kingdom's side was unstable.

Many had come saying this was the chance to lead this army and destroy the capital, but Reine had refused.

*Why did she kill Fiiri,* Reine thought — yet she could not bring herself to think, *I want to kill my mother.*

She knew her mother loved her, and Reine loved and respected her mother too.

She was only sad.

That was all Reine felt.

That her mother had done something that could never be taken back — that was simply, unbearably sad.

"When you returned from the capital, I already sensed as much. If that had been your intent, you would have done it then. ……Elsreine-sama is a kind person; even now, you still love Her Majesty."

When she nodded, *It's alright,* Gorzarius smiled.

"There is no need to heed the words of fools driven by emotion. They will settle down in time. And to that end, we need a new name for ourselves — not the Alberan rebel army."

"A name……"

"Yes. As a new place, a new nation, that Elsreine-sama will build."

"……Build…… a nation?"

"Yes. That would surely be best."

Gorzarius turned his gaze out the window once more.

In the sunlight, birdsong filled the air.

"……In order to gather military strength, we spread word that Her Majesty is evil, a tyrant, and drew many people and nobles to us, but…… I too do not believe Her Majesty is such a person. She was exposed to much malice, and simply lost the ability to trust anyone…… She is a pitiful woman."

"……That is——"

"I do not wish to drag Her Majesty from her throne, nor to raise rebellion. I merely…… want Her Majesty to notice our feelings, and Elsreine-sama's…… that is all."

*Words alone will not suffice,* Gorzarius said.

Squinting against the bright light.

"……But even if words do not reach her, if a nation like the one Elsreine-sama envisions can be built…… if we can show her a place where everyone lives happily and in peace…… then perhaps Her Majesty's heart can be healed. ……I believe something can be conveyed, just as Elsreine-sama's paintings convey something."

Reine watched him, then lowered her eyes.

After a while, she asked.

"……Is that truly possible?"

"Yes. At the very least, I believe so, and will lend what humble aid I can. ……If Elsreine-sama will have me, I swear upon this name — I will devote my life to it."

Reine stood from the bed and stepped onto the carpet.

Gorzarius knelt and bowed his head.

"……Elsren. That would be good."

"Elsren……"

"Just now, that…… the name."

*Is it strange?* Reine asked, and Gorzarius gave a wry smile.

"A small paradise…… it does seem a little incongruous as a nation's name."

"Um, if it's no good……"

"However…… it is a fine name, full of hope."

Gorzarius removed his sword from his belt and held it up to Reine as if in offering.

"……Would you accept this Gorzarius Lukazan as the first subject of that Elsren?"

Reine looked briefly taken aback, but soon smiled.

She took the sword, said *yes,* and returned it to his bowed hands.

Elsren — she pictured Fiiri's smiling face as she had spoken that name.

——The loyal retainer's face, again, could not be seen.

# Chapter 307: Reine VIII

——Alberan Kingdom Year 203.

The fracturing of the great continental power, Alberan, and the birth of the Republic of Elsren.

The nations of central continent — which had only recently been brought under Alberan's eastern territories — welcomed the establishment of a state that opposed Alberan.

Until its formal founding, it had existed as a web of defensive pacts — an anti-Alberan coalition in all but name — but Elsreine established a central parliament to strengthen ties with them.

She incorporated local leaders as members of parliament, and while Alberan dealt with the internal problems arising from the split, Elsren took shape as a nation with astonishing speed.

This owed much to Elsreine's overwhelming military achievements in the still-recent Alberan civil war, and to her miraculous skill in resolving the various problems of nation-building.

Some said she possessed magical eyes that could see the future; she took the gathered funds and turned them into enormous profits in an impossibly short time, laying a financial foundation for the adrift rebel army to stand upon as a nation.

One month to swallow a country, two months to swallow a great power.

In an extraordinarily brief span, Elsreine seized control of the central continent's economy, and the great merchants, astonished by her 'alchemy,' flocked to the capital, Renaria, one after another.

It is said that the reason the two great powers — the houses of Natrias and Liberslebe, who would later become two of the three Grand Dukes of the Elsren Holy Empire — declared their participation was precisely because of this.

"The chairman's sentiments are to be commended…… however, the current priority must be domestic stability and preparation for the Alberan invasion that will surely come. The establishment of orphanages should be tabled; the time and budget are better used elsewhere."

"That is certainly true, but……"

The heart of Renaria — the First Assembly Hall.

Men seated around a large circular table were among Elsren's most powerful nobles.

At the head, beneath a large regional map, sat the golden-haired girl — Reine, who wore a troubled expression as she considered how to phrase her response.

Elsren comprised lands that had only recently been incorporated into Alberan's territory.

War orphans from Alberan's invasions were plentiful, public order was in disarray, and stability was far off.

Originally, the plan had been to address these issues through generous support and restored security after the rebellion — and that plan remained unchanged with the Republic of Elsren.

However, the situation was fundamentally different in one respect: here, a powerful enemy nation called Alberan existed.

The parliament members wanted to prioritize military expansion and pour funds into that.

Reine could understand the logic, but the current state of affairs was more than adequate for a defensive war.

They seemed intent on conquering Alberan, and disagreements arose.

Reine had no intention of killing her mother or conquering Alberan.

She only wanted to make this country a good one where everyone could be happy.

That was all.

But that was Reine's logic alone.

Many had been conquered by Alberan — she could understand their resentment toward her mother, could not flatly deny it, and had no right to.

Reine was the chairman; the nation's affairs had to be discussed and decided together.

"Well now, let us wait. What the chairman is concerned about is ten, twenty years hence — military buildup must be kept to a minimum or expenses will mount. The chairman believes current defenses are sufficient against Albe—against Alberan. Is that not so?"

"Yes, as Liberslebe says. Speaking strictly of a defensive war, Reine believes we have the strength to stand against kaa-sa—Alberan."

"I, too, share some concern with the others, but…… the chairman is more versed in military affairs than anyone here. From what I have heard, I see no major error in that assessment. Does anyone object?"

Kirias Liberslebe, seated beside her, was a lean, wiry man.

His face appeared mild, but his eyes were sharp, gazing at the assembly members as though glaring.

"It seems to me that you are all being led by your emotions. Alberan is, in any case, powerful…… destroying them with a hastily assembled force is no easy task. Even assuming we give our utmost and succeed, a stalemate is the best we could hope for. ……And if that happens, we will inevitably lose before Alberan's national strength. We do not have the foundation to wage a prolonged war."

Reine nodded — he had said what she wanted to.

Kirias was the most influential voice in the parliament.

A sharp mind and a skilled speaker, he understood Reine's intentions well.

"This nation is overflowing with orphans. In ten years, they will descend into vagrancy and severely undermine public order. They are like a debt — left unchecked, it swells; refuse to pay one gold coin now, and in ten years it will demand ten, twenty…… perhaps a hundred coins. By then, it will be too late."

*What we should be thinking about is this nation's future,* Kirias continued.

"Now is the time to solidify our foundation. Only with a nation can your desire to defeat Alberan be fulfilled. The chairman is not proposing orphanages out of simple pity. Understand that. ……I share the chairman's opinion and cast my vote in favor."

When Reine bowed her head and said *thank you,* he smiled wryly and shook his head.

The next to speak was the bearded giant, Oruros Natrias.

"I'm of the same mind. That said, I understand the concern about military preparedness. How about this — each region sends a few more troops to reinforce the western frontier? At the very least, no one here is fool enough to start infighting right now. The westerners might feel a bit more at ease."

He was not particularly skilled in domestic affairs, but he had charisma and held many nobles together.

He had originally held the largest territory in the region, which certainly helped.

There were some issues here and there, but nothing major.

Elsren's start was off to a smooth beginning.

Leaving the First Assembly Hall, they headed to the office.

The vice chairman — Gorzarius Lukazan — spoke.

"The assembly must be tiring. They've gotten comfortable enough to say whatever they please."

"It is a bit tiring, but…… this is how it should be. Reine wants to hear all sorts of opinions, and if Reine were a king, people probably wouldn't be able to say what they really think."

Gorzarius had wanted a nation where Reine held absolute power, but what Reine wished for was a parliamentary republic.

In Alberan, their mother conceived everything and decided everything.

Dissenting opinions were silenced, and those who voiced them were executed.

Dictatorship was not inherently bad, and Reine understood her mother's reasoning.

It was indisputably their mother's achievement that she had united a fragmenting Alberan and expanded it so greatly — and that was precisely because she had consolidated her power and manipulated the nation freely.

Her mother had made the nation large and increased its population.

If a large and stable nation was a good nation, then her mother was a good queen.

But what Reine wanted to build was a place where everyone could be happy.

*'……What society is, to put it simply…… one might say it is something individuals build by supplementing each other's deficiencies.'*

*'Supplementing each other……'*

*'For instance, just as you see my painting and wish to learn something, I too learn what I lack from Elsreine-sama's paintings. A relationship where we each give and receive — that too is a kind of society.'*

*A small one, though,* Fiiri said.

*'One uses one's strengths for others and receives help with one's weaknesses…… the world is made up of people helping each other like that, each living within their given role. Soldiers, craftsmen, even nobles — they are all merely one role within a greater society. What matters in any era is what one fulfills within that role in the larger whole.'*

*'……That's a bit difficult.'*

When she said so, Fiiri laughed happily.

*'Fufu, but I'm sure Elsreine-sama already understands, in her heart.'*

She said such things and laughed.

Reine recalled her smile and closed her eyes for just a moment.

"Reine wants to hear many opinions, to learn, to understand many ways of thinking. Reine is only good at calculations…… even if Reine tries her hardest alone, what she'll end up with is another Alberan, just like kaa-sama's. ……So Reine wants to exchange ideas with many people and build a nation and society where many people can live happily."

*——The heart that seeks to understand others is the seed of a better society.*

Fiiri's talks were always a little difficult.

But she thought that was probably what it meant.

What people liked and disliked, what they wished for and wanted to do.

It was not about being clever or foolish — the important things lay in those parts.

If the intention was good, then even a foolish proposal was fine.

If wisdom was lacking, Reine could help with that.

By listening to various opinions, understanding everyone, and fulfilling their wishes, a better society would surely follow.

Other people were a bit difficult for Reine.

But they were all human — surely connected somewhere, and she believed that, like Fiiri and herself, they could one day understand each other.

And she wanted her mother to see the place born from that.

"……If such a place is built, kaa-sama too, surely……"

"Please refrain from such words in public. Many bear resentment toward Her Majesty……"

"Ah…… yes. I'm sorry…… I keep letting it slip."

Entering the office, Gorzarius gave a wry smile and shook his head.

"Of course, I understand your feelings…… I hope that at least some of Elsreine-sama's sentiments will reach them. Naturally, the dead cannot return, but……"

"……Yes."

"But I'm certain Fiiri would be proud of Elsreine-sama as you are now. She was likely the one who wished most to heal Her Majesty's illness."

When Reine nodded, Gorzarius continued.

"I have heard that…… after Elsreine-sama marched out, Fiiri was summoned by Her Majesty."

"……Summoned."

"I do not know the details, but…… considering the outcome, it seems reasonable to conclude that Your Highness's deployment was to facilitate her disposal. She may have wanted to remove Elsreine-sama and keep it from reaching your ears."

Reine lowered her eyes, and Gorzarius sighed.

"……The day Reine was told to go put down the rebellion happened to be just…… the day Reine and kaa-sama had quarreled about Fiiri."

"You mentioned that before. Whether that was the final trigger cannot be said for certain, but…… it does seem that Her Majesty took Fiiri's life. I had placed operatives to investigate as best I could, in case something was amiss……"

"……Thank you. The sentiment is appreciated, but…… Reine doesn't think kaa-sama would leave spies undetected. You should pull them out."

"……Yes."

——Her mother had said it was not her doing.

It was true that Reine had lost her composure, and perhaps it really wasn't — that thought flickered across her mind — but hearing Gorzarius's words, who had apparently continued investigating, it seemed that Fiiri's killer was indeed her mother.

"In any case, it is the future that matters."

"……Yes."

"The orphanages are a good idea, given the suffering from heavy taxation. Charity is necessary to win the people's support. The approach of casting Her Majesty as villain and sharpening the opposition may sit somewhat uneasily with Elsreine-sama, but……"

"……No. If it is necessary, it can't be helped."

Grabareine the Tyrant — a wicked despot.

Present their mother as evil, and Reine as the righteous opposition, spreading this widely among the people, making the just cause clear, winning popular support.

Reine felt some resistance to Gorzarius's proposal, but building a nation naturally required broad popular support.

The opposition of good and evil was easy for even the uneducated to understand, and when given the choice between good and evil, most people wanted to be on the side of good.

His reasoning was sound, and setting aside emotional objections, she had no argument against it.

Reine still did not doubt that her mother was a fine queen.

She did not believe her mother was evil.

To Reine, she was a kind mother, wise, and had at least tried to build a good nation by her own definition.

If none of this had happened, if she had come to appreciate Fiiri, then in ten or twenty years they might all have been living happily together.

But Reine had her own thoughts and position, and her mother had hers.

She thought it was only a difference of that much.

*'……Good and evil are very frightening words, Reine-sama.'*

*'Frightening……'*

*'From which perspective does something appear right, and from which does it appear wrong? To label someone evil is to stop thinking, to stop trying to understand them…… To label oneself good is to lose the heart to correct one's own mistakes. ……By deciding things, people can become endlessly foolish.'*

*One must not speak lightly of good and evil,* Fiiri had said.

Put into words, the words bind you, and in time become assumptions.

You search only for reasons the other is evil, and only for reasons you are good.

"……However, Reine does not think that approach is a very good one. Once the nation stabilizes, Reine would like everyone to be able to live their days happily."

"Yes. Naturally, I understand Elsreine-sama's feelings, and I feel the same. As we gather the people's support, we can gradually change that as well."

Reine nodded.

Gorzarius always took her feelings into account.

Having someone trustworthy at her side was a great comfort to Reine.

"Regarding the orphanages, I will give it some thought. I plan to exchange ideas with Lord Liberslebe and Lord Natrias, and find a trustworthy person to steer things. If you have any preferences, I would like to hear them first……"

"As long as they have a minimum level of ability, Reine has no particular preference…… Please find someone trustworthy, as Lukazan says. We'll be entrusting quite a lot of money to them……"

"Indeed, I shall live up to your expectations. Well then."

Gorzarius left the office, and Reine went to her desk.

On it were stacked reports — far fewer than the volume her mother processed.

It was not so much a matter of the nation's size as of its structure.

The regional lords were only nominally listed as parliament members; their lands were, at present, their own property.

Their financial situations were opaque in a way they had not been during the Alberan days.

# Chapter 310: Reine XI

It was two years later that the Republic of Elsren changed its name to the Elsren Holy Empire.

Having personally led an elite force to shatter Alberan's great offensive, and then subduing the southern nomadic tribes that had repeatedly caused havoc, Elsreine's prestige among the people continued to rise.

The devastating losses from Alberan's invasion had been caused by glory-seeking nobles.

Had Elsreine's deployment been approved from the start, such damage would never have occurred.

That opinion was widespread among the populace, and many said that Elsreine should guide the nation not as chairman but as empress.

The same sentiment echoed in parliament — Gorzarius, who had been relatively quiet until now, took the lead in arguing that Elsreine should be granted greater authority, and no one could oppose him directly.

Nobles and the populace had a complicated relationship.

Nobles ruled from above and wielded great power, yet their power rested upon popular support.

If popular support was lost, talented people would flow to other nobles, and in the worst case, the populace would rise up violently and try to oust those in power.

A single commoner was powerless against the nobility, but the people, once united as a collective, became a terrifying force that could sever a noble's head with ease.

And in the current Elsren, the populace was remarkably well-informed.

They knew things that should never have leaked — what was discussed in parliament, which noble had opposed Elsreine's deployment.

To grant power to Elsreine after her overwhelming military achievements — anyone who opposed this openly would likely have their name in the people's ears by the next day. This was already understood as an unspoken rule, and even dissatisfied nobles could only offer passive objections.

The one who raised concerns was Kirias Liberslebe.

He pointed out the problem of not Elsreine herself, but the growing power of her trusted advisor, Gorzarius.

There was some friction over this, but Oruros Natrias also spoke — in his view, Elsreine was still mentally immature as a leader, and it was premature to grant her the kind of absolute power Grabareine wielded. At the same time, she needed people to advise and supervise her.

Gorzarius Lukazan — Elsreine's confidant, skilled in both pen and sword.

Oruros Natrias — the military backbone of the Elsren army.

Kirias Liberslebe — a sharp mind and the parliament's coordinator.

After lengthy discussions, these three were established as the Three Grand Dukes, second in authority only to Elsreine.

And as the administrator of law — a neutral entity that bound even the emperor — Naruko Ribenilas, who had been entrusted with Elsren's public welfare, was chosen.

He had renounced worldly affairs to serve as the symbol of the goodwill Elsreine desired — his orphanages. His position was far removed from power, making him a fitting guardian of the law.

For this reason, he was installed as the Pope, head of the newly established Papal Court.

Hence, the Holy Empire.

Not a kingdom where a monarch wielded absolute, unchecked power.

An emperor bound by sacred and righteous law, governing in accordance with it, cooperating with excellent retainers — the new name reflected that vision.

The people welcomed it joyfully, celebrating the new beginning of Elsren — though some would later suggest that, in truth, it was a nation of the Three Grand Dukes, with a puppet empress at its head.

* * *

——In Reine's atelier, built within the castle.

She finished a painting, gazed at it, and exhaled.

As always, in the paradise Reine painted, everyone wore smiles.

With every brushstroke, she poured in her wishes, and each time that unchanging scene materialized on a blank white canvas, she felt relief.

"Truly, every time I see them, your paintings are enchanting. They're beautiful……"

"……Ehehe."

The woman gazing at the painting beside her had chestnut hair down to her shoulders, and a face that always bore a smile, as if reflecting her nature.

Once an orphan, her name was Fenera.

She was Reine's attendant.

After leaving Alberan, Reine had not kept an attendant.

She had never found it troublesome to brew her own tea, and felt no particular need for one.

It was not that she felt anxious unless she did everything herself, but she had grown accustomed to life without an attendant.

To those around her, however, it apparently seemed unnatural, and after becoming empress, she was told repeatedly that she should keep one, at least for appearances.

But an attendant occupied a special position.

The attendant held no political power per se, but could speak to Reine directly — and choosing one came with considerable complications.

If the attendant was noble, there was always a house behind them; choosing one meant not choosing another.

The noble whose kin was selected would celebrate it as a mark of Reine's trust, but those not chosen would harbor resentment.

Reine was honestly tired of such dynamics.

It was then that Gorzarius suggested she take an orphan as her attendant — and that was how Fenera came to fill the role.

She was a good girl, one Reine had known since childhood.

A bit foolish and prone to blunders, but spending time with her — always working with such cheer — was something Reine had grown fond of.

"……Elsreine-sama truly loves painting, don't you?"

"……?"

"Fufu, you always look so happy when you're painting."

After a brief pause, Reine answered.

"I suppose so. But…… lately, before Reine starts painting…… Reine feels a little afraid."

"Afraid……?"

"Whether Reine can really still paint properly…… whether what Reine wants to convey will actually get through."

That was how it had been, lately.

Everything seemed to backfire.

"Reine wants to make this a good country, and she's trying…… but no matter what she says, there are only problems, and she made the nobles angry again the other day, and nothing works…… Reine keeps worrying that even her paintings might end up like that……"

Five years since this nation had changed from republic to empire.

It was beginning to stabilize, and resolving various domestic issues was now the parliament's main focus.

Reine could not monitor every region's economic situation, but from the information she had, she built hypotheses and projections, and proposed solutions.

While not conclusive, corruption was clearly rampant in various places — financial flows showed suspicious irregularities, and Reine believed the state should conduct fair investigations across the regions. But the nobles pushed back.

Their territories were their own to manage, they said; as long as taxes were paid, the state had no business interfering — to do so was a gross overreach. Moreover, the fact that she had even suggested it was tantamount to an expression of distrust.

This was the empire.

Multiple lords — essentially petty kings — gathered under a single emperor.

Reine sat at the apex, but she merely bound them together; she had no right to directly interfere in their domains.

As long as a certain amount of tax was paid, she could not tell them how to govern, nor could she strip them of their territories.

Their argument was, in a sense, correct.

But Reine had intended it as goodwill.

Currently, taxes were set low due to postwar disruption, but in negotiations to raise them to proper levels, the lords had claimed their 'territories were still too unsettled from the war.' If corruption was the cause, a simple review of their books would let Reine identify and resolve the problem.

If their abilities were lacking, Reine would make up the difference.

That was Reine's thinking — she had no desire to punish or suspect them — but her feelings did not come through.

She was asked whether this was just an excuse to expand imperial power and impose the same dictatorship as her mother — and she could find no words to deny it.

Gorzarius and the others also told Reine she was being too hasty — that society could not run on rationality alone, that necessary evils existed in a disordered nation.

Punish one who commits ten offenses and another who commits twelve will take his place — to avoid that, things must be handled carefully, and Reine had been reckless.

Of late, every time Reine spoke in parliament, the room erupted.

Necessary decisions were delayed by the resulting arguments, and problems rolled in the wrong direction.

When Reine noticed this and fell silent, Gorzarius and the others took over, presenting Reine's ideas in softened form, and proceedings ran smoothly.

Whenever Reine tried to point something out, Gorzarius and the others would gently restrain her, and she would feel their troubled gazes.

The empress's words carried weight, and if they provoked a backlash, the nation could fracture.

She was warned again and again to be cautious, and indeed, every word she spoke angered the nobles.

As it was repeated, she grew afraid to speak, left everything to Gorzarius and the others, and sat in uncomfortable silence.

Lately, she simply sat in her chair and watched their discussions.

Fenera listened, thought, and said:

"I see…… so that's why you've been looking so down lately."

"……Perhaps."

When she answered, Fenera crossed her arms and groaned, brow furrowed.

A mysteriously intense grimacing — watching her, Reine let a small smile escape.

"I'm sorry. Complaining isn't good. Please just forget about it."

"No…… as your attendant, I ought to find some comforting words, but…… I can't speak lightly either, and, well……"

*I'm sorry,* Fenera said, her shoulders slumping.

"Lukazan-sama told Fenera that her job is to look after Elsreine-sama so that she doesn't bear things alone……"

"Lukazan……?"

"Yes. When Elsreine-sama chose Fenera as her attendant…… he said that because Elsreine-sama takes things too seriously, Fenera should make sure you don't bottle things up."

Fenera answered, remembering.

"Fenera has no education and isn't…… well, she's not very smart, so she can't offer clever words about matters of state…… but Fenera does believe that Elsreine-sama's feelings are properly reaching those they should reach."

Then, looking at the finished paradise painting, she narrowed her eyes.

"If I may be so bold…… Fenera, too, understands at least a small piece of Elsreine-sama's heart. I truly wish that this nation may one day become the place Elsreine-sama envisions…… and if I could be even a tiny force toward that end, I would be happy. So please, vent as much as you like…… a hundred times, a thousand times. If it lightens Elsreine-sama's heart even a little, then Fenera is content."

Fenera faced Reine and thumped her own chest firmly.

Reine watched her for a while, then fell silent — and Fenera's eyebrows drooped with worry, her gaze wandering.

"Ah, um…… no, Fenera is well aware that she's not even fit as a partner for venting, but……"

"……That's not true at all. Fufu, hearing you say that…… it made Reine a little happy…… it makes her think she has to keep trying."

Fenera looked relieved, and Reine smiled, then gave her own cheek a light tap.

Fenera blinked in surprise.

"A place without conflict, where everyone smiles and lives happily…… Fiiri was so incredibly smart, and yet it puzzled Reine that she dreamed of something so foolish. But Fiiri knew all that perfectly well, and still……"

Seeing the orphanage children smile made her think so.

Little squabbles resolved into friendships by the next visit, everything so simple.

Perhaps that was how things were supposed to be.

The older people got, the more tangled and complicated everything became — and something went wrong.

"If the orphanage children can grow up to be good people…… if a society where there's no need to fight and everyone can be happy can be built…… then someday, Reine thinks, the world might move closer to that. Of course, building Elsren exactly as the dream would be terribly hard…… but, such a place does exist."

Fiiri's painting of Elsren.

The happiness of a dreamlike paradise.

That painting still would not leave her mind.

"……Fiiri is gone now, but…… Reine wants to build the kind of place Fiiri would have been happy to see, and…… just that. Reine wants to prove that all the things Fiiri taught her meant something. ……That because Fiiri lived, this happy place exists."

Fenera smiled at those words.

"……Yes. Fenera can only cheer you on, but…… I truly believe Elsreine-sama will do it someday. No…… it's not that she'll do it — Elsreine-sama is already building it."

"Building it?"

*Yes,* Fenera said.

"At the very least, Fenera is already a resident of the Elsren Elsreine-sama speaks of…… this place, right here and now, is Fenera's Elsren."

Words she had heard once before.

Reine's eyes widened quietly, and seeing that, Fenera blushed shyly.

"Oh, no — I don't mean anything strange by that…… just that, being at Elsreine-sama's side like this, every day is truly…… happy…… well……"

"……No. Fiiri once said something like that too, so Reine was just surprised."

*——In a sense, this place, right here and now, is my Elsren.*

Fiiri's happy smile, not quite understood by Reine.

The sound of happy laughter that had filled the room.

"Elsreine-sama……?"

"Hm……?"

Fenera hurriedly produced a handkerchief and pressed it to Reine's cheeks and eyes.

Noticing the wetness there, Reine was confused, and tilted her head.

"Um, ah……"

"I-it's fine, please don't worry about it……"

Saying that, Fenera pulled Reine close, pressing her face into her chest.

Still confused, Reine nodded and clung to her.

She could feel her tears slowly dampening the apron dress, and wondered why she was crying, bewildered — yet she gave in.

At the very least, it calmed the turbulence inside her.

"Don't worry — Fenera is right here…… take as long as you need."

Fenera's gentle, worried words.

She just nodded and pressed closer.

* * *

In Alberan, there existed churches dedicated to the twelve gods.

The royal family were descendants of these gods, and so their blood was special.

Alberan's nobles, too, received the divine protection of the royal blood — they were special beings, and therefore the people must serve and devote themselves to them.

Reverence toward the royal house and the nobility was synonymous with faith in the gods — a structure that served as justification for ruling the foolish masses.

A vestige of the age when Alberan's people had been barbarians, her mother said.

Religion was well-suited to controlling foolish groups, and under the guise of divine teaching, it was 'convenient' for instilling whatever ethics suited the ruler — used as a system in most every nation.

In some lands, belief was so strong it became law, but in Alberan, faith in the Twelve was comparatively relaxed — used primarily to bolster the royal house's authority.

It was tolerant of other gods, merely holding that the great Twelve stood above all — accepting other deities, even welcoming them.

When conquering lands with their own faiths, those faiths were not denied.

They were honored as genuine divine beings, and praised for the devotion of their followers — only then was it explained that before the great Twelve, and specifically the supreme god of their domain, even those deities fell short.

However great a god of valor might be, none could prevail before Koreis, the supreme god of valor.

In some lands, faith was equivalent to law.

Shattering everything naturally produced lawless chaos.

But by acknowledging the local faith and merely explaining that the people had simply not known of Koreis, their customs, laws, and order were preserved — and the teachings of the noblest god, Koreis, could then be introduced as convenient.

Her mother spoke of religion as a supremely useful 'tool' and taught Reine how to wield it.

Reine thought neither well nor ill of it.

Tools were tools and means were means — if the result was positive, that sufficed, and Reine regarded it as a kind of legal order.

The Twelve's teachings were not extreme, and she had paid them little mind.

It fell under the Papal Court's jurisdiction, but she had not treated it as important, and it was only some time later that she noticed.

"……I only wished to spread the teachings so that people might know Elsreine-sama's vision — the paradise that awaits. Lukazan-sama and the others gave permission, but……"

"Lukazan and the others said…… this was fine?"

"Yes — that the people need something to cling to……"

——Beside the orphanage, a newly adjoined cathedral.

The Papal Court had been building new churches alongside orphanages everywhere, she was told — most repurposing structures built during her mother's conquests.

They now dispensed 'teachings' to struggling people, distributing bread in charitable work.

In the vast cathedral, on a pedestal, was one of Reine's paradise paintings.

Here, 'teachings for being guided to Elsren' were preached — how to live, what to do — and had apparently been going on for two or three years already.

She had discovered it by chance.

The orphanage Reine visited was always the same one, and almost everyone objected to her going out beyond that.

Security concerns, potential public unrest — she understood to a degree — but she had wanted to see the streets for herself at least once.

Her usual orphanage was on the outskirts, in a safe area.

She had heard that several similar facilities existed in Renaria, and the orphanage inspection was a pretext — the real reason was to see how those areas actually looked.

When she insisted on visiting a different orphanage, she found a large church beside it.

Curious, she stepped inside to find the unfamiliar 'Teachings of Paradise' being preached.

The priest, apparently noticing who had arrived, cried *Elsreine-sama* in surprise, and the people in the pews, equally shocked, knelt and clasped their hands.

Before she could make sense of the confusion, the priest ended the day's sermon and ushered everyone out — and before long, Pope Naruko Ribenilas came rushing in.

He explained that these activities had been going on for two or three years, and everything was news to her.

The bread distribution and the teachings were both new to her, and the 'Teachings of Paradise' themselves were entirely unfamiliar — and yet they were being presented as if Reine herself had created them.

The large church, too, had apparently been donated by merchants who sympathized with 'Reine's teachings,' and Naruko assured her there was nothing underhanded about it.

Indeed, Reine had not given the Papal Court the authority to levy taxes or the like.

Its budget was limited to operating costs for the orphanages and similar institutions; donations were largely left to their discretion, and as long as the orphanages ran properly, they were given free rein.

This was because, in Alberan too, donations to churches had never amounted to much.

But learning that multiple churches of this size had been built on 'donations' left her stunned and uneasy.

It was not corruption, nor were they doing anything wrong.

They were acting within their given authority, doing what they believed was good, and even distributing bread with the donations — it seemed admirable, if anything.

And yet, an indescribable unease gnawed at her.

"But Reine's Elsren isn't…… a place you're led to after death……"

"Of course, I understand. However, there were existing local beliefs of that nature…… and we thought it best to start with something familiar. If the people first learn the teachings of peace, and a paradise comes into being as a result, that is good enough. ……The interpretation and the process differ somewhat, but what is taught here is by no means wrong."

"That's……"

At the very least, what she had heard earlier was true.

Love thy neighbor, help one another, forsake strife, and cultivate a tranquil heart.

Naruko smiled.

"Elsreine-sama's deeds speak for themselves, and those who have beheld these paintings — painted by no human hand, it seems — are moved beyond measure, and believe Elsreine-sama to be a divine being who descended from heaven to this land."

"Divine……"

"Yes. A special being…… one who guides the people to the land of happiness. Elsreine-sama is the hope of the people of this land. And as long as they believe that, they will devote themselves body and soul to Elsreine-sama's cause, and live each day as good, orderly citizens."

*This is a wonderful thing,* Naruko continued.

But Reine shook her head.

"But Reine is not a god or anything——"

"Regardless of the truth, what matters is that they believe. Even now, outside the cathedral, many people pray to Elsreine-sama as a messenger of heaven, one who will guide them to Elsren. That is everything…… if, as a result, Elsren is built in this land, that is good enough, I believe."

*Elsreine-sama is too straightforward,* he said.

"Lukazan-sama and the others have said the same. When you believe something should be done, you try to change everything yourself. But…… I, and Lukazan-sama, and the other nobles — each of us is working, in our own way, to guide this nation in a good direction. ……Can you not trust us?"

"N-no, that's not…… it."

"Elsreine-sama said she wished to build this nation together with us. If so…… I would ask you to trust us a little more, to rely on us. I had intended to tell Elsreine-sama about this place in due time, and present it to you in better form. Surely, I thought, it would please you……"

*That makes it a little disappointing,* Naruko said, lowering his eyes, and Reine bowed her head.

*I'm sorry,* she said, and Naruko hastily shook his head.

"I did not mean it as a reproach…… I understand your feelings. Indeed, from Elsreine-sama's perspective, what we are doing must seem terribly foolish."

"Reine didn't mean…… that."

She could not firmly say *no* and lowered her eyes.

In truth, she had harbored some suspicion.

How much the situation being reported to her differed from reality — she had wanted to see that for herself, and had forcefully changed the carriage's destination.

She no longer understood even this city, Renaria, let alone the rest of the nation.

Under the pretext of distributing responsibility, jurisdictions had been divided finer and finer, and the information reaching Reine directly had grown far too scarce.

She had not even known the Papal Court was building new churches.

Whether the reports reaching her were truly accurate — she had wanted to verify that, and yet she could not say there had been no suspicion behind it.

"……I'm sorry."

"No. But I ask you to understand…… changing people's hearts takes time, Elsreine-sama. If something decided today could be accepted by everyone tomorrow, there would be no conflict in this world."

*Yes,* she answered.

"What matters first is making the people believe that this nation, that Elsreine-sama, will lead things in a good direction. With that, the people will follow the law, and if everyone comes to respect the law, things will eventually move the right way. ……We are doing this with this nation's future in mind…… I simply ask that Elsreine-sama trust us a little more."

*I'm sorry,* she said again.

Lately, that was all she ever said.

Every time she did something, Reine apologized.

Halting the parliament's discussions, picking pointless quarrels with the nobles, and now this.

Nothing but regret kept surfacing.

"Elsreine-sama — no petty villain would pose any danger to you, admittedly…… but unplanned outings like today's make your guards quite anxious. ……Please inform us in advance. Elsreine-sama is the empress — we must ensure nothing goes wrong."

"……Yes."

"Lukazan-sama was worried that Elsreine-sama might be overtired. Judging by today, that does seem to be the case. Perhaps you should take a break from state affairs for a while?"

She lowered her eyes.

She had been hearing that a lot, lately.

Nothing worked, everything backfired, and whenever it did, they said, *Perhaps the weight of responsibility is too much.*

They said responsibility should be shared, that the nation was built by all, and piece by piece they carved away Reine's domain and divided it among themselves — and before she knew it, so much had become invisible.

And when something she didn't know about surfaced, her remarks missed the mark, and she was met with weary sighs.

*——Elsreine-sama seems tired.*

She had never felt the weight of responsibility as heavy.

And yet things that should have been simple to handle were growing ever more complex, beyond her reach.

She wondered what she was even doing, and every time she did, 'Fiiri's Elsren' seemed to recede further, growing frightening — and taking up the brush, too, became frightening.

Every time she painted a paradise, she felt relief — and yet she feared that someday, even that would be beyond her.

"……Elsreine-sama is this nation's symbol and its hope. Many have wept at the beauty of the paradise in Elsreine-sama's paintings."

Naruko gestured toward the 'Elsren' displayed on the pedestal and smiled.

"Even merchants consumed by gold and greed have said their hearts are cleansed, and willingly parted with their own coin to give us churches of this scale…… Without straining yourself, Elsreine-sama has already given this nation something more than enough, something precious beyond measure. That is your role — one we cannot fill. ……Please leave the mundane matters to us."

A smile without the faintest trace of malice, gentle and kind.

"We are all oarsmen on the ship bound for Elsreine-sama's 'Elsren.'"

He gestured toward what was called the heavenly paradise of Elsren.

A conveniently fabricated fantasy paradise, used as a tool to guide the people.

Something different from what Fiiri had wished for.

When the conversation ended, she left the cathedral.

Fenera, who had been waiting by the carriage, watched Reine's face with concern. Reine climbed inside with her, past the bowing, praying people.

"Um, Elsreine-sama……?"

"……I'm sorry, just…… for a moment……"

Once no one could see them, in that small, enclosed space, Reine clung to her and closed her eyes.

Hands circled around her back and slowly stroked it, and the nameless nausea eased a little.

What Reine's paintings had tried to convey was not getting through.

Neither her words, nor her feelings, were getting through.

*'At the very least, Fiiri believes that what Elsreine-sama is trying to convey in that painting has reached me. Because I think so — just as you received the circle I drew and took it to be a circle.'*

Round and round, Fiiri's words repeated in her head.

* * *

——A single room, full of paintings.

A private room within the castle, provided for a servant, still cleaned day after day though its occupant was long gone.

Wearing a white dress that was neither plain nor fine — the beautiful queen, at the border between girl and woman, spent time there alone.

She gazed at a single painting.

Never tiring of it, she gazed — now spending as much time here, day and night, as she did in her own chambers.

When the servant silently refilled her empty teacup, Queen Grabareine spoke.

"……Don't you think it's a foolish painting, just like that child?"

The servant, surprised by the sudden question, stared at the queen.

"She doesn't know anything, doesn't understand anything — an ideal world…… fufu, if you wish to be treated gently, people treat you gently; things done with good intentions bear fruit, neatly and cleanly."

*What a lovely paradise,* the queen laughed, and narrowed her eyes.

The servant merely watched that profile.

"Only…… I never had that. So I…… simply did things your way, to avoid being used — I used others first. I was far cleverer than any of you, and better at it, and so I took the throne — and surely, that was right."

She stood and traced her daughter's painted face with a fingertip, lovingly.

"But this foolish child, painting such a silly picture…… I find myself thinking it's fine, even good, and in the end, perhaps I fell right into Fiiri's hands after all."

Then she traced the Fiiri beside her.

Seeing this, the servant bowed deeply.

"……Forgive me. When Elsreine-sama returned, I suspected…… that Your Majesty had done away with Fiiri-sama. ……Perhaps, if I had spoken…… I might have been able to stop Elsreine-sama."

"Yes, Libinia. But…… I have no intention of blaming you now. Killing you would serve no purpose, and it would only sadden that child."

*If that child were to come home,* the queen continued.

"……Tell me — will that child come back? To me?"

"……I believe so, surely."

"She said she didn't understand me……"

"That was…… a passing confusion. ……Elsreine-sama was in turmoil."

*Is that so,* the queen said, and traced Benuure's face as well.

"Perhaps…… I was a little jealous, a little envious. ……She laughed so happily, so innocently, from the bottom of her heart. In this wretched world, yet to her it was a field of flowers…… so it made me a little angry."

*——Why could I never see the world that way?*

Gazing at the distant paradise, the queen murmured vacantly.
