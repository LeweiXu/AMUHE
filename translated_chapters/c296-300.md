# Chapter 296: A Sigh — Two

*'Hmhm, the more I look at it, the better it turned out. Let's hold a feast here tonight to celebrate.'*

*'Yes. Fufu, I anticipated you'd say that, Ojou-sama, and prepared accordingly.'*

*'……Your perpetual preparedness is quite unnerving.'*

The cabin they had built beside the Spirit Hollow was a fine thing.

Sturdy stakes driven deep, earthen walls and solid pillars.

Roomy enough for seven to sleep comfortably——they had been in high spirits, declaring they would camp there from now on for every gathering.

The inaugural feast was a delight, but——

*'What's wrong, Lila?'*

*'Um…… K-Kushenalars-sama arrived, and the downdraft from the landing…… th-the cabin……'*

The cabin, a year in the making, lasted one week after completion.

A dragon's wingbeat and its handmade furnishings were obliterated.

Selene shut herself away for three days.

*'Hmhm, this time it's much sturdier than before. It should hold up against your unnecessarily disruptive wingbeats——'*

*'Oh? Shall I test it?'*

*'What? Wait! It's still unfini——'*

The even sturdier replacement, a year and a half in the making.

Kushenalars destroyed it again just before completion.

Selene shut herself away for three days.

And then the third, fourth, fifth, and sixth were likewise lost to dragon wingbeats.

The seventh-generation Hollow Cabin abandoned the fixation on wood entirely, built from transmuted stone throughout, and enjoyed a considerable lifespan.

Aesthetically dull but sturdy, it survived for about two hundred years——until seven dragons gathered at once and one of them inadvertently stepped on it.

Selene, who had been extending the structure, shut herself away for three days, as expected.

The eighth generation was built against a cliff face to avoid being stepped on, again in transmuted stone.

She aimed for a proper cottage rather than a cabin——and the work was going well, until the landing tremors caused the cliff to collapse and crush it.

Undaunted, Selene spent thirty years hollowing out a recess in the cliff face, expanding it and constructing a robust internal structure modeled on the Great Temple of Arna.

The entrance still looked like a cabin, but the interior was expanded to the size of a small manor, with ongoing extensions.

A direct hit from landing debris had collapsed the entrance once, but it was repaired, and the structure still stood today.

Not just the cabin.

*'Hmhm, how's this? I think it turned out rather well.'*

*'My. Lovely. Then…… yes, let's put it in the entrance hall.'*

Vases, ornaments, all sorts of things Selene had made——

*'S-Selene-sama……'*

*'What is it, Anne?'*

*'I-I'm so sorry……! I was polishing Selene-sama's vase and it slipped…… a-and the horse figurine next to it too……!'*

——destroyed by Anne.

*'……Ojou-sama, about the Mirsh vacation home.'*

*'Mirsh? Oh…… that one. What about it?'*

*'I went to check on it after a long while, and, well, it seems mana crystals were growing up from underground…… through the floor…… I'm sorry. I've already checked the other vacation homes and removed any growths.'*

Other vacation homes had also succumbed to natural collapse.

Selene had endured the instant disintegration of her painstaking work time and again.

And each time, she had recovered and pressed on.

A little setback was nothing new by now——

"Aaah……"

——she hadn't gotten used to it at all.

A single misplaced blow had sent a large crack running through the nearly-complete statue.

Selene dropped to her hands and knees, a pitiful moan escaping her.

"Still as clumsy as ever, Selene-sama. You can't even chisel a stone that isn't moving?"

"I was chiseling it just fine! You shouted at the worst possible moment and *this* happened!"

"Excuses. Onee-sama and I would never make such a blunder."

"You……!"

Her glare had no effect; Kreschenta examined the crack with disinterest, then turned her eyes to Selene.

"Never mind that, listen to me."

"This isn't 'never mind'! Why should I have to listen to your petty complaints when my statue just cracked? It's about Krische and Bery ignoring you while they load the wagon, isn't it."

"It's not petty!"

"By any measure it is……"

Tomorrow was market day.

The other two were busy loading the carts today.

Anne was probably helping as well.

They had about five large carts to prepare, sorted by sales region, packed with wine barrels, produce, Selene's forged swords and silver accessories——particularly the fine wines, which had to be bottled and labeled. Quite a lot of work.

Kreschenta's version of 'helping' generally consisted of *Help, Krische. Help, Bery. Help, Krische-Bery. Help, Bery-Bery-Krische*——spending more time clinging and being a nuisance than actually helping. Eventually, she stopped getting attention and came sulking to Selene.

A routine occurrence.

That such trivial content had caused three months of work to crumble was… hard to express.

Then again, she couldn't even muster the energy to complain.

Kreschenta was on the level of Gururun——a slightly clever animal.

Getting angry at her every antic would wear Selene down first.

She rubbed the bridge of her nose and sighed.

Attempting the statue again was beyond her just now, so she headed for the workshop.

"Wait! Don't ignore me!"

Kreschenta pattered along behind her.

More pet than person.

History's greatest sovereign, they still called her over there——every time such accounts reached her ears, the gap with reality was staggering.

That this child had ever governed a nation was beyond belief.

She had certainly hidden it well.

Of course, like Krische, Kreschenta's intellect was something even Bery couldn't approach, but she was fatally childish and foolish.

Thoroughly self-centered, interested only in her own desires, with the sensibilities of a five-year-old.

Slightly more cunning than Krische, she had kept up a better front——but with no need for pretense anymore, only the sensibilities of a silly five-year-old remained.

Over there, she had once seemed slightly more adult than Krische; here, the positions had reversed.

Inside the workshop, Selene settled onto a half-finished sofa, and Kreschenta climbed onto her lap, sitting sideways.

Another sigh, and she stroked the girl's head.

Kreschenta relaxed her cheeks in satisfaction, as if to say *That's more like it.*

Selene slumped onto the sofa, and Kreschenta draped herself over her.

A kiss on both cheeks, held gently——

"……Sigh. Fine, I'll keep you company for a nap, if that's what you want, Your Grumpy Highness."

"Don't patronize me. But if *Selene-sama* wants a nap companion, I suppose I could oblige."

Kreschenta answered happily, pressing her face into Selene's chest.

The sight was endearing, and in the dim workshop, her beautiful golden hair shimmered faintly red. Selene kissed the top of her head.

"Yes, yes. Kreschenta-sama, would you grace me with your company for a nap?"

"Hmhm, if I must."

Contentedly, she answered and closed her eyes. Selene pillowed her head on her left hand.

With the other, she pulled over a blanket draped on the sofa's back, covered them both, and sighed.

How many sighs had she exhaled since coming here, she wondered, as she patted Kreschenta's back.

The breathing shifted to sleep-breath in an instant——it was nap time, apparently.

Kreschenta slept prodigiously.

Earliest to bed, latest to rise, and when the sun began its afternoon descent, it was naptime.

She had probably come here because Krische had turned her down for a mid-day cuddle-nap.

Aside from her morning second-sleep, Kreschenta always wanted someone beside her when she slept.

Selene poked at a plump cheek, and Kreschenta furrowed her brow. Selene smiled wryly.

How different from the dangerous aura of their first meeting.

Selene had never asked much about her life before they met.

It probably hadn't been a happy subject.

Apart from stories about a servant named Nora, Kreschenta never mentioned those days, and perhaps this was the recoil from that childhood.

The older she got, the younger she became, and now she enjoyed an eternal childhood.

Every day free, without the faintest shadow of restraint——Kreschenta was surely the one enjoying eternity most of all.

Selene stroked her and closed her own eyes.

*Please let the shock be gone from my head by the time I wake up.*

* * *

*Inside a room——on her mother's lap.*

*Her mother had appeared in the middle of a lesson and declared, "Study time is over, Selene," scooping her out of the chair and carrying her to the sofa.*

*As always, Bery offered a reprimand, and Mother ignored it, banging the table and demanding cookies and tea.*

*And always, like a set phrase, she told Selene:*

*"Selene, don't become the sort of person who overthinks everything, like Bery. You'll end up only able to nag."*

*"……Nee-sama."*

*Bery sighed with exasperation but prepared tea and cookies for Mother all the same.*

*Had Bery been sighing every day back then?*

*And yet, despite all Mother's willfulness, Bery somehow looked happy——that always puzzled Selene.*

*On days Mother didn't come, the cookies were for two.*

*On days she did, always for three.*

*Bery was always perfect. She never got it wrong.*

*Irresponsible and careless as Mother was, Bery was her polar opposite——flawless in everything. Yet Mother treated Bery as a cautionary tale.*

*It was obviously Bery who was admirable, but Mother always spoke with inexplicable confidence.*

*"What matters in life is whether you're having fun or not. Sometimes it's important to throw your studies out the window and do whatever you please. Selene takes after me and she's smart. Even if you only do a so-so amount of studying, you'll manage just fine as a grown-up, like your mother."*

*"So-so……"*

*"Sigh. Nee-sama. Ojou-sama was just getting motivated, and you come along to undermine it……"*

*"Your job may be to turn Selene into a proper Christand lady, but my job is to spoil her and love her. The two balance out nicely."*

*That's a bit unfair, don't you think, Bery sighed again.*

*She sat beside them, shoulders drooping.*

*"Selene is like Bogan——oddly serious in the wrong places. I'm worried she'll decide she wants to be like you. You try too hard to be perfect because you think you should be her role model. If she thinks you're normal, that's a tragedy."*

*"I think it would be equally tragic if she thought nee-sama's sloppiness were normal……"*

*"How rude. What a way to talk to your elder sister."*

*"……Nee-sama has never shown me anything resembling courtesy, as I recall."*

*"Of course not, Bery. Sisters receive one-directional courtesy from their younger sisters. The reverse doesn't exist."*

*Mother's arguments were always tyrannical.*

*In a debate, Bery usually had the logical high ground, yet she could never win against Mother, who ignored reason and stated outrageous self-serving nonsense with complete conviction.*

*"A question for Selene. A person with lots of knowledge and a person with none——which is more admirable?"*

*"Um…… the one with more knowledge……"*

*"Then, someone who can calculate quickly and someone who can't?"*

*"……? The quick one……"*

*"Fundamentally wrong. Both incorrect. Whether someone is good or bad with a sword, quick or slow with numbers——none of that has anything to do with being admirable."*

*Bery pressed a hand to her forehead and sighed again.*

*Mother continued.*

*"If being capable were the same as being admirable, Bery would be queen by now. These things are trivial and unimportant——nice to have, sure, but you don't need them to be admirable. Like your wonderful mother."*

*"Y-yes……"*

*"If you lack knowledge, ask someone who has it. If you're slow at arithmetic, leave it to someone who's good at it. You don't have to be perfect at everything yourself——just rely on all sorts of people."*

*Mother said this while squeezing Selene's waist and nuzzling her hair.*

*"In return, when those people are in trouble, you help them. If you could be friends with everyone in the world, then no matter how enormous a problem stood before you, you could solve it. The wisest person in the world, the strongest, the wealthiest——they'd all be on your side. Any problem would be easy, right?"*

*"I…… guess……"*

*"If there were a single most admirable person in the world, it would be someone like that. Your father, whom you respect so much——he found all sorts of reliable people, and that's how he manages his tremendous work. It's not because he's smart or good with a sword or anything like that."*

*Understand? Mother asked, and Selene nodded, only half understanding.*

*Mother laughed and hugged Selene like a stuffed animal, looking perfectly happy.*

*"So first, you need to start by being more affectionate and close with your mother and Bery. Fussy things like studying can wait. They're fine in moderation."*

*"Moderation……"*

*"All that tiresome stuff——just ask Bery and she'll sort it out. Even I do that. Bery will happily do anything I ask. She'll happily do anything you ask too."*

*"……Nee-sama, if I may."*

*"Don't interrupt me when I'm giving Selene a wonderful motherly lecture."*

*Sigh, went Bery, and Mother commanded, as a matter of course, "All you need to say is 'Yes, quite right.'"*

*"Well, being hardworking is one of your good points, Selene. But the world is big. No matter how hard you try, there'll always be someone you simply can't beat. Problems you can't solve by effort alone will someday appear in your path. ……The important thing is to find as many people as you can rely on."*

*"……Mm."*

*"You're lucky in that regard. You have a truly wonderful mother like me, and Bery, who can solve anything in a snap, and your father is the kingdom's finest general. From the moment you were born, you've been surrounded by more people you can rely on than most."*

*End of Mother's lecture, she declared, and ate a cookie.*

*That was the lesson Mother drilled into Selene, over and over.*

*She had thought she understood, but she hadn't.*

*It was decades later before Selene truly grasped her mother's words.*

*Mother had been a very intelligent person.*

*She lacked Bery's vast knowledge, her effortless competence, her calculating mind——she was lazy, careless, rough around the edges.*

*But she was always natural, and she could see so many things.*

*Had Selene truly understood her mother's teaching, she would never have been tossed around by emotions, and most of her worries would have dissolved in an instant.*

*Instead, Selene chased Bery's back, which she could never catch, chased Krische's back, chased Kreschenta's back——just ran and ran.*

*All she had to do was take the hand held out to her, but she was stubborn and pushed herself too hard.*

*Mother would surely have scolded her: 'Silly girl.'*

*Those children aren't asking that of you, she would have said.*

*And indeed, Mother was right.*

*Entrust herself to the wisest person in the world, the strongest person in the world, the wealthiest person in the world, and every problem Selene faced dissolved with absurd ease.*

*Selene became the greatest marshal in the world, war vanished from the continent for a time, and now she lived happily with the people she loved.*

*What she had agonized over seemed laughable in its simplicity.*

*She didn't feel she had surpassed her father, or her mother.*

*She hadn't become as wise as Bery, hadn't become like her.*

*But those things were trivial——utterly unimportant.*

*Selene could only be Selene. Whether she liked or loathed herself, she could be nothing more or less.*

*That was obvious, and surely the same was true of Bery.*

*She must have revered Mother from the bottom of her heart while knowing she could never be her——and that was precisely why she had loved her the way she did.*

*Krische, Kreschenta, everyone else——all the same.*

*Nobody possessed everything, and that was precisely why they admired one another and compensated for one another.*

*What matters in life is whether you're having fun or not.*

*Mother's words were extreme and unreasonable, yet they too must have been the answer she found after staring hard at what she herself lacked.*

*You couldn't arrive at such words without graduating from envy.*

*"I'm telling you too, Bery, not just Selene. Throw your work out the window once in a while and spend a whole day being lazy."*

*"……If I actually did that, the cookies you're enjoying right now wouldn't appear, and neither would the tea."*

*"Don't be silly. You think serving your sister is work? That's your duty as a younger sister."*

*"……Sigh."*

She pictured Bery sighing endlessly at her selfish, willful sister.

*Poor thing,* she had thought at the time——yet Bery had somehow looked happy, and that had in truth been a happy thing.

A single sigh exhaled for the person you love's selfishness.

Exasperated and weary, yet that daily life of trivial annoyances was dear.

Now, Selene knew.

# Chapter 297: A Sigh — Three

*'Fufu, my win. Another round?'*

*'……N-no. ……Thank you for the match.'*

*'……?'*

Childhood sparring.

She had become able to spar with adults, then able to beat raw recruits, then soldiers who were anything but raw.

Most looked surprised, or smiled and praised her——*As expected, Selene-sama is a genius.*

But sometimes, the reaction was different.

A face like the world was ending, brimming with frustration fit to burst into tears.

Seeing them laugh after losing, she wondered: *Why aren't they upset?*

Or seeing them devastated over a single touch: *Why so upset over such a little thing?*

The young Selene had not understood their feelings.

She had been blessed with so much.

Beautiful, with lovely, shining golden hair.

At a banquet, all eyes were on her; she had a good eye for dresses.

Fine-boned, she looked good in anything.

Appearance was subjective——tastes differed——but no one would call Selene plain.

Her mind was sharper than most.

Among her peers, she was a head above; she could put in the work.

She could follow the adults' political discussions; everyone called her a gifted girl.

Much of it was flattery, surely, but objectively she was near the top.

Talented without being a genius, she was excellent, and her tutors had ensured she was well-informed.

She was blessed with the sword as well; since childhood, she had been able to hold her own against adult soldiers.

The more she swung, the faster she improved——effort translated directly into results.

And her father was the famed Northern General, Margrave. A great noble house.

Assessed as a whole, Selene Christand was anything but ordinary.

She was on the envied side of things, blessed in every objective measure, and surely there were those who wished they could be her.

Yet Selene, too, had been someone who could not understand a loser's feelings.

Everyone carried their own pride, and pride was a tender spot that hurt when wounded.

Those who lost to Selene and called her a genius were protecting their own pride by defining her that way.

*A general's daughter with extraordinary talent——it's only natural I'd lose.*

Thinking that spared them the pain.

Being beaten by a child half their height was never pleasant, and the natural reaction was frustration——all the more so for those who had trained and carried pride in their effort.

They had their own efforts, their own pride, their own aspirations, and the young Selene had treated them as nothing more than stepping stones, unable to grasp something so simple.

Curiously, once she did come to know the loser's feelings, she had forgotten the winner's.

Bery was the closest 'winner' in her life.

She had never seen Bery practice swordplay, yet couldn't touch her in a bout.

At least in childhood——had Krische never appeared and those sessions continued, Bery might have stayed above Selene with nothing more than her 'casual practice.'

Her intelligence was beyond ordinary comprehension, her every sense blessed.

As Mother had said, she was a genius, forever beyond Selene's reach.

Selene had always envied the woman for whom everything came effortlessly.

Being outmatched by warriors like her father and his comrades, who had trained all their lives, was a different matter entirely.

A servant who did housework every day and never touched a sword.

And yet, no amount of training could close the gap——it was unbearably frustrating, and she constantly wondered why *she* hadn't been given that kind of talent.

But it was the same thing——surely, the soldiers Selene had beaten as a child felt the same.

Just as Selene had earned what she earned through her own effort, they too had earned something through theirs.

Just as Selene thought Bery was unfair, they must have thought Selene was unfair.

If Selene reached out, most things came within her grasp.

But for some, they never would——and for others, they arrived without reaching at all.

And people could only find value in what they obtained through the struggle of effort.

Selene's talent, worthless in her own eyes because she couldn't beat Bery, would have been desperately desired by the soldiers she had defeated.

Bery, too, probably saw her own gifts as worthless——but to Selene, they were everything she could have wanted.

Wanting what one couldn't have was human nature. People yearned fiercely for the unreachable.

And the girl whose talent dwarfed even Bery's had likewise admired Bery's casual waste of her gifts, devoting her own talent to something as vague as 'making someone happy,' suffering because she couldn't become that.

Selene had her suffering, Bery had hers, Krische had hers, Kreschenta had hers.

Everyone else was the same——her father, her mother, all of them must have had their own.

Blessed didn't mean happy.

Even if someone living hand to mouth obtained a royal life of luxury, that would not end all suffering.

Without knowing enough was enough, you would spend eternity wanting.

What mattered was accepting yourself.

Stopping the envy.

Cherishing the small happinesses.

Royalty or commoner, craftsman or hunter, even one living day to day——if you could accept yourself and rejoice in small blessings, that alone was happiness.

All the more if you found someone to share it with.

If days filled with trivial sighs were satisfying, nothing more was needed.

* * *

A market stall——seated on the long bench of their open-air shop.

"Ehehe, this is delicious."

"……All you ever think about is food."

They had set up a stall selling sundries and wine, and Krische was gnawing on a skewer from a nearby stand, carefree as always.

They had come to Linareseria, a mountain town on the far western edge.

The World Tree that had grown there had made it notable; the town had been settled about a thousand years ago.

A mana crystal vein existed there, and mana was naturally abundant——a small Academy of Magic had been established, which meant that despite the rugged terrain, foot traffic was not negligible.

The World Tree to the south, steep mountains on all four sides.

A single road threaded through the gap between peaks, like the Dragon's Maw.

The land saw little sun and should have been unfit for habitation, yet the World Tree's leaves scattered the sunlight and illuminated the shade beneath.

Curiously, the World Tree's branches and leaves had been indistinguishable from ordinary trees when they first took root, but at some point they had begun to glow in the sunlight.

*It changed because the people living near it wished it so*——or so Krische and the others said, and each World Tree had its own character.

Albernaria's World Tree scattered rainbow-colored petals once every few decades.

The one in Albyagel bloomed with sky-blue flowers.

Here in Dogaruaz, once home to the famed dragon Kushenalars, a single great indigo flower and vermillion fruit adorned the high branches.

They had once plucked and tasted one; it was exceptional.

A fruit brimming with mana as dense as dragon's blood——a literal legendary fruit of immortality.

In some places, literal 'flying lizards' soared overhead; in others, the sprites frequently seen over there danced about. Rich in variety.

Local legends and faiths.

These blended and merged——the more remote and unvisited the World Tree, the deeper its mystique grew; conversely, those in populated areas tended toward the spectacular.

Krische and Kreschenta hadn't intentionally given them personalities, apparently; it had happened on its own, and every time they saw a new one, the usual *Ohh,* slack-jawed.

After so long in the other world, where legends lay at every turn, nothing felt particularly sacred anymore——yet encountering such things here felt oddly fresh.

Human beings were remarkably self-serving creatures, it seemed.

Bery and Kreschenta were at the far eastern edge.

Anne and the others were in Naut Arna.

Sometimes they visited the same town; other times, like now, they split up to sell across different regions.

Mostly on a whim——Kreschenta was probably complaining while snuggling up to Bery right about now.

And Elvena was probably trying to torment Lila while being thwarted by Anne.

Just imagining it was amusing; Selene let her shoulders shake softly.

"……?"

"Nothing. Just a silly thought."

Krische looked puzzled, set down the finished skewer, and gave a small yawn.

Selene smiled wryly, patted her own knee, and Krische happily lay down on the bench, resting her head there.

*How unbecoming in public,* she might once have thought——but traveling had shown her many cultures.

Even a bench at a stall varied. In arid regions, a rug was simply spread on the ground; the proprietor sat directly on it, goods arrayed casually.

In humid climates, a proper table and chair were standard.

This region was no exception——long benches in particular were favored.

Local stall-keepers draped blankets and cushions on their benches.

Lounging languidly while greeting customers was normal here——the convention being that good wares sell themselves, so a laid-back proprietor signals confidence.

A newcomer hawking loudly was considered unseemly——a sign of insecurity in their own goods.

Even when a customer picked up an item, speaking up immediately was rude and pushy.

One waited, or spoke only after the customer opened their mouth.

A relaxed land with good public order——that explained it, probably.

Visiting so many places had taught her how many cultures and norms existed.

Her own common sense had been the common sense of a very small world.

Men should be this, women should be that, nobles ought to behave so, merchants thus, craftsmen thus——etiquette and propriety were *such and such.*

The Alberan norms she had grown up with still formed her core——she was taken aback sometimes, but encountering diverse worlds was enjoyable.

The look on Bery's face when they visited the Kreisharana-like land where dressing up was shameful and minimal fabric was the highest virtue had been priceless.

Two hundred years ago? Three hundred?

Dragging a fleeing Bery back and forming a rare united front with Elvena was a fond memory.

She stroked Krische's head as the girl was already falling asleep, and watched her peaceful face.

This child had conveniently extracted the part about stall-keepers being praised for laziness and was living as freely as she pleased.

Sleep or eat, one or the other——leaving all work to Selene.

She apparently enjoyed running the stall, but at her core, she was eat-sleep-play.

Left to her own devices, she did nothing but what she liked.

"May I take a look at your wares?"

"Of course, please."

Scholars from the Academy of Magic, perhaps.

A black-haired young man in a fine cloak and robe picked up a mana crystal inscribed with formulae.

A simple one——channel mana and it generated heat.

Elvena's work. The perfectionist Bery never put anything she made up for sale, aside from foodstuffs.

The man studied the inscription intently, and his companion took up another crystal.

Selene would have preferred them to look at her forged swords, but plain swords were selling less and less with each passing year.

Not for lack of quality——the stock was exclusively blades that passed Krische's *pretty good sword* threshold——but the current trend favored inscription swords with mana-alloy blades.

Swords with extending mana blades and other gimmicks were popular, and the higher the budget, the stronger the trend.

Selene was still studying inscription work——naturally, she couldn't dash off inscriptions at Bery or Elvena's level. She was learning from the ground up.

Their help could produce masterpieces, of course, but that felt vaguely frustrating; for now, she studied from texts at night.

"……Every one of these is superb. The person who inscribed them——?"

"I obtained them from a traveling mage on my journey. I'm afraid I don't know the name."

"A wandering adept…… a pity. Lodz."

"Yes. We'd like to purchase as many as we can afford."

The companion produced a pouch: ten small gold coins.

She was briefly startled, then shook her head. *That's far too much.*

There were only seven matching crystals on display.

Ten silver coins would have been overpaying.

"Er…… these are heat-manipulation crystals, you know."

"Haha, you don't understand their value, I see. These are not inscribed with the mainstream four-element dual-origin method, but with an extremely precise primordial inscription. That alone gives them significant scholarly value."

"I-I see……"

"Still, if you say so, I appreciate it. All seven, if that's alright?"

"……Yes, certainly."

The companion wrapped each crystal in cloth as carefully as if handling gemstones, placing them into his bag.

The men smiled as if they had found a bargain, and Selene felt as though she were committing fraud.

*'Oh, Elvena made something to sell. That's unusual.'*

*'Yes, just some practice pieces I inscribed casually…… they should work well enough as practical items, at least.'*

*'Mm…… well, I'll put them out for what they're worth.'*

Mismatched valuations happened frequently between their world and this one.

She had intended to sell them for a silver coin or perhaps less, and instead received small gold coins.

Had she planned to sell at that price from the start, it might have been different——but it felt like outright swindling.

Elvena couldn't have put much effort in either, and mana crystals were literally littering the ground over there.

Her conscience stung.

"……A fine purchase. I am Igra Vindesurl, headmaster of the Academy here in Linareseria. Should you ever encounter that mage again, please urge them to visit our academy."

"Y-yes……"

"Then, if you'll excuse us. Come, Lodz."

"Yes, sir."

The man swept off in his cloak, and Selene sighed softly.

Elvena was deft and intelligent.

Casually or not, after more than a thousand years of magical research, her skill was in a different league from any ordinary mage.

Selene had understood this, or thought she had——but she had underestimated a simple heat crystal, and that was the mistake.

Her eyes had been spoiled by Krische and the others; she had lost sight of ordinary standards of value.

"……I'll have to warn Elvena, too."

Honestly, the commerce was more an excuse for sightseeing.

Earning money wasn't the point; it was all just part of the fun.

*It's nice when something you've made sells*——that was all.

In that sense, mana-crystal products that carried too much weight in this world were probably unsuitable.

Long ago, in a fit of pique, Kreschenta had casually forged a sword and declared *I'll show you what a masterpiece looks like*——and that sword was still displayed in a museum as an inexplicable miracle.

They had been careful since then, but this sort of thing still happened from time to time.

At some point, even Elvena had become, in her spare time, a mage who could astonish great sorcerers.

Over there, one lost perspective; coming here threw the gap into sharp relief.

Even Selene was now, if she walked this world, the greatest swordswoman alive.

Remarkable, when she thought about it. Many would be amazed by her skill.

She felt the fruits of years and effort unknowingly accumulated——yet, *so what,* she also thought.

Elvena surely felt the same.

She certainly didn't see herself as an accomplished mage.

The great power she had once yearned for——having obtained it, it was *just that.*

What a trivial thing she had fixated on.

Ability was truly nothing more than an accessory to a person.

Her father and his comrades had not merely been strong.

Gallen hadn't even been able to wield mana, yet he was genuinely revered by both her father and Krische.

Dagra, too——a competent unit leader and nothing more.

Compared to superhumans like Kokys and the rest, he was outclassed.

But he was faithful to his duty, fought according to his convictions, became Krische's most trusted subordinate——her right hand——and took pride in that service.

People were never born equal.

Birth, talent, appearance, intellect——everything was different.

To know that world and know yourself, and still not let it break you, to give everything you had——their way of living still burned in Selene's heart.

The most beautiful, radiant sight.

A mere instant within the span of fifteen hundred years.

But those who lived in that instant, that single shining moment, could never be surpassed——not in all of eternity.

The 'great people' Selene knew were people like that, and it had nothing to do with ability.

No matter how much Selene trained, they remained in a place she could never reach.

She didn't find that frustrating.

Because she had shared time with such people——because the young Selene had once burned with admiration for them——she was here, as herself.

Being able to say sincerely, *I can never match them*——that was the most precious thing Selene had gained.

"……What a dopey sleeping face."

She pinched her sleeping sister's cheek.

Watched the brow furrow, smiled wryly, and stroked the hair.

Back then, she had always been consumed by inferiority.

She knew she shouldn't look at others that way, yet judged them by their abilities all the same.

The love was for the person, yet she envied only the parts that outshone her, seethed with jealousy, and mocked herself as unworthy.

——The eternal birdcage had been an endlessly gentle cage for Selene, too.

Everyone lived as they wished, desired as they wished, spent their days as they pleased.

Ability was worthless, meaningless; no one was attracted to it, no one clung to it; everyone simply lived as they were.

The unparalleled hero, the legendary sovereign——both just children absorbed in being spoiled.

Their servants, too, were carefree; even the dragons were fools engrossed in games.

The petty notion that one person was better than another had been left far behind, outside the cage.

Eternity was stagnation.

There would never be another chance to become more than she was, and she would remain forever short of the dead.

No matter how much she honed her abilities, Selene was Selene.

No destination to aim for, no direction to head in.

No destination she had to aim for, no direction she had to head in.

That this was happiness was something her younger self could never have believed.

Whether something was happy or not depended entirely on how you chose to feel.

"……Fufu."

She stroked the sleeping Krische's hair, narrowed her eyes.

Then looked up, sensing a presence.

"Is that the Dew of Paradise…… I never expected to find it in a place like this."

"Yes, we happened to come across it."

It was a refined elderly couple.

Not Academy people this time——local gentry of some kind, perhaps.

The old man's gaze fell on the wine Selene had set in the box beside her.

Bery's wine had a certain reputation——and apparently fetched a respectable price on the market.

Careful not to wake Krische, she fashioned a pillow from the blanket, took a bottle from the box, and handed it over.

"……No mistake. The same as the one I had long ago."

The old man examined the cap and nodded.

The cap was a mana crystal shaped like a birdcage——a design used for about a thousand years now.

Not entirely impossible to counterfeit, but they distributed enough that it wasn't worth the effort.

"My father let me taste it once."

"Is that so? We did a tasting ourselves, and it's an excellent vintage."

"I see. That's…… tempting."

The old man studied the cap with a quiet smile.

The old woman laughed gently and turned to Selene.

"How fortunate. ……Next week, our son is coming home after a very long time away."

"Oh my. ……How wonderful."

"Yes. Dear, a little splurge now and then is fine, isn't it? Borok will be delighted."

"……That wasn't why I stopped by."

"So stubborn. ……Miss, may we have a bottle?"

Selene nodded, accepted payment, and bowed.

Then she placed Krische's head back on her knee.

Krische opened her eyes blearily but showed no intention of waking, closing them again. Selene sighed, exasperated, and pulled at her cheek once more.

Krische groaned; Selene softened; and the old woman, watching, smiled. Selene noticed and blushed.

"What a lovely little sister."

"Y-yes…… well……"

"Ufufu. Do keep getting along."

The old woman smiled, looked to her husband.

He nodded, stone-faced, took his wife's hand, and they walked away.

The calm composure of the pair——Selene herself had once been far older than either of them, but that was ancient history.

Her appearance and, at some point, her mind too, had become youthful.

Decades of adulthood, overwritten by over a thousand years of childhood.

Whether that was a good thing or a bad thing——

In any case, nothing to be proud of.

Selene sighed again, and——

"Uuu……"

*This is your fault,* she thought, and pulled her sister's cheek.
