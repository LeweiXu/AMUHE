# Chapter 301: Reine II

*――She did the obvious things as a matter of course, and was found unsettling for it.*

*The world was full of people who could not manage even the obvious, and she realized at once that she was the aberration.*

*No one could understand her. They kept their distance.*

*"Otou-sama, I have been considering the matter of trade with Nilse, and as I thought, the current arrangement will not benefit us in the long term. Going forward, the iron ore――"*

*"Shut up! Since when do you presume to advise the head of this house!? Instead of that, learn to close that grating mouth of yours so the other nobles might take a liking to you! And let me make this clear, Reine — you are not to speak a word of such things outside. It is I who will be humiliated by the prattling of some know-it-all little girl."*

*"……Yes. I am sorry, Otou-sama."*

*In her youth, she had been foolish enough to try to bridge that chasm, but it was futile.*

*Her father was a man born to be king, whose only merit was his pride.*

*No intelligence, no wisdom, no ability.*

*He clung solely to the maintenance of his throne.*

*Alberan at the time was a kingdom in decline.*

*The prestige built by the founding king Bazarishe had lost its luster, and the balance of power between the royal house and its lords had crumbled.*

*The feudal system Bazarishe had established was not a bad one — as a measure to incentivize the territorial expansion that had been the urgent priority of the time, rewarding retainers with land was a sound idea — but in terms of stability, the eventual outcome must have been foreseeable even then.*

*Improvements as meager as water on a burning stone appeared from time to time, but they too had reached their limit.*

*Twenty years at best, fewer than ten at worst — Alberan would fracture.*

*In such circumstances, where a strong king was desperately needed, her father was hopelessly incompetent.*

*And that incompetence threatened her own life as well.*

*She had begun to faintly consider it at six.*

*She resolved herself at eight.*

*For his own protection, her father wished for Reine to take a husband from among the powerful nobles.*

*Diminished though it was, the royal house was still the royal house.*

*For great nobles whose only asset was strength, the title of royalty remained significant, and gaining it would give them dominion over Alberan in both name and substance.*

*If the heir to the throne was not a prince but a princess, a husband could marry into the family, and for one born of a ducal house already mixed with royal blood, it would not be difficult to bypass the order of succession and become king.*

*A royal house without princes was an exceedingly attractive prospect to them.*

*Her father had been overjoyed that a princess, not a prince, had been born.*

*A prince would be the primary heir to the throne, and custom would compel her father to name him successor — which, of course, would not sit well with the other nobles.*

*Many would find it more expedient to simply crush the hollow royal house and establish a new dynasty.*

*In that case, there would naturally be no reason to keep her father alive — but if a powerful noble were to marry into the royal house as the princess's husband, that changed matters entirely.*

*Inevitably, her father's authority as King of Alberan would be protected by that noble.*

*And so, her name was something that shone — Reine, meaning "radiance," was her father's light.*

*He could have chosen not to produce an heir at all and simply named a successor, but he likely wished to avoid civil war.*

*The current first in the line of succession was the king's brother — but in terms of actual power, he ranked only fifth.*

*As for those second in line and below, the most prominent noble house, the Gasrel duchy, stood seventh.*

*Finding a justification to name them would be difficult.*

*What her father most desired was for the eldest son of House Gasrel to enter the royal family as Reine's husband.*

*Appearances were what mattered.*

*In substance the two arrangements were identical, but there was a vast difference between Reine and the heir falling for each other and forming a marriage, and directly naming him successor.*

*Nobles valued face.*

*Objecting to an appointment that ignored the order of succession was only natural, but if the princess happened to fall in love with a noble and marry him, then at the very least, the union had to be blessed in form.*

*However dissatisfied one might be, raising a banner of revolt would lack the necessary justification.*

*Her father's conclusion was, at the very least, not greatly mistaken.*

*Reine then decided to learn the art of mimicry, in order to ingratiate herself with House Gasrel.*

*Many men disliked clever women.*

*It was better, in fact, to appear moderately foolish; she ceased voicing her thoughts and observed the women considered attractive.*

*Fortunately, her looks were exceptional — the rest was a matter of manners and bearing.*

*Lovely and pure, gentle of heart — she remade her entire way of living so that she would appear so.*

*Her father, her mother, the servants must have found her unsettling at first, but she told them she had reformed her nature, treated them accordingly, pleased them in appropriate measure, and before long they accepted this Reine.*

*It was an utterly simple thing.*

*Laugh at the right moment, look sad at the right moment, show anger at the right moment.*

*Even when nothing was funny or sad or infuriating, simply performing these things made those around her mistake her for 'one of their own.'*

*What people cannot understand, they fear. They direct hostility and revulsion at it.*

*Perform within a range they could comprehend, in a manner easy for them to understand, and most problems were solved.*

*She began to act in earnest at thirteen.*

*The reason: she had come to feel that she appeared, to those around her, sufficiently attractive as a woman.*

*"It has been too long, Duke Gasrel."*

*"Indeed it has, Your Highness."*

*"I have heard of Albel-sama's recent exploits as well. Quite the achievement, they say."*

*"Ha. A stroke of fortune……I am still no match for my father."*

*"How admirable. You take after your otou-sama, I see. ……Duke Gasrel, I had heard you took an arrow wound to the arm, and I was worried. How is your health?"*

*As she spoke, she reached out naturally to touch Dreil's arm, and she felt him react with a slight flinch.*

*Albel was still young, just returned from his first campaign. The earnest, good-natured young man one might expect — nothing noteworthy about him.*

*What mattered was his father, Dreil Gasrel.*

*Known for his martial prowess, a mighty warrior — but also a womanizer.*

*She knew he had been showing interest in Reine every time they met.*

*"Ha ha, a scratch or two from an arrow barely counts as a wound. The battlefield has forged a hardy body — I had forgotten I was even struck until Your Highness mentioned it."*

*"My. But please do take care of yourself. A body such as Duke Gasrel's cannot be bought with gold coins, after all……oh, forgive me."*

*As if embarrassed, her cheeks colored the faintest pink, and she withdrew her hand.*

*Not flattery — she had touched him purely out of sincere concern for his well-being.*

*That was how it was meant to appear — a lovely, innocent maiden, and most men delighted in such a thing.*

*She could feel Albel gazing at her, enchanted, and she saw the briefest flicker of lechery cross Dreil's face.*

*"Not at all, no forgiveness needed. Though, if Your Highness would comfort me so, I might seek an arrow wound at every battle."*

*"Fufu, you are too kind. ……Actually, Otou-sama told me — since Duke Gasrel has returned from a harsh campaign, I should take special care to let him rest — and so all I could think of were your injuries. Otou-sama so rarely says such things to me that I wondered if perhaps that arrow wound was a truly terrible one……"*

*"Oh? His Majesty said that?"*

*Dreil looked toward her father.*

*Some distance away, her father had been speaking with another noble; noticing the gaze, he raised a hand in acknowledgment and smiled.*

*Her father had said no such thing, but stating it so would lead Dreil to draw his own conclusions about her father's intentions.*

*At the very least, it was not a bad thing for her father.*

*"I see. I would like to pay my respects again after the banquet."*

*Reine smiled as though she did not understand.*

*What Dreil would infer as her father's intention was, in essence, that Reine was a tribute offered to Dreil.*

*Once he took the bait, all that remained was to ensnare him.*

*Reine did not doubt her own talents.*

*Favorable or unfavorable — the latter, certainly. But the return was more than sufficient.*

*It was more than worth that much.*

*"Yes, if Duke Gasrel says so, Otou-sama will be pleased."*

*Just as Reine said this, Hime-sama, came a voice from her attendant.*

*There was nothing left to discuss here.*

*She bade the two farewell and was led away by her attendant's hand.*

*Albel's eyes never left Reine until the very end.*

*Father and son both seemed thick in the head — exceedingly convenient.*

*"Is something the matter, Benuure?"*

*"……No, I apologize. I was lost in thought for a moment."*

*Her long-serving attendant — Benuure — turned an anxious gaze upon Reine.*

*Even the servant who had spent more time by her side than anyone else no longer doubted Reine's 'purity.'*

*The poor princess, she must be thinking — and Reine smiled, pretending not to notice.*

*That evening, Dreil came to Reine's room claiming there was a private matter to discuss.*

*He gave a thorough explanation of the royal family's situation, impressed upon her the precariousness of her position, stated that he had obtained her father's permission, and made his demand.*

*She feigned fright and nodded, and Dreil pinned her down like an animal.*

*――Officially, the betrothed of Albel Gasrel.*

*In truth, the mistress of Dreil Gasrel.*

*She wore a brave face before the servants while appearing to hold genuine affection for Albel.*

*She had already been weakening her father with poison, but now he was of no further use.*

*She adjusted the dosage, and he was confined to his sickbed with what appeared to be a mysterious illness.*

*When the body weakened, so too did the spirit — her once-overbearing father summoned Reine daily, repenting and apologizing for what he had done to her.*

*She wore the face of a daughter who loved her father.*

*She would whisper that it could not be helped, that she loved him, and he would show a relieved smile.*

*Maneuvering him to pass the throne to her was simple.*

*Dreil, too, was a womanizer, but at heart he was cautious and cowardly.*

*A whisper in bed, and his feelings readily drifted toward taking stability rather than seizing the throne outright.*

*Dreil was satisfied with Reine's body, and while he had a mild streak of cruelty, Reine had by then grown accustomed to the act, and performing submission cost her nothing.*

*She showed through words and demeanor that she could not defy him, and like her father, Dreil permitted Reine the queen's throne without much thought.*

*Even if she were given real power, Reine could do nothing with it. He believed she would simply do as he said, and Reine played the puppet.*

*Never knowing he was being guided by Reine, he poured House Gasrel's vast fortune into the royal house, believing he was restoring a rusted throne he thought he had claimed.*

*By then, he must have already thought of himself as king.*

*Fools, father and son alike.*

*She manipulated Dreil by making him believe Reine was his woman, and manipulated Albel by making him believe Reine was a pitiful wife being toyed with by Dreil.*

*She played the tragic princess of a fallen royal house, showed tears and apologized for the adultery, and when she said he was the only one she loved, Albel readily believed it, swearing on his name that he would do anything to save Reine, whispering his love.*

*His head was stuffed with fairy-tale heroic epics — father and son, both brainless.*

*Inwardly, she found it unbearably amusing.*

*Dreil poured a fortune into the royal house, and Albel likewise fattened it in secret, out of Dreil's sight.*

*Once Reine had amassed sufficient power, she assassinated Dreil, absorbed House Gasrel, and disposed of the now-useless Albel as well.*

*By then, some had naturally begun to suspect Reine, but the preparations were complete.*

*No one could match Reine, who held the title of queen and the power befitting it.*

*There were more than enough men who would wag their tails for the privilege of bedding her, and no lord could defeat the army Reine commanded.*

*She crushed rebellions by force, concentrated power in her own hands, and the royal house was secure.*

*There was no longer anything in this world that could threaten Reine.*

*――It was precisely when she realized this that she noticed.*

*What, in such a world, did she even want?*

*What did she want, that she went on living in such a world?*

"Kaa-sama, I finished these documents."

"I see. Good girl, Reine."

"Ehehe……"

Sitting in the chair at her desk, she smiled at the Reine nestled in her lap and nuzzling against her, flipping through the documents the girl had brought.

She did not make mistakes. Reine did not doubt her daughter's excellence, just as she did not doubt her own.

It was merely a task of committing the contents to memory.

When she had finished looking them over, she lifted her daughter, held her close, and stroked her back.

Something that shines. Radiance.

The daughter she had named 'Reine,' the same as herself, possessed the same high intelligence — and in some respects, seemed to surpass her.

Yet in a sense, she was a disappointment.

Though she had turned fourteen, she was inflexible, and her thinking remained childish.

'Pure' sounded pleasant enough, but she was a bit of a 'fool.'

It was perhaps the same feeling one had keeping a pet, Reine thought.

Something close to that, she felt — but at the very least, she found the way the girl clung to her endearing, and holding her like this brought a sense of calm.

In one decisive aspect, her daughter was inferior to Reine, and yet Reine did not dislike that shortcoming — she found it dear.

It might take more time before her daughter could understand her.

Perhaps it was because, unlike Reine, she had grown up in a safe environment.

Even so, she had no desire to force her daughter into anything.

A curious thing, Reine thought.

She, who despised incompetence in others more than anyone, was the one person who felt no displeasure at that quality in her daughter.

Intelligence was her only merit, yet she lacked wisdom — foolish and childish.

Kaa-sama, kaa-sama, the girl would press close, showing her affection without the slightest guard.

Swept along by her immediate desires, she was simple, innocent, careless — and yet, the time spent with such a daughter was the only time Reine could think of nothing else and simply cherish the girl as she was.

As though a ray of light pierced the gloomy, dark emotions swirling in her mind.

A knock sounded, and she permitted entry.

The one who appeared was the long-serving head servant, Benuure.

"Excuse me, Your Majesty."

Benuure, who should by now be called an old woman, glanced at the daughter with a hint of concern.

Reine felt a touch of displeasure, but stroked her daughter's head and told her the work was done.

"Okay, then Reine will go to Fiiri's room."

"……Yes."

Fiiri Neeras. From a cadet branch of House Gasrel.

Mother dead from illness, father killed in the civil war, no siblings.

Attractive in appearance — she had been set aside as a reward for a noble, but Benuure had said her talent would be a waste and requested she be taken on as a servant. Twenty years ago.

Reine conducted a cursory investigation into the backgrounds of her servants, but as long as they came up clean, she more or less left Benuure to her discretion; anyone would do, provided they met a minimum standard of trust.

As one might expect of someone Benuure had specifically requested, her work was not poor.

She enjoyed Benuure's deep trust, and was effectively the number two among the servants.

When considering who to assign as the daughter's attendant, those were the reasons behind the choice, and Reine had regarded her with reasonable esteem — but now, she deeply regretted it.

An unpleasant, impudent woman.

She had removed her from the daughter's side, calling her a bad influence — but the girl remained fond of her.

She had taught the daughter all manner of useless things.

*"Do not fill that child's head with your nonsense. ……Starting tomorrow, I am changing her attendant. Be grateful you are not being thrown out of the palace."*

*"……I am grateful for Your Majesty's generosity. As you command."*

*What she hated most were those eyes.*

*Feigning prostration, yet looking at Reine as though with pity.*

*"What is that look?"*

*"……Nothing."*

*"Speak. Before my mood changes and I decide I do not wish to see your face at all."*

*Those words did not even make her flinch — just a troubled expression.*

*As though she did not understand what manner of being stood before her.*

*"Then……if you would permit me, may I ask a question? ……What does Your Majesty wish for Elsreine-sama to become?"*

*"To become……?"*

*"Elsreine-sama is brilliant, and yet dangerously pure. Each day, she takes interest in many things, and she worries over a great deal. If what Fiiri answered in good faith did not accord with Your Majesty's wishes, then the fault is surely Fiiri's."*

*She did not even comprehend her own position.*

*Before someone who could have her head taken with a single word.*

*"But if that is so, then I would ask Your Majesty to tell Fiiri what it is you envision. ……This Fiiri swears upon her name that Reine-sama's happiness is her foremost wish, and for that end, she is prepared to risk life and limb. ……For a mere servant to question the royal heart is the height of insolence. And yet, if doing so leads to Reine-sama's happiness, then she would prove her loyalty through service to that end."*

*She bowed deeply — what expression could be seen on her face?*

*"My order to you is this: do not fill that child's head with nonsense. ……Leave."*

*An impudent woman. An unpleasant woman.*

*Nothing but disagreeable. Even the servants who had once been wary of the daughter had, at some point, begun treating her like an ordinary child.*

*Casually — as though she were 'one of their own.'*

*And the daughter, too, had fallen under the same illusion.*

*That woman's doing, no doubt.*

*The daughter had none of the cunning needed to construct relationships strategically.*

*The one pulling the strings from behind was that woman.*

*Had she been trying to win the princess over for power, at least that would have had a certain charm.*

*That it was not so made it all the more intolerable.*

"……What is it, Benuure?"

She stopped her rumination and spoke.

Thinking about that woman only compounded her irritation.

"……Seil-sama wishes to meet with Your Majesty."

"What is it about?"

"He wishes to speak with Your Majesty directly."

"Did I not say so? I have no interest. Sound him out for me. Tell him I will grant minor requests."

"……Your Majesty."

Seil was the first prince in name.

He was, nominally, her son — but 'first failure' was more accurate.

Outside of formal occasions, she never met with her biological sons or daughters, never went to see them, and kept them housed separately.

She truly had no interest — she provided them the life befitting princes and princesses, but they were a nuisance.

She had long ago considered provoking them into rebellion so she could dispose of them, and had given them reasonably generous territories for that purpose, but they stubbornly refused to make a move.

"……Could you not spare even a fraction of the love you give Elsreine-sama for Seil-sama and the others? Benuure finds it unbearably pitiable. Seil-sama and the others love Your Majesty――"

"I am weary of your lectures, Benuure. ……And love, you say."

Reine chuckled, her shoulders shaking, and looked at Benuure.

"For the same reason a hatchling cries, I suppose. Those children are simply currying favor — so that I will feed them more. I feed them plenty, and I provide them ample shelter. If I give them more every time they cry, they will only grow more impudent."

Benuure shook her whitened golden hair and lowered her eyes.

"Reine possesses none of their ability — they are creatures that merely beg for feed. That I give them charity at all means I am quite generous, would you not say? ……Is that not what you people call love?"

"……Hime-sama, you are mistaken."

Benuure clenched her fists tight.

Her eyes glistened faintly with tears.

"I have served at Hime-sama's side since you were a child. ……How harsh a road Hime-sama has walked, how you had to kill your heart and discard so much for its sake — Benuure knows better than anyone."

"Fufu, you only believe you do, Benuure. You people think too conveniently."

As Reine mockingly tapped her own temple, Benuure shook her head.

"At the very least, when Hime-sama was young — what you directed at the late king was no illusion, but a certain truth. ……It is because I knew Hime-sama then that I swore upon my name to devote my life to your side, and have remained here."

"I was merely begging for feed, the same way. I was foolish."

"That is not so. And you were never foolish. ……What Hime-sama sought was not something so calculated — it was, without doubt, love from the late king."

Reine laughed in exasperation, and Benuure asked why she was laughing.

"Did you truly believe that a man like the late king — who thought of nothing but his own preservation — would give you something simply because you cried for it? Hime-sama is brilliant; surely you realized at once that it was meaningless. And yet you continued — was it not because you hoped? That perhaps, the late king would love you?"

"Benuure."

Reine sighed and fixed Benuure with a glare.

"Enough. ……You are a servant, and I am the queen. Whatever you say, I will not do anything more for those children, and that opinion will not change. 'I have no interest' — that was too roundabout, was it not? ……To be blunt: they disgust me."

"……Disgust?"

"Yes. That such failures came from me is a stain upon my life. ……The only one I wanted was Reine. ……In a world full of fools like you, the only one who would understand me — that child alone."

Yes, that child alone would surely come to understand her.

My most beloved, my Reine.

The one treasure in all the world.

With that alone, nothing else mattered.

"I suggest you leave before you displease me further. ……If you intend to utter more such drivel next time, know that there will no longer be a place for you here."

With that, she lowered her eyes back to her documents.

There remained a good deal of work to process.

Fires smoldered everywhere, including within the palace.

Before the coming invasion of the eastern continent, she wished to let them flare up in one great conflagration and purge the dissident elements.

To that end, she had deliberately squeezed the populace, burning several villages that had failed to pay their taxes as a public example.

No matter how the masses suffered, Reine was the queen, and what was demanded of her subjects was a simple choice: obey her commands, or do not.

This would cull most of the fools who would resist on the basis of empty notions like justice and righteousness.

Seil's matter was related to this.

That the other princes would undoubtedly become the core of the rebellion was no doubt the reason as well.

With no possibility of succession and no rivalry over the throne, the princes got along well, and she knew Seil had been working to prevent a rebellion before it started.

That this would not solve the problem — he could not even see that. How absurd.

What Reine wanted was justification to kill the troublesome princes — without a rebellion, it only dragged on.

If anything, he was prolonging the problem — endlessly vexing.

"Benuure, you are distracting me. ……Being forced to listen to such foolishness has put me in a foul mood."

"……Yes."

When Reine made it clear she had nothing more to say, Benuure left the room after a moment.

Reine did not so much as glance in her direction.

# Chapter 302: Reine III

Fiiri's room had become an atelier for the two of them.

Not just painting — they collected all manner of pigments and explored new techniques through research.

From soft watercolors to richly contrasted oils, a variety of paintings now lined the room, and an entire separate room had been converted into storage.

Brushwork, the use of color.

Nearly every day, they would talk eagerly about what worked well here and what was beautiful there.

"Fufu……what a lovely painting."

"Ehehe, thank you! But, do you think I should try adding more colors……?"

Before the two of them sat an oil painting by Reine — Fiiri, the servants, and two Reines, all smiling.

A table and chairs in a forest clearing, dappled light filtering through the gaps in the trees.

It was based on a watercolor Fiiri had once painted, rendered in oil by Reine.

"Let me see……at this point, it is really a matter of taste. You could finish it as it is, and it would be perfectly fine."

The painting seemed too real to be an imaginary scene.

As though a real landscape had been captured — painted with vivid, defined colors.

More than sufficient as a finished work — no one else could have replicated it.

Her paintings were always like this.

"But adding more color might be interesting, I will say."

Reine could only produce perfection.

And yet, what she sought lay somewhere slightly different.

"……Color."

"For example……light. Emphasizing the expression of light, perhaps."

Reine favored defined oils and meticulous detail.

But Fiiri preferred soft watercolors that left a certain gentle ambiguity.

Reine was captivated by the ambiguity in Fiiri's work, and Fiiri was captivated by the vivid clarity of Reine's, as though she had cut a slice from the real world.

By now, Reine was clearly the superior in technique, but their relationship had not changed.

Sitting in the chair beside her, tapping the end of her brush against her lips, Fiiri thought, and Reine did the same — tapping the brush's end against her own lips, tilting her head.

"Light, you think."

"Yes. Emphasizing the expression of light more might make it even lovelier."

Fiiri gazed at the painting and smiled.

"Reine is a word meaning 'that which shines.' ……Surely Her Majesty poured many feelings into giving Elsreine-sama her own name. If such things could be expressed in Elsreine-sama's painting, I think it might become something even more wonderful……"

"Many feelings……?"

"Yes. Of course, emotions are difficult things to express in words, but……"

Fiiri swept her brush through the air.

Gently, softly.

"But I believe that a certain touch of the brush, some little something, can express such things. ……The reason I am drawn to painting is because I wish to express something that cannot be put into words. ……If one could paint such a thing, I believe a single painting could be far more eloquent than any exchange of words, conveying all manner of things."

Seeing Reine's face scrunch up in thought, she gave a wry smile.

"Fufu, I apologize. I am saying things that do not quite make sense."

"……No, Reine is just foolish, that is all."

"My. That is not so at all."

As she spoke, Fiiri tickled Reine's cheek with the tip of her clean brush.

That tickles, Reine laughed, leaning back — then she grabbed Fiiri's arm and climbed sideways onto her lap.

She studied her own painting for a while, then nodded.

"Hmm, Reine will think about it a little more."

"Yes. But……it truly is a matter of preference. As far as I am concerned, it is already an incomparably wonderful painting, so please do not fret too much. Elsreine-sama is my teacher now……fufu, this is merely a student's idle thought."

"Fiiri's paintings are really, really beautiful. Reine still can't paint anything like them."

"You are too kind……but I am only making up for my lack of skill by fudging things."

"Reine still can't understand how Fiiri fudges things at all, so Fiiri is still Reine's teacher."

Reine pouted, and Fiiri smiled wryly, puffing out her cheeks, then poked them and shook with laughter.

It was true that Fiiri could not render detail as finely as Reine, and yet her paintings possessed a strange charm.

When the other servants saw Reine's paintings, they would look surprised and offer praise — but when they saw Fiiri's, their reactions were different from when they viewed Reine's.

Some were moved to tears by a single landscape, saying it reminded them of their homeland — surely Reine was not the only one who found that remarkable.

More eloquent than the exchange of words.

Something that could not be put into words did indeed exist in the colors Fiiri chose, in her brushstrokes, in the paintings she completed.

By comparison, Reine's work was far simpler.

She merely extracted images from memory and assembled them like puzzle pieces.

What Fiiri was trying to paint surely lay in a place far deeper than where Reine stood.

"Well……in any case, I am certain Her Majesty will be pleased. This painting seems to hold so many of Elsreine-sama's feelings."

"……Do you think so?"

"Yes. To be honest, I was surprised when Elsreine-sama said you wished to give Her Majesty a painting. Until now, you had always painted things to make me happy."

Reine's cheeks flushed, and Fiiri let out a quiet laugh.

"Her Majesty is not interested in painting. But Elsreine-sama, knowing that full well, has chosen to try to please Her Majesty — to paint a picture as a gift for her. That is a very difficult thing to do……and precisely because of that, I think it is wonderful."

"……Wonderful."

"Yes, very. I am sure the feelings Elsreine-sama has put into this painting will reach Her Majesty."

When Reine nodded and said yes, Fiiri gently stroked her head.

*I hope it reaches her*, Reine thought.

Surely it was just a matter of buttons done up wrong.

Her mother was still admirable, brilliant — Reine respected and loved her dearly.

But, regrettably, her mother despised everyone except Reine.

She had said that everyone besides herself and Reine were fools — a different species.

It was not as though Reine could not understand her mother's reasoning at all.

By now, Reine knew there were bad people in the world, and cruel ones too.

People who raised taxes at will to line their own pockets, people who fabricated crimes to undermine and drag down those around them, people who took pleasure in oppressing others.

As she began helping with her mother's work and learned about the world outside, Reine encountered all sorts — but not everyone was like that.

At the very least, Fiiri was not, and the servants here were not.

And surely, there were many such people throughout the world.

Her mother was simply mistaken, Reine thought.

Just redo the mismatched buttons — that was all it would take to solve the problem. She hoped so.

If her mother could get along not just with Reine, but with Fiiri and the others around her, then surely she could be much happier.

At the very least, Reine was happy now, and she believed the same of her mother.

"Kaa-sama has it hard. She is always thinking so far ahead with her work……but there are so many people who do not understand what kaa-sama is trying to do."

But, Reine continued.

"Reine thinks it would be better if she could help everyone understand what kaa-sama is trying to do……of course, not everyone will understand, but surely some people will, and so if we get along like that, then all kaa-sama's hard work might become a little easier. Just a little."

To those words, Fiiri smiled.

Whenever Reine spoke about the country or work or her mother, Fiiri never said whether it was good or bad.

"Well, the fact that Rougran-niisama will die doesn't change…… Reine wouldn't mind if you died in a duel instead. Honestly, Reine just doesn't want any more pointless deaths among the soldiers."

Rougran smiled bitterly, turned his back, and put distance between them.

Then he faced Elsreine and took his stance.

"……Surprising — that you would show such concern. But…… Lasria and my son understand. When they hear I have lost, they will take their own lives. The moment I decided to stand here, I was prepared for everything."

"……I see."

"I accept the duel. ——The second prince, Rougran Age Vel Nilkrinea Alberan, declares! Let all soldiers upon this field stay their blades!! Upon this name, upon the blood of the royal house, I shall engage in an honorable duel with the fourth princess, Elsreine Alberan!!"

A roar like a howl to the heavens.

The air shuddered as if in answer, and the cacophony of battle that had still been ringing began to fade beneath his voice.

"Let this sacred duel of the royal house, with the royal blood shed herein, be the final bloodshed upon this field! Let all submit to the sole survivor, and let any who defile this rite know the divine retribution of Koreis, god of valor!!"

Having sung out his oath, Rougran faced Elsreine once more.

"I've wanted to settle things with you as much as with my brother…… to fight you, just once. Elsreine — the only sister our mother ever loved."

"……Reine doesn't enjoy this very much, but…… Reine will try to make it painless."

"What confidence. But do not underestimate me——!!"

Faster than a charging bull.

A sprint unthinkable for one in such heavy armor.

With his great shield before him, it was as if a mass of steel had been launched.

The swordsmanship known as Rolka style was, by nature, a defensive art.

Deflect the opponent's advance with the left, seize the after-initiative, and cut them down.

But Rougran, who had trained his body to its absolute limit as a warrior and encased it in heavy armor, was now a force that did not yield even to a mana-beast's charge when he pressed head-on.

Any counterattack would be smashed aside by his great shield; retreat and be crushed by it; dodge and his powerful arms would unleash the greatsword.

There was no room to target the slightest gap.

The great shield blocked the field of vision, and Rougran deftly used his armor's steel to seal the opponent's blade.

Rougran was a colossus of steel.

He was beyond the domain of man — a warrior no single technique could overcome.

No one watching could have envisioned the small-framed Elsreine defeating him.

At the very least, to win, Elsreine would need to employ mobile warfare, leveraging her small build to patiently outmaneuver the flawless Rougran and take his back.

But Elsreine stood directly before him, setting her spear from the spot.

Even Seil, who knew Elsreine's capabilities, believed this was carelessness on her part.

*Power against Rougran — she can't be serious,* he thought.

But — Elsreine leaned gently forward.

And overlapping that, a multi-layered crash of steel that seemed to slip between the instants of thought.

When he noticed, she had passed through the steel colossus and stood behind Rougran, empty-handed.

"Gh, ah……"

The kneeling giant — the close-combat spear had punched through the great steel shield as if it were cloth, pierced the armor at the torso, and the spearhead emerged through Rougran's back.

It was no special spear.

Sturdy, but no different from a common soldier's — an eight-shaku close-combat spear.

And yet it had effortlessly skewered the steel shield that had deflected Seil's blade, and the armor behind it.

Everyone was struck speechless.

Rougran stared dumbfounded at the spear buried in his chest — then for an instant, turned his gaze toward Seil.

A faint smile crossed his lips, and he collapsed face-first, still impaled.

As Elsreine's adjutant proclaimed her victory in a resounding voice, Seil simply gazed at his brother's form, and at the still figure of Elsreine.

Her face showed neither the joy of victory nor the sorrow of having killed a brother — expressionless.

She turned a cold gaze upon her surroundings, so like her mother's.

*What do you think of the current state of things?* — when he had asked, she answered vaguely, evading.

When he told her she would end up killing her brothers, she had said, *I suppose so,* with an air of sadness.

Benuure had trusted her more than anyone.

She was sharp — closer to their mother than Seil.

She surely knew their mother more deeply than Seil ever could.

And yet, Seil could see no will in her to accomplish anything.

She sat upright in her chair, prim and proper, and yet, in contrast to that appearance, she was like water.

Push hard and she would retreat endlessly; scoop her up and she would fill your hands.

Every word she gave Seil was uncertain and vague.

*'……Do you not wish to change things as they are?'*

*'Forgive me, but…… Your Highness has been saying the strangest things. Fiiri is a servant…… she desires nothing beyond her master's happiness.'*

*'Even if our mother…… is mad?'*

When he asked, she smiled vaguely again.

It was the kind of look one gives a troubled child.

*'Whatever makes you say Her Majesty the Queen is mad?'*

*'……If she were not, she would not deliberately provoke her own sons into rebellion just to dispose of them. That she has human feeling…… I cannot believe it.'*

*'Is that so? Her Majesty is simply a very pure person…… perhaps it is we who are the mad ones.'*

Fiiri narrowed her eyes and traced the rim of her teacup.

*'She had not seen you for so long, and yet she invited you to her chambers — that was surely because she granted Benuure-sama's wish, was it not? If Her Majesty were truly without feeling, she would not have done such a thing.'*

*'That is……'*

*'Would a person without feeling dote upon Elsreine-sama as she does? If you call closing one's heart madness, then I too, and you, Your Highness, and everyone else is the same. We all close our hearts with reason. There is no difference.'*

*It is only a matter of degree,* she continued.

*'Surely, she was once like Elsreine-sama. Without shielding herself behind reason or logic, open and defenseless, opening her heart to everything she saw. ……But this world is too harsh a place for someone that pure. People close their hearts because they know the pain of being hurt…… to call that madness is rather a rough way of putting it.'*

*'……But even so——'*

*'Your Highness and I stand in different positions. I cannot claim to understand the pain of not receiving Her Majesty's love. ……The frustration you feel toward the current state of things, the injustice — those are yours, Your Highness. The breadth of the worlds we see, the places we stand, everything is different. And besides……'*

*I am a servant, after all,* she told Seil.

With those limpid blue eyes.

*'Of course, I do wish for the day when Her Majesty's closed heart may open once more. And that Elsreine-sama, who is such a kind soul, may go on without being hurt. ……But all I wish for is such happiness, close at hand. To watch over and believe in the two of them — that alone is my role……'*

Seil held back the words he was about to speak and lowered his eyes.

The tea he had not touched reflected his own face.

*'……No matter how much Her Majesty is scorned as heartless, I will simply believe and wish — for ten years, twenty years…… or perhaps far longer — that the day will come when that world she desires can be seen. If such a day comes, if it is for that…… I will accept whatever Her Majesty does. At the very least, I…… believe that world will be more beautiful than any other.'*

He gazed at her smile.

Red-glinting golden hair — the eyes that peeked from beneath it were like jewels.

*'……Why can you say that with such certainty?'*

*'Because I have never doubted the love Her Majesty directs at Elsreine-sama. ……If Her Majesty loves Elsreine-sama, if she desires Elsreine-sama's happiness, then the world built from that will surely be a very gentle, wonderful paradise.'*

Her transcendent bearing called to mind their mother.

At the very least, she saw things differently from Seil.

She did not fear their mother — the one before whom all trembled and knelt.

And with calculating coldness, she had cut away everything else.

She could not be unaware of the people's suffering.

Nor of those with conscience whom their mother had killed, nor of Benuure who had given her life for their mother — she dismissed it all with the single word 'sacrifice' and calmly wished for the happiness of mother and Elsreine.

She was not on Seil's side.

*'What is it you desire, Your Highness?'*

*'……I——'*

*'Do you wish to save the suffering people? Do you wish to save Their Highnesses Rougran and the others? Or do you wish to save Her Majesty…… or perhaps, yourself?'*

She pressed her glossy lips to the teacup — sipping quietly, lowering her gaze.

The tantalizing movement of her tongue glimpsed through the gap caught his eye.

Her words clung to him like puppet strings.

*'……What a person can reach for is limited. Even Her Majesty is no different. And what one desires is not always obtained, even by throwing oneself into the attempt. ……Your Highness, you should consider once more what it is you truly desire.'*

*'……You're telling me to…… cut things away?'*

*If that is what you wish,* she said with a wry smile.

*'The world is unfair, unreasonable, and cruel. ……And the only thing everyone possesses equally is the right to wish for happiness. Your Highness has yours, and I have mine…… The one who should answer that question is not I.'*

Then she turned her gaze to the window where the sun had begun to set, and stood.

*'……May the paths we walk lead to the same place.'*

*——That is all I pray for.*

What was reflected in her eyes as she continued?

At the very least, Seil could not tell at the time, and even now, he was still lost.

"……Come in?"

Seeing Seil standing frozen at the tent's entrance, Elsreine set down her brush and tilted her head.

*It's nothing,* he said with a shake of his head, then sat in the central chair and looked around at the beautiful paintings placed here and there.

Works that seemed beyond human hands — paintings of divine creation.

That she painted was surprising; the paintings themselves were not.

Elsreine's talents allowed her to effortlessly embody the aspirations of countless ordinary people.

The aftermath of the battle was done, and things had settled.

Burial of the dead, processing of prisoners — most of that had originally been his responsibility.

Elsreine's army of twenty thousand had sustained no losses. Before arriving here, she had destroyed two armies and killed three brothers with a loss of only 3,000.

Her army, arriving as reinforcements at this battlefield, had replenished its strength to full capacity, and had ended the battle against Rougran's forces without a clash, settling it in a duel.

She had expected Elsreine to leave first, but the reason she remained was apparently on the advice of her adjutant.

Seil's army was exhausted from the fight with Rougran.

The war might be over, but this land, far to the east of Wulfenite, had always been unstable.

Most had risen alongside Rougran, and stability was far from assured.

When told that both armies should cooperate on post-battle operations to prevent any mishaps, Seil — having lost much of his strength — could not refuse.

Her adjutant, Gorzarius Lukazan, was said to be a valiant man, but apparently a cautious one.

At least, it was evident from these past few days that Elsreine relied on him as her adjutant — but Seil could not quite bring himself to trust the man.

It was nothing specific — perhaps a kind of instinctive wariness or aversion.

Many nobles of good conscience had offered counsel to their mother and been killed for it.

Most nobles remaining were fence-sitting mediocrities — at the very least, people who had watched in silence.

That said, calling him another mediocrity without really knowing him was unreasonable, but at the very least, he could not form a good impression.

*A blunt soldier who keeps his distance from politics* — for such a claim, the man held a rather powerful position.

For someone born to a baronial house, he had risen remarkably well.

——No, this was not the time to think about such things.

He sighed and spoke.

"……I came to apologize again for the other day. As you said, it was misdirected anger…… I was a bit on edge about mother."

"Reine doesn't really mind…… but Reine thought it was cruel to blame Fiiri, that's all. Reine doesn't want to quarrel with Seil-niisama either."

"……Nor do I. I understand it rationally, yet sometimes I lose myself. I'll try to ensure it doesn't happen again."

Elsreine nodded as she prepared tea.

She appeared to be holding in some lingering displeasure — looking at her now, she was very much a child.

At least, he knew she was an innocent, kind girl.

If one insisted on calling her mad, it would only be that she was a girl who could go on living like an innocent child after killing four of her brothers.

She had taken Rougran's life without feeling, and showed no sign of being troubled by it.

Her fundamental values were different from Seil's.

Was what he felt toward her simply his own confused sentiments?

She had slain the enemy commander on the battlefield.

That was all she had done, and by rights it was correct — the result of a duel that deserved praise, and blaming her for not grieving was nothing but unreasonable.

He understood that in principle, yet his emotions refused to accept it — what he felt toward her and their mother was something like that.

A mother who used her son's life with the single phrase, *for the sake of future stability.*

Still, Rougran and the others, who had ultimately turned their blades on the kingdom, were in the wrong.

If the kingdom's stability followed, they were nothing more than rebels who defied a rare and brilliant ruler.

At the very least, the people's suffering would not have continued forever.

If everyone had refrained from raising the banner of revolt and demonstrated blind submission to their mother, she would surely have halted things by simple calculation of costs before the nation was bled dry.

Their mother was a ruler truly worthy of the name Alberan — the most brilliant leader in this world.

In the end, everything was arranged so that all would turn out well — their mother would undoubtedly create a perfect kingdom.

Seil held that certainty, and yet, only his emotions refused to consent.

The suffering simply existed.

"……I cannot understand you or mother."

"……?"

"There are parts I do understand. The reasons for provoking this civil war, and everything else — I believe I can comprehend the logic, to a degree. But…… emotionally, I cannot accept it."

As she poured tea into a cup, Elsreine looked at him curiously.

The purple that gleamed through the gaps in her golden hair shone unclouded.

"……I am proud to have been born as mother's son. Mother is a ruler worthy of that pride, and she will surely make this nation more prosperous and radiant than ever before. I have no intention of doubting that, and you are the most fitting person to stand at her side. I have known for a long time that you possess the ability."

Seil was blessed with more talent than most.

And without letting it go to his head, he had devoted himself to grueling training.

And yet, this girl before him soared past everything he had accomplished as easily as drawing breath.

Elsreine was truly one of the divine lineage — their mother's child.

What Seil could never obtain no matter how he wished and longed, she had possessed from the moment of her birth.

"In truth, I wanted to be the one standing at mother's side, as you do. ……So I envy you, for being able to understand mother. I am jealous. Those are my honest feelings."

"……You envy Reine?"

"Yes. ……At the very least, I do not dislike you. We siblings must have seemed cold to you. I cannot say the same for everyone, but…… at the very least, what lay at the root was a similar feeling."

*I'm sorry,* he said simply.

It was not Elsreine's fault.

She had merely possessed what their mother sought — the talent.

"This is not just about the other day, so I won't be so convenient as to ask you to forgive everything up to now. But…… for the sake of what mother desires…… I want to do everything I can. ……For the sake of Rougran and the others who were sacrificed."

Elsreine gazed at Seil, looking as though she were thinking it over.

She stirred her tea — generously filled with milk and honey — with a teaspoon, and lowered her eyes.

"Even if you say that…… all Reine can say is 'I see.' Why do you say this now? If that's how you felt, Seil-niisama should have shown it from the very start. If you had, Benuure might not have died."

"……Yes."

"Reine was told by kaa-sama that Benuure died to fulfill the wish Seil-niisama begged for — to be allowed to speak with Reine. What was it you wanted to do, Seil-niisama?"

Elsreine glared at Seil.

"Suppose kaa-sama had listened to Seil-niisama, lowered the taxes, and Rougran-niisama and the others hadn't rebelled — then everything done for that purpose would have been wasted. All the people who were sacrificed by the crackdown — it was to prevent everyone else from becoming victims in the future…… was letting them die for nothing really the result you wanted?"

What had he wanted to do?

She was right.

*'——Why didn't you side with Rougran and the others?'*

He recalled his mother's mocking face.

What Seil had done was merely stir up the situation.

Whether it had meaning — it had been nothing but self-satisfaction.

He had known, deep down, that their mother had no intention of granting Seil's wish.

If Seil truly cared about the people, he should have led Rougran and the others in revolt, or else demonstrated absolute submission to their mother from the beginning and made their deaths as meaningful as possible.

There had been only those two choices.

"……Reine knows you were close with Rougran-niisama. Reine doesn't think Rougran-niisama was a bad person, and Reine doesn't want to think that Seil-niisama — who Benuure sacrificed herself for — is a bad person either. ……But, Seil-niisama, what you say and what you do are backwards."

By holding Rougran back from rising, the people's suffering was prolonged.

And for Seil's sake, Benuure had been made to die for nothing.

"At the very least, because of that pointless action, Benuure — who didn't need to die — died for Seil-niisama. ……Reine, honestly, can't bring herself to like Seil-niisama very much."

"……I have no excuse. You're right…… Benuure's sacrifice is the result of my foolishness."

Indecisive, cowardly, and foolish.

If he wanted to change things, he should have resolved himself to die from the start, like the many loyal retainers who had been killed. If he had coordinated with Rougran and the others to jointly petition in a public forum, then — weighing costs and benefits — perhaps she could have been persuaded.

The rational grounds for executing not only the counseling retainers but all the princes could not easily be found.

Seil had wavered, unable to act, merely dancing between their mother and siblings.

There was nothing in his heart but regret.

"……The other day, after that…… I spoke with Fiiri."

"With Fiiri……?"

Elsreine furrowed her brow, and Seil nodded.

"That is why I decided to speak with you properly. I——"

Just as Seil was about to continue, the sound of a galloping horse.

*Your Highness,* came a voice from outside the tent, and the two stood.

"……Lukazan? Reine is speaking with Seil-niisama right now……"

"It is a matter of utmost urgency."

"Urgency…… come in."

Elsreine approached the entrance, and the large man with the burn-scarred face entered.

Kneeling, Gorzarius Lukazan spoke gravely.

"A subordinate I had left behind to report has sent word of a disturbance at the royal castle."

"A disturbance……?"

"……Surely not — a coup? Is Her Majesty safe, Lukazan?"

"Yes. Her Majesty is unharmed. However, the one killed, from what I have been told, was…… a castle servant."

Gorzarius looked up and stared at Elsreine.

"A servant named Fiiri."

"……What?"

* * *

——It was the day after Elsreine had left the royal castle.

The queen's chambers — elegant, yet bleak and cold.

The one who bowed upon entering had red-glinting golden hair.

Dressed in an apron dress, she was a beautiful servant.

The woman in white — Queen Grabareine, who sat sipping tea alone — noticed her and said, *Come in.*

As the queen smiled, her golden-thread hair swayed gently.

"I have come as summoned, Your Majesty the Queen."

"Yes. Why do you think you were called?"

"……To keep you company for tea, perhaps?"

The servant also smiled, and asked, *Or——*

"Or have you at last grown tired of Fiiri and wish to dispose of her?"

The queen narrowed her eyes, gazing coldly with those purple irises.

"Oh my, you came knowing that? Have you given up? Or are you counting on being Reine's favorite to keep you safe?"

*What a strange thing to say,* the servant replied.

"Fiiri is Her Majesty's subject, and this body and life belong to Her Majesty. When called, I answer, and devote all to my duty. ……If you command it, I shall slit my own throat before you this very instant."

*What are your orders?* the servant said, smiling once more.

The queen, by contrast, showed her displeasure, and indicated the opposite chair with a glance.
