# Chapter 256: The Cage of Sin

"I truly do believe I am the most fortunate person in the world."

She said it the way she always did.

Holding Kreschenta in bed, her voice calm and measured.

"Not only to have Krische-sama's love……but to be so deeply cherished by the great Queen of Alberan herself, a woman whose name is etched deeply into history."

A soft, teasing laugh tickled her ear.

Kreschenta opened her mouth to say something, then immediately stopped.

The inside of her chest kept seizing and spasming with tension, making it hard to breathe, and she did not want to let this servant hear such a pathetic sound.

She pressed her lips together and pushed her face into the servant's chest.

Over her eyelids, over her lips — as though to seal everything away.

The servant tightened her embrace and stroked her head.

"My, you don't deny it. ……For the record, silence is also taken as confirmation, so do keep that in mind."

The teasing voice was gentle, and it was infuriating.

She knew Kreschenta couldn't lift her face, and said it anyway.

"But do rest assured. Such an embarrassing secret of yours shall be kept strictly confidential — locked away in the breast of your servant. A good servant fulfills her master's wishes without being told."

The infuriating servant piled on her worthless words and stroked her head.

Always, always — when it suited her, she was Onee-sama's servant, the estate's servant, Christand's servant.

— And Kreschenta's servant.

She appeared serious but was really careless, and far too clever with words.

Calling herself a servant was just pretense — an individualist who would scoff at social order and class structure.

From the standpoint of the 'ideal kingdom' Kreschenta envisioned, she was the most offensive woman imaginable.

She spoke of everything in terms of emotion, piling on plausible sophistry, bewitching others with her petty delusions, a pest who sought to shape the world to suit herself.

A ship did not need two helmsmen.

And it was Kreschenta who was queen — this woman was, in essence, a political rival.

How much better it would have been if she had killed her sooner, Kreschenta thought.

Then she would never have had to endure such lofty words from above.

*How did it come to this?* she wondered.

Perhaps at some point, she had been taken in by this woman's sophistry without realizing it.

"……Fufu, I keep trying to say things, but I end up thinking too much and can't manage it. Talking about old memories or things to come — you'd be surprised how little you can actually say."

She laughed as if troubled and kept stroking her head.

Her skin was cooler than Kreschenta's, and it felt like soaking in lukewarm water.

Neither scalding nor cold.

Lukewarm water that drained away all strength and made you not want to think about anything.

"I hold no grudge, and I am neither angry nor sad. As an ending to a life, this is good enough, I think."

She said this and rubbed her cheek against the top of Kreschenta's head.

"It is true that it was painful and agonizing. I would have loved to wail and scream about it……in that sense, I truly am grateful."

"……!"

She grabbed the back of the servant's negligee and clenched it tight.

"Lies, all lies. ……No matter how you dress it up with words, you are dying. Being killed……by me. So why should I have to be comforted by the person I am killing?"

"……My, you really are just like Krische-sama, Kreschenta-sama."

*What a beautiful person*, she laughed again.

Happily, joyfully, lovingly.

"Even without being judged by law, you feel that because you did something wrong you deserve to be punished — that very feeling is itself the punishment. ……You understand right and wrong in your heart, Kreschenta-sama, no matter what you say, and anything more would be excessive."

She stroked her head the way she always did and combed through her hair.

Thump, thump — the beat of her heart echoed, and Kreschenta could only sniffle quietly.

"Moreover, I am not a guardian of the law — I am but a humble servant who loves Kreschenta-sama dearly. Comforting you is only natural. And a crying child being comforted is also only natural."

"……I am not crying."

"Oh my, you're right. I did promise to keep it locked away."

She giggled softly and kept stroking.

The strength in her embracing arms was gradually fading.

Understanding that the medicine was taking effect, Kreschenta's spine went rigid as though frozen.

"……Please take care of Krische-sama. She will surely be very saddened."

She laughed, looking a little lonely, and continued vaguely.

"I had promised not to do anything that would make her sad, but……"

Kreschenta lifted her face in a rush and sent mana coursing into her body.

Through her blurred vision, she could see the servant smiling.

Her palm touched Kreschenta's cheek, and her thumb wiped beneath her eyes.

"Ufufu, just like Krische-sama……a crying face really doesn't suit you, Kreschenta-sama."

"……!"

"We mustn't let anyone else know. ……You are my beloved, wonderful, greatest queen in all the world, after all."

The words caught in the back of her throat and wouldn't come out.

She watched the sleepily wavering voice and the trembling eyelids, then grasped both her cheeks.

She rubbed their noses together, drew her face close, and the touch of their lips was simply soft.

For just a moment, the servant opened her trembling eyelids wide, gazed at Kreschenta with her light-brown eyes.

Then slowly narrowed them, and mouthed, without sound — *I know.*

As always, with that teasing smile.

After saying it, she closed her eyes, and let go of consciousness just like that.

"ah……"

Immediately, she wove mana into her.

She thought to stop time, then hurriedly changed the mana into threads.

*Maybe*, she thought. *If it works*, she thought.

She was simply, purely afraid of something.

Her breathing grew ragged, something seemed to crawl inside her chest.

As if fleeing from it, she simply kept weaving formulae.

The mana frayed and split like threads, and formed a cage.

Wrapping around her body, imprisoning everything down to her blood, flesh, bone, and nerves.

*Stop time* — that was the only thought.

With enough time, anything was possible.

Ten years from now, twenty years, even a hundred — someday, it could be done.

All that was lacking was time and room to breathe.

That was all she wanted.

From one end of her to the other, engraving it into every part of her, again and again.

Perfection layered upon perfection, fold upon fold.

As if to prove that nothing was impossible for her, that she had made no mistakes.

— But regardless of all that, results were everything.

Blood seeped through her negligee.

Even the blood that seeped turned instantly into mana and dissipated.

Parts of her body crumbled away like a sandhill blown by the wind.

Mana was poured in, within the cage sealed shut in countless layers.

Inside it, her body turned to blue light — and only the clothing she wore remained.

Unable even to make a sound, she reached out in a daze.

She grasped the negligee the servant had been wearing.

Stared at it.

Held it to her chest.

— How long had she stayed like that?

"Your Majesty! What——"

Before she realized it, someone had called out from the doorway.

The voice seemed to echo from far away.

"Where is Argan-sama……?"

In her arms was only a thin, flat sensation.

Still dazed, she stared at the negligee.

It had belonged to the servant that Kreschenta killed.

She pictured Nora, with the knife driven into her chest.

She pictured the red-haired servant, traced her own lips, and closed her eyes.

Behind her, she heard the sound of someone collapsing to the floor, and she steadied her breathing.

"……Argan-sama has passed away. It happened suddenly."

Then, as always, with perfect composure, Kreschenta layered on her lies.

She had never made a mistake. She was a perfect being, always.

Even if it was a lie, once spoken it became truth.

"Onee-sama and the others will be saddened. ……For now, I suppose we should prepare the funeral."

For just a moment, she pressed the negligee to her face.

As if wiping something away, beneath both eyes.

— To Kreschenta, other people had always been like tools.

As it had been until now, so it would continue to be.

She raised her face, got down from the bed, and looked at the full-length mirror.

Her usual self was there, gazing back at Kreschenta with perfect composure from the other side.

*That's fine*, she thought.

"A shame."

A tool that had been useful had broken down, had come to trouble Kreschenta's own hands, had been beyond saving — so she had simply settled things at the end.

There was nothing that needed thinking about, she told herself, and placed the negligee on the bed.

Glancing at the servant who had collapsed and covered her face, she reached for the door.

"I shall go fetch Elvena-sama. Please rest a moment."

As if looking away from something that clung to her, she turned her eyes to what needed doing.

She stepped outside the room.

What was done was done.

There was nothing to be done about it.

She averted her gaze, looked away.

— Telling herself that eventually, she would stop remembering.

* * *

It was like a cage made of facing mirrors.

The inside of the small mana crystal was carved with precise patterns layered upon themselves, fold upon fold.

In appearance it was small enough to fit in one hand, but it was a multidimensional crystal with space itself bent and folded.

Visually, it seemed as though an almost infinite brilliance unfolded within it from every angle.

Its inner radiance changed each time she looked, and a slight shift in angle revealed a different face.

Within it, five lights burned.

Each time she looked, they caught in the girl's eyes from all manner of directions, from all manner of angles.

She turned the crystal, searching for something, and fixed her gaze on one of the lights.

She thought that someday she wished it would go out, that she wished it would simply vanish — yet every time she looked, that light leapt into her eyes.

Still wearing her white one-piece dress, she rolled onto the bed and let her long hair spread across it.

She looked at it lying on her side, held it up to look, and occasionally enclosed its brilliance within her hand.

She thought that doing so might eventually extinguish its light — yet when she looked again, it was there unchanged.

It was an irksome, hateful light, shining obtrusively.

She brought it close before her eyes again and stared.

Was there no angle from which its light would disappear? She examined it from every possible direction, idly.

— *I hate you.*

Her lips moved as they always did, and the words echoed into the void.

No response came, and the light did not dim.

No matter how many times she repeated it, her brilliance was there, asserting its presence in the girl's eyes.

No angle changed it, and even with her eyes closed, it would not leave the backs of her eyelids.

Overbearing, arrogant, and ill-mannered.

The only thing worth praising was the food.

That thoroughly infuriating servant's mocking little smile, and every single word echoed in her mind, repeating.

She remembered that sensation and traced her own lips.

Something dark and viscous swirled inside her chest, and she closed her eyes at the unpleasantness of it.

When she recalled that face, it overlapped with a face contorted in agony.

Suffering night after night, tears brimming.

The vacant look in her eyes had burned into her mind and would not leave.

As if cowering from pain and death, the figure begging for help was engraved into her memory day after day.

*'……Even if I last, how many years do you intend to wait?'*

*'Of course, as many decades as Krische-sama wishes.'*

And after showing such a sight, she would tell Kreschenta with complete composure.

That she was still happy, as if nothing at all had happened.

She simply conveyed her gratitude.

*Thank you*, she would repeat.

*Thanks to you, another happy day continues* — she would say, gesturing at those days drenched in agony.

Yesterday and the day before, today and tomorrow and the day after, and the day after that.

All Kreschenta was doing was prolonging it.

Making her repeat suffering that even the servant could not hide anymore, suffering with no end in sight.

Every time she received those words, she wondered — gratitude for what?

And so she decided she would end it — she would bring it to a close.

"……I did nothing wrong."

The words rang hollow.

When she opened her eyes, the light was still shining, unchanged from a moment ago.

So hateful she wished she could make it never appear in her sight again, so irksome — yet when she changed the angle, it showed her all manner of colors.

How much better it would be if it were ugly, she thought.

If it were so ugly she couldn't bear to look at it, she'd never have to see it again.

Why had her sister brought such a thing?

Without this, she wouldn't have to remember, wouldn't be imprisoned — but because it was here, it was as if she were locked in a cage.

She tried to escape but couldn't; her own thoughts formed the cage, and the very thing she had meant to imprison was imprisoning her.

— *I love you. I love you.*

Always, always repeating such flimsy words.

They were nothing but lip service and illusion.

Even at the point of being killed, she had kept repeating such words.

She had simply given up.

Because voicing resentment would have been pointless, she had said such things.

Just her usual stubbornness, mouthing pretty words.

No one would be happy about being killed.

No matter how you dressed it up, death was death. There was no way she held no grudge.

So surely, in her true heart——

She enclosed the mana crystal in her hand.

Covered it so its light couldn't be seen.

She didn't care.

— So long as it remained imprisoned forever, those had been her final words.

As long as things stayed this way, she would never speak another word beyond that.

As long as it shone here, the pretty words remained pretty.

No matter how flimsy, they would be etched forever as honest, unfaltering words.

*That's fine*, she thought, and peeked through the gap in her palm once more.

She gazed at the light imprisoned within the mirror-world and stroked it.

That light would remain in Kreschenta's hand for eternity.

* * *

"Kreschenta, Krische is home."

"……Welcome back, Onee-sama."

She slid the mana crystal from her chest, tucking it inside her one-piece dress.

Then she sat up and looked toward the entrance.

Onee-sama was wearing her usual apron dress with a curved sword hung at the back of her waist, and Kreschenta tilted her head.

"What were you doing?"

"Scraggly said he wanted to spar, so Krische was keeping him company. Took Gururun for a walk while Krische was at it."

She unfastened the belt from her waist and set the curved sword on the shelf.

She toddled over, knelt on the bed, stroked Kreschenta's cheek, and kissed her slowly.

They stayed that way for a while, eyes closed.

When she opened them, her sister's purple was peering in.

A gaze that looked slightly troubled, as if wavering over something.

"Scraggly" was, as always, one of the silly pet names her sister had bestowed.

One of the few survivors among the people who knew what her sister desired.

He would probably be the long-lived type, like Felwors.

Bodies of flesh had what could be called a regeneration limit.

Past a certain span, blood and flesh lost their ability to regenerate normally, gradually declining in quality and accelerating in deterioration — regeneration could no longer keep up.

Aging was deterioration.

For those like Onee-sama and Kreschenta, who could achieve complete mana control over their physical bodies, preventing deterioration was simple — but ordinary people could not.

However, among ordinary people, there were those somewhat more adept at it.

Functional decline as aging progressed to a certain degree.

Most people around that time began to prioritize mana over flesh in their bodily control, stabilizing in an aged state — and such people tended to live quite long.

Of course, much depended on the body one was born with, but Aleha appeared reasonably robust physically, and his mana-based bodily control was of quite fine quality.

Since aging had already begun, an ordinary person like Aleha had his limits — but at least for the next few decades, his body seemed like it would hold.

— *If only he would hurry up and die.*

She gazed at her sister's eyes and concluded that he had probably been filling her head with unnecessary ideas.

If time would just wash everything away from around her sister quickly enough, then her sister would no longer have to waver.

One person was enough to be precious to her sister and to be held precious by her — and that was Kreschenta.

"……Kreschenta, um……there's something Krische wants to talk——"

"I'm hungry."

She stared at her sister, cutting off her words.

She extended both hands and hung them around her sister's neck, then reached out with mana to operate the mana crystal in the bathroom and start filling the bath.

Her sister stared at Kreschenta with that troubled look again, then smiled quietly.

"……Then, Krische will cook and we'll eat, and take a bath. ……After that, we'll talk. All right?"

When Kreschenta didn't answer but pressed close, *what a spoiled child*, her sister said, and picked Kreschenta up just like that.

The troubled smile looked very much like that servant's, somehow.

* * *

In the kitchen she made rakura pie, pumpkin soup, and braised lamb.

With just the two of them, they didn't need that much.

She was well-practiced, could make it with her eyes closed.

Standing side by side, the waiting time was longer.

She stirred the soup and gazed at the shelves.

Spices, dishes, cooking tools — their arrangement hadn't changed in a long time.

Originally, it had been optimized for her sister to cook alongside that servant, and after it became just the two of them, there had been room for improvement.

When the people changed, the division of tasks changed too.

It was only natural — and yet whenever her sister tidied up, she always returned things to this arrangement.

From knives to dishes, she polished everything until it gleamed, but never changed where anything went.

— Just as it was when that servant was here.

She didn't take risks the way that servant had.

She made fine adjustments to flavor and used new ingredients when they became available.

But she never said, *Let's try something we haven't done before.*

*'……I'm sorry. Today's experiment was a failure.'*

She remembered that face, smiling ruefully, and touched her lips to the ladle.

The soup was as delicious as always, unchanged from when that servant was here.

With decades of making the same dishes, failure simply didn't exist.

Her sister would deny it, but that servant had many failures.

She was a woman who could produce 'reasonably delicious food,' but took risks that seemed almost deliberate.

Making sauces or desserts from fruits that were obviously going to taste terrible.

She usually failed, wasting ingredients.

And yet watching her, her sister always looked so happy.

On those rare occasions when something worked, she celebrated as if it were the discovery of the century.

*'Kreschenta. This is not a failure — it is simply a challenge that did not go well. There is more to learn from failure than success in this world.'*

Pure parroting, delivered as if it were golden wisdom, finger raised and all.

Onee-sama truly was a fool.

Kreschenta didn't think it was entirely without merit, but it was just food.

One more delicious thing was all it added.

There were plenty of delicious things already; the stability was more than sufficient — so what was the point of reaching for more? She always watched those irrational attempts with exasperation.

She had learned most of the dishes and caught up, but reluctantly went along with such foolishness, got swept up in it.

This kitchen used to be quite chaotic, she thought.

After that servant was gone, it settled into stability, with no more failures — so it would have been more rational to rearrange the tools' placement.

And yet, even now, it remained as it was.

The pungent, hard-to-use spices still sat at the front of the shelf.

Dishes, knives, frying pans, and pots — their positions were all that servant's preference.

— Every time she entered this kitchen, every time she cooked here, she remembered.

The figure of that servant standing here would overlap, and the figure of her sister laughing happily would overlap, and Kreschenta's own figure, complaining but going along with it anyway.

Surely, her sister felt the same.

Repeating the same unchanging, failure-free dishes, yet floating an inexplicably happy smile.

Whether together or alone — that servant lived inside her sister's head.

"……Kreschenta?"

When she furrowed her brows in displeasure, her sister came close and took the ladle.

Then tasted the soup and tilted her head.

She must have thought there was a problem with the seasoning.

"Ehehe, it's delicious."

"……That's good."

She sighed and said so, and her sister pressed her lips to hers with a troubled look.

There was the faintly sweet taste of pumpkin soup.

A taste sickeningly unchanged from back then.

She would go on tasting this for eternity, no doubt.

She noticed then — every dish Kreschenta made had been learned from that servant, and no matter which she tasted, it carried that servant's flavor.

All throughout the estate, too — as people disappeared one by one, that presence slipped into the gaps.

She traced her lips with her fingertip and confirmed the sensation.

Even now, the feeling remained as if it were yesterday.

— *I know.*

The soundlessly formed words echoed in her mind, still unfading.

* * *

They finished their meal, soaked in the bath, changed, and went to the bedroom.

Her sister held Kreschenta on her lap and ran a comb through her hair.

The light of a crescent moon shone in through the window.

Yet the moon never truly changed its shape.

Sunlight from the far side was blocked by the earth, and so the moon merely gained shadows.

The moon was always whole, hanging in the sky.

Only shadows appeared upon it.

"……Kreschenta."

"I don't want to hear it."

Kreschenta had always been a perfect being.

Unchanged from birth, born already complete.

It was the world that was wrong, the world that was twisted.

The moon always hung in the sky, unchanging.

It only appeared to wane because those below could only perceive what they saw as it was.

To insects clinging to the earth, it surely did look like a crescent.

But Kreschenta was not waning.

Nor was her sister — it was everything else that was wrong.

That was the truth, the one certain thing.

And yet, if that were so.

She had changed the world as she wished, obtained everything the world had to offer, become a perfect being acknowledged by all — so why was she still thinking about that servant?

Why was she trapped in thoughts that went round and round?

"……I don't want to hear that kind of talk."

She had decided she didn't need to remember — it was supposed to be a settled triviality.

The only one who could stand beside her was her sister; everything else was accessories her sister happened to like.

They should have been nothing more, and must not be anything more.

They *had* to be nothing more.

— *Because if they weren't, then I……*

She thought this, and again, something unpleasant churned inside her chest.

Her sister pulled her close and pressed her cheek against Kreschenta's back, from behind.

"Kreschenta said that eternity exists here, but……probably, what Krische wants isn't eternity."

Wrapping her in both arms.

As though enclosing her entire body in a gentle cage.

"Just like Krische told Kushenalars-san, eternity is merely a process — what Krische really wants is what lies beyond it……"

And lovingly.

"Rather than a goal, it might be more like Krische's selfishness."

Her sister said it shyly.

"……Probably, in the end, Krische simply wants to see Bery again."

*Ah*, she sighed without a sound.

At the very least, Kreschenta knew that her sister's heart was always there.

"Krische just wants to say, *I'm home*, and hear, *Welcome back*. ……Krische just wants to be hugged the way she always was, and hear, *Good work today*."

Her sister was a child, a little more foolish than Kreschenta.

To meet that servant again required means beyond the ordinary, and to fully enjoy that required a world at peace — and naturally, that had simply become the word *eternity*.

"Krische doesn't understand difficult things like eternal love or any of that……but Krische thinks everyone probably saw right through Krische's silly ideas."

Her sister's ideal world. Her ideal paradise. A childlike utopia.

Her sister had wanted everything she desired, so Kreschenta had let her be.

She knew that her sister's desired eternal paradise could never be accepted, and that only a rare few, peculiar people would accept it.

She knew her sister would be hurt each time she was refused, and that eventually she would stop expecting anything from others — and she had thought that then, perhaps someday, her sister would think only of Kreschenta.

— *Without thinking about anything unnecessary.*

"……What Krische wants is for Krische. What other people want is for other people. When you think about it, it's a really simple matter. Krische is Krische, so Krische just wants to see Bery, and is thinking selfish things like wanting to stay together forever."

*It's all Krische's selfishness*, she said, smiling.

"And on top of that, Selene and everyone said they'd go along with Krische's foolishness."

So utterly happy.

"Krische doesn't know what the future holds. Maybe someday they'll leave Krische's side……but that would surely be because they were satisfied, not because they got tired of Krische."

Kreschenta's flimsy cage of words, like a hollow facade, crumbled easily.

She had known it would come to this immediately.

It felt like it had happened awfully fast, and yet not so fast at all.

"Even if that's not the case, it's fine."

Either way,

"——Krische will choose to see it that way, and interpret it however is most convenient."

Her sister would never forget that servant. She would never let Kreschenta forget, either.

She clenched her fists and lowered her eyes.

Not just her sister — this room too, the kitchen too, the mana crystal hanging from her neck — and in her memories, that servant's scent had soaked into everything.

"……You sound just like Argan-sama, Onee-sama."

She said it, utterly weary.

"Always spouting words that are pleasant to hear. ……That woman was not as admirable a person as you seem to think."

"Whether she was admirable or not is a trivial matter. Krische loves Anne dearly even though she's not admirable at all."

*Krische isn't admirable either*, she smiled.

"Krische is fine no matter how things turn out. Even if, as Kreschenta says, they someday get fed up with Krische……even so, Krische wants to see Bery and the others."

She stroked Kreschenta's cheek, and the words came out as if wrung from her.

With a sincerity that sent a chill down the spine, unadorned.

She spoke exactly as she wished.

"That's Krische's conclusion. ……So——"

"No."

With a single word she cut off her sister's sentence and grasped her arm.

"Why not?"

"……Unlike you, Onee-sama, I have no interest in these people. I'm far happier and more at ease now, with just the two of us."

"Liar. Why do you always pretend to be a bad child, Kreschenta? You feel the same about Bery and the others——"

"I've had quite enough. Stop deciding things on your own."

She shook free, stood, turned to face her, and glared straight at her, sharpening her tone.

"How many times must I say I hate them before you understand, Onee-sama? I only showed them consideration because you held them dear."

The more she thought about it, the more infuriating it became.

Revulsion crawled through her chest, and there was nothing she could do about it.

Each time she recalled it, she remembered the feel of *that scrap of cloth*.

"You really are living in a fantasy, aren't you. Bery, Bery, Bery, like a broken record……I'm truly sick of it."

"……Kreschenta."

"My patience is truly at its limit. ……If you say the name Bery one more time, I will be genuinely angry."

She was astonished at how unstable her own emotions were.

Her body was shaking involuntarily, her breathing shallow, something with nowhere to go swirling inside her.

"If Kreschenta truly says from the bottom of her heart that she wants to be alone with Krische, that she hates Bery and the others and doesn't want to see them, then Krische will consider it."

Her sister looked straight back at her and continued.

"But that's impossible, so even if you get angry, Krische won't stop."

"And how would you know that, Onee-sama?"

"Because Krische is Kreschenta's onee-san. Krische doesn't understand other people's feelings at all, but……"

As always, she smiled gently.

"Krische thinks she understands Kreschenta's feelings better than anyone."

Seeing that, she froze.

"……What could you possibly understand, Onee-sama?"

She squeezed her hands until blood seeped.

— *There was no way her sister could understand.*

She was the only one who had seen.

The things her sister had refused to look at — she alone had watched, right beside her, all that time.

Day and night, repeating every day.

That was why — it had to be that way — otherwise, she would never have done *such a thing*.

"Somehow, this feels just like when Selene and Bery used to bicker."

Her sister said, as if remembering, and stood.

"……More importantly, you said Bery just now, Kreschenta."

She walked over and opened the balcony doors.

"You're always pretending to be angry, always putting on a front. ……If you truly feel that way, why don't you try being genuinely angry for once?"

With the moonlight at her back, her purple eyes gleamed.

— Her sister was serious.

From head to toe, it was as though she were freezing solid.

She swept her right hand — the mana around them erupted in transformation.

Even for her sister, this had gone too far.

"Saying such things……don't blame me if you come to regret it, Onee-sama. Are you aware of the situation? Right now, I can command all the mana in the world."

"Krische knows."

Her sister replied, and extended a thread of mana toward the mana crystal sitting on the desk behind her — and shattered it.

Then, with the tiny amount of mana she gained, she donned blue curves of light and floated into the air.

"But Krische is your onee-san."

"……Do you honestly think I am inferior to you, Onee-sama?"

"Kreschenta is just as clever as Krische. ……But Krische still wins."

*Because Kreschenta isn't serious*, she said.

Those purple eyes narrowed behind silver lashes.

"Do you think I cannot kill you, Onee-sama?"

And Kreschenta likewise narrowed her gold-rimmed crimson eyes.

"I need only create a new body and seal your soul inside it so you cannot defy me."

Cold and dark, that color gleamed.

"……I never imagined you could be this much of a fool, Onee-sama."

Her sister was trying to create distance.

All the mana from Zenith was in Kreschenta's hands.

She couldn't believe her sister didn't understand the disadvantage of giving up this range — the only one where she held superiority.

— She understood, and was giving Kreschenta the advantage anyway.

It was like pouring oil on fire.

For this reason, debate continues to this day regarding her final disappearance alongside her elder sister, Alberinea, in the event known as 'the Great Rite of Heavenly Change'――whether it was a tragedy born of a falling-out, or something that had been planned from the start.

Regardless, the fact that she remained a great queen in the hearts of the kingdom's people, despite all those preparations, was beyond doubt.

At the milestone of the hundredth year of the Unified Calendar, the parliament decided to disclose the Queen's absence, and the people――who had vaguely understood it already――were plunged into grief.

Opinion split on whether a new monarch should be installed, and the nation was divided, reaching a stalemate.

The head of the Academy of Magical Studies, Elgeinst Ramiru, who had been secretly advancing the seizure of the magical weapons created by Alberinea, cooperated with part of the military's central command.

The swift occupation of the kingdom's core and the capture of many parliamentarians brought the one hundred and twenty-three years of continental stability crashing down.

Jaleia Gashea, Baumje Ira, Pishuune, and others――the crystallizations of her wisdom had by then become a force more formidable than the military itself.

And magic inscribed upon the open air――having discovered the existence of magic by studying Alberinea's mana crystals, they used the abundant mana saturating the atmosphere and these weapons to rapidly suppress surrounding resistance forces, absorb them, and from the Kingdom of Alberan forge anew the Mage Empire of Kleinmeil.

In the Old Tongue, its name meant moonlight's legacy.

True to the name, they were those who had inherited the wisdom left behind by the princess of the waning moon.

The magical civilization spanning a thousand years began from that point, and――

"Okay, okay, enough. I'm stuffed, that's plenty. I've heard Mee-sama's gracious lecture at least thirty times now."

In what had once been the imperial capital of Kleinmeil and the royal capital of Alberan.

A snowy landscape――on the bustling streets of Albernaria, a woman spoke with an utterly fed-up air.

She wore her long black hair loosely gathered behind her, and an apron dress.

And she had cat-like black ears and a long tail.

She was of the species known as cat-people among beastkin.

Their origins were said to lie in human experimentation during the Kleinmeil era――attempts to crossbreed humans with magical beasts――and their superior physical abilities had led to them being treated as combat slaves for a time.

Even hundreds of years after Kleinmeil's collapse, there were still those who directed prejudice toward people like her, but since they also had high aptitude for mana and excelled in its manipulation, such discrimination was relatively mild in Albernaria, where magical research still flourished.

"Don't abbreviate it to 'Mee-sama'! How many times do I have to tell you it's 'Meedeliarieze-sama,' Karshe!?"

Shouting with her adorable face flushed crimson was a golden-haired girl in fine black and gold robes.

Upon her modest chest hung a bronze pendant in the shape of a crescent moon.

It was the emblem marking her as a student of the Albernaria Academy of Magic, located beside the World Tree.

With an exaggerated show of covering her ears at the outburst, her attendant Karshe sighed.

"If we're being precise, my name is Karsheria too, you know……Carrying on like that, you'll be scolded by your father again for being unladylike. And if you keep shouting right next to my ears like that, they might actually go bad."

"……Shall I tear those eyesores off for you?"

"Wow, cat discrimination. How despicable."

"Which of us is despicable here!?"

*There, there, don't get angry*, Karshe patted the girl's head and laughed.

"Oh, Bery, sea-devil dumplings! Sea-devil dumplings!"

"Fufu, yes. But it's our little secret from the young ladies, eating snacks before we've finished our errands."

Frolicking through the snow like a rabbit――elegant silver hair.

A girl swaddled in a hand-knitted white hat and scarf wound around her cloak, and a red-haired attendant likewise wearing a black cloak――seeing them, Karshe looked toward the stall they were heading for.

The eight-legged sea-devil――an eerie-looking creature, to be sure, but quite tasty.

Sea-devil dumplings were pieces of it wrapped in batter and cooked round.

They were a fairly common food in the south where Mee's family was from, but a rarity this far inland, so distant from the sea.

"Come on, Mee-sama, sea-devil dumplings! How about some before the museum?"

"Look……fine. ……Besides, you're the one who asked in the first place, aren't you? 'What's so fun about a museum,' you said."

"……Honestly, it is boring for me."

"……Hah. It's sad, being uneducated. Your tail and ears must be siphoning your brains, Karshe."

"Must you always say things like that. I'm one thing, but other cats would be furious."

Confirming that no cats were nearby, Karshe fell into line behind the girl and attendant, and spoke.

Even without the extra provocation, Mee already had a tendency toward one or two unfortunate remarks, and when angry she lost sight of her surroundings entirely, casually saying things on the edge――Karshe was always having to mind the people around them.

Growing up in a land full of cats, Mee was a young lady who possessed no such lofty values as 'prejudice'――but possessing none at all meant she was fundamentally without restraint.

"It's your fault for always making fun of me, Karshe. I'm going to become an Alberinea, so I need to be well-versed in Alberan's history."

At those words, the girl ahead glanced back.

Large purple eyes――she was a girl of breathtaking beauty.

Long silver lashes and clearly defined double eyelids.

A well-shaped nose and cherry-blossom lips.

Every feature seemed crafted to embody beauty, arranged upon her young face without a single flaw.

Long silver hair held back by a pale-pink flower ornament, flowing lightly――even that was fantastical.

Despite standing right before them, she had an otherworldly presence, as if floating apart from her surroundings.

The attendant beside her seemed to have noticed too.

She turned, red hair swaying, and she too was beautiful.

An indescribable air of maturity about her, yet her features were refined, and large, pale-brown eyes lent her a touch of youth.

Like the girl, she appeared small and slender, yet through the gap in her cloak, her figure traced beautiful curves beneath an apron dress.

A sudden sense of unease struck Karshe, and she scanned their surroundings.

Walking along a thoroughfare like this, these two should have been conspicuous.

Karshe had been trained since childhood as this young lady's bodyguard, and she should have been alert to the atmosphere created when someone drew that kind of attention.

Yet no such atmosphere existed here, and the pair had blended perfectly, ordinarily, into the scenery.

Karshe had headed for this stall after hearing their words.

But it was only now, when they turned around, that she felt she had first recognized their existence――an indescribable sensation.

She had heard that mages of extremely high rank could render their very presence invisible, but there was no sign that the two before her were employing any such magic.

"……What?"

Unlike Karshe, who felt a faint prickle of caution, her mistress stared back flatly, returning the girl's gaze with a glare.

She was currently in the midst of a thoroughly irritable mood.

The silver-haired girl stared intently at Mee, then at Karshe beside her, and let out a small "Oh," before openly whispering to the attendant beside her.

Karshe's keen hearing picked out the words *Mia* and *Kalua*, but she could not fathom what they meant and tilted her head.

But the attendant seemed to understand, showing a flicker of surprise before glancing sidelong at them.

"……Hey. I asked if you needed something."

Mee addressed them again, displeased, and the attendant bowed her head with an "I'm sorry."

"You bore a striking resemblance to some people we know……so we couldn't help but look. If we caused any offense, I apologize."

"Ah, no, our end too……my young lady is in a bit of a mood at the moment."

"……Karshe, shut up."

*Resembling acquaintances*――perhaps what she had heard were names.

That would make sense, Karshe thought, though a subtle undercurrent of unease lingered.

Ordinarily, the correct thing would be to leave this place promptly.

Karshe prided herself on the reliability of her instincts, and letting her mistress associate with someone who triggered that unease was not ideal.

She did not think the girls were dangerous, but it would be better to avoid involvement. She began thinking up an excuse to leave, when――

"Well, never mind. You――are you a new student at the magic academy?"

"……? You mean Krische?"

"Yes. Walking around here with an attendant. You must be from somewhere notable?"

――her mistress, Mee, had already started talking.

An attendant meant she was from a family of some standing, and her demeanor suggested she was younger than Mee.

The deduction likely stemmed from that. Mee was prone to assumptions.

Karshe studied the girl.

The left breast of her black cloak.

Beneath the drooping scarf, there was a faint glimpse of a crest rendered in dull, monochrome grey: *the emblem of thunder and hawk*.

It seemed somehow familiar, but Karshe searched her memory and was relieved to confirm it did not belong to any prominent house in this area.

At the very least, no particularly serious trouble seemed likely.

"I'm Meedeliarieze Vahnashtel. A student in the academy's advanced division. What's your name?"

"Um……"

The girl looked at her attendant with a troubled expression, and in response the red-haired attendant bowed and said, "Pleased to meet you."

"My apologies for the late introduction. This is Krische Argan-sama. I am called Bery. A pleasure, Vahnashtel-sama."

"Meedeliarieze is fine, Bery. This attendant of mine is Karsheria. Nice to meet you, Krische."

"Oh, um……yes……"

Still looking troubled, the girl――Krische――took Mee's offered right hand.

Mee smiled, eyes narrowing.

"I see……Krische. You were given a splendid name."

"Splendid……"

"Didn't you know? The first Alberinea's name was Krische Christand. I'm sure it was given to you in hopes you'd become a great mage like Alberinea."

The girl's expression grew even more troubled as she glanced at her attendant.

The attendant, too, shifted her gaze with a slightly troubled air――and then, for an instant, her eyes met Karshe's.

Karshe sensed their discomfort and was about to speak up, but――

"And Krische Argan with a Bery, no less……fufu, I knew it. You two were heading to the museum too, weren't you?"

"Huh?"

"We were just about to go as well. The museum has all sorts of relics associated with Alberinea. If you'd like, shall I show you around?"

Mee, apparently eager to play the senior, puffed out her flat chest and declared this with gusto.

"M-Mee-sama, listen……not everyone is like you, you know……"

"What? This may be nothing to you, but for those who study magic, Alberinea is an incredibly important figure. This girl is coming to the academy, so of course she knows about Alberinea properly――unlike you. Right, Krische?"

"Um, yes, more or less……"

Once more Krische glanced at the red-haired attendant.

Perhaps she was the shy type.

"Meeting here must be some kind of fate. Fufu, yes, I'll treat you to sea-devil dumplings too."

But whatever she made of their manner, Karshe's mistress, as always, carried on regardless, puffing out her chest――what little there was――and declaring just that.

* * *

The magical civilization had met its end with the collapse of Kleinmeil.

Hatred of the mages who had ruled the world until then――the hundred years that followed were an age of mage-hunting.

Countless masterworks of magical technology, invaluable documents and materials, were burned and lost, and the World Tree, that symbol of the mages, was reportedly set ablaze time and again.

But each time, the World Tree would envelop itself in water and extinguish the fire, and when damaged, it would sway and regenerate, until eventually even those who had tried to do something about it came to accept its form as something sacred.

And after a hundred years, even hatred fades.

*It is not the World Tree nor magic itself that is evil, but the hearts of those who wield it.*

The monks of Albyagel, the mountain where the ancient dragon once dwelt, traveled the world preaching this, and alongside the World Tree, the existence of mages too was gradually accepted once more――and as time flowed on, the world regained stability.

Mages were once again permitted to conduct various research, ostensibly in the name of peaceful advancement.

Though joining a magic academy subjected them to significant restrictions on independent research without approval, and they were occasionally troubled by inspections from the Peace Church, they managed to obtain a reasonable degree of freedom.

Mages possessed tremendous power beyond that of ordinary people.

The true reason was likely fear that harsh repression and persecution would drive them to rebellion.

Many mages understood full well that the magic academies were cages designed to control them, yet given the preceding events, the system was accepted by most, and so it endured to this day.

In the final wars of Kleinmeil, millions had died, and entire cities had been erased along with their inhabitants without a trace.

Among the mages, particularly the long-lived great sorcerers, there remained living witnesses to those decades of warfare, and their appeals to their fellow mages had been instrumental.

――*The purpose toward which our wisdom must be directed is not destruction, but great and peaceful advancement.*

The Albernaria Academy of Magic was one product of that movement――a prestigious institution among the prestigious.

Unlike the academies established across the land merely to manage those with magical aptitude, its entrance was a narrow gate.

Only talented magic apprentices who had devoted themselves to the utmost effort from a young age and passed demanding examinations were permitted to set foot inside, and a mage who graduated from here was accorded special respect merely for being an Albernaria alumnus.

Naturally, such graduates were in high demand. Meedeliarieze, daughter of the great magical house of Vahnashtel, was one of those who studied there.

"Well? How about that?"

*There she goes again*, Karshe thought, watching with exasperation.

The two did not seem especially well-informed, and while eating sea-devil dumplings and delivering one of her extended lectures, Mee conjured a puppy from gathered water elementals atop her finger and made it walk through the air.

The puppy trembled slightly and moved somewhat stiffly, but apparently, manipulating water into a single mass and moving it like a living creature was surprisingly difficult――it was one of the promotion examinations in the advanced division.

Her mentor had praised her, saying her skill at such a young age was impressive, and the elation of that was apparently boundless.

She had been showing it off everywhere.

The girl who had been cat-tongue-blowing on her sea-devil dumplings to cool them finished eating and glanced beside her as if wanting to say something, her expression slightly troubled. The attendant caught the look and, with a wry smile, applauded.

Mee, now fully in senior-student mode, had not noticed, but Karshe had already realized that these two were far from ordinary mages.

Cats used their tails to sense mana with extraordinarily high precision.

Physical enhancement――the construction of virtual musculature from mana.

What they wore even now was a primitive form of magic, the kind mages were made to practice from a young age to acclimate to internal mana manipulation.

Mages of the pre-Unified Calendar era――the Age of Heroes――who routinely wielded swords were said to have used it daily, but now only those like Karshe whose primary duty was personal protection, or mages intended for combat, seriously trained in it.

What was primarily expected of mages was spell-casting, and ordinary mages discarded physical enhancement to focus on their magic. It was an extreme feat to inscribe formulae in the air and manipulate magic while simultaneously maintaining physical enhancement.

Karshe herself had abandoned spell-casting to master it. That was the nature of technique.

To gain something, one inevitably had to give something up.

But viewed from that perspective, these two were decidedly abnormal.

They were clearly accustomed to physical enhancement.

A stillness so profound that even Karshe, who had trained in physical enhancement well beyond an ordinary level and excelled in mana perception, had not immediately noticed.

That stillness was proof they wore virtual musculature on a daily basis; the faintest ripple that should accompany every movement simply did not exist.

The masterful level of that proficiency was beyond extraordinary, and the more she considered it, the more uneasy she felt.

Karshe had already concluded that these two could not possibly be a mere new student and her attendant.

Her eyes met the attendant's.

With those pale-brown eyes, she regarded Karshe, then seemed to consider something before standing and turning her attention back to Mee.

"Very impressive. But if you add an inscription here, it should stabilize a bit more."

"Wh……?"

She directed a fingertip toward the floating puppy and traced a blue mana line.

Fluid――the ripple of mana was minimal.

When the thread of mana touched the water puppy, it turned at a sharp angle and inscribed a formula of dazzling elegance.

The trembling puppy froze perfectly still, and Mee's eyes went wide.

"Think of it as a kind of faucet. If it's difficult to stabilize the mana, then building a mechanism that restricts output to a fixed level will make things much easier. This way, no matter how much you pour in, the mana output remains steady."

"Y-yes……"

"……Of course, it does introduce a small amount of waste, but stabilization should take priority first. You have quite a talent for inscribing formulae, Meedeliarieze-sama, and as you grow accustomed to it, the waste will diminish and efficiency will improve."

Mee stared at the water puppy in astonishment, then flushed to her cheeks.

Like lecturing a dragon on history.

She had apparently realized it at last.

"U-um……could you possibly be a mentor, or something of that sort……?"

"Well……something along those lines, I suppose. Please forgive my presumption."

"No! I-I've learned a great deal!"

Bery said with a wry smile and a bow, and Mee hurried to bow in return.

Intervening in another's formula and rewriting it.

The ease with which she had done that was clearly, even to the untrained eye, an advanced feat.

Matching the mana's color, preventing interference between mana fields, and without disturbing or collapsing the deployed formula, inscribing and integrating a new one.

Impossible without understanding and grasping every aspect of the active formula――yet she had done it effortlessly after a single glance.

Even among mentors officially certified to instruct in magic, this was the upper echelon.

On top of that, her physical enhancement showed not a single tremor.

Despite her appearance, the attendant's bearing radiated a settled composure.

If told that she was, for instance, some great mage of that caliber, one could indeed nod in agreement.

Among high-ranking mages――those classified as adepts or great sorcerers――there were those who could be called virtually ageless.

Most were eccentrics, and some among them might well serve as tutors and attendants to young ladies of great houses.

That this was the case with her――Karshe could more or less accept that reasoning, but the problem was the girl beside her.

Watching this, the girl had worn a faintly satisfied smile.

There was no surprise in it――had she, too, noticed Mee's issue and been thinking the same thing as her attendant?

Such an imagination presented itself.

Compared to this attendant, the girl looked her apparent age――no, her manner seemed even younger than her appearance.

She had seemed as though she wanted to say something, yet she had watched her attendant's actions as a matter of course, looking satisfied.

Mee was remarkably talented for her seventeen years, a head above her peers.

Even within the Albernaria Academy of Magic, that gathering of elites, she ranked among the top achievers.

That this girl, clearly younger, possessed abilities far exceeding hers was abnormal.

"Krische, this person teaches you every day?"

"Teaches……yes. Bery is Krische's teacher. Krische learns all sorts of things."

Krische said with a touch of pride, and Bery offered a wry smile.

A young genius and a high-ranking mage serving as her attendant――put that way, it was not impossible to see them as such.

There was something that clicked.

But the attendant's earlier manner when their eyes met, and this instruction just now.

It felt as though she had been told, *"That's what's going on, so please accept it"*――and that nagged oddly.

That said, if asked *"Then what are they?"*, Karshe could offer no answer; there was nothing beyond a vague *nagging*.

Krische stood up cheerfully and linked arms with her attendant.

The sight was pure child――in a way, it exuded an indescribable sort of prodigy-ness, Karshe mused, lost in thought.

"I'm envious……she'll be the talk of the intermediate division. Having a private mentor and all."

Mee sighed as she watched Krische, then glanced at Karshe.

"……Compared to that."

"That's a bit hurtful. My specialty is martial arts and physical enhancement, and I've told you I'll teach you as much as you want in that regard."

Karshe's ears and tail twitched as she shot back.

"……Well, I am, as you say, very ordinary and uneducated, so there are limits when one's student is so staggeringly uncoordinated, so utterly hopeless at anything physical, so――"

"A-are you saying that's me……?"

"Oh no, perish the thought. Is something ringing a bell, Mee-sama?"

"Grr……"

*Now, now*, Bery tried to smooth things over with a troubled look. The girl, meanwhile, held Bery's arm and stared steadily at Karshe and Mee.

Then those long silver lashes swayed, and her cheeks softened into the most delighted of smiles.

――Rare, jewel-like purple.

Those eyes and that smile, seen for the first time, felt strangely, inexplicably nostalgic.

Kleinmeil had inherited much of Alberan's legacy.

Naturally, a great deal was burned in the collapse, and she had heard that a considerable number of historical documents and materials were lost.

Still, where there were those who destroyed, there were also those who preserved.

Much had survived, passed through many hands, and the Albernaria Academy of Magic had put great effort into collecting and preserving these remnants of the past.

The great advancement of Kleinmeil was rooted in Alberan.

Investigating that past, they believed, would lead to future progress.

Due to the mage hunts, magical technology had regressed significantly from its zenith.

There were many cases where finding the wheel was faster than reinventing it.

The collection of materials for the investigation of the past was conducted alongside ongoing research, and the items displayed in this museum were a portion of that――things not currently under study, or things that held only historical value unrelated to the magic of the time.

Of no practical use, as Karshe saw it, and entirely uninteresting to her, though apparently some people came from the provinces to see them.

Provincial scholars or artists, perhaps.

Many were earnestly scribbling in notebooks with the fashionable lead pencils, seriously examining the exhibits.

While even the uninterested Karshe had mustered some effort to enjoy the first visit, by the seventh time Mee had dragged her along, she was thoroughly fed up.

The two beside her were far more interesting than the exhibits, and she spent most of her time watching them.

"This may be rude to say, but……you do seem to enjoy this, surprisingly."

"……?"

Upon entering, the attendant had taken the girl's cloak and folded it neatly along with her own.

She had also taken the hat, leaving the girl in a white scarf, blouse, and black skirt.

Not ostentatious but refined, the picture of a proper young lady through and through.

When Karshe asked, the girl swayed her silver hair and tilted her head slightly, tracing her lips with her index finger.

A gesture full of allure――perhaps a habit when thinking.

The attendant was the same; this pair's small mannerisms were strikingly alike.

Though they had seemed troubled by Mee's proposal, once inside they appeared to be enjoying themselves more than expected.

With their arms still linked, they seemed cheerful about something.

Krische did not appear indifferent to Mee's tiresome commentary either, and she was apparently quite knowledgeable about the local history.

There were few people who could keep up with Mee's Alberan trivia.

But it was the attendant, Bery, who responded to Mee's remarks.

Krische listened with evident enjoyment while following her around.

"Like it……ehehe, perhaps Krische does. It's fun."

"Someone your age taking an interest in this sort of history――Mee-sama is about the only one."

"Hmm……well, Krische is a child, after all."

She seemed to consider this for a moment and then nodded with an oddly confident air.

*I didn't say that much*, Karshe thought with a wry smile.

"'A child,' really. Most of the people who like looking at this sort of thing are getting on in years, so I was just a little surprised. Please don't mind me."

Behind magically reinforced glass――swords and spears from the era, art objects, letters from Queen Kreschenta, and more. Among mages, it was mainly the older generation who enjoyed these sorts of things.

Younger ones wanted to achieve results and tended to focus on the more refined and numerous artifacts from the Kleinmeil era.

Pure historical research was something undertaken by those who had already established their position, or scholars pursuing it as a half-hobby.

For instance, there were plenty of young people interested in 'the mana crystals inscribed by the once-in-an-age genius, Alberinea,' but not many young people interested in period documents like these.

Unless investigating them might birth new magical theory, human beings were pragmatic creatures.

It was only natural that few would undertake research that demanded enormous effort with little return.

That the attendant Bery, who was conservatively a mentor-class mage and clearly not what she appeared, would be interested in such things was at least comprehensible.

With mentor-level ability, she would be in high demand wherever she went. That she chose to serve as a mere attendant made her an eccentric, so it would hardly be strange for her to have eccentric interests.

Still, for this girl to be so knowledgeable about this period of history was somewhat unexpected.

Perhaps the tutor's interests had rubbed off directly.

Walking deeper, they arrived at a section where military banners were displayed.

One caught Karshe's eye.

The Black Flag Special Service, said to have been led by Alberinea――the black flag with its crescent skull, and beside it, the emblem of thunder and hawk.

The design was more elegant and vivid, but the thunder and hawk depicted seemed quite similar to those on the girl's cloak.

――*So that's where I'd seen it before.*

Karshe nodded and looked at the cloak folded in the attendant's arms.

The crest was naturally not visible now.

Given that Bery's cloak bore no such markings, it was likely the Argan family crest.

"This is the Christand house military banner. And this is the Black Flag Special Service――as you know, the banner of Alberinea's personal troops."

"For something so old……it's remarkably well preserved."

With Bery offering reactions to each explanation, Mee was thoroughly enjoying herself.

Krische, too, let out a small "Oh," and appeared impressed.

Karshe heard Krische whisper to Bery, as if confiding――*Vulture* and *Nyan-nyan*.

What could *Vulture* and *Nyan-nyan* possibly mean? Karshe tilted her head, but Bery seemed to understand.

Was *Vulture* a misidentification of the hawk? But what did *Nyan-nyan* refer to?

The mystery only deepened.

Perhaps she was looking at something entirely different from what Karshe was seeing.

"Yes. A merchant apparently kept these privately as collector's items. They seem to have been magically preserved as well, which is probably why they survived in such fine condition."

"I see……the embroidery on military banners is beautiful, and if one thinks of them as art, one could certainly appreciate them that way."

The attendant made no mention of the girl's family crest.

Mee either had not noticed or had noticed and dismissed it as unremarkable, saying nothing.

This banner had been in use over thirteen hundred years ago.

Both thunder and hawk seemed fitting motifs for a martial house, and she had seen similar combinations in other crests.

Perhaps it was simply normal for similar designs to emerge.

This area contained many items closely associated with the final period of Alberan――Queen Kreschenta, her confidant Alberinea, and the Christand household――and the two had slowed their pace since entering.

Alberan――the ancient Age of Heroes.

The way they gazed at these relics seemed strangely like nostalgia, subtly different from Mee and the other visitors.

"Is something the matter?"

"N-no……"

At Mee's voice the attendant shook her head with a slight start, and her cheeks colored faintly pink.

Displayed there was a painting――a red-haired girl in an apron dress, and a silver-haired girl in a dazzling gown kissing her.

And from outside the frame, a great dragon peeked in.

"This is Filipéne's *The Divine Being and the Maidens*. It's said that Alberinea challenged an ancient dragon and forged a pact with it in order to save her beloved attendant……ah, but of course you would know that."

"Y-yes, of course……it's a beautiful painting."

"The theme of the Dragon's Pactsworn is used alongside the Hawk Princess as a popular subject for paintings of Alberinea, but Filipéne left an especially large body of work. Actually, his grandfather was said to have been a member of the Black Flag Special Service……this painting is also said to have been based on a picture his grandfather made as a hobby in his later years."

Mee raised a finger authoritatively.

"The kiss between the two caused quite a stir at the time, and this was the work that launched Filipéne's career as a painter……but the original was unfortunately lost to fire, and what's displayed here is a forgery made while the original still existed. It's still valuable and, I think, an exceptionally fine piece."

Karshe gazed at the painting and then at the person beside her.

Red-haired attendant.

Beautiful silver-haired princess.

Purple eyes――of course, it was merely a painting, and the faces did not match, but it was a curious coincidence.

"I-indeed, it's a very fine painting……"

The Alberinea depicted in paintings always had silver hair and purple eyes, regardless of variations in attire.

Both were rare, yet numerous records described them as her distinctive features.

Moreover, if Karshe's memory was correct, the red-haired attendant's name was indeed Bery.

She was fairly sure Mee had said as much at some point, and Mee's reaction upon hearing their names seemed to confirm it.

*So what of it*――but still, something nagged.

Krische gazed at the painting and then at the attendant, whose cheeks had taken on a pleased pink tinge.

She clung to her arm and would not let go, a closeness that defied description.

If the two were to kiss right before her eyes, Karshe felt she could only think, *I see.*

Their relationship eerily mirrored the one in the tale.

Neither Alberinea, nor her adopted sister Selene, nor the Queen Kreschenta had left behind any bloodline――there were no common tales of romance about them, and they had apparently sequestered themselves in their manor with their attendants, living happily together. The prevailing view was that they had been in *that sort of relationship*.

It was said that Alberinea had ultimately died in a quarrel with Queen Kreschenta, or in some sort of experiment, but according to one theory they had transcended humanity and become divine, and dubious accounts claiming encounters with them were scattered across the world.

Perhaps these two mysterious girls really were――

"……No, no."

――*I'm not Mee, for goodness' sake.*

This was an uncharacteristic flight of fancy, and Karshe was exasperated with herself.

Even if those two still existed, there was no way they would be strolling through the city streets.

Yet the mysterious air around them kept steering Karshe's thoughts in that direction.

Considered from the other side, her only grounds for denial lay solely in *that*――and looking around, she confirmed once more that nobody was paying attention to these beautiful girls.

More than that, it felt as though Karshe and Mee, too, had dissolved into the atmosphere.

Karshe did not doubt her own good looks, and Mee, childish personality aside, was easy on the eyes.

Ordinarily, this group of four should have drawn more gazes――yet she felt fewer eyes on them than when she and Mee walked the streets alone.

She sensed no mana signatures of any perception-inhibiting kind.

The atmosphere, the feeling of being there, was not unpleasant, and if she stopped paying attention, it would cease to matter entirely.

*Just don't think about it*, she told herself, yet as she followed the three and watched their backs, the itch returned.

A vaguely restless feeling.

Even if she asked, whether she was right or wrong, she would not get the answer she wanted.

And doing so felt as though it would shatter this atmosphere. Seeing how much the two were enjoying themselves, probing further somehow felt wrong.

A museum she had grown sick of seeing――yet walking through it with them felt oddly enjoyable, and she shook off the restless feeling, deciding it was not bad to leave things a little mysterious.

"――Krische, it's *Jaleia Gashea*, not *jaragasha*. Don't call the crystallization of Alberinea's wisdom by a silly abbreviation."

"……It's jaragasha."

"I-I'm terribly sorry. Krische-sama has been fond of that name since long ago……"

"It's not that Krische is *fond* of it……ugh."

Watching Krische eye Mee with dissatisfaction, Karshe turned to the exhibit.

Two enormous swords mounted on its arms. Displayed was an eight-shaku iron war-golem――Jaleia Gashea.

A Kleinmeil-era reproduction――not a late-period copy, but apparently an original built during the Alberan era.

The command-tower head core was not on display, but the cores at each joint had been removed and were displayed for viewing.

Manufacturing combat-use mechanical dolls was currently punishable by summary execution――strictly forbidden. However, mechanical dolls for menial tasks and prosthetic limbs were, under certain exceptions, permitted.

Still, even magic academy students would not normally see an original Jaleia Gashea unless they specialized in the field. Having one on display was a rare event.

This was Mee's true objective, and she could not conceal her excitement.

"The inscriptions are truly artistic……how on earth did Alberinea mass-produce something like this?"

Exquisite formula inscriptions, evident even to the untrained eye.

Not something even a skilled magical engineer could easily replicate.

The death-dealing machines of the battlefield, said to possess power on an entirely different level from the imitations Kleinmeil had produced――Kleinmeil had monopolized the rumored three thousand originals, and that had made their dominance unshakeable.

In the final days, even when some mages, disgusted by Kleinmeil's excesses, lent their aid to the coalition forces, the combat strength and mobility of the originals was so extraordinary that――

"They were absolutely not to be fought. Masters of the battlefield. ……That makes their end rather amusing."

"Their end……?"

"Oddly enough, these Jaleia Gashea had a strange weakness――they would not raise their swords against red-haired women."

A red-haired female soldier――the sole survivor of a unit annihilated by a Jaleia Gashea.

She testified that it had felt as if the mindless killing machines had deliberately spared her.

By the final stages of the war, many women had joined the coalition as soldiers, and similar cases arose. It was a single strategist who realized this was no coincidence.

A former historian, he happened to know several tales of Alberan and Alberinea.

"The Dragon's Pactsworn is a famous motif, inspiring paintings and more……that Alberinea deeply loved her red-haired attendant is something most students of history know. He investigated whether that might hold the key to defeating the Jaleia Gashea, and having verified it, he formed a special unit composed entirely of red-haired women――the 'Red Goddesses.' They successfully captured one, and that became the turning point that allowed the coalition to reverse its losing position."

Mee nodded with satisfaction as she poured forth her knowledge, and Bery's mouth fell open.

Then, cheeks flushing again, she turned a bewildered look toward Krische.

Krische's face was not visible from this angle, but she clutched Bery's arm tightly, seemingly embarrassed.

"……Opinions on Alberinea are many and varied, but I believe she was, in the end, someone of unparalleled power who was tossed about by fate. This is the proof……the love hidden within these killing machines was what ended that war across a thousand years. That is no exaggeration."

"I-I see……"

White skin flushed crimson; her voice wavered slightly upward.

Karshe watched this 'red-haired attendant named Bery' and felt the restless feeling she had tried to shake off swelling once more, and could not help how she felt.

"It is also true that these machines built centuries of peace and stability……after all, it is human hands that create and wield tools. Looking at this, I too am moved to reflection, living in such a peaceful age."

Mee spoke with deep feeling and nodded to herself.

The attendant, too, who had been blushing, quietly nodded.

"Yes, I quite agree. ……Alberinea surely created these to protect a modest, everyday life."

――*At least, that is what I believe.*

She continued thus, and Karshe watched as she lovingly stroked the girl's silver hair.

* * *

When they emerged from the museum the sun had already begun to tilt, and the chill deepened.

The two expressed their thanks to Mee and Karshe for an enjoyable time, and bade them farewell.

*Let's meet again soon*, Mee called out, and the two offered troubled, wry smiles――they apparently had other companions waiting, as several women stood a short distance away.

A girl with elegant golden hair raised her eyebrows and scolded them for where they had been and what they had been doing, and a girl with peach-colored hair did the same.

Behind them, two attendants looked on with troubled, amused smiles.

Recalling that the pair had mentioned something about shopping, Karshe noticed what was behind them――a massive magical beast yawning as it pulled a cart, and her body went rigid.

Piled on the cart bed was an enormous quantity of goods.

No matter how you looked at it, it was a conspicuous carriage with conspicuous women.

Yet they blended into the air as naturally as could be, and Mee, standing right beside Karshe and looking at the same thing, showed not even a flicker of surprise at the magical beast――the enormous Jade Tiger――pulling the cart, seemingly not noticing at all.

Karshe no longer believed for a moment that the girl and her attendant were merely a new student and her escort, nor did she believe that the women she could see in the distance were ordinary people.

The girl tugged the red-haired attendant by the hand, rushed over to one of the attendants on the other side, and whispered something in her ear.

The distance and the noise of the crowd swallowed the words beyond hearing.

But Karshe clearly saw the attendant look surprised and then turn her gaze toward them.

A beautiful attendant with neat black hair cut precisely at the shoulders.

When the girl said something more, the attendant shook her head with a wry smile.

Then she stepped forward, smiled, and simply offered a deep, graceful bow.

Karshe did not understand the intent or meaning――but something about it felt strangely familiar, and she bowed in return, carrying an inexplicable emotion.

Mee, too, seemed unsure but followed suit, and when they looked up, the woman smiled once more and called out to the girl.

The girl, together with the red-haired attendant, gave a light bow and turned away, walking off with the others.

At the corner ahead, Karshe broke into a run on impulse――but there, their retreating figures were nowhere to be found.

As if no one had been there from the very beginning.

* * *

"――Why didn't you tell me!?"

"Why, you ask……hmm, well, the vibe, I guess……"

――That evening, the great library of the magic academy.

Tall towers encased orderly rows of bookshelves and spiral staircases.

The third floor of what was known as the Tower of Knowledge held books on Alberan-related topics that were relatively tangential to magic, and this had become something of Mee's designated seat.

She spent most of her spare time here, while Karshe passed the time browsing old tales, legends, plays, and short stories.

At first Mee had also been skimming books to satisfy her own interests, but today, unusually, Karshe was flipping through Alberan-related volumes and searching for something, which had apparently piqued Mee's curiosity.

When asked why she was reading such things, Karshe held nothing back and recounted her observations about the two――and Mee was absolutely furious.

"The *vibe*!? Do you have any idea how monumental――"

"Meedeliarieze! If you carry on like that, you'll be banned from this floor again!"

"Y-yes!"

She flinched at the head librarian's voice from below and fixed Karshe with a glare.

Karshe laughed and tried to calm her.

"Mee-sama didn't seem to notice, and they both looked like they were having fun……even if my guess is right, that's probably not what those two would want. You'd definitely make a big scene about it."

"Grr……b-but!"

"Or would Mee-sama have preferred I told you on the spot, so you could interrogate them about every little thing while they were enjoying themselves?"

"Ugh……but……"

"It's probably best to just look back on it and think, *well, that was something special*, and leave it at that."

Mee groaned in frustration, her eyes darting about.

'But-But Syndrome.'

The deeply serious affliction in which, when confronted with a logical rebuttal to an emotional argument, all she could manage was *but* and *because*.

She looked so upset she was nearly in tears. Karshe gave a wry smile, patted Mee's head, and gently repeated, *It's fine the way it was*.

"It was a precious experience, a precious encounter. Unknowing as you were, you still got to talk with the person you admire. Isn't that enough? And besides, there's always the chance I'm simply wrong."

Even as she said it, she was aware that she did not actually think she was wrong.

It was only instinct, but Karshe knew her instincts were usually right, and the more she investigated, the deeper her conviction grew.

The emblem of thunder and hawk――the Christand family crest.

It differed from the one on the military banner, but it was surely the genuine article.

Tracing through books, she had found the exact same design.

Not strictly a history text, but a military treatise.

Remembering that the current military structure was called the 'Christand system,' she had opened one such volume, and there, printed at the very beginning of the book, was the same crest that had been on the girl's cloak.

*In honor of the great achievements of House Christand*, it read, noting that this was the standard version of the Christand family crest used on clothing and personal effects.

And in any case, nobody used a crest still recorded in books like this.

There were nobles who used or were granted the same crest as a past hero's out of admiration, and in other nations, similar crests might exist.

But the history of Kleinmeil was long.

Nearly every family that currently bore a crest had received it during the Kleinmeil era. A *living crest*――one still documented in texts――would never be granted.

The Christand thunder and hawk was not merely a 'dead crest' preserved only in books.

"……What are you looking for, Karshe?"

"Hmm, what indeed."

"Hey, are you mocking me?"

"Not at all. I just got a little curious and I'm browsing."

The book she was now turning through was titled *In the Manor of the Noble Hawk*.

It was apparently a transcription of writings left by a servant of the Christand household.

An exceedingly difficult text, heavily ornamented――though it was written in western common tongue, the ancestor of the modern Meilian language, deciphering it was an ordeal. But the contents were closer to a diary than an autobiography.

It was reasonably thick, but stripping out the padding, it could likely be halved.

From the author's birth to her encounter with the esteemed Christand servant, Bery Argan.

Upon reading this passage, Karshe was exasperated at the clumsy lie the attendant must have improvised on the spot, and equally exasperated at her own mistress.

Very few documents mentioned the name of the attendant Alberinea had loved.

That she was red-haired was well known among scholars, but in most cases she was simply referred to as 'the attendant,' appended to Alberinea.

And yet this book, which Mee must have read many times, recorded her name, surname, and physical description in painstaking detail across half a page.

One would think she might have felt some dissonance――but Mee had not questioned it.

The attendant had most likely been unaware that her name was preserved in this way.

Otherwise, calling themselves *Krische Argan* and *Bery* showed hardly any intention to hide.

Come to think of it, while they had seemed very knowledgeable about Alberan, their knowledge of subsequent history and Kleinmeil had seemed quite thin.

With that depth of knowledge, she should have recognized famous paintings from the Kleinmeil era, yet she had seemed as if seeing them for the first time.

Flipping through pages, she read of the manor's residents.

Queen Kreschenta. The future Marshal, Selene.

Their figures overlapped with the two she had glimpsed near the Jade Tiger at parting.

If that was them, then one of the remaining two attendants was likely the person who had written this.

The cast of characters was not large.

Three princesses, two attendants――and one more after the civil war.

"……Elvena."

When she said the name aloud, it felt strangely right, and she traced the characters with her fingertip, narrowing her eyes.

A beautiful attendant with neat black hair cut at the shoulders.

The author, Anne, had apparently been a peer and rival to her.

"……You're already that far in? You've been skimming way too much."

"I mean, there's a lot of irrelevant stuff. ……Is there no continuation?"

"No. It burned in a fire. ……I can't believe they didn't even make a copy of something this important."

The book was nearly finished.

There had originally been a second volume, but it no longer existed.

It had been a gift to the author's father at the Giterns estate, buried among that vast collection for years.

When the estate burned, its existence was forgotten until a subsequent head of house discovered it, recognized its historical value, and sold it to a scholar――and so this single volume survived.

Karshe gave a wry smile at Mee's huffy anger and turned the page.

After the civil war, the text dwelt at length on her rival, Elvena.

It then described Krische establishing the famous Black Flag Special Service, and how its members began occasionally visiting the royal manor.

Apparently this was quite unusual for the time, and the author did nothing but heap praise upon her mistress Alberinea's character――welcoming all, regardless of noble or commoner birth, without a shred of discrimination, Elvena included.

Judging by what she had seen today, she was indeed such a person.

When described as *pure and kind*, Karshe could nod in agreement.

Elvena had apparently had an older sister who belonged to the Black Hundred, the precursor to the Black Flag Special Service, and she and the unit's adjutant were particularly frequent visitors, the book recounted.

That name was――

"……No way."

Her ears stiffened atop her head, her tail shot straight out.

She felt her wryly smiling cheeks twitch and rubbed the bridge of her nose.

"What is it? Did you find something?"

"Nah, just tired from reading a book I'm not used to."

"……I trust you know, but we're pulling an all-nighter tonight."

"What? Why?"

"We're writing down everything before we forget. Every last thing they said, everything we noticed. This is an event that should be recorded for posterity. Once I become important enough to have a say, I'm going to present it to the world."

Mee was serious――she had already produced ink, a fountain pen, and a notebook at some point.

Karshe's cheeks twitched again.

"……Um, Mee-sama, can we do this tomorrow?"

"If Karshe sleeps, she'll have forgotten everything by the next day, so no. This is your mistress's order, understood?"

"Haah……is it so impossible to just keep it as a memory?"

"Quiet. I'm writing it down so the memory won't be forgotten."

Sighing at those words, Karshe turned her eyes back to the book.

*'To quarrel as much as they were close――that phrase was made for these two. Krische-sama always watched them with a troubled look, and Elvena-sama would soothe them. With a wry smile, yet looking so very, very happy.'*

Then she closed her eyes briefly and recalled the girl's purple gaze.

Just as at the museum, gazing at all those relics, the eyes she had turned upon Mee and Karshe had seemed, to Karshe, like eyes that were remembering something dear.

She recalled the attendant's slight surprise, and that smile.

A smile and manner that felt strangely familiar.

She thought it must be Elvena, and somehow, she did not doubt it.

Karshe did not believe in reincarnation, nor did she think that was the case.

But thinking that way, this day of seemingly chance encounters felt just a little like destiny, and Karshe quietly softened her cheeks.

The mundane, amusing daily life inscribed in the book had no proper ending; it trailed off mid-stream, spilling into whatever came next.

The unrecorded days were surely still continuing, unchanged――and would they, too, look upon today's small memory and smile?

The thought painted all sorts of scenes into the pages beyond the book's end, and Karshe gave a wry smile to herself.

Only a few pages remained.

Yet what lay beyond them seemed to stretch out endlessly, impossibly vast.

And yet, just as depicted here, it also seemed to be something cheerful, something happy.

Mee, who had begun scribbling today's events into her notebook, looked up and pursed her lips as if to say *help me already*.

Then she glanced at the passage beneath Karshe's finger and remarked, exasperated.

"She was something, wasn't she. That person."

"Hm?"

Karshe tilted her head, and Mee continued.

"Calling *that* Alberinea by a nickname――I can't even imagine."

At those words, Karshe gave a wry smile and traced the characters with her finger.

"Really? Fufu……well, unlike Mee-sama, I can relate to that person."

She recalled the girl frolicking in the snow like a rabbit.

Why had she thought of a rabbit at all?

Pondering that, she smiled and let the word hum softly from her lips.

"I think she's adorable. ――*Bunny.*"
