# Chapter 321: Obligation – The End

The three-month stay came to an end, and exchanges with Kreisharana continued smoothly afterward.

Scholars were invited to examine the land, and attempts were made to cultivate new seedlings there.

Since they only accepted practical goods like alcohol, people with skills and knowledge were brought.

Alberan's rich technology had been refined through many cultural exchanges.

Their skill, having achieved unique technological progress in isolation, was by no means immature, but Alberan's artisans like blacksmiths and carpenters, and scholars, possessed broader knowledge.

Even if they couldn't fight, these were people dedicated to the pursuit of knowledge and skill throughout their lives.

Alberan gained its collective strength based on their endeavors.

Whether directly or indirectly, though their battlefields differed, they too were respected warriors, and the people of Kreisharana gradually began to accept their existence.

Those who dedicate themselves to their knowledge or craft were no different from those who dedicated themselves to the sword or spear.

*If the sword I forged turns out dull, I will thrust the blade into myself and die.*

Some old blacksmiths declare this boldly, as if challenging fate.

*If my proposed method is wrong, I wouldn't mind being killed.*

Some scholars declare this calmly, with a smile.

From a warrior's perspective, they were individuals who couldn't even become squires in Kreisharana, but voices disparaging their existence quickly faded.

Their conviction and resolve, which spared no life, and their pride in their own pursuits.

It was precisely because they disliked ostentation and valued inner qualities that they understood.

If there is a superiority among people, it resides within their hearts.

Never in their outward appearance.

Their way of life, which placed no value on those who couldn't fight, was indispensable in that harsh land.

Otherwise, it would be a land where even survival was difficult.

Their culture was synonymous with life itself; no one could question its rightness or wrongness.

However, it was different now.

They no longer needed to be isolated in that harsh land.

The option of moving to a more livable land could exist in their future.

They could find meaning beyond just fighting, be needed, and be allowed to be proud.

The possibility of such a future was what Argoush wanted to convey.

*"Individuals like Krische-sama appeared, and individuals like Her Majesty the Queen appeared, and then individuals like you appeared... We will surely slowly fade away. But, Giterns-dono, there is no need for you to worry. For we shall fade away of our own accord"*

Vinsreel and the others spoke thus.

*"Besides, if there are those like Giterns-dono, born outside, who lament our passing and find our way of life beautiful... then even in the future after we have faded, our way of life will surely be inherited in some form. By someone's hand, for as long as it is needed."*

They accepted their own demise with a smile, saying it was fine.

Argoush truly thought they were strong people.

For that reason, and so that they wouldn't regret it, Argoush busied himself.

And so, day by day, he deepened his exchanges with Kreisharana, communicating many times through letters.

He entrusted the affairs of his territory to his splendidly grown sons, and his days, though busy, were fulfilling—then, one day, Anne sent him two books.

"At the Noble Hawk's Mansion..."

The binding was beautiful, with a stately black leather and silver thread embroidery—two volumes.

The delivery person simply said it was an item requested by Anne, and there was no letter in the package.

He was dumbfounded, wondering if she had forgotten to include a letter, but also pondered why she would specifically send him books.

As usual, he was troubled by his daughter's eccentricities on the sofa in his private room.

Perhaps it was a truly good book, good enough to go to the trouble of sending it.

If so, he could understand.

Although he wasn't particularly fond of books, Argoush collected various books for self-improvement and usually read when he spent time at home.

The mansion had a rather splendid library, and there was a high possibility that Anne misunderstood him as a great book lover, so choosing books as a gift for her father was not strange.

In fact, some of the servants also thought Argoush liked books, and in any case, it could be said that his being a reader was an objective fact.

If anything, he only read books as a part of his studies and education.

If asked again if he liked books, he couldn't say yes, but if asked if he disliked books, he would say he didn't.

Broadly speaking, he was someone who liked books—and if it was a gift from his daughter, he was interested enough to wonder what kind of book it was.

"You, what did the letter say—?"

Black bean tea and cookies on a tray.

His wife, Raina, who had returned, gazed at the book in Argoush's hand and tilted her head.

Her tea-colored hair hung loosely in front of her neck, and although some signs of age were visible, she was still his lovely and adorable, proud wife.

Dressed in a simple navy one-piece dress, even as the wife of the Marquis Giterns, she still baked cookies herself and diligently worked on the mansion's cooking and laundry, just as she had when she was a servant.

He had tried to stop her, saying she was his wife now, but she seemed uncomfortable being taken care of—ultimately, Argoush relented.

In fact, what attracted Argoush himself was her hardworking and caring nature.

Seeing Raina unchanged always calmed him, and he left his personal affairs to his wife.

Perhaps growing up seeing such a mother also had a great influence on Anne.

Thinking this, he showed her the book and gave a wry smile, saying only the book had been sent.

Well, she too smiled wryly.

"Did she forget to put in a letter?"

"Probably something like that. Typical Anne."

"Fufu, even serving at Her Majesty the Queen's side, her clumsiness hasn't changed, has it?"

Relaxing her mouth, Raina took the lower volume, briefly checked its contents, and frowned.

"...This, is this her handwriting?"

"Anne's?"

Finally opening the book, the first thing that caught his eye was the table of contents.

Prologue—From Anne Giterns to Royal Household Servant.

Chapter 1—Ideal and Reality. And the Eternal Goal, the Encounter with a Great Servant.

Chapter 2—The War of the Crowns and the Noble Hawk Princesses.

Chapter 3—

Argoush was dumbfounded.

<br /><br />

Was this an autobiography or something?

He imagined his beloved daughter confidently sending her father a mysterious autobiography, and while he was exasperated, he accepted it, thinking it was entirely possible for Anne.

Anne was someone who occasionally did unexpected things.

According to Raina, she sometimes had deep thoughts (though sometimes they were just impulses), but it was difficult for Argoush, who had watched her since she was a baby, to discern her true intentions.

Indeed, the handwriting was Anne's.

Anne's writing, which had a slightly fastidious quality, was neat and polite.

It was not bad even when compared to professional scribes.

However, one's personality shows in their writing.

During the civil war, Selene's handwriting, sent to Argoush regarding Anne, was a powerful and dignified script that conveyed the bravery and strong will of Christand, Lightning Quick's daughter.

The head maid, Bery, who had sent letters several times on her behalf, had elegant, refined, and delicate handwriting.

A permit Alberinea had once entrusted to Kreisharana was so identical to Bery's that it was indistinguishable, suggesting she had learned from and imitated her.

Queen Kreschenta's handwriting was exactly what you'd expect from a queen.

It was vivid and beautiful, clearly expressing variations in speed, strength, and weakness. While all were beautiful, her inner self seeped through as a distinct style.

Anne's handwriting, too, conveyed her meticulous nature.

Overall, it was beautiful and well-written, but it subtly conveyed a slightly pretentious aspect, as if she was trying a bit too hard to create an impressive script, paying attention to appearance.

Unlike the other four, whose styles were naturally developed, Anne's characters, which strived to express beauty through effort, had a charm that was distinctly Anne's.

The table of contents was strong and dignified like Selene's. When explaining others' good points it was flamboyant like Kreschenta. Her usual handwriting attempted to imitate Bery's fluidity but with a slight awkwardness, as if she was forcing herself to imitate the flowing lines.

Just looking at it, the image of Anne with her strange strong preferences came to mind, and it was somehow amusing.

However, the more he read the content, the more worried he became.

It spoke of the people he had met since heading to the royal territory, with the Christand family as the main subject.

Basically, everything was the highest praise imaginable.

While speaking about their personalities was not bad, he couldn't quite understand what she wanted to achieve by having her father read sentences like *"Bery, though petite, has ample breasts and a beautiful waistline,"* or *"Selene's slender body is as attractive as a lithe cat."*

He understood she was praising their appearance, but it was an utterly unnecessary sentence.

And then, when the Queen and Alberinea, who were royalty, were added, he started to worry about Anne.

"That girl, really..."

"Well, it's very Anne-like, isn't it?"

Raina, who had been peeking at the book from beside him, chuckled as Argoush held his head in his hands.

He had understood to some extent what kind of people they were when he met them the other day, and he could see that they were kind to Anne and felt affectionate towards her.

The more he learned about their lives and their daily routines through the book, the more he understood how much Anne loved them, and how much they cared for his daughter.

Considering this, they probably wouldn't be enraged or punish her for writing this book, but still, the content was terrifying—at least, not something that could be shown to others.

Moving to the second volume, the content became even more radical.

*"Her Majesty the Queen, who, as usual, was sitting on Argan-sama's lap, being made to mimic a dog and spoiled—"*

And so on, scattered throughout the narrative of their daily lives were inexplicable, nonchalant mentions of the extraordinary.

After finishing the book, he was determined that it must be sealed or burned, yet he didn't stop reading.

He found himself turning page after page, drawn by Anne's emotions seeping through the chaotic daily life of the mansion that she described.

When he considered why she recounted her life from birth to her daily routine in the mansion with such apparent enjoyment, he began to grasp the reason why this book was written.

"I need to start preparing dinner soon... Let me read the rest later."

"Ah, go ahead."

Even after Raina left, he continued to turn the pages.

The content expressed in the writing was probably only a small fragment.

He imagined the many interactions that must have occurred between the lines as he read on.

Bery being stabbed, and the everyday life that felt extinguished until Krische returned with the dragon.

Their struggles and mental fatigue during the Five Great Nations War were touched upon, and because their usual daily life was depicted so brightly and oddly, those moments stood out even more.

Towards the end, there was increasing mention of Gallen, Krische's adopted grandfather, which subtly led to a realization.

The elderly man whom Anne revered, often mentioned in their daily life.

A martial artist from a hunting background, genuinely admired even by Krische, an undeniable genius, and respected by many military personnel.

He was around the same age as Argoush—but he wasn't a magic wielder, and it was probably because of him that his daughter wrote this book-shaped letter.

Argoush had heard that he had passed away a little while ago.

Anne recounted each of the things she learned from him and the things he taught her as if they were precious treasures, and these entries became more frequent as the book neared its end.

And so, when the death of Gallen was recorded, Anne finally began to speak about the book itself.

She recounted how she was born to admirable parents and surrounded by wonderful people, yet she felt she was an ordinary person, incomparable to them.

And she recalled Argoush's words, and pondered what her future should be.

The future of marrying a good gentleman, starting a family, and raising children was certainly a happy one, and unlike them, she was a very ordinary woman with no special talents.

She wondered if she was too unsuitable to spend her life in this glamorous world.

*"Gallen-sama said that I, who claimed to be ordinary, was actually a special presence in this glamorous world, and that someone like me was very important to this mansion. He said that not only are those who string the bow needed, but sometimes those who loosen it are too...

because everyone in this mansion strains the string too much."*

The daily life depicted featured characters like those in a picture book.

The royal sister and the queen, worthy of being called children of God.

The servant who spoiled the two like children.

The noble lady who became a marshal at fifteen and fulfilled that role as a matter of course.

Even the servant she called her rival was extraordinarily talented, and everyone associated with them naturally possessed something exceptional.

*"Argan-sama also says that I'm always helping her, even though I'm constantly causing trouble instead of being helpful, and can't do anything right. She says with a laugh that sometimes you need someone who makes the master angry or bothers them. I don't know if she means the same thing, but she says such things to me."*

Her heart was probably the same.

Anne had many flaws, but she was a good daughter Argoush could be proud of.

He believed that they truly saw Anne's good qualities.

*"I don't think I'm a respectable person who is needed by anyone, as those people say. I think they say such things to comfort me, trying to be considerate, but even so, I hope that there's even a little truth I haven't noticed in their words. I wanted to believe that if someone like me could be of even a little use to those people."*

As he had intuited mid-way through, this was a farewell letter.

A letter indicating that she had made a decision about her life from her own will.

*"Of course, it might be my foolish misunderstanding. Perhaps they are thoroughly exasperated, and I might someday be driven out of the mansion. But until that time comes, I wish to serve the revered individuals in this mansion, striving to my utmost—"*

That's why, upon reading that sentence,

*"—and fulfill my obligations as a servant."*

Argoush pressed his fingers to his eyes.

*"Father, who is still risking his life in a distant land, fulfilling a difficult obligation. Mother, who gently taught me many things and watched over me. And my brothers, to whom I may cause much worry. But, as one of the nobles born into the Giterns family, inherited from our ancestors, I have decided in my heart to dedicate an unchanging vow, for all eternity, to this precious name given to me."*

—Please, forgive your selfish daughter.

He pictured Anne's face, heard her words of resolve, and lowered his head.

Yet, he couldn't hold back his tears, which trickled down his nose.

*"From the Noble Hawk's Mansion, with love from Anne Giterns, child of my beloved Father and Mother."*

He pressed his sleeve to his eyes at these words written at the end of the book.

Until Raina's knock echoed, calling him for dinner.

Raina, who came to call him, nestled beside Argoush and gently stroked his head, smiling wryly, "What's wrong, you're old enough to know better."

"Later, read it."

"Yes. ...Was it something sad?"

"No," he shook his head, sniffled, and looked up.

"It's the most joyous letter, truly. ...As the head of the Giterns family, I couldn't be prouder."

Then, closing the book, he traced the letters on the cover.

"As a father, it's a little lonely... that kind of letter."

"...I see."

At the Noble Hawk's Mansion—and now, from the Noble Hawk's Mansion.

"Let's go when you've calmed down. The food will get cold."

"Ah, sorry."

Leaving the mansion where she was born and raised, Anne was now cemented in its history.

The next day, Argoush wrote a letter and returned to his routine.

He tirelessly traveled to various regions, meeting scholars, artisans, and merchants, and commuting between his mansion and Kreisharana as a bridge between them.

He was concerned that the round trip took too much time and was difficult, so after a while, a warrior began staying at Argoush's mansion, partly to study lowland culture.

If one flew from the foot of the mountain, Kreisharana could be reached in less than half a day.

It wasn't the case for large groups, but they gradually became cooperative, with several warriors providing escort for a few people, making the journey much more peaceful.

During a major plan to cooperate with the Alberan army—to create a path between Alberan and Kreisharana—they suffered damage from magical beasts, and he was plagued by stomachaches.

But when he was tired, he would look at the book Anne had sent him, repeatedly reviewing the words written there, and refresh his spirits.

Moreover, the Queen wasn't entirely leaving everything to him.

When large plans were underway, Alberinea would occasionally appear, riding a Jade Tiger with a nonchalant air, solving difficult problems as if they were nothing, and then returning, saying she was busy with work at the mansion.

As Anne had been worried about the Stormwing, it was hunted as a welcome gift to Kreisharana, and near the planned road, nearly ten magical beasts were hunted in just half a day.

Seeing Alberinea solve problems that plagued Argoush as if they were "just a small errand," there were times when he wondered if his presence truly had meaning, but even so, Argoush had no choice but to earnestly fulfill his obligation.

When he felt the presence of the extraordinary Alberinea and the Queen, he understood his own insignificance.

The existence of Bery, beloved and adored by them, and Selene, who naturally lived as the mistress of the mansion where they resided.

They were literally figures above the clouds, residents of a storybook world. Thinking of his beloved daughter's daily life with them, and of Anne Giterns, who had sworn to her obligation and her name, there was no time in his life to sit idle.

For his proud daughter, as a proud father.

Just as Anne sought to fulfill her obligation, so too did he.

To remember this, he turned the pages of the book countless times until they were worn.

After reading that letter, his reply was as a noble.

He could not allow any more of such strictly guarded forbidden books to be created.

The majority of the letter was filled with reprimands for Anne regarding the content she had written, telling her to reform her mindset as the Queen's servant and repeating that she should never write anything similar again.

And at the end,

*"Above all, I earnestly wish that Anne Giterns fulfills her obligations. Though in a distant land, as a noble who, like her, carries the Giterns name, and as a father who, with his proud daughter, seeks to fulfill his  own—"*

—With love for the servant who will open this at the Noble Hawk's Mansion.

He recalled writing those words every time he turned a page, renewing his resolve.

He was an ordinary, insignificant person. But that was no reason to abandon his obligations.

He was himself, and whether he had talent or not, everyone simply fulfilled the obligations corresponding to the abilities they were given.

There was no difference for the Queen, for Alberinea, no matter how blessed they were, or even if they could easily solve many people's problems.

Each of them surely had their own struggles and hardships, and it was precisely for that reason that they would even challenge a dragon, risking their lives.

To do one's best for something more important than oneself.

In that, there was no superiority or inferiority whatsoever.

He would face it honestly and earnestly, and he didn't care if he was embarrassed or ridiculed.

Even if it was undignified or pathetic, he would still devote his efforts and proudly declare,

"Do you like making a fool of yourself, you idiot? How many times have you been sent rolling? You have no sense and your movements are slow.

It's the same thing over and over, no matter how many years I deal with you... If it's too painful and you want to quit, you can complain all you want."

"No, please, one more time, Dukaran-dono."

"Then move your body before you move your mouth. Whether it's a cowardly ambush or whatever, fight desperately and take even one point from me.

Even if you make a fool of yourself and crawl on the ground, if you get results, I'll evaluate you."

"Yes, sir!"

He smiled and proudly stated that such was his obligation.

She knew of the theory that this earth was a great, round sphere.

*――What manner of world might this truly be?*

It was Grabareine, the Queen of Alberan, who had put an end to the many competing theories.

Astrology — divining the future from the movements of the stars. In the lands surrounding old Alberan, it had long been believed that the positions and movements of celestial bodies signaled auspicious and inauspicious omens, influencing all manner of things in the world.

At the time, such astrology had been valued as one factor in political decision-making, and in order to silence it, she had demonstrated through theoretical calculation of orbital and physical laws that this world was merely one star adrift in a vast expanse — the universe.

Gravity and centrifugal force binding together a vast cosmos of stars.

None could refute Grabareine, who had unraveled the divine mystery into physics. Astrology lost its mystique, and her theories remained in use as the foundations of astronomy to this day.

Grabareine had likely held no interest in the cosmos itself.

She had unraveled it merely because it was necessary.

Though she had laid the perfect foundation, she had never written anything beyond it, and astronomical research had been treated more as a dilettante's pastime.

Bery had felt much the same, and had never been driven by strong enough curiosity to pursue the research herself — but looking at the rough diagram of the cosmos drawn on the parchment upon the desk, she could not help but feel her heart stir.

"I see…… it really is an immense scale, isn't it."

In the room sat Selene and Bery on the sofas, and Krische and Kreschenta upon their laps respectively.

The usual scene — including Elvena standing attendant beside them, all eyes were directed to the desk.

"That you feel that way only proves the low caliber of your mind."

"……Yes, you're quite right——"

"Honestly, it's unbearable that you can't even manage basic arithmetic. Must I teach you from a child's four operations? Despite going to all the trouble of explaining it myself, and the best you can manage is 'it really is an immense scale, isn't it' — there truly are limits to how foolish——"

"……Woof."

"Wh-what was that……?"

Bery maintained her smile as she pressed a cookie into Kreschenta's mouth to silence her.

"Mgh……"

"Woof, if you would, Kreschenta-sama."

Then she deepened her smile, ruffling Kreschenta's head roughly, as one would a dog.

Ignoring Selene's exasperated gaze, Bery lowered her eyes to the parchment.

Beautiful circles that seemed impossible to have been drawn freehand were inscribed there in countless number, conceptually depicting this star's surroundings and the world beyond.

Great celestial bodies and small ones, innumerable objects tracing spirals, drifting through the endless void.

Noticing that Elvena had also been peering at the parchment, she asked.

"Are you interested in this sort of thing too, Elvena-sama?"

"Yes, it's quite fascinating. I know nothing at all about astronomy, but……"

"Fufu, I'm not particularly knowledgeable myself either, but…… it's fun to let the imagination wander, isn't it? With such an unfathomable number of stars, I wonder if there really might be aliens out there."

A sound that would normally vanish in an instant across the unfathomable void.

The Fuyopita network — the world-law that would later be called the Ether Limit — closed the pathways leading to the source, imposing limits on magical inscription through mana.

*It would be nice if they existed, alien-san* ——

Star to star, as if to fill every last corner of that endless void.

*It would be nice if they existed, alien-san* ——

A journey through the source, transcending space-time. The network merely traveled onward, filling the discrepancy of distance with time.

Lizard-like faces, two proud horns at the temporal region.

They had no scales, but possessed dull, lustrous gray skin, and their bodies were sheathed to the tip of their tails in suits of a rubbery purple texture.

Apart from silver metal protectors on the chest, groin, feet, and gauntlets, the design was relatively simple. Their four-fingered hands, wrapped in rubber, moved freely, each manipulating a spatially projected holographic display floating in the air.

The engineering-form fleet was cutting through a swarm of dangerous cosmic organisms — a colony of space amoebae.

The horde of Arnia — grotesque monsters that consumed organic matter and proliferated.

If even a single one slipped through, it could easily devour an entire planet.

Whether they had gained wisdom through the long war despite their low intelligence, they were beginning to mimic the shape of the fleet's own vessels.

They fused and split freely, and when enlarged, they far exceeded the size of their own combat vessels, which housed crews of ten thousand.

Through unknown means, they formed particle accelerator cannons internally, and once they breached a ship's shield and attached themselves to the hull, they easily chewed through the nano-coated high-density armor and infiltrated inside, proliferating.

Not a single one, not a single fragment, could be allowed to approach.

The main cannon at the prow incinerated the majority deployed ahead, and the fleet passed through the center while scattering secondary fire in every direction to maintain the breakthrough.

"98.7% of the Arnia have been destroyed! Remaining individuals: 18,375!"

"Good. Cancel the warp-out, move to mopping up. All vessels, come about. Launch the Fortelus."

The one raising his voice from the especially large central chair was Milganga, commander of the 237th Expeditionary Fleet.

The horns on his brow were adorned with gold and jewel decorations, and instead of a suit he wore cloth.

A cloak draped from both shoulders, its interior emitting a faint, warm glow.

——It was a heater.

The Minalshi, who proclaimed themselves rulers of the galaxy, were a sapient species possessing robust physiques and rich intellect — yet they had a notable weakness: extreme sensitivity to temperature changes.

They fell into a dormant state when cold.

For this reason, the suits they wore were always designed to maintain a constant body temperature, but that was merely the minimum preservation of warmth, far from comfortable.

They were extremely averse to cold, but due to their racial characteristics, warmth in turn brought about a state of mental laxity — in other words, they also possessed the concurrent flaw of becoming self-indulgent.

That Milganga could overcome such racial traits, warming his body in direct, cozy comfort while still maintaining discipline and a commanding presence, was worthy of being called a great hero.

Everyone regarded him with gazes of respect for that fortitude.

The fleet came about — at the command, a vast number of carrier-borne craft launched, proceeding to mop up the remnants.

The cutting-edge Fortelus fighters, directly linked via brainwave interface, scattered particle accelerator fire from both arm units while executing inertia-defying, irregular maneuvers to annihilate the remaining small Arnia.

Designed also for planetary subjugation, the three-legged configuration of feet and tail was dead weight, but thanks in part to the brainwave link device, their performance was not bad.

The confusion among the Arnia was clearly visible from the environment sensors.

"I'd wondered what sort of toy they were, but not bad at all. Technical officer, send me the combat data afterwards."

"Yes, sir!"

"However……"

Milganga turned his head to the side.

There stood one Minalshi, small for their kind — gray skin patchy and balding in places, indicating the nearness of death.

"That was a close call. To think they had spread even to such a place. Had the authorization come any later, the jewel you spoke of may well have been swallowed by them."

"Indeed, you are absolutely right, sir. ……But what magnificent command — a flawless victory. Against a swarm of that size…… had it been any fleet but yours, Milganga-sama, who knows what would have become of us."

"If you're astonished by this, your tail would snap off just watching a Hive assault operation. In the old Kudaras assault, there were over 100,000 times as many——"

『Data cross-reference: 135,347 times, sir.』

"Details are unnecessary. Well, there were that many Arnia swarming about."

Smiling wryly at the AI, Milganga continued.

"I would rather not imagine. A Hive that has swallowed an entire planet…… in that assault, my grandson and great-grandchild — ten of my kin — gave their lives for Minalshi."

"Many died. It would be harder to find someone who did not lose family in that battle. Including those who had lived on Kudaras."

Milganga closed his eyes, reaching out as if to touch his right horn, and the old Minalshi, Giryurya, likewise closed his eyes.

For them, this was the gesture of prayer.

"After the mopping up is complete, we'll conduct another ultra-long-range search. If there are no issues, wormhole entry is in thirty-two Minalshi standard time units. Giryurya, get some rest and compile the data on Benua. I want to review it once more before arrival."

"Sir. As you command, Milganga-sama."

Habitation zones enclosing entire stars, artificial celestial habitation zones.

The Trans-Galactic Minalshi Empire had established many celestial structures beyond planets and had developed through them.

But no matter how far they went, these were artificial ground.

Many Minalshi harbored a longing for real ground — a naturally habitable planet.

Crashing ice asteroids and icy bodies to adjust orbital paths and rotation speeds.

Moving stars into habitable zones, providing abundant water, planting vegetation — sequential terraforming was underway on many planets.

That said, even with a technological level capable of remaking entire star systems, it took many long years to make a single planet habitable.

Currently, ninety percent lived in artificial habitation zones — most of them ended their lives without ever setting foot on real ground.

There was no doubt that the cosmos still held many undiscovered habitable planets, but the universe was unfathomably vast.

Even having obtained means of travel far exceeding lightspeed, no matter how many survey ships they dispatched, they remained minuscule beings.

Even with their numbers reaching the hundreds of billions, they were but a speck of dust in the cosmos.

Naturally habitable planets were not so easily found.

"Milganga-sama. Warp-out near planet Benua complete. Coordinates identified, spatial stabilization finished. No anomalies across all vessels."

"……Very well. I want to see it with my own eyes. Remove the shielding."

"Sir!"

The frontal shielding that had been closed for warp navigation.

The bridge's protective barrier opened left and right, revealing a small, jewel-like celestial body.

At the sight, Milganga's eyes widened, and his tail swung vigorously from side to side.

"This is…… Benua. How beautiful……"

It was a celestial body confirmed not long ago.

Abundant water resources. Covered in green, with many mineral resources likely sleeping beneath its surface — a cosmic jewel, planet Benua.

Like Milganga, many who laid eyes on it wagged their tails.

They lacked facial expressions; instead, their emotions manifested in their tails.

Many were overwhelmed and overjoyed by the beauty of planet Benua.

——It was long ago that the Minalshi had taken to the cosmos and gained the technology to traverse the stars.

At times they had waged wars with comparable spacefaring civilizations — wars that consumed entire star systems over a single habitable planet.

Even so, when one turned one's eyes outward, the cosmos was truly vast.

Before the beauty of a star such as this, it seemed almost comical how small a world they had been fighting over.

The cosmos truly overflowed with what could only be called an infinite number of stars.

Blue ether covered Benua like a veil, casting light and shadow across its verdant surface.

Despite having encountered many astonishing adversaries, gazing upon what could be called the ultimate beauty of a celestial body, one still felt the reverence toward divinity in one's horns.

Calling to mind something that ought to be called a god — something that had faded with scientific advancement — he closed his eyes and touched his right horn.

"……Seeing it in person is indeed different from mere data."

"Truly. That I could bring you to this star during my own lifetime, Milganga-sama…… it is an honor beyond words, the pride of my life."

The small lizard standing beside him — Giryurya — trembled his tail, eyes glistening.

"Giryurya. I will personally convey your service to His Majesty. ……Surely a gold horn will be permitted."

"Sir. I am deeply grateful for your words."

All of Minalshi was ruled by the Trans-Galactic King, Minashea.

Permission to adorn one's horns was generally limited to royalty like Milganga, or to those few who had been recognized for extraordinary service.

Though gold horns ranked below the jeweled horns of Milganga's kind, for a commoner it was the highest honor — bestowed upon those who deserved to be recorded in Minalshi history.

And planet Benua was well worth it.

Abundant water resources, a stable climate, a gravity ratio nearly identical to their homeworld Minardria, and extremely similar oxygen concentrations.

Planets that required no terraforming at all — where Minalshi could set foot without even protective suits — numbered only a handful in all their long history.

Giryurya was a renowned survey technician to begin with.

When word of his discovery of planet Benua spread, no one would object to bestowing the gold horn upon him.

"As planned, we'll send the advance party down tomorrow. Giryurya, this is the star you found. You shall ride in the first boat."

"……Are you certain, sir?"

"Yes. The inhabitants are pre-spacefaring — there should be no danger, but…… heh, don't get too excited and wander about."

"Sir……!"

Eyes closing as if overcome with emotion, tail swishing happily.

Watching Giryurya, Milganga laughed.

Planet Benua was inhabited by various organisms, and among them, bipedal intelligent life forms called Benuans had been confirmed.

Organic life forms, a species of endotherm.

They reproduced through copulation via reproductive organs, bearing live young.

——From the Minalshi perspective, they were closely related.

Considering silicon-based life, gas-based life, celestial-scale cosmic organisms, and amoebae capable of solo space travel — compared to such incomprehensible life forms, beings like these inspired a sense of kinship.

The ecological survey via drones had been largely completed.

Their vision relied on light.

They possessed hearing and smell, and their sensory organs were broadly similar to Minalshi.

They were capable of fairly advanced conversation through sound exchange via vocal organs, and while they could not perceive heat through sensation as Minalshi could, they possessed the astonishing ability — in no small proportion — to sense and manipulate psychokinetic particles: ether.

Despite having developed a reasonably advanced society, their technological stagnation was likely due to this.

Ether appeared useful at first glance, but due to fluctuations in large-scale operations — the existence of the Ether Limit — its scalability was low, and it would not lead to the kind of technological development that could carry one across galaxies.

Being able to wield it in daily life was certainly convenient, but that very convenience was presumably what slowed the development of their other scientific technologies.

And low technological level narrowed the world.

Currently there was no unified government, and multiple nation-states were engaged in territorial disputes.

It was a scene reminiscent of when Minalshi themselves had still been crawling across their homeworld.

High-rise buildings lined the streets, and on the battlefield, low-level kinetic weapons using ether-powered launch mechanisms were in use. Their space travel technology was similarly primitive.

Debris-like 'toys' had been confirmed orbiting in satellite trajectories, but at the very least, it would be a long time before they could colonize other planets.

They likely believed this single planet was the entirety of the world.

Establishing a coexistence relationship with beings who had not yet achieved planetary unification would have its difficulties, but this was their duty as the vanguard of civilization, as their predecessors.

Having thus elevated, protected, and managed many intelligent species was of course in part for the Minalshi's own benefit — economic reasons existed — but it was not only that.

The cosmos held many dangerous life forms and species with which communication was not possible.

Though lacking in intellect, the instinct-driven, organic-consuming, endlessly-dividing amoebae called Arnia — if even a fragment reached this world, it could easily destroy and swallow a civilization like this.

To address such threats, technological enlightenment of these beings was indispensable.

And the cosmos still held many problems that even the Minalshi could not solve.

If these beings, who could intuitively sense and manipulate psychokinetic particles, could be brought to a comparable level and an alliance formed, it was possible that breakthroughs could be found for several cosmos-scale challenges that currently existed.

The richness of the planet, the species dwelling upon it.

They had to bring them under their protection before other hands could reach them.

Not only alien life forms, but even among closely related species there was no shortage of barbarians who sought to enslave others.

It was fortunate that their hand had reached this world before such people could.

Now then, the means — currently, Benua possessed no effective weapons capable of breaching their ship shields.

A simultaneous landing at key planetary locations as a show of force would be sufficient to bring them to the negotiating table.

Military force kept to the minimum as intimidation, with as little cultural destruction as possible.

As he was considering the path ahead, an alarm sounded.

"……!?"

『High-density ether entity intrusion on primary bridge. Repeat: high-density ether entity intrusion on primary bridge——』

Before him, at some point, stood a Benuan — a small female intelligent life form with long silver hair (on her head).

No larger than Milganga's waist. A tiny creature.

She showed no sign of fear, tilting her head curiously, pressing the tip of her right finger to her lips, gazing up at him with violet eyes.

Then she clapped her hands together, leaking sounds in an incomprehensible language — something like *oh, it's an alien*.

How had she appeared here?

Preparations for warp-based close-quarters combat were more than adequate.

The internal defenses were robust, and breaching the spatial barrier powered by energy from the degenerate reactor required an equivalent-scale vessel or installation.

Yet no such installation had been detected within this star system, and judging from the current technological level, it would be thousands of cycles before they could even grasp the rudiments of such technology.

Naturally, concealment of it should have been impossible.

——The academic questions could wait.

He immediately recovered from his confusion and aimed his left hand at the silver-haired female in her black-and-white garment.

A compact particle accelerator pistol deployed instantly, and he thrust out his right hand in a halt gesture.

Simultaneously, he accessed the ship's internal network through his neural implant device and activated the defensive systems.

"Halt!"

The portable particle accelerator's safety was disengaged, battery cartridge connected.

A pull of the trigger would discharge energy sufficient to easily vaporize a single life form.

He caught the peripheral movement of defense drones beginning to deploy and, with that, finally steadied his breathing.

The silver-haired female turned those peculiar violet eyes left and right.

Even without understanding the language, she seemed to grasp the situation.

"That's right, good——?"

He stopped mid-sentence not because he had forgotten to activate the translator.

It was because another alarm was sounding.

『Ether alert. Cracking has been performed——access granted. Welcome, Administrator.』

One of the defense drones floated over to the female and was casually grabbed.

The female patted and examined the armed defense drone with evident curiosity, and Milganga looked about in bewilderment.

He could see one of the technicians touching the holographic console and being denied access — and realized a moment later that the ship's controls had been hijacked.

"You……!"

It was a reaction born of primal fear.

He pulled the trigger aimed at the female, and a particle accelerator blast was discharged.

An excessive dose of energy was fired directly at the life form.

"……!?"

But the light did not incinerate the female.

Before her, it vanished as though swallowed by the void.

The female watched it with no apparent surprise, then walked calmly toward him and swept her hand.

——In that instant, every Minalshi on the bridge was crushed to the floor.

As if every cell in their bodies was being pressed against the deck.

"GA——!?"

Milganga was no exception, flattened against the floor, barely managing to keep his eyes on that single creature.

The female crouched, drone still in her arms, and looked down at Milganga with a softening of her lips.

『First meeting. Greeting. Space primitive Minalshi. I servant name Krische.』

Alarms warning of powerful ether interference echoed.

A bizarre sensation of meaning being transmitted directly into his mind.

『Star. What business?』

Innocently undulating psychokinetic particles.

The unfathomable pressure they carried was more terrifying than any enemy he had ever encountered.

For the Minalshi, it struck at an ancient, abhorred instinct.

『Question.』

All around, countless sounds of *pichi-pichi* — snapping.

The tails of many Minalshi on the bridge had been severed at their instincts' command, and were bouncing across the deck.

Milganga felt the ache at the base of his own tail — and wrestled down the instinct that was trying to sever and flee with an act of will.

The silver-haired female watched this, uttered a mysterious sound — *hoeee* — and tilted her head.

Only then did she apparently remember to activate the translator.

From the data Giryurya had previously collected and analyzed, it informed: 『"Hoeee": exclamation, or vocalization of similar nature.』

『Greeting. Attack. Bad.』

Again, meaning transmitted directly into his mind, and the pressure lifted from Milganga's body.

He rose to find those around him in a similar state — still confused, staring at him.

『……What are you?』

"Oh, you can speak in this language."

『I am using a translation device. ……Answer my question. How did you intrude?』

"Mm…… how, you ask — Krische just sort of hopped over……"

Reviewing the translator's linguistic analysis — 『"hopped": repetition of "hop" for emphasis; onomatopoeia for jumping』 — he simultaneously scanned the female before him.

Externally, she appeared no different from the intelligent life forms inhabiting Benua, but the critical difference was the extraordinarily high-density ether observed emanating from this individual.

The ship's AI classified her as a high-density ether entity — at the very least, not a simple biological organism.

Several subspecies of Benuans had been documented, but no such entity had been confirmed.

Giryurya, who had finally gotten to his feet beside him, shook his head.

Indeed, an unidentified species.

What she called "hopping" was likely a form of specialized warp.

At the very least, it was an unknown method of travel.

"Let Krische try again…… Nice to meet you, space-lizard-san. Krische is a servant. If you have no hostile intentions, Krische won't do anything terrible either, so please don't worry."

*What brings you to this star?* — the female, Krische, tilted her head.

He glanced at Giryurya, who nodded silently.

It was best to answer honestly — that seemed to be the shared assessment.

With an ether wielder of this caliber, there was a possibility of her reading their thoughts.

This individual had subdued the fleet flagship — packed with the latest technology — as easily as twisting a baby's tail.

And terrifyingly, this one was a servant.

Literally — if her earlier telepathy and Giryurya's translation analysis were correct, there existed a master above her.

What manner of being could possibly surpass this trans-dimensional ether entity?

『I am Milganga, Supreme Commander of the Minalshi Expeditionary Fleet, in service to His Majesty the Trans-Galactic King Minashea. Allow me to apologize for the earlier discourtesy. ……We came to establish exchange with the intelligent life forms of this planet, for mutual prosperity, and to bring technological enlightenment. We are also considering colonization after deepening relations.』

"Enlightenment, colonization……"

『We intended to share technology with intelligent life forms who have yet to achieve space migration, and to provide the strength to confront the many unknown threats that still lurk.』

That she showed no hostility was fortunate — or rather, she simply had no need for it.

The other party had instantly seized control of this state-of-the-art large combat vessel and casually stopped a particle accelerator blast that could penetrate an infantryman's shield.

Paying closest attention to the behavior of this fearsome unknown entity, Krische, he continued.

『To be honest, we had considered that some degree of military force might be necessary in the process of forming a cooperative relationship. This fleet was intended as intimidation…… however, I swear by this horn that we harbor no intention or malice to conquer and destroy this star.』

"Hmm, I see…… so that's how it is. That was quite a large amount of energy, so Krische might have jumped to conclusions a little. Umm……"

As she pressed her finger to her lips, the ether alarm sounded again.

What manifested instantly was a large, old-fashioned double door.

The Minalshi stared wide-eyed, exchanging glances — and when the door opened, a different individual with red hair (on her head) peered through.

"Ehehe, Bery, they're space lizard-sans."

"Krische-sama, you mustn't. The aliens probably have business with the people over there, and Ojou-sama is absolutely furious that you should bring them back quickly…… um, hello……?"

The red-haired female also bowed her head, looking confused at the many tails snapping and bouncing about.

Then she beckoned to Krische.

"Yes. The reason for their visit seemed peaceful, anyway."

"That's good to hear. Well then, shall we head back?"

*Please excuse us,* the one called Bery said, leading Krische through the door and closing it.

The door vanished without a trace, like a passing storm — and Milganga stared blankly at Giryurya.

"Giryurya, that individual……"

"I don't know. In appearance, she looked like a Benuan, but…… a being composed of ether…… perhaps? It was a sight beyond belief, and yet……"

The hijacked ship AI had returned to its original state, but the tails of the Minalshi bouncing across the floor — *bichi-bichi* — were very much real.

"……No, it truly is beyond belief. To breach the ship's protective field and then so easily seize control of our cutting-edge ship AI……"

"……Yes."

"The former is at least comprehensible as an unknown ether-based method of travel, but the latter is not something ether alone could accomplish. The AI itself was not destroyed — it means she instantly understood the complex structure and rewrote it…… even if that entity itself functioned as a computational device, it exceeds any physically possible processing speed."

Giryurya displayed Benuan data on his holographic window.

The average intelligence level was slightly lower than Minalshi.

Perhaps due to the limited data, the variance seemed large, but even at the high end, it was within realistic biological levels.

Nowhere near sufficient to make what had just occurred possible.

At the very least, she was an anomaly even among Benuans.

"She was beyond anything we've encountered…… but the word 'jumped to conclusions.' At the very least, she came to investigate our intentions. We possess combat vessels more than sufficient to destroy this star. She was likely wary of that."

"And the other one spoke of 'the people over there,' which suggests…… that there are beings like this, distinct from Benuans, lurking on this star?"

"That would be the reasonable conclusion. ……On this star, there exist transcendent beings, as if the gods we had forgotten truly walked the earth."

Gods — yes, that was the only way to make sense of it.

At the very least, they were higher-dimensional entities, fundamentally different from themselves.

"Shall we…… withdraw?"

Giryurya asked in a voice that seemed to suppress something anguished.

An overwhelming anomalous existence against which their technology was entirely useless — the danger level was extreme, and abandoning interference with this star was one possible course.

"No. We continue."

"But……"

"If she had meant us harm, we could have been killed at that very moment. She merely came to observe us — that was the atmosphere. Withdrawing now would only increase our concerns…… rather, we should take the initiative in deepening relations with them before some hardliner, hearing what happened, decides to come here."

Milganga declared this with authority and looked at Giryurya.

"But we proceed with the utmost caution. We will conduct a thorough new round of observations, and the landing and negotiations will follow after. Giryurya, I leave the command of that operation to you."

"Sir!"

* * *

The embassy established for the Minalshi was quite grand, proportioned to their size.

Ceiling heights, corridors — everything on the scale of an art museum, and the conference rooms were the size of party halls.

Why such dimensions were necessary was immediately apparent upon seeing a Minalshi in person.

『I must say, the more I learn, the more fascinating this place is. The various equipment and tools utilizing ether…… you are literally a gathering of mages.』

"Ha ha, we were indeed called that in the old days, sir. Ether use has advanced considerably, but in the pre-historic era it was not as widespread as it is now, and terms similar to what you describe were commonly used."

Standing twice the height of an adult man — the Minalshi were extremely large.

The first time he had seen one, the sheer intimidating presence had stiffened his body, but after fifty years of association, there was no longer any fear.

Commander Milganga projected the dignified bearing of a career military man, but his temperament was mild.

Fifty years ago, they had appeared at locations across the planet with overwhelmingly advanced warships, gathered the leaders of every nation in one place, and leveraged their absolute power to establish an armistice among all nations.

Over the following thirty years, they had devoted themselves to establishing a unified federal government, followed by exchange and technological enlightenment.

Before their arrival, there had been endless debate about the existence of extraterrestrial life, but everyone agreed: one could hardly ask for more ideal aliens.

Cultural differences notwithstanding, they were truly sincere, and several years ago, special residential zones had been formally established for them on the surface.

"A cycle of peace and warfare, development and decline repeating…… In the pre-historic era, there apparently existed a calendar called the Unified Calendar. The topic you inquired about the other day relates to that."

『Oh……?』

"To me, it's something like…… well, mythology, I suppose. To be honest, I have reservations about sharing such things with you, Milganga-dono, but……"

『I don't mind. I am very interested in this subject.』

It was surely mythology.

Legends from thousands of years ago.

And yet, among the tales still passed down even by scholars, the story of the ancient Queen Kreshient remained.

The theory that the world tree — still standing today — and the ether rainbow that once enveloped the planet had been produced by a powerful ether wielder remained stubbornly popular.

He had heard that even prominent scholars who supported the natural-occurrence theory had defected to that camp; perhaps when confronted with the incomprehensible, one simply wanted to throw up one's hands.

That said, the problem was that beings from a civilization as advanced as the Minalshi were showing interest in it.

*——I wish to speak with someone knowledgeable about powerful ether wielders, including the distant past.*

That was what he had been earnestly asked the other day, and so the only option was to bring in scholars from what was essentially a hobbyist's circle — ether researchers with expertise in history.

An old-fashioned apron dress, silver and red hair.

The names Krische and Bery.

*This is extremely important,* he had been told, and after extensive searching, the two who had shown the strongest interest were a pair of rural scholars living beside the world tree.

More like eccentric archaeologists than ether scholars, really.

He had his doubts about whether they would do, but at the very least he could not be blamed for the outcome of his best efforts.

In the room with the large round table sat two women — one dark-haired, one blonde.

The two had been seated with their backs to the entrance, but rose and bowed deeply.

"A pleasure to meet you, Milganga-sama. I am Alurize Quiros, and beside me is my assistant, Milshi Geeshia."

The blonde, Alurize, spoke with the composure of a refined lady.

The dark-haired Mil nervously followed with *a pleasure to meet you*, he could tell.

Their counterpart was Minalshi royalty — the de facto ruler of this planet.

Nervousness was understandable.

The blonde woman regarded Milganga with evident curiosity, while the dark-haired one seemed somewhat intimidated by his enormous frame.

『The dark-haired one, there is no need for fear. I have no intention of eating you — I merely came to listen. Thank you for your time today; I am counting on you.』

"Y-yes……"

Milganga took a seat at the far end of the round table, and he sat beside the two women.

The blonde woman projected a document she had brought with her right hand.

"First…… I would like to confirm what information you are seeking. Whether you wish to learn about ether wielders in history generally, or specifically about the beings known as Krische and Bery."

『The latter. Judging from your manner, you have some idea, then.』

"Yes. At the very least, the one called Krische is recorded in pre-historic legend as a great ether wielder. The name Bery also appears in a limited number of references, as her attendant, however……"

*There is a slight question,* she said.

"The name Krische is fairly well-known…… there are many written records, and she even appears in casual games — a general of the continent-unifying nation, Olbern. She is more commonly known as Olbrina, however……"

『Olbrina?』

"The heavenly sword…… or divine sword, meaning a soldier of the gods——"

"……Alberan and Alberinea."

"Pardon — the pronunciation varies with the old languages…… the pronunciation that Milshi here used is considered closer to the original, but it means something along those lines. Even now, with many records lost, a great number of plays and research materials from the era survive, and there is no room for doubt that she was a peerless general and powerful ether wielder."

*However,* she continued.

"Those who also know of Bery are quite rare by comparison. Most sources only refer to a red-haired attendant, and knowing that attendant's name was Bery requires a fairly specialized knowledge."

『……I see. That I know those names — that is what struck you as odd.』

"And so Milshi wondered whether perhaps you, Milganga-sama, had actually met the two of them…… that was her inference."

"You two — is it not rude to put forward such a fantastical——"

『It's fine, Garan. I see…… you are indeed the people I was hoping for.』

Milganga let out a *karararara*, a throaty sound.

The distinctive Minalshi laugh.

"You met her!? You met Alberinea!?"

The nervous one, Mil, was the one who slammed both hands on the table and stood.

She was immediately pushed back into her chair.

"M-Mil…… Stay."

"Ngh, yes……"

Milganga nodded magnanimously.

『As you inferred…… at the very least, I have met her. Even now, I can scarcely believe what I witnessed, but…… shortly after arriving here, my flagship was seized. By this ether entity called Krische.』

"That can't be……"

Garan stared at him, stunned.

*Karararara* — Milganga laughed again, and displayed a holographic window in the air.

『The footage is what it is, so I have never shown it to a Benuan before…… but the two of you should be fine. These are visual records from optical and ether-spectrum observation.』

The footage showed the enormous bridge.

Minalshi in uproar at the sudden appearance of a silver-haired ether entity.

Her outline was blurred, radiating intense light.

The image data, corrupted by unknown noise, had been painstakingly restored and corrected using data from various sensors and the neural recordings of Milganga and others — the figure of the ether entity, which had been captured clearly, was now indistinct.

A powerful particle accelerator blast easily deflected — and in the next moment, Minalshi prostrate on the floor. The countless tails bouncing about were clearly visible.

『We were rendered completely helpless by powerful ether interference, and — incredibly — the ship AI was cracked and hijacked in an instant. Even now it seems a nightmare…… but the records exist, so I cannot dismiss it as a dream.』

"This is Alberinea……"

Milshi's eyes sparkled as she gazed at the holographic window, and Alurize also appeared astonished.

Garan, too, was thunderstruck by the footage.

Having served as a minor official assigned to the Minalshi, Garan had seen many astonishing Minalshi technologies, and his amazement was all the greater for it.

# Chapter 328: The Creation of Heaven and Earth, an Eternity of Journey — End

"He's the emperor now……he's grown up splendidly."

*He was about this tall at first,* Krische-sama said, holding her hand at hip height, and she and Argan-sama laughed.

Krische-sama and Kreschenta-sama never forgot anything they had seen or heard.

They could rattle off what they had done on any given day decades ago, down to the schedule — they could memorize a book word for word after flipping through it once. Strictly speaking, they did forget things, but apparently that just meant having to go find the book in the library inside their heads (she rather wanted to peek inside), and there was nothing they couldn't recall.

Perhaps because of that astonishing memory, Krische-sama in particular seemed to burn first impressions deeply — if someone was a child when they first met, she would treat them as a child forever after, no matter how old they grew.

*Onee-sama is clumsy about that,* Kreschenta-sama said (apparently she didn't have that tendency), but somehow, it was very Krische-sama, and she liked it.

In Krische-sama's mental library, the covers of the books probably had the faces from those first meetings drawn on them.

Argan-sama had said so, delighted, and she couldn't remember how many years ago that was.

She kept a diary every day, so she could check — but the diaries alone had become a substantial collection.

Selene-sama had built her a diary archive (she felt guilty about making a master do that), and she would sometimes hole up there and leaf through them, but even that took several days.

Every word, every scene, every phrase — to have all that, even more vivid than the diaries, constantly inside one's head was astonishing.

The mental library probably had bookshelves stretching to the horizon.

At the desk in the center where Krische-sama sat, there were surely mountains of recipe books and volumes of the *Collected Sayings of Argan-sama,* and just imagining it was a little bit fun.

The words Krische-sama used when lecturing Kreschenta-sama were mostly borrowed from Argan-sama, and each time, Kreschenta-sama's anger was redirected toward Argan-sama — a regular event in the manor.

*Argan-sama teaches nothing good* — that was a view shared by both Selene-sama and Kreschenta-sama, and watching Argan-sama deftly dodge their interrogations with honeyed words was a secret pleasure.

The days of quaking through such exchanges were long past.

Arguments and scuffles between Argan-sama and Selene-sama were constant in the manor — and how peaceful were the days filled with such arguments and scuffles.

Through civil war and wars and tragedies — and beyond all of that, childish bickering.

A manor of eternity, hidden quietly outside the world.

The words painted a lofty image, but what existed here was not some otherworldly grandeur, only perfectly ordinary daily life — and that was what everyone wanted.

Each time she felt that, she was relieved from the bottom of her heart.

Everyone treasured these trivial days — feeling that, she was reassured.

"It feels strange, somehow, walking through this city."

"Strange……?"

"……I should have lived here for ten years, and yet it's like visiting for the first time. I used to go shopping with Krische-sama every day when we were in Gurgain, and yet……"

Argan-sama had spent almost all her time in the royal domain, inside the manor.

It was inevitable given her position — in terms of time spent outside, Argan-sama had probably the least of anyone.

"Just walking through town like this, shopping, doing the most ordinary things — I never did any of that back then……fufu."

Argan-sama's gaze softened as she looked around at the town.

"……I just remembered something I'd been thinking for a while — that someday I wanted to go out to town with no particular errand, just strolling around, looking at things, shopping."

Then, *another dream come true,* she said, and giggled happily.

Whenever something needed doing, she and Elvena-sama had volunteered.

They'd told themselves they mustn't waste the time of Argan-sama, the most capable of them all — but hearing those words, she froze, wondering if it had actually been the opposite.

In hindsight, Argan-sama was the one who most valued precisely that kind of seemingly wasteful time.

*'Argan-sama, where are you going?'*

*'It seems Ojou-sama forgot her lunch basket, so I thought I'd bring it to her……'*

*'Leave it to me! I'll go instead!'*

*'Um, well, I'm already done with work, and Kreschenta-sama won't be up for a while, so Anne-sama, why don't you take a little bre——'*

*'I'm fine! You rest, Argan-sama……!'*

*'……Y-yes. Well then, please do……'*

There must have been days when she wanted to take a leisurely stroll outside.

Argan-sama would create slack in the schedule, and then spend that time doing nothing in particular, using it extravagantly — she loved that.

*Argan-sama is lazy and wastes time.*

Kreschenta-sama often said so, and in a way it was an accurate assessment.

Argan-sama herself often said she was lazy, and she now understood that it was truly not modesty but heartfelt.

Finishing work at superhuman speed, then spending ages indulging Kreschenta-sama's whims, or having tea, or playing with Gururun — using time elegantly, extravagantly, wasting it away — that was Argan-sama.

She used to wonder: *she's so busy; is she really all right?* But most likely, Argan-sama did her daily work precisely *for* that purpose.

She had simply assumed that because Argan-sama settled into the royal domain so effortlessly, even shaping the environment to suit her, she was someone who could carry on perfectly anywhere.

But surely, compared to life in Gurgain, there had been inconveniences, troubles, hardships — things she endured. One or two at the very least.

Only now, belatedly, did she realize such an obvious thing, and her eyes darted.

"Ehehe, from now on Krische will take you to all sorts of places. It's peaceful now, so places like Arna that we said we wanted to visit together, and all kinds of mountains and seas……"

"That sounds lovely. I'd love to see many different places."

"Yes! Space, and……oh, the moon would be nice too. There's nothing but sand and rock, but the stars are beautiful, and this planet sparkles like a jewel. Krische thinks Bery would like it too!"

"My. Fufu, I can't wait."

Looking at Krische-sama, she thought: *ah.*

Krische-sama probably understood Argan-sama all along.

They had been avoiding this area since people who knew them might still be around, but once they could go out again, Krische-sama had been making an unusual number of travel suggestions.

Under the guise of shopping trips, she had apparently taken Kreschenta-sama all around the world in advance, scouting beautiful scenery.

*It's like this and this, and the view is really beautiful,* Krische-sama would explain to Argan-sama — and most likely, Krische-sama's idea of a 'nice view' or a 'beautiful place' was exactly that.

A view that Argan-sama would enjoy was, to Krische-sama, a beautiful view.

Just walking through town, and Krische-sama's eyes seemed to sparkle.

As if the scenery around her were radiant, its brilliance reflected in those eyes.

Darting happily around Argan-sama.

*This shop, that shop* — beckoning, Krische-sama's delighted figure.

Just watching from behind warmed her chest — and that warmth nearly rose to sting her eyes, and she shook her head.

The great war that accompanied Alberan's fall had been not so long ago.

They had decided not to interfere with this side of things, but they hadn't been indifferent, and she had been somewhat anxious about how things were going.

But Albernaria, visited again after so long, was almost exactly as she remembered it.

And in this place, Krische-sama wore a truly happy smile alongside Argan-sama.

Without a care, without a worry.

She had thought there might be something nagging — some lingering feeling.

All things considered, this was the best course — Krische-sama and Selene-sama had made many arrangements with Aleha-sama and the others on this side, doing what needed to be done, bringing a hundred years of peace to a war-torn continent.

They had built, for a time, a historically remarkable peace — and yet, perhaps there had been a slight sense of guilt.

Depending on how you looked at it, they had abandoned the possibility of even greater peace and found happiness for themselves alone.

It could be seen that way, and if people knew, some would think so.

Because of that, the kingdom had split, war had broken out, and people who might not have died had died. That was a fact.

A lingering guilt left behind.

If the war had dragged on and devolved into a quagmire, that guilt would have grown far stronger — and so she was glad that the war had ended so quickly, that the world was moving toward a new era.

Remembering the boy (whose eyes really were very narrow, matching his nickname) who had gazed at Krische-sama with sparkling admiration — was it too much to think that this peace, too, might have been something offered to Krische-sama?

In any case, this peace and happiness were not theirs alone.

"Over there is Mia and Kalua's dojo. It's a pretty big dojo, and——"

As she took in the nostalgic scenery and savored the small joys of each day, her gratitude for all those people welled up.

Such scenery would not last forever.

Someday it would surely fade — but that was not a sad thing.

It would simply melt and blur into the gentle haze of memory.

In that soft parting, it would remain, never truly disappearing.

*——I won't last much longer.*

One day, drinking tea in Gallen-sama's room, she heard those words.

"These days, I often think about what will happen after I'm gone."

"Gallen-sama, you mustn't say such——"

"It's not a gloomy subject. People die eventually; I'll simply part from Krische and you all a bit earlier than the rest……that's all."

True to his words, there was nothing dark in his voice — the same gentle tone as always.

The same gentle, softly-laughing smile.

"Grace and Gorka……When Krische lost her parents, I lamented how little time I had left. I wanted to stay at that child's side and watch over her, to take their place……but there was nothing to be done about age. After Krische killed the bandits to protect the village, the villagers feared her, and she had no place to belong……I argued with Galla over and over — she'd been looking after Krische in Grace's stead — about what to do."

He brought the black soybean tea to his lips and looked up at the ceiling.

"That's why I entrusted her to Bogan.……If I'd been younger, I might have left the village and lived with her somewhere else, but given my age, I wouldn't last long. She was clever, talented — she'd have no trouble surviving on her own. But precisely because of that, I wanted her to find someone she could rely on."

And he narrowed his eyes.

"I wanted someone who would love her, watch over her."

To her ears, those words sounded like a prayer.

"Back then, I couldn't have imagined how things are now.……That Krische would always be right here, at my side……I truly began to feel she might be with me to the very end."

His voice caught, slightly hoarse, but continued with warmth.

"But as it turned out, I'm here alone. Krische is at the manor, laughing happily, living surrounded by many people……and I've become just another old man who drops in from time to time to dote on his granddaughter."

"Gallen-sama……"

"Feeling a little wistful, yet……strange as it is, truly happy."

Then, with a wry smile, he turned to her.

"Now I'm just one among many who love her, and she loves me as one among many. The people who love her and are loved by her are too many to count, and I'm no longer someone special to Krische. She'll grieve my passing……but that grief will fade amid so much love, and in time I'll become just one of many memories."

She wanted to say *that's not true* — but couldn't.

Gallen-sama was roughly the same age as her father, but her father was still youthful, and Gallen-sama looked far older.

He was neither noble nor mana-wielder; even if he lived several more years, he wouldn't live for decades.

No words would change anything, and he wasn't seeking comfort.

He had already accepted all of it.

"Her Majesty the Queen, despite her youth, is a wise sovereign. Now that the recent war is over, peaceful days will continue……and as long as they do, Krische is the gentlest of girls, one who loves such days. Not everything will go smoothly, of course, but she is surrounded by many kind people — including you — and what happened in that small village won't happen again.……I can't see the future, but that child's future is quite bright……surrounded by much love."

His voice carried an inexpressible tenderness.

He didn't stand at Krische-sama's side the way Argan-sama did — yet surely he held the same depth of feeling, treasured in his chest even across the distance, and she felt that, and lowered her gaze.

"Do you think parting is a sad thing?"

"……At least, right now, yes."

"Of course, sad partings exist……but so do many that aren't. I've commanded many subordinates. There are many I haven't seen since leaving the military, but quite a few are surely living happy, peaceful lives on different paths. Whether they're alive or dead, I may never know."

*Parting is like that,* he said.

"Life is full of such partings. Not just death. I'll never see those people again. But each walked their own path, and parting as a result is not a sad thing. Surely you have friends like that too?"

"That's……yes."

Karen-sama, who had been like an older sister, had returned home and sent a letter saying she was to marry.

Her partner was a lower-ranking noble but a fine young man — the letter was bubbling with excitement.

They would probably never meet again.

She had felt a little wistful, but it wasn't a sad thing.

"That's how it is. If you live long, there will be many more such partings. Death is just one such parting, and if it turns out to be a good one — that's enough."

Gallen-sama looked at her gently.

"I'm glad you're fond of this old man……but don't feel you need to grieve. The same is true for Krische. I feel death drawing near, and yet I can smile, sit here drinking tea with a kind girl like you, and feel happy.……You may not understand yet, being young, but it's really not a bad thing."

At some point the tears had started falling, and Gallen-sama smiled wryly, stood, and gently stroked her head.

"As many partings blur into shining memories, fading and wavering with time into recollections, each time something catches your eye, you'll find yourself smiling without knowing why.……One day, you too will see that kind of scenery. And when that day comes——"

The hand was rough, yet strangely soft.

"If you remember an old man's idle musings over tea and smile — I'd be glad."

*——As one trivial memory among many.*

Few things have been described with as many words as the beauty of the moon.

A waxing crescent turned the sky to deep black; a full moon painted it deep indigo.

It was always the moon that set the color of the starry sky.

Not as dazzling as the sun, its outline clear — perhaps that modest radiance was what made it so.

Gazing at it every day, the moon held an allure that made one lose oneself.

What it was, she understood in some academic terms — but for her, the moon was a distant vista, a kind of painting.

Something one could never touch — a concept, a beauty that existed only in the imagination.

*——To think she was now standing in such a place.*

Stretched before her, an undistorted horizon of perfect clarity.

It nearly threw off her sense of perspective.

Part of it was the sand and rock, and part of it was the scenery being far too visible in the distance.

Though the sun was visible, the sky held the stars of night.

Far away, a blue sphere — the world where they normally lived — floated against a backdrop of stars and void.

Hanging there as naturally as anything, shining like a jewel.

"Is that black soybean tea?"

"Yes, I was in the mood."

"Maybe I'll have some too. I haven't really had any since coming here……"

At Selene-sama's words, she nodded and poured some into a cup.

Having finished packing up the tent and supplies they'd brought, this was a moment's rest before heading back.

Selene-sama sat on the crate beside her, brought the black soybean tea to her lips — and frowned.

"……Isn't this a bit strong?"

"Oh, shall I dilute it?"

"No, it's fine, but……you do like it strong, don't you."

"Black soybean tea, well……Gallen-sama preferred it about this strong, and I got in the habit."

*I see,* Selene-sama chuckled, and cradled the carved-wood cup in both hands, gazing at the blue star.

Argan-sama was strolling with the two excited ones perched on her shoulders. Lila-sama and Elvena-sama were apparently performing some Kreisharana ritual——

With solemn expressions, they bounced about, stacking moon rocks into a mysterious tower.

Trips usually ended in these groupings.

The energetic duo and the ritual-starting Lila-sama were too much energy, and Selene-sama, looking a bit tired, often spent these moments lounging beside her.

"No matter how many times I see it, it's beautiful. We've seen all kinds of beautiful scenery, but……"

"Truly. I never imagined we'd come to a place like this. I got a little carried away, at my age."

"Fufu, they were talking about how Selene-sama didn't come back for dinner."

Strangely, everything was lighter on the moon.

The heavier the celestial body, the stronger the gravity; the lighter, the weaker — and the light bouncing of what seemed like a short walk had led Selene-sama to get lost for half a day.

"It's a bit lonely, though. The sky is beautiful and the floatiness is fun, but there's nothing else but sand and rock……"

"Yes……it's enjoyable as a trip, but if someone told you to live here, it would be rough."

Normally one couldn't even breathe, and there wasn't a single living creature.

Not a blade of grass grew — apart from the starry sky, the scenery could be called bleak.

"I'm pretty satisfied, and I think I'll be fine for the next twenty or thirty years. It's not like the ocean or mountains where you want to go back every year……I just want to relax at the manor for a while. My body's sense of balance is probably off too."

"I bet it'll feel incredibly heavy."

She laughed wryly, and Selene-sama nodded, sipping her tea.

She too savored the bitter taste, and slowly turned her eyes skyward.

As if cutting out the view, sealing it away into memory.

"Black soybean tea while gazing down at the earth from the moon……after a hundred years of such absurdities, time's just flown by. We're using time so extravagantly, I almost feel guilty about it."

"It's true — these are days I couldn't have imagined when we were over there."

"Same here. I'm appalled at how lazy a person I turned out to be. I was supposed to humor that idiot, and before I knew it, I'd become a fellow idiot. I wonder what Aleha and the others would think if they saw us."

She watched Selene-sama sigh, laughed, thought for a moment, and spoke.

"……When I was dying over there, I felt strangely happy."

"……?"

"Happy, old memories kept surfacing, wrapping around me……and I imagined the scenery I hadn't yet seen. I thought: even if I don't wake up, that might be all right. It was that comfortable."

That must have been what Gallen-sama had spoken of.

Simply comfortable, happy — a gentle time, gradual days.

"When Gallen-sama passed, I was terribly sad at the time……but having stood in that same place myself, I understood that Gallen-sama was truly happy. And it probably wasn't just him……when we went to Albernaria the other day, I felt something similar."

"Ah, you did seem pensive during dinner."

"Yes……fufu, only we are here. We drifted to a different place from all those people, and we parted.……But when we taste that nostalgic scenery, that nostalgic food, somehow……"

She gazed at the distant figures of the two, riding happily on Argan-sama's shoulders.

"I think it's because many people tried to leave something behind. In their final moments, perhaps they pictured us as we are now, and departed smiling……I can't help but feel that."

Albernaria remained as it was — the beautiful White City.

From Kalua-sama and the others' swordsmanship academy came the lively voices of children, and at Koza-sama and the others' restaurant, they savored cooking as delicious as ever.

Surely each had their own reasons.

But among those reasons, she thought, there was surely something like that.

"A blessing for a new world's journey……so that the departure might be a little less lonely."

She saw Gallen-sama's kind face reflected in the dark tea.

"……As a benediction for the long road ahead."

She closed her eyes, and a smile came naturally.

Most of those people were now only memories.

Wistfulness came and went, but more than that, a warmth settled in her chest and colored it.

Diluted by the ever-growing happy memories, much would fade and blur and lose its shape.

But such gentle memories would surely go on casting their faint light within her heart forever.

"You've been staring off into space by yourself, but is that what you were thinking about?"

"Well……drinking black soybean tea just reminds me of Gallen-sama, and I get lost in thought……"

"……Please don't burst into tears out of nowhere. I always think something's happened."

"Th-that's……I'm sorry……"

She let her gaze wander, and Selene-sama smiled wryly and gently stroked her head.

"But, well, I agree with you. Being able to enjoy the moon like idiots with those idiots — that's thanks to people like that. Alberan ended about as ideally as it could, and now it's a new era……we made preparations for that, sure, but that alone doesn't make things go smoothly."

"……Yes."

"It's because so many people thought: let that idiot's selfishness be just a little bit happier. Being here now isn't just because she was amazing, or anything like that……it's because all those people were watching over us."

*We should be grateful,* she said with a gentle smile.

"……Not that you of all people would ever forget that."

The palm was harder than a woman's should be — and strangely soft.

She felt Gallen-sama's hand in it, and shook her head.

"No, I wouldn't be so sure. I'm the type who can't even remember what I ate three days ago……since the first century has passed, I thought I'd write a book — a summary of everything from back there through these hundred years, using my diaries."

"……Didn't you write a similar book about fifty years ago? Those absurdly thick three volumes?"

"Those covered only the days over there, so it's a bit different. This time I want to include that gratitude, everything from coming here through the first hundred years."

"I-I see……"

"This time it will likely be five volumes. Actually, I've already decided on the title——"

# Chapter 329: Koten Suigetsu

*——In the east of the continent lies the nation of Tosho, and at the foot of the imperial capital where the Emperor resides stands a particular dojo.*

Its students number over a thousand — the greatest martial arts dojo in the imperial capital.

The name Koten Suigetsu-ryuu is known to all in Tosho.

Its fame had resounded even across the continent.

*Know all the hundred martial arts and yet know not enough — if you would know the way of the warrior, knock upon the gate of Koten Suigetsu.*

Even the sparrows in the streets repeated this, and the supreme mastery of its head instructor, Kazama Tesshuusai, had long since reached the domain of peerlessness. As the Sword Saint who bore the blade of Fuji, he was revered by countless warriors.

In the broad, wooden-floored dojo illuminated by eternal magic lamps, the old man's mere presence — simply sitting there — emanated a quiet yet indescribable pressure, and the men facing him felt as though they had swallowed lead.

"……I forbid it."

With a slight stirring of his long, snow-white beard, Tesshuusai spoke with grave weight.

He wore nothing but a simple kimono, bore no sword, and yet his words carried a sharpness and heaviness like a blade pressed to the listener's throat.

"I shall keep in my breast the foolish words you have uttered in defiance of the Emperor's will. If you have time for such idiocy, devote it to polishing your own skills. That is all from me."

"But…… are you truly saying this is acceptable, Master!? Groveling before the foreign color-hairs, forbidden even to look skyward! They are mere outsiders beyond the sea — if we band together, we need not submit but rather——"

"I said I forbid it. If you would say more, then face me in combat, Seijuurou. If you intend to walk this foolish path, the responsibility is mine…… cut me down first, and then go."

Over a hundred years old, his flesh wasted away, yet standing six and a half shaku tall.

When Tesshuusai rose and looked down upon them, the men before him stiffened at the force of his presence — his fighting spirit made manifest.

"Twelve senior students — all of you here, and you lack the nerve to kill a single unarmed old man, yet you speak such petty words? There are two choices, Seijuurou. I shall give you one chance to wipe away the disgrace you have spat. ……Choose."

He drifted toward him, seized his collar, and fixed him with a glare.

Just past forty — still young enough to be mistaken for a youth, the man met the killing intent in his master's eyes, wavered briefly, and lowered his gaze.

"I…… do not possess a blade to turn against you, Master."

Hearing this, Tesshuusai exhaled deeply and roughly released his grip.

"Then go. I too shall forget today's events. And you shall never speak of this again. You who cannot even swing a stick properly, yet presume to challenge the Emperor's will — the height of arrogance. ……Before turning your eyes toward the heavens, polish what lies within. Understood?"

"……Yes, sir."

And so the men departed, and in their place came a single man.

He resembled Tesshuusai some twenty years younger, exuding a dense aura of martial prowess.

"Why did you allow this to happen, Kojuurou?"

"I believed that words from you, Father, would serve Seijuurou better than mine…… Of course, should he push the matter further still, I shall fulfill my duty as his father."

Kojuurou leaned against the wall with arms folded.

Then he looked up at the ceiling.

"Nearly thirty years since then…… most alive today know nothing of those events. Please forgive today's incident as youthful folly."

"It is because I think so that I forgave it. But should it go further, there will be no second chance. If this school must be weighed against this nation, I shall show no mercy, even to a grandson. ……That country cannot be defeated."

So saying, Tesshuusai gazed at his own palm and sighed.

"I reached desperately for the lonely moon in the empty sky…… and when at last I grasped its reflection on the water, only then did I comprehend the unfathomable height of the moon shining at the zenith."

Then he gazed at the moonlight streaming into the dojo.

"……To realize that one is an iron boat sunk to the bottom — the present age is far too peaceful for that."

* * *

The sound of warfare from across the sea had reached their shores as well.

The great nation of Orburan, which had conquered everything from the far west to the far east of the continent.

The combined armies of over a dozen nations had been swept aside like dust from a sleeve.

Breaking through like a bursting dam, they had taken control of the eastern continent in the blink of an eye — so it had reached Tesshuusai's ears.

At the time, his name had been Kazama Fujisai.

True to the name — peerless, second to none in swordsmanship.

Recognized as the greatest swordsman under heaven, there was none who did not know his name, and having completed his long warrior's pilgrimage, he had been appointed as the Emperor's chief sword instructor.

The position had briefly satisfied Fujisai, but the role of sword instructor was not what he truly sought.

For Fujisai, who had lived by crossing blades with talented swordsmen, threading the boundary between life and death, the days were intolerably tedious.

It was during such a time that news from the continent reached his ears, and his heart raced with excitement, his chest swelling at the prospect of a battle over a nation's very survival.

Yet while many warrior houses cried for total resistance, the Emperor alone remained calm.

To fight head-on was reckless — for the sake of the people, war must be avoided and friendly relations established, he declared. Against the overbearing Orburan, which looked down upon them as a minor nation, he adopted a conciliatory stance.

He resolved to avoid war at all costs, silently accepting every condescending demand from the other side.

Many warrior houses naturally rebelled.

They declared it an act of abandoning the pride of this nation, which had maintained its independence against the continent's threats for so long.

Fujisai had no interest in such matters of state, but his heart lay on that side all the same.

He yearned for the chance to swing his sword again on the boundary of life and death, and it was around that time.

A proposition was brought to him.

In three months, the envoy of Orburan would arrive, accompanied by the kingdom's supreme commander and hero — Orburina.

They would eliminate them here to demonstrate their martial prowess, and change the Emperor's mind.

A crude plan, certainly not one to be praised.

However, aside from the Emperor, everyone desired war with the continent's barbarians, and if Fujisai declined, someone else would surely carry out a similar plan.

If the dam was going to burst regardless, let it be by his own hand.

Not for the nation, not for pride — simply because it was more interesting.

With that reasoning, Fujisai had agreed.

An imperial exhibition match timed with the arrival of Orburan's envoy.

Before the Emperor, many renowned warriors would compete in sword, spear, and naginata.

Fujisai's task was to draw Orburina — the effective pinnacle of martial prowess in Orburan — into the exhibition match, and kill her while making it appear an accident.

Meanwhile, others would take advantage of the chaos to dispatch the supreme commander who should have been at the Emperor's side.

*Following his victory in the exhibition match, he wished by all means to test his sword against the renowned Orburina* — the Emperor had approved Fujisai's prior proposal to this effect.

The response came with the caveat *provided the other side also agrees*, but likely the Emperor himself wished to demonstrate their martial strength.

While accepting the other side's terms yet refusing to submit — the idea of defeating Orburan's gold standard, Orburina, in a match carried great appeal as a way to show that in the event of war, they would not go down easily. The Emperor had personally clapped him on the shoulder and nodded proudly.

The Emperor's smile, seen after so long.

It had been ages since he had seen a smile on the face of that slender-faced young man he had known since childhood.

In hindsight, it must have been a decision made in anguish.

The Emperor had not been blessed with build or natural talent for the sword, but he was keenly intelligent for his youth.

He must have seen more than anyone, and amid the warrior houses clamoring loudly for war with the continent, he had no allies.

Even so, he had not been swept away — the young Emperor alone had assessed the times with a clear eye.

Seeing that smile, Fujisai had nearly changed his mind — yet the Fujisai of that time was a fool.

In the end, he decided to let events take their course.

——The arena was spacious.

The inner garden of the Tenpuu Palace in the center of the imperial capital was wide, its white sand meticulously maintained each day.

Fujisai stood calm before the giant facing him, squinting against the sunlight, listening to the wind.

The man wielded a cross-bladed spear — Fujisai did not remember his name.

*Here, I should like us to fight with live blades.*

The man had declared as much, and what he held was not a wooden replica but a real cross-bladed spear.

His stance was respectable, but he was no match for Fujisai.

When two combatants skilled in ki manipulation faced each other, the length of their weapons did not simply determine superiority.

In a fight between two who could not wield ki, the longer weapon conferred a direct advantage, but that was only because they lacked the footwork to crush the distance in a single step.

It was a matter of reach and striking range.

When the first move was missed — or when one was a beat too slow — the length of the weapon exposed the weakness of poor handling.

And in a ki-enhanced single-step strike, that opening was enough to take a life.

The cross-spear wielder was a respectable martial artist, having won his way through the imperial exhibition unperturbed.

Against Fujisai, who held his sword casually over his shoulder, he chose a watching stance.

Caution born of recognizing Fujisai's caliber at a glance.

But it was not a wise choice.

Rather, his only hope of victory lay in pressing the attack with the reach of the cross-spear, seizing the initiative constantly.

By surrendering the initiative — he had already lost.

Fujisai leaned his weight forward in an almost-toppling step.

"——!!"

In that instant, the spearman's eyes flew open, and he unleashed his fastest thrust.

But Fujisai felt no alarm.

This thrust had been *drawn out* by Fujisai.

Sliding the sword that rested on his shoulder smoothly forward, he let the neck-aimed thrust glance off by slightly tilting his body, angling his blade against the cross-guard's branch to deflect its trajectory, then pressed deeper — as the cross-spear's path was diverted upward and the man's torso lay exposed, Fujisai drove his bent elbow into the solar plexus mid-step.

The man folded at the waist. Fujisai seized his left arm, circled behind him, twisted the arm while bearing down his weight, and drove him face-down into the white sand.

Pinning the giant prone and pressing his blade to his throat was as effortless as drawing a breath.

"——Match decided! Victory to the Chief Sword Instructor of the Imperial House, Kazama Fujisai!"

He heard the cries of admiration and applause from the dais, sheathed his blade, rose, and knelt with bowed head.

Having dominated three consecutive bouts against warriors who claimed to be the greatest under heaven — a five-consecutive-championship feat in the imperial exhibition — yet nothing in his heart felt settled.

Victory was a foregone conclusion; he had not experienced a perilous fight in years.

Applause rang, and a voice called to him. He raised his eyes.

One step up from the white sand, the hall of the imperial dais — at the center, the Emperor's seat, enclosed by bamboo blinds.

To the right sat some ten prominent figures, including the warrior houses.

To the left sat four foreigners.

Apart from a large man who radiated unmistakable martial power, they were all women.

He could not be certain which was Orburina.

All of the women, despite their sex, exuded an air of martial capability visible even from a distance, each suggesting they were no easy opponent.

The strongest presence came from the black-haired woman.

But judging by the atmosphere, she was likely a guard.

That left the blonde woman and the silver-haired girl.

The blonde wore an elegant military uniform, while the silver-haired one wore a fluttering outfit beneath her cloak.

The visitors had demanded the right to carry swords, citing religious reasons.

Likely it was out of wariness toward their hosts — and yet, the choice of clothing clearly unsuited for combat raised questions.

He stole a glance, careful not to be rude.

The silver-haired girl's eyes were narrowed, seemingly focused — no, something was different.

Her lips moved in a subtle chewing motion.

The blonde beside her quietly reached toward the girl's thigh as she sat, and the silver-haired girl flinched, eyes widening, pressing her hand down on the other's and turning to stare.

Whispered something by the blonde, she nodded hastily.

He considered seriously whether this might be some kind of signal, but a ridiculous image intruded — a sister pinching a drowsy sibling's thigh to scold her.

In any case, the blonde seemed to hold the superior position.

If so, the silver-haired one was likely Orburina — but she looked impossibly young.

Of course, it was rare to see foreigners at all.

Their appearance and age might differ greatly from those of eastern birth, but at first glance, she looked like a beautiful young girl.

Even assuming she had stopped aging early, it was hard to believe she was over forty.

And yet, he felt an intense sense of wrongness.

In terms of appearance, she was someone who should inspire no wariness at all.

Her demeanor was unmistakably that of someone who did not fight.

And yet, Fujisai *felt the presence of martial arts from her as well.*

Something beyond the five senses was sounding an alarm.

He could see the Emperor communicating something to an attendant behind the bamboo blinds.

The attendant stepped out and went to the foreigners.

This was likely about the match with Fujisai.

Through the interpreter at their side, the three who heard the message looked to the blonde woman.

And she looked to the silver-haired girl.

The blonde seemed briefly uncertain, then nodded, and turned to the silver-haired girl.

Whispered something in her ear, the girl nodded, stood, and conveyed something to the attendant. She had what were presumably her own leather boots brought over, and carefully put them on.

On the girl's feet, they looked crude.

Thick leather boots with steel reinforcement at the toes and heels.

That these were no ordinary footwear was obvious at a glance.

"Fujisai-dono, one more bout, if you would."

"……Yes. As you command."

He was called, stood, and made his way to his position, waiting for her.

The girl, having put on her boots, trotted over in that same childish fashion, and stood before him.

"Thank you for this. Please do your best."

She spoke in fluent local language.

Fujisai, who had not expected her to know their tongue, blurted in surprise:

"A-an honor beyond words, my lady."

"An honor……?"

"The sword arm of Orburina is renowned even in these lands."

"Orburina……"

"……?"

She twisted her head in thought, then clapped her hands.

"I see — the pronunciation changes like that over here, does it?"

"My apologies for any inaccuracy."

"No, Krische's speech might be a little odd too…… this is the first time Krische has actually used it. Shall we use real blades?"

"There are blunted blades and wooden swords available…… what would you prefer?"

It made no difference — in earnest, a single blow would end it regardless.

It was not a meaningful question, but for the sake of appearances before the audience, he needed to ask.

"Hmm…… well, anything is fine. You won't accidentally kill or be killed with real blades, so don't worry. Feel free to go all-out."

The words, delivered with a smile, were the height of arrogance.

She said this having watched the matches that preceded this.

Any underestimation of the girl was now gone.

"Then, as we are."

"Krische should stand over there?"

"……Yes."

She trotted to the opposite position in that childlike way.

Turning to face him, they exchanged bows — and it was the moment Fujisai drew his sword and she drew a curved blade from behind her waist with her right hand.

"……"

As if fair weather had suddenly become a storm — the air changed, the pressure changed.

The wind took on an unpleasant chill across his cheeks, and the bright sunlight seemed to dim.

The call of *begin* echoed and faded like a distant voice.

The conviction that he harbored no complacency, felt just a moment ago — he realized its foolishness in that very instant.

That very conviction was nothing other than complacency.

What the girl held was a notably small curved blade.

The edge barely a shaku long — more the length of a billhook than a sword.

No matter how little the length of a weapon determined the outcome, this was an absurdly short weapon.

And yet, the sinuous curve — like a woman's waist, an obscene line — and the spine-crawling presence it bore made it feel as though the tip were already resting against his throat. The violet eyes that watched him caused his body to stiffen.

The gaze of a serpent. Inorganic. A predator's eyes.

Even the great serpent he had once slain had not possessed an aura so chilling.

As if to sever that invisible pressure, Fujisai brought his drawn sword into a middle guard.

A quiet murmur of surprise rose from around them.

For the past decade, Fujisai had transcended the constraint of fixed stances, residing in the freedom of formlessness.

As water flows, he carved fluid, infinite forms in each fleeting instant, seizing advantage and overwhelming any opponent regardless of their stance.

He had believed this to be the pinnacle of the sword, and had proven it without cease.

But she, too, was formless.

The small curved blade dangled limply, like a drooping willow — and its pressure.

Before that, Fujisai felt terror at placing himself in the anchorless domain of formlessness.

When the heart was muddied, formlessness became finite.

As frozen water cannot flow, stagnation became a binding.

He needed the anchor of a middle guard, the tip directed forward, to cut through her pressure — otherwise he could not even stand before this girl.

At a glance he knew — a formless stance surpassing his own.

So this was Orburina.

Before Fujisai, who had been called peerless under heaven, the solitary moon that he had gazed upon and reached for was right before his eyes.

Even if captured and held in the mirror of water, it was a peak that could never be grasped.

"……Magnificent. I never imagined I would encounter something like this today."

"……?"

"The name Heavenly Sword, bestowed upon you — it is no illusion reflected in a water mirror."

What spilled from his lips was pure admiration.

Orburina stood in a realm beyond the reflection of the moon in water — her stance alone was enough to make Fujisai sense defeat.

He had heard that "Orburina" meant "heavenly sword."

But titles were always inflated.

He had assumed she would be a respectable swordsman, while resigning himself that she would fall far short of his own conception of a heavenly sword.

The heavens were beyond human reach — that was their nature.

He had named his school Suigetsu-ryuu precisely because he was certain he would never arrive there.

Not only himself, but no one in this world — not even those who searched to the end of the future — would ever reach the lonely moon in the sky.

That resignation was overturned by this girl simply standing before him.

"And yet, your small frame must have its limits. ……This is not about superiority in a match."

In technique and spirit, she was decisively superior.

But in that small body, in those arms and legs, there were limits.

The body — the talent one was born with. On that single point, the match could be fought.

All that remained was how close he had come to her realm.

The heart he had thought perfectly still now rippled.

The technique he had thought perfectly polished now showed clouds.

Even so, this body, forged and tempered, bore the weight of all the years behind it.

From the moment he could first remember, he had swung a sword through uncountable seasons.

Eating, sleeping, swinging — gnawing at the path with the instinct of a creature, never doubting that he would reach the summit.

The tenacity engraved in his flesh roused the fighting spirit to challenge this sudden, towering wall.

"……Here I come!"

Fujisai struck first — and the distance between them seemed to collapse, an illusion that left those watching in awe. A transcendent step.

Not fast — simply, purely, *early.*

So smooth and free of waste that human perception failed to track it.

A single step forward.

That act alone was proof of the peerless under heaven.

Even those who knew the concept of a heavenly sword, who knew what Orburina was, would have been moved to awe and admiration.

And yet the silver-haired girl showed no surprise, shifting her weight slightly back — by a hair's breadth.

The horizontal slash aimed at her neck from the left — a blow that would have literally frozen the air — was dodged by a tissue's width.

Fujisai felt no shock. Only unhesitating admiration.

This girl was truly one who had mastered the solitary heavenly sword.

A single swing from the far edge of range, leveraging every inch of his reach.

A divine stroke — not a fraction too much, not a fraction too little — designed solely to cut one inch across the throat.

That she dodged it — literally without a shred of wasted motion — meant Orburina was already beyond human.

A step stripped of every conceivable inefficiency, and the trajectory of the blade.

Her eyes had seen through it, down to the fraction and the hair's-breadth, with total certainty.

She had understood at a single glance — even if Fujisai wielded his body with absolute freedom and delivered the finest single stroke of which he was capable — it would fall short by a hair's-breadth of where she stood.

And having dodged that stroke, the initiative in that instant belonged to Orburina.

The distance had closed.

The girl's strange weapon — the weakness of its shortness negated, the advantage now hers — Fujisai could not win.

Just as Fujisai delivered a stroke of divine quality, she too would deliver one — as effortlessly as breathing.

At theoretical best, he could survive two exchanges.

He was certain that by the third, she would take his head.

Bearing resignation and admiration alike, he committed his all to defense — to bring this match to its rightful conclusion as a fight beyond all precedent.

His intuition told him that, at his best, he could survive two exchanges.

A third exchange, reached by threading the needle — that was where defeat awaited.

If he could not make that real, everything he had devoted to the sword would be meaningless.

The girl's right arm shimmered like a heat haze.

An opening movement that, repeated a thousand times, would sever a thousand throats — and yet it was impossibly gentle.

No tension, no tell.

Though possessing physical form, it was like a breeze — and in the next instant, a gale.

Only in a concentration so deep that even an instant could be distinctly perceived was it possible to perceive this blade.

Even so, by the time he noticed it, it was already halfway through — on the verge of slicing his throat.

Merely dodging would be too late.

Blocking would not suffice either.

And so — the only choice was to attack. To dodge through a strike committed to mutual destruction.

Angling his blade, he unleashed the fastest possible downward diagonal slash.

The girl's reaction was no longer a matter of speed — it bordered on precognition.

The instant he began to angle his blade, she withdrew hers. Ducking beneath his downward slash, she moved to Fujisai's right side.

Without killing her momentum, she planted one weightless foot and pivoted left, spinning behind him.

That footwork was the very incarnation of death in a thousand changing forms.

He sensed that her landing behind him was also a step — a step that, in the next instant, would effortlessly cut his throat.

A wind like a sickle-weasel — an invisible blade seemed ever pressed against his neck.

Even dodging her first strike was a feat one-in-ten-thousand might achieve.

A fleeting exchange invisible to most human eyes.

Even those who could follow it would have offered nothing but lavish praise.

Had anyone but Fujisai accomplished the same, Fujisai himself would have thought so.

But Fujisai was not satisfied.

The true test was the next move.

Only by surviving it would all that he had devoted to the sword be redeemed.

Splitting the instant, in a time that seemed frozen, thoughts bloomed and scattered like sparks——

"——Now! Strike down the foreigners!!"

The shout rang out without warning.

The step that should have cut into Fujisai was instead directed past him.

By the time he looked, she was already on the dais.

Not the same group Fujisai had been in contact with.

Others, who likewise rejected submission to Orburan and sought to sabotage the talks here.

Originally, Fujisai was to have killed Orburina, and the signal would be given.

Whether they had learned of his plan in advance or it was coincidence — regardless, what he felt in that moment was fury.

Everything Fujisai had accumulated to this point.

The decisive bout that should have been the culmination of it all — the supreme defeat — had been defiled.

"Kill the traitors!!"

He roared with fury, and immediately gave chase — and because of that, he saw clearly.

The instant the warrior lord who had given the call drew his sword and stepped toward the foreigners, a silver wind passed through, and a red blossom opened at his throat.

Several who had risen with him stood frozen in disbelief. The comrades who appeared through the sliding screens stared likewise.

In the next instant, she leapt into the air, kicked off the ceiling, and took another man from above.

Fluid and unconstrained.

She had not deliberately exploited an opening.

Despite the acrobatic movement, it registered to the eye as perfectly natural.

As one walks, runs, and steps in — she kicked off the ceiling as a matter of course.

By the time Fujisai had cut down one, Orburina had already cut down two more.

The beauty he glimpsed from the corner of his eye was the very embodiment of natural law.

Each time her blade was swung, it traveled a trajectory that seemed the only possible one, and was delivered exactly as it should be.

By the time Fujisai had cut down two more, she had felled three.

She was not merely killing.

While cutting down men, she was clearly planning the next move, and embodying the ideal in every motion.

It was not only the earlier one-on-one.

The fundamental nature of her swordsmanship — and his — were different, and she overwhelmed him.

Her blade had likely been forged in the chaos of the battlefield.

The remaining three foreigners likewise drew their swords and cut down several, and gathered on the white sand.

In concert, Orburina also alighted on the white sand.

"Silence!! Before my very eyes — how dare you commit such a disgrace……!!"

"Your Majesty……"

The slender-faced young man emerged from behind the bamboo blinds, anger plain on his face, eyes sweeping left and right.

Fujisai immediately moved to shield him.

"Enough, Fujisai! If any among you would add further insult to my guests and mock me, then cut me down first. I shall show you the Emperor's sword……!!"

And taking up a fallen blade, the Emperor glared at all around him.

A voice of piercing resolve.

And yet — whether from excitement or fear — his hand trembled ever so slightly.

Seeing it, Fujisai roared.

"——You are before the Emperor!! ……The matter is decided — lay down your arms!"

Swordsmanship had never been the Emperor's strength.

That was Fujisai's role.

At his shout, the men set down their swords and prostrated themselves, and——

"Seize them!!"

Those who had come running at the commotion took the men into custody.

The Emperor, paying them no mind, descended to the white sand and bowed deeply.

Voices called *Your Majesty*, but the Emperor did not heed them.

"No amount of bowing can atone for this, but I offer my deepest apology for this disgrace."

Hearing this, Orburina spoke to the blonde woman as if asking her to interpret, and the blonde replied.

Turning back to face him, Orburina opened her mouth and spoke.

"Um, please raise your head. Your Majesty's feelings have been well received, but since blood has been spilled, we shall take our leave for today. We would like to arrange a proper meeting on another day, where we can discuss things calmly. ……That is the message."

"……I am grateful."

When the Emperor raised his head, Orburina pressed a fingertip to her lips in thought, tilted her head, and looked at Fujisai.

"Also, our match was interrupted, but — should we continue?"

Fujisai was momentarily dumbfounded, and met the Emperor's eyes.

She was utterly unperturbed, as if everything that had just occurred might as well not have happened.

In truth, for her, it may have been nothing more than that.

"Your gracious words are more than I deserve — however, that bout revealed to me the shallowness of my training. If the opportunity should present itself again someday, I would be most honored."

"Hmm…… is that so? Ehehe, then perhaps some other time."

Seeing her then, he was struck by a realization.

In that brief span of time, she had cut down six men, and he had cut down three at her side.

Fujisai was drenched in blood from head to toe, and yet — on her fluttering cloak, on her beautiful silver hair — not a single drop of blood had fallen.

She remained as she had been during their match — beautiful and untouched.

The instant he noticed this, he understood the height at which she stood, and the strength drained from his body.

What he had been looking at was not even a reflection in a water mirror.

He had merely been gazing up at the wavering moon-shadow from the bottom of the water.

Having fought his way to the surface, how beautiful the radiance above.

From that day, Fujisai changed his presumptuous name.

He was an iron boat sunk to the bottom — the lonely moon in the sky remained far, far beyond.

* * *

Fortunately, Orburan did not demand anything great as a result of the rebellion.

It had been an assassination attempt against the supreme commander and the King's sister.

The Emperor, who had been prepared for war, was instead offered the opportunity for dialogue as promised, and on the condition that submission be pledged henceforth, the incident was treated as largely pardoned — even considering the mountainous, forested terrain and the island's distance from the continent, it was extraordinarily lenient.

The Emperor was overjoyed, relieved, and clapped Tesshuusai on the shoulder.

*I am fortunate to have a loyal retainer like you at my side,* he said.

In the Emperor's unknowing mind, that was how it was.

Though Orburina had not been defeated, neither had Fujisai lost; he had acted swiftly in response to the warriors' treachery and helped stop it — a loyal retainer.

The Emperor believed this had also made a strong impression on the other side.

The truth — that Tesshuusai had himself been among those plotting the treachery — never once crossed the Emperor's mind.

His swift action had been born of fury at having his bout interrupted.

It was neither righteous indignation, nor political calculation, nor devotion to the Emperor — it was, from start to finish, a laughably impulsive act driven by personal outrage.

And yet the Emperor trusted Tesshuusai, praising him as a retainer without equal.

From that day forward, Tesshuusai swore in his heart to never betray the Emperor's trust, to never make those words a lie — to serve faithfully as the loyal retainer he was believed to be.

* * *

Some thirty years had passed — relations between the two nations had been good, thanks in no small part to the Emperor's statesmanship — but the events of that day were becoming a thing of the past, fading from memory.

That Orburina had not been defeated was, perhaps, worse than if she had.

The word was spreading that Tesshuusai was a Sword Saint equal to or surpassing Orburina, and that in a genuine fight, the victor would be Tesshuusai.

The greatest swordsman under heaven was Tesshuusai, they said.

Even the continent's greatest hero could not defeat this island nation's Sword Saint.

And so, they said, Orburan could certainly be beaten by the strength of this nation's warriors.

The current state of groveling before that country must be overturned, and the Emperor's majesty made known.

Such voices were growing, and shamefully, even among Tesshuusai's own students, there were fools drawn to this thinking.

It was then that the Emperor informed him of Orburina's impending visit.

Tesshuusai bowed before the Emperor and petitioned for one final battle in his life — the continuation of that day's bout.

He wished to inscribe upon his life the third exchange — the defeat he had missed — the greatest bout under heaven.

The venue was the dojo — before the eyes of his arrogant, blinkered senior students.

When the announcement of her arrival came, the one who appeared was a girl unchanged from those days.

"It's been a long time, Fujisai-san. Krische heard you wished to continue that bout from a long time ago."

Ignoring those who looked dubiously at her unchanged appearance, Tesshuusai bowed deeply.

"Yes. I wished…… to cross swords with you once more."

At those words, she placed her hand on her sword and rose.

Glancing at the men to either side and then at him, she smiled and asked.

"Yes. Well then…… shall we begin right away?"

That day, it had been three exchanges.

But thirty years had passed — this day, the culmination of his life's swordsmanship.

How close had he managed to come?

"If that is agreeable, by all means."

And today — how much closer could he come?

As when he had first taken up the sword as a child, the instant he drew his blade, the premonition of defeat was there.

And yet, even as she took up her curved blade, his heart was still — a mirror's surface, reflecting the moon on high.

Once, he had called his school Suigetsu-ryuu.

The solitary moon in the empty sky — from his resignation that it was merely a phantom, beyond human reach, he had likened his own sword to the moon reflected on water.

And in truth, what his hand held was but a water-moon.

But what stood before him now was not.

In her hand was the radiant principle of the moon, a true and certain mastery.

Not a phantom, not an illusion — a heavenly sword that laughed away his foolish resignation.

The phantom moon he had once caught in a water mirror was now nothing but foam — and from that day, he had captured only the outline of that moon.

Both alike, the swords they held were the formless, infinite drooping willow.

That he could stand in that form before her filled him with pride.

"Whenever you are ready, Fujisai-san."

"I changed that name, on that day. ……It was presumptuous, before one such as you."

"……?"

He sank down as if crumbling, and she sank with him.

Even a single step — at the uttermost extreme of principle.

The solitary moon remained far above — he gazed at it, smiled, and narrowed his eyes.

What he was about to deliver was the culmination of his sword.

But it was not a sword wielded to win.

——It was a sword to reclaim the defeat of that day.

○Toran Tribal Confederation Difficulty: ★★★★

Economic Power: 2 Military Power: 4 Technology: 2 Population: 3 Personnel: 3

One of the three major powers in the southern part of the eastern continent. A nomadic confederation controlling the vast southern plains, they subjugate and rule numerous small nations built by agrarian peoples. They believe that an agrarian lifestyle weakens their strength, maintaining their nomadic lifestyle as they have for hundreds of years.

Despite their inferior economic and technological capabilities, they are a strong tribal nation where almost every male is a warrior, with nomadic cavalry forming the core of their military.

Their mobility and information dissemination speed are unprecedented, providing a strategic advantage.

Though their numbers are limited, tens of thousands of nomadic cavalry can easily defeat a much larger force if used effectively.

They can also draw upon the military strength of multiple subjugated nations, boasting a considerable infantry force and a few siege weapons, making their invasion capabilities more than sufficient. They maintain friendly relations with surrounding smaller nations based on military might, with tributes as their primary source of income.

They maintain a strong antagonism toward the Gerganik Kingdom, but due to their proximity to the Sui Empire in the east, they are preoccupied with minor skirmishes.

They are slightly disadvantaged against the Sui Empire due to the difference in national power.

Conversely, the Sui Empire holds a superior position.

A highly refined nomadic tribal confederation with exceptional potential.

★Recommended Troop Type

○Nomadic Cavalry

Their horsemanship is unparalleled, with their light cavalry and light mounted archers possessing overwhelming skill. In plains battles, they can defeat most opponents solely through ranged attacks from horseback.

Only the Gerganik Kingdom in the east can match them; if you can handle their cavalry, you'll have no other enemies in the east's mostly flat terrain.

The advantage of having tens of thousands of cavalry as their main force, rather than infantry, is significant, giving them a great advantage in battles outside of forests or sieges. However, the key is conserving their main forces and winning in such battles.

△Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Granting the nomadic cavalry free rein will likely result in defeat. The key is to restrict their freedom and secure key points.

Their scattered settlements are their weak points; attack them, accepting losses, to limit their options. Focus on interrogating prisoners to obtain information. Due to their tribal nature, significant defeats lead to a loss of authority (affecting the loyalty of subordinate nations and tribes, particularly influential in tribal confederations), causing them to become fractured.

○Units to Watch Out For in the Main Scenario

・Sardo Toran

The Toran chief, possessing high leadership and strategic skills, making him a formidable opponent when commanding nomadic cavalry. Although his charisma is not low, his notoriety is high, so using intrigue to create discord among his subordinates is a viable strategy.

・Rafael Ziga

The king of a small nation that has long been subordinate to the Toran.

A commander with military prowess, leadership, and strategy, he is undefeated in siege warfare, earning him the moniker "Destroyer of a Hundred Cities." His "Hundred Cities Breaker" trait enhances his tactical success rate in siege and siege defense battles, making open field battles preferable.

Gain a strategic advantage before engaging him.

○Sui Empire Difficulty: ★★★★

Economic Power: 4 Military Power: 5 Technology: 4 Population: 5+

Personnel: 4

One of the three major powers located at the easternmost point of the eastern continent.

Possessing fertile land and a long coastline, it has the largest territory and population in the eastern continent.

Although not as powerful as Elsren, its national strength is exceptionally high, making it arguably the strongest nation in the east.

However, it is plagued by constant internal strife, facing civil war every few years.

Misjudgment can lead to national collapse in the face of external threats.

Conversely, it has accumulated significant military expertise, boasting impressive training and military technology.

The quality of its commanders is high; if unified, conquering the east would not be difficult.

The emperor in the main scenario, while not incompetent, is mediocre. If aiming for unification, replace him early.

Like Gerganik, its distance from Alberan in a historical difficulty setting makes continental unification impossible; however, unlike Gerganik, a victorious alliance is comparatively easier to achieve.

★Recommended Troop Type

○Crossbowmen, Heavy Crossbowmen

Their crossbows, refined for efficient use of conscripted troops, instantly transform civilians into soldiers. While lacking in range and rate of fire compared to bows, their mid-range power is comparable, with their easy-to-train troops and sheer numbers as their strength.

Though not as skilled as trained archers, such troops are not infinitely available.

If you defeat them today, you can wear them down over the next year or ten years. Their vast human resources allow them to eventually surpass their enemy in numbers, a king's battle tactic that guarantees victory against any adversary.

△Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Stimulating them could unify them; thus, a better approach is to use intrigue to cause division. Engaging the Sui's seemingly endless manpower in a direct confrontation risks exhaustion before victory. If engaging them, aim for a swift victory through overwhelming force, as Alberan did during the unification war.

Their division negates their plentiful manpower and talent, removing the need for fear.

Exercise caution, especially in forested areas where his tactical success rate increases.

・Lu Zen

The Sui Empire's most brilliant strategist, possessing superior leadership and strategy. His other abilities are above average, making him a potential threat equal to, or even greater than, Sinfha.

・Liu Roulai

The Sui Empire's first prince, though young at the start of the main scenario. He grows into a capable ruler, making a unified Sui Empire the strongest in the east; eliminate him early.

○ Toushou Country Difficulty: ★★★★★

Economic Power: 3 Military Power: 4 Technological Power: 4 Population: 3 Personnel: 4

A nation that rules an island located to the east of the continent.

Essentially detached from the continental wars, it is constantly embroiled in internal conflict, achieving unique technological and cultural advancements.

Despite much of its land being covered in forests, it is a rich land, particularly blessed with mineral deposits. However, it is prone to natural disasters, frequently suffering damage from storms, volcanic eruptions, and earthquakes.

Because of this, the mountainous and forested terrain leads to a high ratio of magic users, resulting in high-quality soldiers.

While coveted by the continent for its abundant resources, Toushou has maintained its sovereignty and spun a long history.

If continental dominance is conceded, securing an alliance with Alberan wouldn't be particularly difficult.

★ Recommended Troop Type

○ Heavily Armored Bow Cavalry

A special cavalry unit that mounts heavily armed warriors on sturdy horses, but whose main weapon is the longbow.

Strategic flanking maneuvers using horses are difficult to avoid, giving them a significant advantage as long as the battle takes place within their own territory.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Impassable obstacles are everywhere, and its isolation from the continent makes it difficult to obtain geographical and other information. Fortunately, there is little chance of them attacking the continent, but attacking them will be extremely difficult.

Even if captured, they cannot be trusted, and if they escape, they will continuously attempt ambushes.

They can be described as culturally and technologically advanced, uncivilized tribes that do not shy away from cruelty.

Causing casualties significantly lowers morale on your side.

There is no other option than to eliminate them completely, although the rewards are significant, considerable hardship must be anticipated.

Even after negotiations with the Emperor, there is a high probability of rebellion, leading to the overthrow of the nation and a complete restart.

Negotiations are best carried out only after eliminating dissidents.
