# Chapter 321: Duty — End

The three-month stay came to an end, and exchanges with Kreisharana continued smoothly thereafter.

Scholars were summoned to investigate the land, new seedlings were obtained, and attempts were made to root them there.

To those people, who would accept little more than liquor and practical goods, were brought instead human beings possessed of skill and knowledge.

Alberan's rich technology was something refined by countless cultural exchanges.

Their own craft, advanced in isolation, could by no means be called immature, but Alberan's blacksmiths and carpenters and other craftsmen, and its scholars, possessed a wider scope of knowledge.

Even if they could not fight, they were people who had resolved to dedicate their entire lives to the cultivation of knowledge or skill.

Built upon their dedication, Alberan had obtained its strength as a collective.

Whether directly or indirectly, the battlefields differed, but such people too were warriors deserving of respect — and they too gradually came to accept this.

One who devotes himself to his knowledge, or to his craft, is no different from one who devotes himself to sword or spear.

If the sword I forge should prove dull, then I shall plunge that very blade into myself and die.

There were old blacksmiths who proclaimed it grandly, almost as a challenge.

If the method I propose is mistaken, then I do not mind being killed.

There were scholars who declared it serenely, with a laugh.

As fighters, they could not even have become levied warriors among Kreisharana — yet voices belittling such people likewise faded swiftly.

Conviction and resolve that did not begrudge even one's life, and pride in one's own dedication.

Precisely because the Kreisharana disliked ornament and prized the inner self, they were able to understand.

If there is to be superiority or inferiority among people, it lies within the heart.

It is never something on the outside.

Their way of life, which placed no value on those who could not fight, had been indispensable to that harsh land.

Without it, mere survival would have been difficult.

Their culture was synonymous with living itself, and no one could question its rightness or wrongness.

Only — it was different now from what had come before.

The Kreisharana no longer needed to remain isolated upon that harsh land.

To migrate to a more livable land — the future ought to hold such an option for them as well.

That meaning could be found in things other than mere combat, that one could be needed, that one could be permitted to take pride.

That such a world existed too — that was the future, the possibility, that Argoush wished to convey.

『That a person such as Krische-sama would appear, that a person such as Her Majesty would appear, and now a person such as you……we shall most likely fade slowly out of the world. But Lord Giterns, there is no need for you to grieve over it. We shall fade because we ourselves wish it.』

So Vinsreel and the others spoke.

『Moreover, if there are those born outside, like Lord Giterns, who lament our passing and find our way of life beautiful……even in the future after we are gone, our way of being shall surely be carried on in some form. So long as it is needed, by someone's hands.』

Saying it was enough — accepting their own passing with a smile.

They are strong people, Argoush thought from the depths of his heart.

For their sake, and so that they should not come to regret it, Argoush ran himself ragged.

In this way, day after day, exchanges with Kreisharana accumulated, and letters too were exchanged again and again.

He entrusted matters of the territory to his sons, who had grown into fine men, and his days, though busy, were full — and one such day, what came to him from Anne were two volumes of a book,

"At the Manor of the Noble Hawk……"

The binding was beautiful, weighty black leather embroidered in silver thread — an upper and a lower volume.

The deliveryman had only conveyed that it was a request from Anne; the parcel within contained no letter.

Wondering whether she had simply forgotten to enclose one, but also why on earth she had taken the trouble to send him a book,

Argoush worried over his daughter's customary eccentricity from the sofa of his private study.

Could it be such a wonderful book that she had gone to the trouble of sending it on purpose?

If so, it was not impossible to understand.

He was not particularly fond of books, but Argoush bought all manner of them for the sake of his learning, and when he spent time at home he generally read.

The mansion had a respectable library too, and there was every chance that Anne had misunderstood her father to be a great book-lover; choosing a book as a gift for one's father was no strange thing.

In fact, among the servants too there were those who thought Argoush loved books, and however one looked at it, it was an objective fact that he was a reader.

If anything, he read books only as one form of learning, of cultivation.

If asked outright whether he loved books he could not say so, but if asked whether he disliked them he would answer that he did not.

In the broad sense he was the bookish sort — and if it were a gift from his daughter, his interest was piqued enough to wonder what sort of book it might be.

"Dear, what does the letter sa――……?"

Black soybean tea and cookies on a tray.

His wife Lyna, returning, looked at the book in Argoush's hands and tilted her head.

Her tea-tinted hair hung loosely down before her neck; though some signs of age had begun to show, she was still his lovely, charming wife, his pride.

Clad in a modest navy one-piece dress, even as the wife of Marquis Giterns she still baked cookies with her own hands and threw herself into the household's cooking and laundry alongside the servants, just as she had in her own days as a servant.

He had once tried to make her stop, saying that she was now his wife — but the position of being waited upon seemed uncomfortable for her, and in the end Argoush gave way.

In truth, what had drawn Argoush to her in the first place had been the figure of an industrious, caring woman.

Seeing that Lyna had not changed put him at ease, and he left the matters of his daily life to his wife.

Perhaps the fact that Anne had grown up watching such a mother had also been significant.

Thinking this, he showed her the book, and with a wry smile said only that the book had been sent without anything else.

Oh my, she said, smiling wryly in turn.

"Did she forget to put in a letter, I wonder?"

"Something like that. That's just like Anne."

"Fufu, even after coming to serve at Her Majesty's side, her scatter-brained side hasn't been mended."

Loosening her mouth, she picked up the lower volume, lightly checked its contents — and creased her brow.

"……This is that child's handwriting, isn't it?"

"Anne's?"

Only then did he open the book, and what first leapt to his eye was the table of contents.

Prologue — Anne of the Giterns family, until she became a servant of the Royal Domain.

Chapter One — Ideal and reality. And the eternal goal: meeting the Greatest of Servants.

Chapter Two — The War of Royal Authority and the princesses of the noble hawk.

Chapter Three —

Argoush was struck dumb.

Was this an autobiography of some kind?

He imagined his beloved daughter sending her father a mysterious autobiography, full of self-confidence — well, with Anne it was entirely possible — and he resigned himself to it.

Anne was the sort to do something out of the blue from time to time.

According to Lyna, within Anne herself there was apparently deep deliberation behind such things (though sometimes, it seemed, it was simply a passing whim), but even Argoush, who had watched her since she was an infant, found it hard to read her heart.

The handwriting was certainly Anne's.

Anne's writing, somewhat methodical, was clean and careful.

It was not poor writing, even compared to a professional scribe's.

Yet handwriting reveals character.

The hand of Selene, who had written to Argoush concerning Anne during the civil war, had been forceful and dignified, expressing the bravery and strong will of the daughter of Christand of the Lightning Strike.

The hand of the head servant, Bery, who had several times written letters in her stead, had been graceful, refined, and delicate.

The permission slip Alberinea had once entrusted to Kreisharana had been so identical to Bery's that one could not tell them apart — a hand most likely learned from her, an imitation of her writing.

Queen Kreschenta's hand was queenly through and through.

In a vivid and beautiful script that clearly expressed all gradations of stroke and force — all of them were beautiful, but the inner nature of each seeped out as habit.

Anne's hand truly conveyed her temperament: that she did things carefully.

It was a beautiful hand on the whole, but with a slight forced quality from worrying about how it looked, a faintly vain quality of trying to express a smart-looking script.

Unlike the other four, whose handwriting had simply become what it was naturally, Anne's letters tried by effort to express that beauty — which was, after all, an attractively Anne-like charm.

The table of contents, like Selene's, was forceful and dignified; in passages explaining the merits of others, it was as flowery as Kreschenta's; her ordinary script attempted to mimic Bery's grace, while striving to imitate by effort the flowing run of the line, leaving a slightly mismatched quality.

Just looking at it, the figure of Anne with all her peculiar, stubborn quirks rose to mind, and somehow it was funny.

But the more he read, the more the contents began to worry him.

The book recounted those people whom she had met after coming to the Royal Domain, the chief subject being the Christand household.

Essentially everything was praise of the highest order.

So far as it spoke of their characters, it was not so bad — but Bery, though small, has full breasts and a beautiful waistline; Selene's slender body is as sleek and fascinating as a cat — what on earth she meant her father to take from such passages, he could not understand.

That she was praising their appearance he could grasp; but no matter how one looked at it, the lines were unnecessary.

And on top of that, Her Majesty the Queen and Alberinea — both royalty — were thrown in too, which only made him worry about Anne all the more.

"That girl really……"

"It does sound like Anne, though……"

To Argoush, who held his head, Lyna, who had been peering at the book from beside him, smiled wryly.

What sort of people they were, he had been able to grasp to some degree on meeting them recently, and he had clearly seen how kindly they treated Anne, how affectionately they regarded her.

The more he learned of their figures and daily lives as told in the book, the more was conveyed to him of how dearly Anne loved them, and of how dearly they cherished his daughter Anne.

Considering that, he was unlikely ever to fly into a rage at the book she had written, or to punish her for it — but the contents were dreadful all the same — at the very least, not something to be shown to others.

The lower volume grew yet more extreme in content.

『As ever, sitting on Lord Argan's lap and being made to play at being a dog and indulged in, Her Majesty the Queen――』

Such mysterious extraordinary moments were tossed off lightly here and there in the daily life she described, and after finishing it he resolved, ever more deeply, that this book must be sealed away, or burned — and yet he did not stop reading.

He kept turning the pages onward and onward, because he could read in the chaotic daily life of the mansion that Anne described, the feeling that seeped from between the lines.

From her own birth through to her daily life at the mansion, the reason she spoke of it all so happily — when one considered why, the reason for which this book had been written also became roughly understandable.

"It's almost time I started supper preparations…… let me read the rest later."

"Yes, go ahead."

Even after Lyna left, he kept turning pages.

What was put into words must surely be only a small fragment.

Imagining the countless exchanges that must have taken place between the lines, he read on.

Bery being stabbed, and the daily life like a candle gone out, until Krische returned bringing the dragon.

The hardships and worries of those people in the Five-Nations War were touched upon, and because the everyday life she ordinarily told of was so bright and so absurd, those passages stood out all the more.

Toward the end, references to Krische's adoptive grandfather, called Gallen, became more frequent, and from this he could vaguely guess.

The old man whom Anne deeply revered, whose name had often come up in those daily lives.

A warrior of hunter origin, beloved from the heart even by the unmistakable genius Krische, and held in high esteem by many soldiers.

His age was no different from Argoush's own — only, he was no mana wielder, and the trigger that had led his daughter to compose this letter in the form of a book likely lay with him.

Argoush had heard that he had passed away a little while ago.

The words she had learned from him, the things he had taught her — she wrote each one down as if it were a precious treasure, and as the book neared its end such passages multiplied.

And then, when the matter of Gallen's death was set down, Anne at last began to speak about this book itself.

She wrote that, while she had been born to parents she could respect, and had passed her days surrounded by wonderful people, she herself was an utterly ordinary person, incomparable to any of them.

And that, recalling Argoush's words, she had thought about what she ought to do with her future.

To marry a fine gentleman and enter into a household, to raise her own children — that future too was certainly a happy one, and unlike them, she was a perfectly ordinary woman with no particular gift.

Was she perhaps far too unsuited to spend her whole life in this brilliant world?

『Lord Gallen said that I, who call myself ordinary, was on the contrary a special existence in this brilliant world, and that such a one as I was a most precious existence in this manor. That it is not enough only to draw the bowstring tight; sometimes one is needed who slackens it……because everyone in this manor draws their strings too taut, he said.』

What was depicted in their daily life was a cast of characters out of a picture book.

The royal sister and the queen, fit to be called children of the gods.

The servant who could indulge those two as if they were children.

The noble daughter who at fifteen had become Marshal, and who fulfilled that duty as a matter of course.

Even the servant whom she herself called her rival was extraordinarily talented; everyone connected to them had, as a matter of course, some excellence beyond ordinary people.

『Lord Argan too says that, far from being attentive to me, he is the one always made to attend to me — that he is always being saved by me, who can do nothing well. He laughs and says that one is sometimes needed who can anger one's master, or trouble her. Whether he is saying the same thing as Lord Gallen I cannot quite tell, but to me, even so, he says such things.』

Their hearts must surely be the same.

Anne did indeed have many flaws, but she was a fine daughter of whom Argoush was proud.

He thought that they were truly seeing Anne's good points.

『I cannot believe myself to be the splendid, needed person they say I am. I think they are being kind to me, comforting me with such words, and yet I also think — if even a faint truth I have not noticed lay within those words…… If even a little, a person such as I might be of use to them — that, I wish to believe.』

As he had begun to suspect partway through, this was a letter of farewell.

A letter declaring that, by her own will, she had decided the rest of her life.

『Of course, it may all be my own foolish misunderstanding. Perhaps in truth they are quite exasperated with me, and one day I may yet be cast from the manor. But until that time comes, I shall serve at this manor the people whom I revere, and put forth all the effort that lies within me――』

It was for that reason, on reading this very line,

『――that I wish to fulfill my duty as a servant.』

Argoush pressed his fingers to the inner corners of his eyes.

『Father, who even now stakes his life in a far-off land and fulfills a great office. Mother, who taught me many things kindly and watched over me. And my brothers, to whom I may bring much worry. Yet, as one of the nobility born into the Giterns family handed down from our ancestors, I have resolved in my heart to dedicate to this precious name granted me an oath that shall not change unto eternity.』

——Please, forgive your willful daughter.

Picturing Anne's face, hearing those words of resolve, he lowered his head.

Yet still he could not hold back his tears, and they ran down the bridge of his nose to fall in droplets.

『From the Manor of the Noble Hawk, with the love of Anne Giterns, child of her revered father and mother.』

At those characters set down at the end of the book, he pressed his sleeve to his eyelids.

Until a knock sounded, and Lyna came to call him for supper.

Lyna, when she came, drew close to Argoush's side and, with a wry smile, ran her hand over his head and said, At your age — what is this?

"……Read it later, please."

"Yes. ……Was it something painful?"

No, he shook his head, sniffing, and lifted his face.

"More than anything, it is a letter to be glad of. ……As head of the Giterns family, I am proud above all."

And closing the book, he traced the letters drawn upon its cover.

"As one father, it makes me a little lonely…… that is the kind of letter it is."

"……I see."

At the Manor of the Noble Hawk —— and now, from the Manor of the Noble Hawk.

"Once you've calmed down, come along. The food will get cold."

"Ah, sorry."

Having flown her birth-nest, Anne now lived within that other manor.

The next day he wrote a letter, and Argoush returned to his daily life.

Energetically running about the various lands, he met scholars, craftsmen, and merchants, and as the bridge between Alberan and Kreisharana, he traveled back and forth between his manor and Kreisharana.

The round trip took too long, was too much trouble, they said in concern, and after a time one of their warriors began to stay at Argoush's manor, partly to study the culture of the lowlands.

If one flew from the foot of the mountain, one could reach Kreisharana in less than half a day.

When it came to many people that was not so simple, but they too became gradually more cooperative, and a few of their warriors would carry small numbers there and back, bringing things into a much warmer relationship.

In cooperation with the Alberan army — when the great plan to make a road between Alberan and Kreisharana was underway, magical beasts caused damage, and he was tormented by stomach pains — but when he was tired he would gaze at the book Anne had given him and read its words again and again, refreshing himself.

Nor did the Queen leave it all entirely to him.

When the great plan was at hand, Alberinea sometimes appeared mounted on her Jade Tiger, looking carefree, would solve what was difficult as if it were nothing, and saying that she had work back at the manor and was busy, would return home.

Storm-wing too, some time after Anne had been worrying, was hunted in passing on a visit to greet Kreisharana, and on the route of the planned road nearly ten of them were taken in only half a day.

When he saw Alberinea solving, as a "little errand," problems that made him hold his head, there were times when he wondered whether there was any meaning at all in his own being there — but even so, Argoush could only earnestly fulfill his own duty.

Sensing the existence of the unfathomable Alberinea or the Queen, he could understand his own smallness.

The being of Bery, who was loved and revered by them, and the existence of Selene, who as a matter of course passed her days as the master of the manor where they too dwelled.

They were literally above the clouds, inhabitants of a storied world; thinking of his beloved daughter's daily life passed alongside such people, and thinking of Anne Giterns who had sworn upon her duty and her name to go on living it, there was nowhere in his life for time to let himself rot away.

That his proud beloved daughter might have a father to be proud of.

Just as Anne sought to fulfill her duty, so too did he.

To remind himself of that, he turned the pages of the book until they were grimy with the oil of his hands.

After reading that letter, he had written his reply as a noble.

He could not have her producing more such forbidden, off-limits books.

The greater part of it was about the contents she had written, packed with rebukes that, as the Queen's servant, she should mend her bearing, and over and over he repeated that she was never to write the like again.

And at the very end,

『Above all, I earnestly desire that Anne Giterns shall fulfill her duty. Even in this far-off land, as one nobleman who likewise inherits the name of Giterns, and as one father who, together with his proud beloved daughter, would also fulfill his own duty.』

——At the Manor of the Noble Hawk, with love to the servant who unbinds these pages.

Each time he turned the pages, recalling that he had written so, he renewed his resolve.

He himself was ordinary, an unremarkable man.

Yet that was no reason to throw aside his duty.

He was who he was; whether one had talent or not, each one fulfilled the duty fitting to the abilities he had been given.

The Queen had hers, Alberinea had hers; however blessed they might be, however easily they could solve the difficulties of many, there was no difference in this.

Each, surely, had her own toils and trials, and that was why they staked their lives even against dragons.

To put forth one's whole strength for the sake of something more precious than oneself.

In that there was no superiority or inferiority at all.

Confronting it earnestly, sincerely, he did not mind being shamed or made a fool of.

Even if it were unsightly, even if it were pitiful, still he would dedicate his effort, and proudly speak.

"Do you enjoy making a fool of yourself, idiot? How many times is it now you've been knocked flat? You have neither talent nor quickness. However many years I associate with you, it's the same thing repeated…… If it's hard and you want to quit, you can whine all you like."

"No, once more, if you would, Lord Doukaran."

"Then move your body before you move your mouth. Whether by cowardly ambush or anything else, give it all you have and at least take a single point off me. Even if you are a fool sprawled in the dirt, if you produce results I shall acknowledge it."

"Yes."

That this was his duty — laughing, dignified.

— Storm-wing Subjugation Battle

"……So Storm-wing's nest is on this cliff face."

Above the nest where Storm-wing slept — silver hair seated behind Vinsreel.

Krische looked down with a "hoheee."

Storm-wing's nest existed within a great hollow at the top of a tall cliff face that loomed up like a wall.

In the depths of a colossal fissure in the cliff, like a yawning maw, Storm-wing was said to sleep quietly.

This place was originally not a cliff but a mountain, said to have been gouged off on one side and born from the strife of dragons.

In fact, viewed from the other side the cliff was a complete mountain, and on the cliff face one could see traces as if the rock had melted.

That hollow too, no doubt, had been formed by the same effects.

Why Storm-wing, who in these past several hundred years should once have been slain, made even this place its lair, was unknown — perhaps there was some reason unknown to them, or perhaps it was due to the creature's wisdom.

So as not to be detected, they descended slowly from above onto the top of the cliff, and Vinsreel began to explain.

"Long ago, I have heard, there were brave men who set foot directly into the nest. But the great fissure of the cliff like a yawning maw…… the depth where Storm-wing sleeps is some distance in, and they could not even approach against its wing-beats; they were repulsed and slain……"

"Wing-beats……"

"Literally, like a storm flattening the forest. Of course it cannot match Yagelnaus-sama's, but even so, for a human body, in that confined place the storm raised had no answer. ……When that occurred, Storm-wing awakened in fury, and the settlement too suffered grievous damage."

"I see…… that's hard."

It would be no easy thing to slay Storm-wing inside that nest.

For Storm-wing, who could raise a storm with a single wing-beat, the wide and deep fissure of the cliff was an excellent environment in which to repulse and slay any intruder with ease.

A single storm raised by those wings would blow the opponent back out into open air.

Krische's miraculous skill, rivaling even a divine being's, was known — but it was far too dangerous to set foot within.

First, Vinsreel and the others would act as bait, draw it out of the nest somehow — and at the moment Storm-wing exposed itself outside the cliff, they would have her finish it.

That was likely the optimal solution.

"Yes. So we shall act as the bai――"

"All right then, Krische will go take a quick look."

"――Krische-sama!?"

Before he could call her back.

Krische, gripping her spear, dropped down the nearly vertical cliff face as if it were nothing.

——Had she even been listening to him at all?

Stupefied, Vinsreel and the others mounted their griffins and dove in haste, but could not catch up,

"――!?"

The instant Krische's hand caught the upper edge of the fissure and disappeared inside, what rang out was the cry of a monstrous bird, freezing one's innards — and in an instant it broke off, and a tremor ran through the earth.

What Vinsreel and the others at last saw on arriving was, in the depths of the great fissure, the figure of the monstrous bird, wings spread, having lost everything from the neck up, crumbling to the ground.

And Krische, holding down her skirt, tilting her head slightly.

"I was listening, but it really is rather large after all. Hmm, I wonder if it'll be tasty……"

——A gentle breeze brushed Vinsreel's cheek.

— Magical Beast Hunt

Atop a griffin — above the mountains.

Krische sat sideways behind Lila, gripping her spear and peering downward.

"Ah, there's another one. Lila, a little further back."

"E-ehm, yes…… Larnel."

What on earth could she be seeing?

Lila watched Krische, while still seated, hurl her spear straight downward at terrifying speed.

The spear was sucked into a gap in the trees, and there came the death-cry of a Jade Tiger.

The trees shook and toppled as it thrashed about, and after a little while that too quieted.

"The shake threw off my aim a tiny bit. Poor thing, what a pity."

"I-I'm sorry……"

"No, just now Krische was simply clumsy. Errr, that one over there…… Mister Ruuza. I've finished it off, so please bleed it and dress it."

"U-understood……"

"Hmm, what else……"

Speaking to the bewildered griffin riders, she dangled both legs idly.

——That day, one Indigo Deer and seven Jade Tigers vanished from the mountains.

# Chapter 322: The Threat Lurking in the Abyss

Note: A diversion-piece "future-if." It's fairly rough, so anyone who dislikes the worldview being broken (

That this earth is a great round sphere — that much was already known.

——What sort of thing was this world, after all?

The one who put a stop to all the various theories was Alberan's queen, Grabareine.

Astrology, divining the future from the movements of the stars — in the lands surrounding old Alberan, the positions and motions of the stars were thought to indicate omens, good and ill, and to influence many things in the world.

For that reason astrology was at the time prized as an important factor in political judgment, and to silence it she set forth, theoretically and by physical law, that this world was one of the stars floating in a vast space — the cosmos — calculating its orbit and the laws of motion to demonstrate it.

A vast world and stars, bound together by gravitation and centrifugal force.

There was none who could refute Grabareine, who had unraveled the mystic as physics; the mysteries of astrology were lost, and her theory is even now used as the foundation of astronomy.

Grabareine likely had no interest in the cosmos itself.

She had merely unraveled it because it was necessary.

Although she had built a perfect foundation, she set down nothing beyond, and astronomical research thereafter was treated more as the diversion of dilettantes.

Bery, too, thought of it that way, and had no strong interest of the kind to undertake such research deliberately, but on the desk — gazing at the schematic of the cosmos drawn on parchment, even her heart was set leaping.

"I see…… so it's an unimaginable size, isn't it."

In the room, on the sofa, sat Selene and Bery, and on each of their laps, Krische and Kreschenta.

The usual scene; with Elvena attending at one side as well, all eyes were directed at the desk.

"That you should feel that way is because of your low standard of intellect."

"……Yes. Just as you say──"

"Honestly, you can't even do simple arithmetic properly; how disagreeable. Must I, of all people, teach you the four operations of children? I went to the trouble of explaining it to you, and after all that the impression you finish with is 'unimaginable size'? Of all the foolish——"

"……, woof."

"W-what is it……?"

Bery, smile still in place, pressed a cookie to Kreschenta's mouth on her lap and silenced her.

"Mmgh……!"

"It's 'woof,' Kreschenta-sama."

And while ruffling her head as one might a dog's, she deepened her smile.

Ignoring Selene's exasperated gaze, Bery lowered her eyes to the parchment.

Countless beautiful circles, impossible to think had been drawn freehand, were inscribed there, depicting conceptually this star and the worlds beyond.

Great celestial bodies and small, countless bodies tracing spirals, drifting through endless emptiness.

Noticing that Elvena was likewise peering at the parchment, she asked.

"Do you like this sort of thing too, Elvena-sama?"

"Yes, it's very interesting. Astronomy is something I know nothing about……"

Elvena answered, eyes likewise lit with curiosity.

"Fufu, I'm not so well-versed myself, but…… one can imagine all sorts of things, and it's fun. With such an unimaginable number of stars, I wonder if perhaps space-people exist."

"……Space-people?"

"Yes. Just as we happened to be born, perhaps other people happened to be born too…… Considering the number of stars and the years, it doesn't seem out of the question."

"I see, that's true. Insects, fish, lizards, snakes…… perhaps even clever plants."

While giggling together and feeding cookies to Kreschenta's mouth, the drowsy Krische atop Selene asked,

"Bery, do you want to see space-people?"

"Let's see…… not so much wanting to see them, more that imagining them is fun."

"I see…… ehehe, it would be nice if they exist. Mister Space-People——"

"It would be nice if you exist, Mister Space-People——" the voice of a small creature on a small planet.

In the unimaginable void, an echo that should ordinarily vanish in an instant.

That small creature, in conversation, had noticed a hole in her own "safety net," and rewrote the network inscribed at the source of the world, propagating that network.

The Fuyopita-net — the world-rule that would later be called the Ether Limit — closed the path leading to the source, and set a limit upon the inscription of formulae by mana.

Restraining the leaping advance of magical power, it would induce disorder in formulae of large-scale description that exceeded a certain range, suppressing them.

It was a "Rule" inscribed in the deepest place of the world to prevent the destruction of that world by overwhelming magic.

And so the maiden's voice, connected to the source, was carried unintentionally onto the network.

——It would be nice if my beloved servant enjoyed it.

That unconscious wish, from the source, sent her voice resounding through the unimaginably vast world of stars.

It would be nice if you exist, Mister Space-People——

From source to source.

The propagating network, twisting space-time, carried the voice through the void.

From star to star, as if to fill that endless emptiness one by one.

It would be nice if you exist, Mister Space-People——

A journey through sources transcending time and space. The network bridged distances by filling them with time, simply going further and further.

As might be expected, there were many familiar faces, and I sometimes encountered acquaintances.

I had just come to the royal castle storeroom to fetch tea leaves.

There was Karen Schlotte-sama.

A truly approachable person, with charming chestnut hair.

Born to a marquis house likewise, she had come to the royal domain about two years before me.

She had served as my mentor, and as we shared a room we had quickly become close; while working in the royal domain we had often paired up.

Since I had become a Christand servant and returned to the royal domain, we had glanced at each other from afar a few times, but it had been a long while since we had spoken properly like this.

But, in contrast to my smile,

"Wellll……"

Karen-sama, seeing me, knit her brow into a strange face.

Then with hand to forehead she sighed.

"Long time no see. ……Honestly, I still can't believe you, of all people, are a servant at Her Majesty's side. ……Drive really is a frightening thing."

"Even when I left the royal domain, I never thought it would come to this……"

I smiled wryly and answered Karen-sama, who had leaned her back against the shelf.

When I left the castle it had been only the simple feeling of wanting to learn at Argan-sama's side, but by what fate things had become turbulent.

The country fell into civil war, and Christand was at the heart of it — Princess Kreschenta-sama took refuge there — and I had a series of surprises.

"But, well…… everyone is so dumbstruck — it's a little ticklish."

"Of course they are. Anyone would be dumbstruck. There's clumsy-Anne walking next to Her Majesty as if it were normal. When I first saw it, I thought my eyes would pop out. I doubted them not twice but seven times over."

"S-seven……"

"You said something nonsensical like 'I've met my fate,' suddenly left the royal domain, and then a little later you come back as Her Majesty's attendant — who could believe that?"

Karen-sama sighed again.

She was right; I could not deny it.

"S-strictly speaking I'm not exactly an attendant either……"

"You're not?"

"The Christand house has only three servants, so by role it often turns out that way…… but in terms of servant rank and ability, and in terms of Her Majesty's trust, formally Argan-sama is the attendant, and I am her stand-in, I suppose."

In fact, for important ceremonies, festivals, and foreign guests, Argan-sama always accompanied her.

Considering Her Majesty's position, that is how it should be at all times, but Argan-sama was busy with various matters such as Christand household financial management.

For ordinary times that were not particularly important, Elvena-sama and I accompanied her, and since Elvena-sama usually assisted Krische-sama, by elimination it was I who attended Her Majesty.

It was not as if it was clearly defined who was Her Majesty's attendant; in the Christand household it was treated as something seemingly trivial (a careless attitude for so important a duty, often decided by the flow of conversation).

But at the very least, between Elvena-sama and me, the common understanding was that the official attendant was Argan-sama.

Her Majesty was a person whose moods were easy to read, and in fact, when Argan-sama was at her side, her mood was excellent.

The better Her Majesty's mood, the more talkative she became, and when ill-humored she fell silent — but at Argan-sama's side, all day she would happily grumble, complaining of "this is missing, that is missing," speaking selfish things she would not say to Elvena-sama or me.

Her appearance, like a child wanting to be paid attention to, was so adorable it tightened the chest.

"Argan-sama is busy with the management of all manner of Christand household affairs in her hands, so it is difficult for her to leave the manor every day."

"There's hardly a duty more important than being Her Majesty's attendant, I'd think…… could one not just hire a new servant — well, the situation is what it is. And bringing in someone unknown to Her Majesty's side in this situation would be unsettling."

"Yes. Her position being what it is."

Her Majesty was lovable to no end, but at the same time someone who clearly distinguished outside from inside.

It must be because she was raised in an environment beyond what I could understand.

Her wariness, with the recognition that her own life was always being targeted as a basic premise, was very strong.

She paid attention to truly trifling changes.

For example, when she had left a room in the royal castle, even the slightest difference — the position of a teacup, signs that something had been moved — would put her on guard.

Whatever room she used, she always stationed guards there, and even the castle's working servants were forbidden from entering or leaving while she was in the castle.

When I once thoughtfully tidied up secretly, she silently went over the entire room.

Realizing belatedly what was happening, when I explained that it was I who had done it, I was scolded sharply for doing such confusing things — fresh in memory.

In ordinary times too, she kept everything she wore at the manor, since she could not know what might be done; she did not leave any daily items or personal belongings in the royal castle, and was very strict about such things.

The Her Majesty at the manor and the Her Majesty even one step outside it had a slightly different air; outside one often felt a slight tension.

Precisely because she was that kind of person, increasing the number of close servants seemed difficult.

Elvena-sama had been saved by Krische-sama from slave status, and was deeply indebted to the Christand house.

Her elder sister was also a private soldier whom Krische-sama valued highly; that part too was trusted, I think.

I too had come to Christand purely with Argan-sama in mind.

It was not as though I came with the audacity of becoming Her Majesty's attendant; it was a coincidence.

Perhaps there was the thought too that a clumsy woman like me could not be an assassin after her life — but at any rate, I had come to be trusted.

That said, given those circumstances, considering bringing in another servant besides Elvena-sama and me, who lived under the same roof, would surely require commensurate reason.

The number was indeed small.

For ceremonies and festivals we had to leave the manor unattended; if there were one more like Karen-sama, that might bring peace of mind, but I did not think Her Majesty would keep her near for the reason that she was a senior servant whom I trusted.

"There were apparently such stories from when Her Majesty was young…… several princes died suspicious deaths."

"Yes; I do not understand politics, but……"

It was only recently that the rumor had spread that it was Her Majesty who killed her brothers — under His Highness the Royal Brother.

The royal palace was a dangerous place where intrigue ran rife, and Her Majesty's good mood at Argan-sama's side surely came also from that.

Generally she might be considered nervous.

She paid attention to every minute thing so as not to trouble Her Majesty, and when Argan-sama was at her side, the air around Her Majesty was as soft as when she spent her time at the manor.

Trust and reliance — even though she trusted Elvena-sama and me, in the true sense the only one she relied on was likely Argan-sama.

Lacking numbers was a wish for what one didn't have — first I should become the kind of servant who, alone, gave such trust that she would not feel any shortage.

"Enviable, but not enviable, that's the position. ……Christand is a flower-garden, with no men, and seems likely to be tiring in various ways."

"Flower-garden……"

"Of course, serving Her Majesty or the Marshal is something to admire…… but it's all women. For someone like me there's only stress without reward, sort of……"

"Karen-sama is — well, frank……"

"Quiet, you. That's normal — most servants are looking for romance."

Karen-sama said it almost glaring.

"From a regular servant's perspective, you're abnormal. Being happy just to live as a servant — are you planning to spend your whole life like that? What about marriage?"

"For now, not particularly…… I do have a desire to enter a household of course, but I'll think about it once such a chance comes."

"At that rate you'll be past your prime in no time. You're a shame in various ways……"

And Karen-sama brought her face close.

"You're quite scatterbrained and an airhead, hopelessly clumsy, but your house is a historic name even if regional, and you're a hard worker, kind, and pretty. ……If I were a man I'd want to woo you myself."

When I blushed, Karen-sama gave a small laugh and stepped back.

"M-me, hardly…… there are far more wonderful peo——"

"Your eyes have grown spoiled. Living in a manor like that, of course it would be so. Cake of course is fancy in look as well as taste, but cookies are delicious too, aren't they? In that, you're a top-grade cookie."

"A top-grade cookie……"

It was probably praise, but a baffling assessment.

Karen-sama sighed.

"Sigh. I wish I'd been born with a happy-go-lucky personality like yours. You always look happy, you know."

"Karen-sama, you've been blessed with more than enough…… you're beautiful, dexterous, and clever."

"If half-clever, foolish is more relaxed. Unless one has talent beyond ordinary…… like Selene-sama or Krische-sama. ……Ah, I should be calling them Margrave-sama and Alberinea now."

That was not limited to Karen-sama.

Generally that was the common opinion; Selene-sama was often spoken of in the same breath as Krische-sama.

Daughter of the late hero, Christand of the Lightning Strike.

In place of her father, who fell in the civil war with honors, she at fifteen took command of the army and won victory over His Highness the Royal Brother, dreaded as the Black Lion in the surrounding world, avenging him.

Now made Marshal, even so, without making any great complaint heard, she stood above experienced senior warriors and carried out her duties with dignity, as a matter of course.

For a life of merely fifteen-some years, an extravagant career.

But the impression I had, being close to her, was different.

Of course she was a remarkable person, and I shared the opinion that she was a rare prodigy.

But at the manor she returned later than anyone, and even when Anne went to bed light still leaked from her room; always paperwork or study, or some kind of writing.

Not slovenly — rather, she did everything with great care — but her hair was always slightly mussed, and weariness showed in her eyes.

To stand beside Krische-sama, that incomparable genius.

Because I could understand that hardship, even faintly.

Selene-sama's greatness lay not in her superior talent but in her spirit, I thought.

Even after the late head of the family fell in battle, she recovered in a short time and, with a smile, bound together experienced soldiers, played the role others demanded of her by pushing herself, and never voiced complaint.

Such a person being a girl younger than myself — when I see Selene-sama, I straighten my back.

"Well, enough small talk. Whatever else, it's a promotion; congratulations. There'll be hardships, so if anything comes up, lean on me. As your senior, I'll help with what I can."

"Yes, thank you very much."

"The Christand household itself aside, there must be plenty of fine men around it; storing up favors with you might benefit me too."

"……Frankness and honesty are good points, I think, but you might want not to say such things……"

"It's a sign that I trust you."

She gave a small laugh, took some tea leaves from the shelf, and opened the door.

"Well, see you. Watch out you don't trip and anger Her Majesty."

"I-I'll be careful……"

"Just that one thing has me worried."

And waving her hand lightly, Karen-sama left.

Wonderful as ever.

I smiled as I saw her off, took the tea leaves, packed them into a small jar, and left the storeroom.

And just at the entrance of the royal castle — at the entrance hall — I ran into Selene-sama.

Catching sight of me, she came down the staircase, lifting her hand lightly.

"Good work, Selene-sama."

"Mm, you too. An errand for Bery?"

"Yes, just some tea leaves."

The gatekeepers standing at the entrance — the moment they saw Selene-sama, they straightened up to the limit and opened the great double doors.

She, descending the steps, gave them a glance and a small smile.

And then walked through the doors with Anne.

Such bright efficiency in a small exchange.

She really was born to stand above people.

Compared to when I had passed through, the soldiers' bearing was clearly different — taut, hard air.

Without any words, a smile alone changed everything.

Brilliance without any unpleasant edge, a noble air.

Truly a person of remarkable presence.

"How is it? Even though for you this was your old haunt."

"I worked at a guest manor, so 'old haunt' isn't quite right…… but seeing acquaintances, they are surprised. Just now, in fact, I had some such conversation with a senior who had taken care of me."

"I'm sure. I feel out of place too. To think that just the other day I received the regular knight investiture like a stone statue."

Selene-sama smiled wryly with a look of exasperation.

"Krische and Bery have settled in completely; they walk around as if they were born and raised here. Having you and Elvena around is a help. You have a normal sense of values."

"N-normal sense of values?"

"Yes. Krische, as you can see; Bery is also quite strange. The place we live, our position — all sorts of environments have changed, but in three days they're back to normal. It's a sickness, a sickness."

The way of putting it aside, I could understand.

Krische-sama seemed happy wherever she was so long as Argan-sama and Selene-sama were there, and seemed not to care a bit about her own position (in fact in such discussions she treated it as someone else's matter).

Argan-sama, for her part, in the first few days made the rounds and met all the gatekeepers, storehouse keepers, and servants involved in their royal-domain life; she had broadened acquaintance even with people I, who had lived here for years, had never met.

Frightening gatekeepers and difficult storehouse keepers were passed easily once she said it was Christand household business; even unfamiliar senior servants showed consideration to the likes of me.

Doing the obvious as a matter of course is, I think, very difficult.

Smooth proceedings are an ideal, and everything ought to be so, but it often is not.

There are stern people, difficult people, ill-natured people.

Friction occurs everywhere, and before I had been troubled by it.

But since returning to the royal domain, everything had gone strangely smoothly.

We were not turned away for not having permission; we were not refused things we wanted on the grounds of low stock; we were not unfairly saddled with unrelated tasks.

Making the obvious obvious — making "obvious" actually function — that is what Argan-sama, as a servant, did.

In the sense of being abnormal, Selene-sama's argument was sound.

"Things have changed and it's been hard on me; how about you? — that's the ordinary, normal kind of conversation I want. But Krische and Bery look as if it's somebody else's problem when I say things like 'that must be hard,' and the only one who can't keep up with the environment is me. It makes me look like the fool."

"I see……"

"Honestly, why am I Marshal? Imagine the feelings of people who have to call a girl not yet twenty 'Marshal-kakka' — it's worse than a bed of nails. ……Ah, I really wish I could share in Krische's lightheadedness."

She must have a lot pent up.

Words came one after another, as if waiting; Selene-sama sighed.

I could only understand it through trite imagining, but it must be hardship beyond imagining.

A spectacular promotion — but if it happened to me, joy would not be the response.

If suddenly placed in a position from which I could even order around the servants of the royal domain or my old Irune Estate or even Argan-sama, I would be unable to bear it, and would take to bed in three days.

No, half a day would be too much.

Even the bracket "servant" was frightening; Selene-sama was the kingdom's Grand Marshal.

Nobles trace back to warriors who serve the king.

For instance, even between nobles of the same rank, because of the gravity of standing on the dangerous battlefield and bearing national defense, there is a custom in the kingdom of placing military nobles above.

The pinnacle of those military nobles — Marshal — by rank-of-office is second only to His Majesty.

Effectively, the head of all the kingdom's nobility.

For that reason, fame and lineage and accomplishments were strongly weighed, as in the late Duke Keesliton or His Highness the Royal Brother.

Even where there was achievement, when family rank was insufficient to be made Marshal, it was common to marry a princess to the man and grant him a duke's title forcibly — such adjustments were made to fit one to stand above all nobles, and for someone to be Marshal while remaining a margrave was something exceptional among the exceptional.

By formality, custom, and so on, objectively making Selene-sama Marshal was forced and unsuitable.

『Oh, fine. Let Marshal be Selene-sama, then.』

『"Fine" is not the answer! Father is one thing, but I am, by every measure, unsuitable, I'm telling you! I just received the regular knight investiture the other day!』

『Onee-sama isn't motivated at all, and Margrave Velraigh and the others all say Selene-sama is suitable. If you say so, persuade them yourself again.』

『Ugh……』

— such, just the other day, had been the discussion.

Hearing it, from my standpoint, it was inevitable — the Christand nobles had truly thought from the heart that Selene-sama alone was fit to be Marshal, and circumstance allowed nothing else.

Yet that was internal; whether it could be understood by other nobles was another matter.

"Faren-sa— Faren is being considerate, and Korkis is running about talking to people, so it's somehow holding together — but every time I meet other military men, the heavy air nearly suffocates me……"

Margrave Faren, the assistant marshal, was a former superior of the late head of the family.

Krische-sama called him by the dreadful 'pet name' Skeleton, but he was old enough to be Selene-sama's grandfather, even great-grandfather; the Christand commanders were all veterans of fearsome service.

In the brief time I served as adjutant to the late head, watching her, I understood that Selene-sama held strong respect for those men, who had been her father's brothers-in-arms.

Circumstance or no, to stand above them, in peacetime, would meet considerable resistance.

Her mental burden was beyond my imagining.

"……Sigh. No good. Voicing complaints like this."

"N-no! As a servant, to have such words spilled in my hearing is gladdening…… er, it is an honor."

"A-an honor……?"

"I'm sorry…… how shall I say, easing one part of one's master's mental burden is also a duty of a servant. So please don't say that, and tell me as much as you wish. If even a little of it eases your mind, I would be glad."

"H-haa……"

Selene-sama wore an indescribable face.

I felt pathetic for being unable to say something more apt.

"E-em…… sharing in someone's burdens, serving someone — I had longed for that, and being told a master's complaints, it actually makes me happy…… while I was at the royal domain there was nothing like that."

"Ah, well…… in that kind of manor it would be different from being a household servant like Bery."

"……Yes. Although there were guests, there was no master to serve…… of course, if one says Her Majesty was, then that is so, but then, unlike now, she was distant."

Selene-sama thought a moment, and smiled wryly.

"Fufu, wanting to serve someone — you really are a bit unusual. Well, considering you came barging into the fortress alone out of admiration for Bery, that's old news now."

"T-that is……"

"……But, well, that's how it is. I, too…… insisted on going to the battlefield to help Father. Not only that, of course."

She said it with a slightly distant look, lonesomely.

Complaints were not allowed in the midst of war.

She had lost her father, and in a short time recovered, taken over the army, and led it.

I had thought she was simply someone very strong, but when Selene-sama said "Father" in this way, she seemed lonely; I was reminded that she was a girl her age.

Her mother had passed early, and her father too.

For soldiers, that is not so rare a story.

But just because it is not rare does not mean one accepts it.

In that respect I was fortunate.

I had left home in a great quarrel, but my brothers and the rest were all alive.

They were not soldiers, and I had not even known such anxiety.

If even one of them died, I would weep unsightly, and would drag it about for some time.

I do not think I could put on so fine a face as this.

The people of this manor had all lost many close ones, and had each suffered fitting tragedies.

Argan-sama had lost her parents and elder sister, and Krische-sama, I had heard, had her foster parents killed by bandits.

Her Majesty had lost her attendants, and even from the brief account given me of Elvena-sama's life, surely it had not been days that could be called happy.

Probably, that was part of it.

Selene-sama was a serious person, who tried to be strong.

Even having lost her dear father, she would not allow herself to pour out her grief like a child.

"Everyone has overcome tragedy in proportion," she would think, and bear it.

But, after all, for a girl of fifteen, the position was painfully heavy.

The Marshal position was the same — Kreschenta-sama is Her Majesty, Krische-sama is Alberinea, all the manor is overseen by Argan-sama; everyone was too capable.

Even after saying so often that she was not suited, once it was decided there was nothing for it but to do it, and Selene-sama would do it.

And Selene-sama had the ability to do it, if she pushed herself.

Whether that was good or bad, I do not know.

"Well, no point sulking now. ……Don't tell Bery anything as unsightly as my voicing complaints."

"Y-yes…… Selene-sama?"

"I'll do some practice swings to refresh, then go back. Tell Bery that too."

When we arrived in front of the manor, she said this and drew her sword from its sheath.

Holding it as if fixing on something straight ahead, she stood——

"Wow……"

The garden in front of the manor — sword in hand, blade swung, dancing in elegant golden threads.

In the blink of an eye countless flashes of the sword ran, the cutting wind sounding in layers.

Not merely swinging — there must be opponents in her imagination.

She moved her body at times to avoid something, twisted her hips — a brilliant sword-dance.

A swordsmanship out of place with her beautiful girlish appearance.

But the polished steel-like presence and tension stole the breath, the eyes.

Selene-sama, whom Krische-sama called clumsy, had a thrilling power for someone like me.

Father too was a quite skilled swordsman.

In our hometown "Margrave Giterns is a noble of both the brush and the sword" was famous.

Watching him train my brothers and the bodyguards, overwhelming them, I think objectively he was very skilled.

Therefore, when I first saw Selene-sama's sword, I was astonished.

Of course it would not do to compare a born military noble like Selene-sama directly with my father, but the sword of her at fifteen had been brilliant beyond words, captivating the eye.

The father's sword in my memory was that of a man past fifty, polished by time; Selene-sama was still fifteen — almost a child.

At the spirit and pressure unbecoming of such a girl, "Are there really people like this in the world? — As expected of the daughter of the famous Christand house," I had been moved.

Conservatively put, Selene-sama may rightly be called a genius — and on top of that she gathers respect even from far-older soldiers, an effort-maker not arrogant in talent.

Even with circumstance, she was named Marshal and discharged its duties as a matter of course — she is, indeed, an outstanding person no one can fault.

It was through Selene-sama that I learned the breadth of the world.

"Whew…… Want to try, Anne?"

"No, I……"

"To get used to your body, exercise is best. I've gradually shed the strangeness too……"

"I thought Selene-sama had completely gotten used to it; still a little?"

"In small motions, yes."

Selene-sama opened and closed her right hand to check, then raised both hands.

Bending her back, she swayed her supple, slender body left and right.

If Argan-sama is feminine charm condensed into a thick essence, Selene-sama is feminine charm refined into something sharp.

Slender waist and long limbs — even in plain training trousers and shirt, she was so beautiful it was almost unfair.

Whether in dress or military uniform, even in such an outfit, the words "doesn't suit her" did not come to mind.

Selene-sama, twisting her hips, made a dissatisfied "hmm."

In these two months Selene-sama seemed completely accustomed to this body, but some slight oddness must remain.

"Changing clothes, writing, turning the pages of a book — things I used to do without using mana — there's still some strangeness. With the sword, I had been depending on muscle less for some time, so I got used to it quickly, but…… how to put it, a sense of being unable to quite reach hasn't gone away."

"That is true……"

The feeling of a body one ought to have mastered suddenly being inconvenient.

When you get used to it, that's easier and better, Argan-sama had said, and indeed depending on mana to move the body is less straining and produces more force; but unaccustomed is unaccustomed.

Difficulty there was.

I held out a towel; with thanks she received it and wiped her face and neck.

Sweat comes, pain comes; on waking she felt heavy; the hair grew.

Sensation-wise there was little difference from before, but it seemed our bodies were thoroughly protected against injury — gripping a knife by the blade did not cut the hand; being struck with a hammer did not damage the body, apparently.

When still unaccustomed I had slipped on the stairs and struck the back of my head — though the impact was such that death would not have been surprising, there was only impact, no pain.

Mid-level pain seemed to remain — pain not reaching injury, such as crushing Kreschenta-sama by tripping, was unchanged.

I remember Lila-sama, who had hit her little toe in the bathroom, screaming and crouching with tear-filled eyes — fresh in memory.

If you stop breathing it feels uncomfortable, but in fact even breathing was unnecessary.

If you ran you would tire and lose breath, and there was no major sensory difference there — the concept being "as similar to before as possible, yet a very durable body that will absolutely not die."

The problem was self-collapse — perception fading and reverting to a state of soul.

Even now, every day, Krische-sama and the others were checking our bodies, and it would continue for several years, it seemed.

When trying to make my mind blank to get used to the body and concentrating my consciousness on mana, I had nearly set off for the place called the source — for several days afterward, Krische-sama, while repeatedly scolding me huffily, had stuck to my side.

Lately she had been saying we were quite stable, but unease must remain.

It seemed Krische-sama and Kreschenta-sama went over to the other world to shop, but until we were completely settled, she had not even allowed Argan-sama, who had used her body well from the first day, to accompany.

Once you returned to soul-state, it was a do-over from the start.

It would be a long time before I would be allowed to accompany.

"You don't have to keep me company, you know? Spend it as you like…… With Bery and Krische so eager, work hardly counts. Watching me swing a sword can't be that fun."

"No, it isn't…… Selene-sama's sword is worth seeing."

"Said that way, I'm a little embarrassed."

Selene-sama smiled wryly and looked at the sword in her hand.

The blade was slim and elegant, but the back was thick and gave a sense of strength.

Selene-sama's beloved sword — apparently, the one her late father had given her, which she still used carefully.

The decoration was carefully polished, the leather wound at the hilt new — almost as if new, but the remaining nicks did give a feel of weight.

"My father loved swords too and was quite a swordsman……so I think I can recognize true skill."

"Fufu, true skill, eh. Times I've used the sword in actual combat — first battle and the civil war — you can count them. The sword, well, I'm decently accomplished."

"Just decently," she said, and laughed brightly.

A voice and expression like a cool wind passing through; the appeal opposite to Argan-sama's, which envelops and captures.

Selene-sama had a noble bearing, the so-called "ardent spirit" — she always wore an air that made one straighten one's back.

The air — yes, the air.

The intelligent yet adorable ideal servant.

The young, lofty, deeply affectionate girl-Marshal.

The two princesses, with talent called the children of gods, yet purer than anyone.

They were truly inhabitants of a story.

They themselves were people who deeply loved peaceful daily life, but their existence, their way of being, was so vivid and brilliant.

That, surely, even changed the air of a place.

When walking with Argan-sama, a comfortable time flowed in the surroundings.

Like, say, wildflowers that catch the eye.

In the soft atmosphere she gave off, those around her would, mysteriously, ease their cheeks.

When walking with Selene-sama, a hard, glassy resonance.

Like, say, a flower that takes root on a steep summit.

In her noble, brave spirit, those around her would, mysteriously, set themselves right.

When walking with Krische-sama, a strange world all around.

As though one had seen a walking flower.

A fantastical fairy — those around fell into a fairy-tale.

When walking with Kreschenta-sama, every step rang out high.

As though one had seen a great flower from legend.

Capturing every gaze, dragging all surroundings into a brilliant picture-tale.

——A presence that, by being there alone, changed everything.

The people of the manor I served all had something of that kind, each in their own way.

For instance, even with a starry sky overhead, one's eyes are drawn to the moon's brilliance — a vivid radiance draws human eyes whether one wills it or no.

Even in a world where rank and all else were gone, such an air did not change.

Surely something other than mana.

Even in a world wrapped in soft air, Selene-sama was always noble.

"If you say true skill, your father, who faced the Jade Tiger head-on, is more impressive. He's even acknowledged by the warriors of Kreisharana."

She swung her sword as if dancing lightly, and continued.

The blue mana the wind carried whirled and traced spirals.

"My sword is more or less a hobby. In technique and skill I have some confidence, and one-on-one I'd not lose to most opponents. But that's a different matter. I have not wielded a sword that bet my all."

"But during the civil war, you faced His Highness the Royal Brother yourself."

# Chapter 329: Lone Sky, Water Moon

"……It will not do."

Slightly swaying his now-pure-white long beard, Tetsushūsai said heavily.

Past a hundred years old, his flesh wasted, yet six-shaku and a half tall.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

The basics against Alberan are how to avoid Alberinea-related events. Once "Krische Christand" achieves merit and gains rank, she becomes unstoppable; once Alberinea events progress she gains "Alberinea" and automatically becomes a top-tier commander unit.

Even if you avoid these historical events, or have them turned off, this country remains very strong, and given enough time "Krische Christand" automatically becomes the northern army's commander unit, so when conquering, again avoid combat with the northern army and aim for swift capital fall.

Particularly on Difficulty: Historical, "Krische Christand" has the trait "Heavenly Sword," which dramatically increases tactic-success rate; she will frequently force one-on-one duels on your commander units. Also, when she holds a fortress, large siege engines are destroyed by the same "Heavenly Sword" effect, so siege-and-seal with massed forces is the answer.

Only Galshan can ignore the north and aim for capital-fall by all-out attack from the start. Before northern reinforcements arrive, you must defeat "Dagren Garkha," "Gildanstein Alberan," and "Felwors Keesliton," which is very hard, but afterward the difficulty drops greatly.

However, "Kreschenta Alberan" has an extremely high probability of joining the northern army, triggering Alberinea-related event "The Two Cursed Children." After that, with the event "Princess as Banner," remnants will rise and oppose, so swiftly raise affinity through interaction to prevent and contain her — that is the prerequisite.

Thereafter, protecting the north and keeping the two's happiness gauge (※) high allows stable territorial expansion — an ideal start. Some call it player-cheat.

(※) A trigger for rebellion and so on, one of the personnel-unit parameters. It increases or decreases by personality traits and events. But for these two, isolating them in the same place and leaving them automatically raises happiness above max — a mysterious specification (possibly a bug); unless triggered to hostile action they are completely neutralized. Reports say that when she shows up in random events in this state, she is wearing an apron dress (verification needed).

○ Sui Empire — Difficulty: ★★★★

Economy: 4 — Military: 5 — Tech: 4 — Population: 5+ — Personnel: 4

One of the east's three great nations, easternmost. Rich soil and long coast; the east's largest land area and population. Not as much as Elsren but very high national strength; in pure power the east's top nation, but plagued by recurring civil wars; once every few years troubled by civil war. A misjudgment leaves them exposed to outside threats and they collapse.

Conversely, military know-how is built up; soldier-training and military tech are notable. Commander quality is high; if united, eastern unification is not hard.

Main-scenario emperor isn't witless but is mediocre; if aiming to unify, replace him early.

On Difficulty: Historical, like Gelganik, distance from Alberan rules out continental unification; unlike them, allied victory is comparatively easier — try it.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Crossbowmen — Heavy crossbowmen

How to efficiently use conscripts as army backbone — improving for that point, their crossbows turn amateurs immediately into combat power. Inferior in range and rate, but at mid-range power rivals bows; fast-train and quantity are strengths.

Trained bowmen do not appear endlessly; even if defeated today, in a year or ten years you whittle them down — eventually you outshoot. The king's tactic, born of vast manpower, eventually wins against any opponent.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Provoking can unite them; espionage-driven splitting is the orthodox. Against the inexhaustible Sui in head-on, you'll lose endurance first. If fighting, aim for short-term decisive victory by overwhelming force, like Alberan in the unification war.

If split, abundant personnel and strengths are lost; nothing to fear.

○ Kingdom of Skrif — Difficulty: ××

In the southwest of the continent, adjacent to Karent; surrounded east by Toran and north by Gelganik, but maintains sovereignty long without subjugation. All cities in the great forest are fortified; despite small national scale, blessed in personnel; commander quality consistent across eras.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Light infantry

Light infantry specialized for forest combat. In forests they overwhelm normal light infantry, with greatly reduced fatigue. But Skrif has very few heavy infantry, so light infantry forms the line. Not in melee but head-on, the strengths are lost; against equipment-superior opponents in formation, easily overrun. Use with care.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

So long as you don't attack first, they stay in their territory; leaving alone is fine. Attack when finishing eastern unification, advancing west. Especially "Belzadia Nitrias" is a top-class continent commander; forest-combat capability is overwhelming. Esteem is extreme, raising soldier ability greatly. If you don't mind notoriety, burn the forest.

But a relatively dialogable nation; non-aggression proposals are accepted; not fighting and bypassing is one option. Resists invading Karent; pre-stoke the discord.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Belzadia Nitrias"

The "war-god"; top-class continent commander unit. Effectively defending Skrif's land and sovereignty alone in this strife; in forest combat, even doubled forces fall easily. To fight, prepare a comparable commander and ample forces.

○ Holy Spirit Nation of Futaria — Difficulty: ×

Economy: 1 — Military: 3 — Tech: 2 — Population: 2 — Personnel: 3

A nation built by descendants of the once-flourishing great empire Lusheran. Capital built at the foot of the mountain leading to Dogaruaz where the magical dragon sleeps; declared strict defense-only. High mana-holder ratio; tame the giant blue eagles inhabiting the mountains as mounts — nature close to Kreisharana but not as extreme; closer to a normal nation.

Few in population, but strong soldiers; with many sky-conqueror Blue Eagle cavalry, known in the east.

Legends say past pride summoned the dragon's wrath; faith forbids extravagance, and despite a fine location with quality magical crystal, economy is not vibrant; processing tech is behind.

For continental supremacy, many issues including these to resolve; a hard country, but mere survival is not so hard.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Blue Eagle cavalry

Once feared as messengers of death, these giant blue-feathered eagles. Their wing-beats whip up gusts; arrows do not pass; mounts of theirs cannot be weak.

Like griffin-riders, with sky-bypass, formations are meaningless; the elite-mounted ones raise "Head-Hunt" success greatly. Even doubled force, used well, can take the commander's head and turn the tide.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Like Kreisharana in the "Founding King Bazarishe" scenario — a brute nation with strong soldiers and air cavalry. They declare strict defense; if you don't touch them, they don't strike. Leave alone is best. Decent relations leads to alliance via the Wetharius Pact.

If you must destroy them, ensure high-force units as commanders/escorts to counter "Head-Hunt." Basically no fortified cities, so defense itself is not great; destruction itself is not hard.

But never touch Dogaruaz. The magical dragon is literal; mere attempts at interaction can ruin a country, in some cases lay the entire continent to waste.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Kotrūz"

Continent-leading force; commander unit. As commander he lacks strategy and charisma, but his force and "Heavenly-Wing Knight" feature aim only for "Head-Hunt" — a foul brute; beware.

· Other "Heavenly-Wing Knight" units

Inferior to Kreisharana's in force and Head-Hunt success but more numerous. "Rear Descent" and similar lets them take rear, get pinch-trapped; commanders should have force and strategy together.

A nation governing the island east of the continent. Basically free of the continent's strife, but inside the island, civil wars endlessly; unique tech and cultural evolution.

Impassable obstacles everywhere; distant from the continent so terrain info is hard to obtain. Fortunately, they barely advance to the continent, but invading them is very tough.

Sacred Spirit Pact does not work; even captives are not safe; if missed they will ambush endlessly. Culturally, technically advanced unbroken tribes — not shy about cruelty; losses devastate your morale. Literally exterminating one by one — gains aren't small but expect corresponding hardship.
