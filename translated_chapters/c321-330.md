# Chapter 321: Duty — End

The three-month stay came to an end, and exchanges with Kreisharana continued smoothly thereafter.

Scholars were summoned to investigate the land, new seedlings were obtained, and attempts were made to root them there.

To those people, who would accept little more than liquor and practical goods, were brought instead human beings possessed of skill and knowledge.

Alberan's rich technology was something refined by countless cultural exchanges.

Their own craft, advanced in isolation, could by no means be called immature, but Alberan's blacksmiths and carpenters and other craftsmen, and its scholars, possessed a wider scope of knowledge.

Even if they could not fight, they were people who had resolved to dedicate their entire lives to the cultivation of knowledge or skill.

Built upon their dedication, Alberan had obtained its strength as a collective.

Whether directly or indirectly, the battlefields differed, but such people too were warriors deserving of respect — and they too gradually came to accept this.

One who devotes himself to his knowledge, or to his craft, is no different from one who devotes himself to sword or spear.

If the sword I forge should prove dull, then I shall plunge that very blade into myself and die.

There were old blacksmiths who proclaimed it grandly, almost as a challenge.

If the method I propose is mistaken, then I do not mind being killed.

There were scholars who declared it serenely, with a laugh.

As fighters, they could not even have become levied warriors among Kreisharana — yet voices belittling such people likewise faded swiftly.

Conviction and resolve that did not begrudge even one's life, and pride in one's own dedication.

Precisely because the Kreisharana disliked ornament and prized the inner self, they were able to understand.

If there is to be superiority or inferiority among people, it lies within the heart.

It is never something on the outside.

Their way of life, which placed no value on those who could not fight, had been indispensable to that harsh land.

Without it, mere survival would have been difficult.

Their culture was synonymous with living itself, and no one could question its rightness or wrongness.

Only — it was different now from what had come before.

The Kreisharana no longer needed to remain isolated upon that harsh land.

To migrate to a more livable land — the future ought to hold such an option for them as well.

That meaning could be found in things other than mere combat, that one could be needed, that one could be permitted to take pride.

That such a world existed too — that was the future, the possibility, that Argoush wished to convey.

"That a person such as Krische-sama would appear, that a person such as Her Majesty would appear, and now a person such as you……we shall most likely fade slowly out of the world. But Lord Giterns, there is no need for you to grieve over it. We shall fade because we ourselves wish it."

So Vinsreel and the others spoke.

"Moreover, if there are those born outside, like Lord Giterns, who lament our passing and find our way of life beautiful……even in the future after we are gone, our way of being shall surely be carried on in some form. So long as it is needed, by someone's hands."

Saying it was enough — accepting their own passing with a smile.

They are strong people, Argoush thought from the depths of his heart.

For their sake, and so that they should not come to regret it, Argoush ran himself ragged.

In this way, day after day, exchanges with Kreisharana accumulated, and letters too were exchanged again and again.

He entrusted matters of the territory to his sons, who had grown into fine men, and his days, though busy, were full — and one such day, what came to him from Anne were two volumes of a book,

"At the Manor of the Noble Hawk……"

The binding was beautiful, weighty black leather embroidered in silver thread — an upper and a lower volume.

The deliveryman had only conveyed that it was a request from Anne; the parcel within contained no letter.

Wondering whether she had simply forgotten to enclose one, but also why on earth she had taken the trouble to send him a book,

Argoush worried over his daughter's customary eccentricity from the sofa of his private study.

Could it be such a wonderful book that she had gone to the trouble of sending it on purpose?

If so, it was not impossible to understand.

He was not particularly fond of books, but Argoush bought all manner of them for the sake of his learning, and when he spent time at home he generally read.

The mansion had a respectable library too, and there was every chance that Anne had misunderstood her father to be a great book-lover; choosing a book as a gift for one's father was no strange thing.

In fact, among the servants too there were those who thought Argoush loved books, and however one looked at it, it was an objective fact that he was a reader.

If anything, he read books only as one form of learning, of cultivation.

If asked outright whether he loved books he could not say so, but if asked whether he disliked them he would answer that he did not.

In the broad sense he was the bookish sort — and if it were a gift from his daughter, his interest was piqued enough to wonder what sort of book it might be.

"Dear, what does the letter sa――……?"

Black soybean tea and cookies on a tray.

His wife Lyna, returning, looked at the book in Argoush's hands and tilted her head.

Her tea-tinted hair hung loosely down before her neck; though some signs of age had begun to show, she was still his lovely, charming wife, his pride.

Clad in a modest navy one-piece dress, even as the wife of Marquis Giterns she still baked cookies with her own hands and threw herself into the household's cooking and laundry alongside the servants, just as she had in her own days as a servant.

He had once tried to make her stop, saying that she was now his wife — but the position of being waited upon seemed uncomfortable for her, and in the end Argoush gave way.

In truth, what had drawn Argoush to her in the first place had been the figure of an industrious, caring woman.

Seeing that Lyna had not changed put him at ease, and he left the matters of his daily life to his wife.

Perhaps the fact that Anne had grown up watching such a mother had also been significant.

Thinking this, he showed her the book, and with a wry smile said only that the book had been sent without anything else.

Oh my, she said, smiling wryly in turn.

"Did she forget to put in a letter, I wonder?"

"Something like that. That's just like Anne."

"Fufu, even after coming to serve at Her Majesty's side, her scatter-brained side hasn't been mended."

Loosening her mouth, she picked up the lower volume, lightly checked its contents — and creased her brow.

"……This is that child's handwriting, isn't it?"

"Anne's?"

Only then did he open the book, and what first leapt to his eye was the table of contents.

Prologue — Anne of the Giterns family, until she became a servant of the Royal Domain.

Chapter One — Ideal and reality. And the eternal goal: meeting the Greatest of Servants.

Chapter Two — The War of Royal Authority and the princesses of the noble hawk.

Chapter Three —

Argoush was struck dumb.

Was this an autobiography of some kind?

He imagined his beloved daughter sending her father a mysterious autobiography, full of self-confidence — well, with Anne it was entirely possible — and he resigned himself to it.

Anne was the sort to do something out of the blue from time to time.

According to Lyna, within Anne herself there was apparently deep deliberation behind such things (though sometimes, it seemed, it was simply a passing whim), but even Argoush, who had watched her since she was an infant, found it hard to read her heart.

The handwriting was certainly Anne's.

Anne's writing, somewhat methodical, was clean and careful.

It was not poor writing, even compared to a professional scribe's.

Yet handwriting reveals character.

The hand of Selene, who had written to Argoush concerning Anne during the civil war, had been forceful and dignified, expressing the bravery and strong will of the daughter of Christand of the Lightning Strike.

The hand of the head servant, Bery, who had several times written letters in her stead, had been graceful, refined, and delicate.

The permission slip Alberinea had once entrusted to Kreisharana had been so identical to Bery's that one could not tell them apart — a hand most likely learned from her, an imitation of her writing.

Queen Kreschenta's hand was queenly through and through.

In a vivid and beautiful script that clearly expressed all gradations of stroke and force — all of them were beautiful, but the inner nature of each seeped out as habit.

Anne's hand truly conveyed her temperament: that she did things carefully.

It was a beautiful hand on the whole, but with a slight forced quality from worrying about how it looked, a faintly vain quality of trying to express a smart-looking script.

Unlike the other four, whose handwriting had simply become what it was naturally, Anne's letters tried by effort to express that beauty — which was, after all, an attractively Anne-like charm.

The table of contents, like Selene's, was forceful and dignified; in passages explaining the merits of others, it was as flowery as Kreschenta's; her ordinary script attempted to mimic Bery's grace, while striving to imitate by effort the flowing run of the line, leaving a slightly mismatched quality.

Just looking at it, the figure of Anne with all her peculiar, stubborn quirks rose to mind, and somehow it was funny.

But the more he read, the more the contents began to worry him.

The book recounted those people whom she had met after coming to the Royal Domain, the chief subject being the Christand household.

Essentially everything was praise of the highest order.

So far as it spoke of their characters, it was not so bad — but Bery, though small, has full breasts and a beautiful waistline; Selene's slender body is as sleek and fascinating as a cat — what on earth she meant her father to take from such passages, he could not understand.

That she was praising their appearance he could grasp; but no matter how one looked at it, the lines were unnecessary.

And on top of that, Her Majesty the Queen and Alberinea — both royalty — were thrown in too, which only made him worry about Anne all the more.

"That girl really……"

"It does sound like Anne, though……"

To Argoush, who held his head, Lyna, who had been peering at the book from beside him, smiled wryly.

What sort of people they were, he had been able to grasp to some degree on meeting them recently, and he had clearly seen how kindly they treated Anne, how affectionately they regarded her.

The more he learned of their figures and daily lives as told in the book, the more was conveyed to him of how dearly Anne loved them, and of how dearly they cherished his daughter Anne.

Considering that, he was unlikely ever to fly into a rage at the book she had written, or to punish her for it — but the contents were dreadful all the same — at the very least, not something to be shown to others.

The lower volume grew yet more extreme in content.

"As ever, sitting on Lord Argan's lap and being made to play at being a dog and indulged in, Her Majesty the Queen――"

Such mysterious extraordinary moments were tossed off lightly here and there in the daily life she described, and after finishing it he resolved, ever more deeply, that this book must be sealed away, or burned — and yet he did not stop reading.

He kept turning the pages onward and onward, because he could read in the chaotic daily life of the mansion that Anne described, the feeling that seeped from between the lines.

From her own birth through to her daily life at the mansion, the reason she spoke of it all so happily — when one considered why, the reason for which this book had been written also became roughly understandable.

"It's almost time I started supper preparations…… let me read the rest later."

"Yes, go ahead."

Even after Lyna left, he kept turning pages.

What was put into words must surely be only a small fragment.

Imagining the countless exchanges that must have taken place between the lines, he read on.

Bery being stabbed, and the daily life like a candle gone out, until Krische returned bringing the dragon.

The hardships and worries of those people in the Five-Nations War were touched upon, and because the everyday life she ordinarily told of was so bright and so absurd, those passages stood out all the more.

Toward the end, references to Krische's adoptive grandfather, called Gallen, became more frequent, and from this he could vaguely guess.

The old man whom Anne deeply revered, whose name had often come up in those daily lives.

A warrior of hunter origin, beloved from the heart even by the unmistakable genius Krische, and held in high esteem by many soldiers.

His age was no different from Argoush's own — only, he was no mana wielder, and the trigger that had led his daughter to compose this letter in the form of a book likely lay with him.

Argoush had heard that he had passed away a little while ago.

The words she had learned from him, the things he had taught her — she wrote each one down as if it were a precious treasure, and as the book neared its end such passages multiplied.

And then, when the matter of Gallen's death was set down, Anne at last began to speak about this book itself.

She wrote that, while she had been born to parents she could respect, and had passed her days surrounded by wonderful people, she herself was an utterly ordinary person, incomparable to any of them.

And that, recalling Argoush's words, she had thought about what she ought to do with her future.

To marry a fine gentleman and enter into a household, to raise her own children — that future too was certainly a happy one, and unlike them, she was a perfectly ordinary woman with no particular gift.

Was she perhaps far too unsuited to spend her whole life in this brilliant world?

"Lord Gallen said that I, who call myself ordinary, was on the contrary a special existence in this brilliant world, and that such a one as I was a most precious existence in this manor. That it is not enough only to draw the bowstring tight; sometimes one is needed who slackens it……because everyone in this manor draws their strings too taut, he said."

What was depicted in their daily life was a cast of characters out of a picture book.

The royal sister and the queen, fit to be called children of the gods.

The servant who could indulge those two as if they were children.

The noble daughter who at fifteen had become Marshal, and who fulfilled that duty as a matter of course.

Even the servant whom she herself called her rival was extraordinarily talented; everyone connected to them had, as a matter of course, some excellence beyond ordinary people.

"Lord Argan too says that, far from being attentive to me, he is the one always made to attend to me — that he is always being saved by me, who can do nothing well. He laughs and says that one is sometimes needed who can anger one's master, or trouble her. Whether he is saying the same thing as Lord Gallen I cannot quite tell, but to me, even so, he says such things."

Their hearts must surely be the same.

Anne did indeed have many flaws, but she was a fine daughter of whom Argoush was proud.

He thought that they were truly seeing Anne's good points.

"I cannot believe myself to be the splendid, needed person they say I am. I think they are being kind to me, comforting me with such words, and yet I also think — if even a faint truth I have not noticed lay within those words…… If even a little, a person such as I might be of use to them — that, I wish to believe."

As he had begun to suspect partway through, this was a letter of farewell.

A letter declaring that, by her own will, she had decided the rest of her life.

"Of course, it may all be my own foolish misunderstanding. Perhaps in truth they are quite exasperated with me, and one day I may yet be cast from the manor. But until that time comes, I shall serve at this manor the people whom I revere, and put forth all the effort that lies within me――"

It was for that reason, on reading this very line,

"――that I wish to fulfill my duty as a servant."

Argoush pressed his fingers to the inner corners of his eyes.

"Father, who even now stakes his life in a far-off land and fulfills a great office. Mother, who taught me many things kindly and watched over me. And my brothers, to whom I may bring much worry. Yet, as one of the nobility born into the Giterns family handed down from our ancestors, I have resolved in my heart to dedicate to this precious name granted me an oath that shall not change unto eternity."

——Please, forgive your willful daughter.

Picturing Anne's face, hearing those words of resolve, he lowered his head.

Yet still he could not hold back his tears, and they ran down the bridge of his nose to fall in droplets.

"From the Manor of the Noble Hawk, with the love of Anne Giterns, child of her revered father and mother."

At those characters set down at the end of the book, he pressed his sleeve to his eyelids.

Until a knock sounded, and Lyna came to call him for supper.

Lyna, when she came, drew close to Argoush's side and, with a wry smile, ran her hand over his head and said, At your age — what is this?

"……Read it later, please."

"Yes. ……Was it something painful?"

No, he shook his head, sniffing, and lifted his face.

"More than anything, it is a letter to be glad of. ……As head of the Giterns family, I am proud above all."

And closing the book, he traced the letters drawn upon its cover.

"As one father, it makes me a little lonely…… that is the kind of letter it is."

"……I see."

At the Manor of the Noble Hawk —— and now, from the Manor of the Noble Hawk.

"Once you've calmed down, come along. The food will get cold."

"Ah, sorry."

Having flown her birth-nest, Anne now lived within that other manor.

The next day he wrote a letter, and Argoush returned to his daily life.

Energetically running about the various lands, he met scholars, craftsmen, and merchants, and as the bridge between Alberan and Kreisharana, he traveled back and forth between his manor and Kreisharana.

The round trip took too long, was too much trouble, they said in concern, and after a time one of their warriors began to stay at Argoush's manor, partly to study the culture of the lowlands.

If one flew from the foot of the mountain, one could reach Kreisharana in less than half a day.

When it came to many people that was not so simple, but they too became gradually more cooperative, and a few of their warriors would carry small numbers there and back, bringing things into a much warmer relationship.

In cooperation with the Alberan army — when the great plan to make a road between Alberan and Kreisharana was underway, magical beasts caused damage, and he was tormented by stomach pains — but when he was tired he would gaze at the book Anne had given him and read its words again and again, refreshing himself.

Nor did the Queen leave it all entirely to him.

When the great plan was at hand, Alberinea sometimes appeared mounted on her Jade Tiger, looking carefree, would solve what was difficult as if it were nothing, and saying that she had work back at the manor and was busy, would return home.

Storm-wing too, some time after Anne had been worrying, was hunted in passing on a visit to greet Kreisharana, and on the route of the planned road nearly ten of them were taken in only half a day.

When he saw Alberinea solving, as a "little errand," problems that made him hold his head, there were times when he wondered whether there was any meaning at all in his own being there — but even so, Argoush could only earnestly fulfill his own duty.

Sensing the existence of the unfathomable Alberinea or the Queen, he could understand his own smallness.

The being of Bery, who was loved and revered by them, and the existence of Selene, who as a matter of course passed her days as the master of the manor where they too dwelled.

They were literally above the clouds, inhabitants of a storied world; thinking of his beloved daughter's daily life passed alongside such people, and thinking of Anne Giterns who had sworn upon her duty and her name to go on living it, there was nowhere in his life for time to let himself rot away.

That his proud beloved daughter might have a father to be proud of.

Just as Anne sought to fulfill her duty, so too did he.

To remind himself of that, he turned the pages of the book until they were grimy with the oil of his hands.

After reading that letter, he had written his reply as a noble.

He could not have her producing more such forbidden, off-limits books.

The greater part of it was about the contents she had written, packed with rebukes that, as the Queen's servant, she should mend her bearing, and over and over he repeated that she was never to write the like again.

And at the very end,

"Above all, I earnestly desire that Anne Giterns shall fulfill her duty. Even in this far-off land, as one nobleman who likewise inherits the name of Giterns, and as one father who, together with his proud beloved daughter, would also fulfill his own duty."

——At the Manor of the Noble Hawk, with love to the servant who unbinds these pages.

Each time he turned the pages, recalling that he had written so, he renewed his resolve.

He himself was ordinary, an unremarkable man.

Yet that was no reason to throw aside his duty.

He was who he was; whether one had talent or not, each one fulfilled the duty fitting to the abilities he had been given.

The Queen had hers, Alberinea had hers; however blessed they might be, however easily they could solve the difficulties of many, there was no difference in this.

Each, surely, had her own toils and trials, and that was why they staked their lives even against dragons.

To put forth one's whole strength for the sake of something more precious than oneself.

In that there was no superiority or inferiority at all.

Confronting it earnestly, sincerely, he did not mind being shamed or made a fool of.

Even if it were unsightly, even if it were pitiful, still he would dedicate his effort, and proudly speak.

"Do you enjoy making a fool of yourself, idiot? How many times is it now you've been knocked flat? You have neither talent nor quickness. However many years I associate with you, it's the same thing repeated…… If it's hard and you want to quit, you can whine all you like."

"No, once more, if you would, Lord Doukaran."

"Then move your body before you move your mouth. Whether by cowardly ambush or anything else, give it all you have and at least take a single point off me. Even if you are a fool sprawled in the dirt, if you produce results I shall acknowledge it."

"Yes."

That this was his duty — laughing, dignified.

— Storm-wing Subjugation Battle

"……So Storm-wing's nest is on this cliff face."

Above the nest where Storm-wing slept — silver hair seated behind Vinsreel.

Krische looked down with a "hoheee."

Storm-wing's nest existed within a great hollow at the top of a tall cliff face that loomed up like a wall.

In the depths of a colossal fissure in the cliff, like a yawning maw, Storm-wing was said to sleep quietly.

This place was originally not a cliff but a mountain, said to have been gouged off on one side and born from the strife of dragons.

In fact, viewed from the other side the cliff was a complete mountain, and on the cliff face one could see traces as if the rock had melted.

That hollow too, no doubt, had been formed by the same effects.

Why Storm-wing, who in these past several hundred years should once have been slain, made even this place its lair, was unknown — perhaps there was some reason unknown to them, or perhaps it was due to the creature's wisdom.

So as not to be detected, they descended slowly from above onto the top of the cliff, and Vinsreel began to explain.

"Long ago, I have heard, there were brave men who set foot directly into the nest. But the great fissure of the cliff like a yawning maw…… the depth where Storm-wing sleeps is some distance in, and they could not even approach against its wing-beats; they were repulsed and slain……"

"Wing-beats……"

"Literally, like a storm flattening the forest. Of course it cannot match Yagelnaus-sama's, but even so, for a human body, in that confined place the storm raised had no answer. ……When that occurred, Storm-wing awakened in fury, and the settlement too suffered grievous damage."

"I see…… that's hard."

It would be no easy thing to slay Storm-wing inside that nest.

For Storm-wing, who could raise a storm with a single wing-beat, the wide and deep fissure of the cliff was an excellent environment in which to repulse and slay any intruder with ease.

A single storm raised by those wings would blow the opponent back out into open air.

Krische's miraculous skill, rivaling even a divine being's, was known — but it was far too dangerous to set foot within.

First, Vinsreel and the others would act as bait, draw it out of the nest somehow — and at the moment Storm-wing exposed itself outside the cliff, they would have her finish it.

That was likely the optimal solution.

"Yes. So we shall act as the bai――"

"All right then, Krische will go take a quick look."

"――Krische-sama!?"

Before he could call her back.

Krische, gripping her spear, dropped down the nearly vertical cliff face as if it were nothing.

——Had she even been listening to him at all?

Stupefied, Vinsreel and the others mounted their griffins and dove in haste, but could not catch up,

"――!?"

The instant Krische's hand caught the upper edge of the fissure and disappeared inside, what rang out was the cry of a monstrous bird, freezing one's innards — and in an instant it broke off, and a tremor ran through the earth.

What Vinsreel and the others at last saw on arriving was, in the depths of the great fissure, the figure of the monstrous bird, wings spread, having lost everything from the neck up, crumbling to the ground.

And Krische, holding down her skirt, tilting her head slightly.

"I was listening, but it really is rather large after all. Hmm, I wonder if it'll be tasty……"

——A gentle breeze brushed Vinsreel's cheek.

— Magical Beast Hunt

Atop a griffin — above the mountains.

Krische sat sideways behind Lila, gripping her spear and peering downward.

"Ah, there's another one. Lila, a little further back."

"E-ehm, yes…… Larnel."

What on earth could she be seeing?

Lila watched Krische, while still seated, hurl her spear straight downward at terrifying speed.

The spear was sucked into a gap in the trees, and there came the death-cry of a Jade Tiger.

The trees shook and toppled as it thrashed about, and after a little while that too quieted.

"The shake threw off my aim a tiny bit. Poor thing, what a pity."

"I-I'm sorry……"

"No, just now Krische was simply clumsy. Errr, that one over there…… Mister Ruuza. I've finished it off, so please bleed it and dress it."

"U-understood……"

"Hmm, what else……"

Speaking to the bewildered griffin riders, she dangled both legs idly.

——That day, one Indigo Deer and seven Jade Tigers vanished from the mountains.

# Chapter 322: The Threat Lurking in the Abyss

That this earth is a great round sphere — that much was already known.

——What sort of thing was this world, after all?

The one who put a stop to all the various theories was Alberan's queen, Grabareine.

Astrology, divining the future from the movements of the stars — in the lands surrounding old Alberan, the positions and motions of the stars were thought to indicate omens, good and ill, and to influence many things in the world.

For that reason astrology was at the time prized as an important factor in political judgment, and to silence it she set forth, theoretically and by physical law, that this world was one of the stars floating in a vast space — the cosmos — calculating its orbit and the laws of motion to demonstrate it.

A vast world and stars, bound together by gravitation and centrifugal force.

There was none who could refute Grabareine, who had unraveled the mystic as physics; the mysteries of astrology were lost, and her theory is even now used as the foundation of astronomy.

Grabareine likely had no interest in the cosmos itself.

She had merely unraveled it because it was necessary.

Although she had built a perfect foundation, she set down nothing beyond, and astronomical research thereafter was treated more as the diversion of dilettantes.

Bery, too, thought of it that way, and had no strong interest of the kind to undertake such research deliberately, but on the desk — gazing at the schematic of the cosmos drawn on parchment, even her heart was set leaping.

"I see…… so it's an unimaginable size, isn't it."

In the room, on the sofa, sat Selene and Bery, and on each of their laps, Krische and Kreschenta.

The usual scene; with Elvena attending at one side as well, all eyes were directed at the desk.

"That you should feel that way is because of your low standard of intellect."

"……Yes. Just as you say──"

"Honestly, you can't even do simple arithmetic properly; how disagreeable. Must I, of all people, teach you the four operations of children? I went to the trouble of explaining it to you, and after all that the impression you finish with is 'unimaginable size'? Of all the foolish——"

"……, woof."

"W-what is it……?"

Bery, smile still in place, pressed a cookie to Kreschenta's mouth on her lap and silenced her.

"Mmgh……!"

"It's 'woof,' Kreschenta-sama."

And while ruffling her head as one might a dog's, she deepened her smile.

Ignoring Selene's exasperated gaze, Bery lowered her eyes to the parchment.

Countless beautiful circles, impossible to think had been drawn freehand, were inscribed there, depicting conceptually this star and the worlds beyond.

Great celestial bodies and small, countless bodies tracing spirals, drifting through endless emptiness.

Noticing that Elvena was likewise peering at the parchment, she asked.

"Do you like this sort of thing too, Elvena-sama?"

"Yes, it's very interesting. Astronomy is something I know nothing about……"

Elvena answered, eyes likewise lit with curiosity.

"Fufu, I'm not so well-versed myself, but…… one can imagine all sorts of things, and it's fun. With such an unimaginable number of stars, I wonder if perhaps space-people exist."

"……Space-people?"

"Yes. Just as we happened to be born, perhaps other people happened to be born too…… Considering the number of stars and the years, it doesn't seem out of the question."

"I see, that's true. Insects, fish, lizards, snakes…… perhaps even clever plants."

While giggling together and feeding cookies to Kreschenta's mouth, the drowsy Krische atop Selene asked,

"Bery, do you want to see space-people?"

"Let's see…… not so much wanting to see them, more that imagining them is fun."

"I see…… ehehe, it would be nice if they exist. Mister Space-People——"

"It would be nice if you exist, Mister Space-People——" the voice of a small creature on a small planet.

In the unimaginable void, an echo that should ordinarily vanish in an instant.

That small creature, in conversation, had noticed a hole in her own "safety net," and rewrote the network inscribed at the source of the world, propagating that network.

The Fuyopita-net — the world-rule that would later be called the Ether Limit — closed the path leading to the source, and set a limit upon the inscription of formulae by mana.

Restraining the leaping advance of magical power, it would induce disorder in formulae of large-scale description that exceeded a certain range, suppressing them.

It was a "Rule" inscribed in the deepest place of the world to prevent the destruction of that world by overwhelming magic.

And so the maiden's voice, connected to the source, was carried unintentionally onto the network.

——It would be nice if my beloved servant enjoyed it.

That unconscious wish, from the source, sent her voice resounding through the unimaginably vast world of stars.

It would be nice if you exist, Mister Space-People——

From source to source.

The propagating network, twisting space-time, carried the voice through the void.

From star to star, as if to fill that endless emptiness one by one.

It would be nice if you exist, Mister Space-People——

A journey through sources transcending time and space. The network bridged distances by filling them with time, simply going further and further.

Riding the undulations of space-time, going back through cosmic ages, through emptiness from before life had spread upon the maidens' star, it proceeded only as the processing it had been commanded to perform.

A woman who had spent decades alone in a forest simply to uphold a vow.

She had once yearned for such beauty and allure — but now she thought differently.

It was precisely *because* she was ordinary that she could be moved.

The people of this manor were fundamentally unaware of how wonderful the things they possessed were.

A murmuring, ear-tickling voice.

"……Huh?"

——as Elvena-sama wiped Lila-sama's mouth with a handkerchief she had produced from somewhere.

Lila-sama let out a bewildered sound and opened her eyes, staring at the handkerchief — and Elvena-sama alone was smiling brightly.

"Fufu, you had a bit of cookie crumb on your lip……all clean now."

And as if nothing had happened, she returned to her own cushion, studied their beet-red, frozen faces, and tilted her head with genuine curiosity.

"Um……is something the matter?"

"N-no! N-nothing at all……"

"N-no, nothing……oh, r-right, let me make more tea!"

*Thank you,* Elvena-sama said with a beaming smile.

The usual gentle smile — and yet somehow it looked more amused than usual.

But perhaps seeing it that way was only because her own heart was impure.

# Chapter 328: The Creation of Heaven and Earth, an Eternity of Journey — End

The first year flew by in an instant.

A year of bewilderment with new bodies and surroundings, and joy at reunion.

The first decade flew by too.

A decade of going out to shop and traveling to see all sorts of towns and cultures.

The first century also flew by.

A century of watching, from afar, a world that no longer had its great Queen.

She had once been at what could be called the center of the world.

The heroic epic and continental unification chronicle of the great Queen, Krische-sama, Selene-sama and the others.

She had lived in a small manor at the heart of the Kingdom of Alberan — a kingdom that would be etched into history and become legend. To call it 'the center' was not far from the truth.

If anything, adding 'she supposed' to that phrasing was the more unnatural thing.

*The eye of a storm, viewed from above, is calm,* they said.

Perhaps her sense of detachment came precisely from living at the side of these women — the storm itself.

There had probably been a fundamental disconnect all along.

For example, Argan-sama's death — viewed from outside, it was a truly small event, one the outside world wouldn't even notice.

A personal servant of the Queen falling ill — it wouldn't even make gossip.

But from her perspective — and from everyone in the manor — it was an earth-shattering event. And the ripples of that upheaval had spread to engulf the entire world.

She had felt, somewhere deep down, that this was natural — and yet, thinking about it, it was utterly unnatural and unreasonable.

*——The manor was, in a sense, the realm of gods.*

In the mythology worshipped in Alberan, the stamping of the enraged chief god Ordoris had shattered the beautiful, smooth earth into rises and valleys, creating mountains and gorges. The tears shed by the goddess of beauty, Shaserene, had brought rain, forming rivers and seas.

Through the gods' emotions alone, the sun was born, the moon was born, the stars were born, day and night were born, and seasons were born.

As a child she had been awed and delighted, but as an adult she came to think: *what troublesome gods.*

Beings who remade heaven and earth on a whim of emotion.

For those living there, it was nothing but a nuisance — naturally they would fear and worship the gods and beg forgiveness.

'God' was a word for the irrational, and 'Alberan' was a word meaning 'child of god.'

The 'Alberan' she had watched were innocent and pure, ageless like children, beautiful girls who wished only for the happiness of their small, immediate world.

Many people loved them, but just as many hated and feared them — and each time, she had felt sad.

They might have been blind, she had thought — but the ones who were truly blind were probably herself.

Beings who remade the world out of grief over a servant's death.

Beings who united a continent almost in passing.

They were truly, literally, the stuff of mythology — Alberan, who shook heaven and earth with a single emotion.

The world must have looked very different to those people than to her, who had unknowingly wandered into the realm of gods.

If a single emotion could change everything, anyone would be frightened.

And perhaps, dimly, they had understood that others would feel that way.

The Kingdom of Alberan in those days had been, unmistakably, a realm of gods.

Had Kreschenta-sama wished it, she could have brought hundreds, thousands of years — an eternity — of peace and stability. But for everyone else, that would have meant swallowing every injustice the gods deemed unacceptable, enduring every irrationality imposed upon them.

Watching the seemingly invincible Alberan crumble and give way to the age of Kleinmeil, she felt a certain guilt and loneliness — yet also a sense that this was right.

Krische-sama, who had decided on this form of things, had probably thought something similar.

Whether consciously or not.

"Ehehe, the name was really long and hard to say, so Krische gave him a nickname — Nemunemu. His eyes were sooo narrow that the first time Krische met him, Krische thought he was standing there asleep."

"I see……fufu, that's very like you, Krische-sama."

She watched the surroundings as she walked behind Krische-sama and Argan-sama.

Albernaria looked much the same as before — the only real difference was a slightly different crest displayed here and there.

A crescent moon crowning the city's emblem — Kleinmeil.

It meant *moonlight's legacy,* she was told, and true to the name, the emperor was an acquaintance of Krische-sama's.

The grandson of the second Director of the Academy of Magical Studies (the first being Krische-sama herself) — she had never spoken to him, but she had seen him a few times when he was still young.

If Krische-sama called him *reasonably clever,* he must have been a genius among geniuses.

Krische-sama said *decent enough, not bad in the head* — to an ordinary person, that meant brilliant. She had never heard Krische-sama use the phrase *really smart* for anyone besides Argan-sama, and apart from Margrave Faren, she rarely even used *clever.*

That was why he had stuck in her memory — and to think *he* had become emperor was quite moving.

"He's the emperor now……he's grown up splendidly."

*He was about this tall at first,* Krische-sama said, holding her hand at hip height, and she and Argan-sama laughed.

Krische-sama and Kreschenta-sama never forgot anything they had seen or heard.

They could rattle off what they had done on any given day decades ago, down to the schedule — they could memorize a book word for word after flipping through it once. Strictly speaking, they did forget things, but apparently that just meant having to go find the book in the library inside their heads (she rather wanted to peek inside), and there was nothing they couldn't recall.

Perhaps because of that astonishing memory, Krische-sama in particular seemed to burn first impressions deeply — if someone was a child when they first met, she would treat them as a child forever after, no matter how old they grew.

*Onee-sama is clumsy about that,* Kreschenta-sama said (apparently she didn't have that tendency), but somehow, it was very Krische-sama, and she liked it.

In Krische-sama's mental library, the covers of the books probably had the faces from those first meetings drawn on them.

Argan-sama had said so, delighted, and she couldn't remember how many years ago that was.

She kept a diary every day, so she could check — but the diaries alone had become a substantial collection.

Selene-sama had built her a diary archive (she felt guilty about making a master do that), and she would sometimes hole up there and leaf through them, but even that took several days.

Every word, every scene, every phrase — to have all that, even more vivid than the diaries, constantly inside one's head was astonishing.

The mental library probably had bookshelves stretching to the horizon.

At the desk in the center where Krische-sama sat, there were surely mountains of recipe books and volumes of the *Collected Sayings of Argan-sama,* and just imagining it was a little bit fun.

The words Krische-sama used when lecturing Kreschenta-sama were mostly borrowed from Argan-sama, and each time, Kreschenta-sama's anger was redirected toward Argan-sama — a regular event in the manor.

*Argan-sama teaches nothing good* — that was a view shared by both Selene-sama and Kreschenta-sama, and watching Argan-sama deftly dodge their interrogations with honeyed words was a secret pleasure.

The days of quaking through such exchanges were long past.

Arguments and scuffles between Argan-sama and Selene-sama were constant in the manor — and how peaceful were the days filled with such arguments and scuffles.

Through civil war and wars and tragedies — and beyond all of that, childish bickering.

A manor of eternity, hidden quietly outside the world.

The words painted a lofty image, but what existed here was not some otherworldly grandeur, only perfectly ordinary daily life — and that was what everyone wanted.

Each time she felt that, she was relieved from the bottom of her heart.

Everyone treasured these trivial days — feeling that, she was reassured.

"It feels strange, somehow, walking through this city."

"Strange……?"

"……I should have lived here for ten years, and yet it's like visiting for the first time. I used to go shopping with Krische-sama every day when we were in Gurgain, and yet……"

Argan-sama had spent almost all her time in the royal domain, inside the manor.

It was inevitable given her position — in terms of time spent outside, Argan-sama had probably the least of anyone.

"Just walking through town like this, shopping, doing the most ordinary things — I never did any of that back then……fufu."

Argan-sama's gaze softened as she looked around at the town.

"……I just remembered something I'd been thinking for a while — that someday I wanted to go out to town with no particular errand, just strolling around, looking at things, shopping."

Then, *another dream come true,* she said, and giggled happily.

Whenever something needed doing, she and Elvena-sama had volunteered.

They'd told themselves they mustn't waste the time of Argan-sama, the most capable of them all — but hearing those words, she froze, wondering if it had actually been the opposite.

In hindsight, Argan-sama was the one who most valued precisely that kind of seemingly wasteful time.

*'Argan-sama, where are you going?'*

*'It seems Ojou-sama forgot her lunch basket, so I thought I'd bring it to her……'*

*'Leave it to me! I'll go instead!'*

*'Um, well, I'm already done with work, and Kreschenta-sama won't be up for a while, so Anne-sama, why don't you take a little bre——'*

*'I'm fine! You rest, Argan-sama……!'*

*'……Y-yes. Well then, please do……'*

There must have been days when she wanted to take a leisurely stroll outside.

Argan-sama would create slack in the schedule, and then spend that time doing nothing in particular, using it extravagantly — she loved that.

*Argan-sama is lazy and wastes time.*

Kreschenta-sama often said so, and in a way it was an accurate assessment.

Argan-sama herself often said she was lazy, and she now understood that it was truly not modesty but heartfelt.

Finishing work at superhuman speed, then spending ages indulging Kreschenta-sama's whims, or having tea, or playing with Gururun — using time elegantly, extravagantly, wasting it away — that was Argan-sama.

She used to wonder: *she's so busy; is she really all right?* But most likely, Argan-sama did her daily work precisely *for* that purpose.

She had simply assumed that because Argan-sama settled into the royal domain so effortlessly, even shaping the environment to suit her, she was someone who could carry on perfectly anywhere.

But surely, compared to life in Gurgain, there had been inconveniences, troubles, hardships — things she endured. One or two at the very least.

Only now, belatedly, did she realize such an obvious thing, and her eyes darted.

"Ehehe, from now on Krische will take you to all sorts of places. It's peaceful now, so places like Arna that we said we wanted to visit together, and all kinds of mountains and seas……"

"That sounds lovely. I'd love to see many different places."

"Yes! Space, and……oh, the moon would be nice too. There's nothing but sand and rock, but the stars are beautiful, and this planet sparkles like a jewel. Krische thinks Bery would like it too!"

"My. Fufu, I can't wait."

Looking at Krische-sama, she thought: *ah.*

Krische-sama probably understood Argan-sama all along.

They had been avoiding this area since people who knew them might still be around, but once they could go out again, Krische-sama had been making an unusual number of travel suggestions.

Under the guise of shopping trips, she had apparently taken Kreschenta-sama all around the world in advance, scouting beautiful scenery.

*It's like this and this, and the view is really beautiful,* Krische-sama would explain to Argan-sama — and most likely, Krische-sama's idea of a 'nice view' or a 'beautiful place' was exactly that.

A view that Argan-sama would enjoy was, to Krische-sama, a beautiful view.

Just walking through town, and Krische-sama's eyes seemed to sparkle.

As if the scenery around her were radiant, its brilliance reflected in those eyes.

Darting happily around Argan-sama.

*This shop, that shop* — beckoning, Krische-sama's delighted figure.

Just watching from behind warmed her chest — and that warmth nearly rose to sting her eyes, and she shook her head.

The great war that accompanied Alberan's fall had been not so long ago.

They had decided not to interfere with this side of things, but they hadn't been indifferent, and she had been somewhat anxious about how things were going.

But Albernaria, visited again after so long, was almost exactly as she remembered it.

And in this place, Krische-sama wore a truly happy smile alongside Argan-sama.

Without a care, without a worry.

She had thought there might be something nagging — some lingering feeling.

All things considered, this was the best course — Krische-sama and Selene-sama had made many arrangements with Aleha-sama and the others on this side, doing what needed to be done, bringing a hundred years of peace to a war-torn continent.

They had built, for a time, a historically remarkable peace — and yet, perhaps there had been a slight sense of guilt.

Depending on how you looked at it, they had abandoned the possibility of even greater peace and found happiness for themselves alone.

It could be seen that way, and if people knew, some would think so.

Because of that, the kingdom had split, war had broken out, and people who might not have died had died. That was a fact.

A lingering guilt left behind.

If the war had dragged on and devolved into a quagmire, that guilt would have grown far stronger — and so she was glad that the war had ended so quickly, that the world was moving toward a new era.

Remembering the boy (whose eyes really were very narrow, matching his nickname) who had gazed at Krische-sama with sparkling admiration — was it too much to think that this peace, too, might have been something offered to Krische-sama?

In any case, this peace and happiness were not theirs alone.

"Over there is Mia and Kalua's dojo. It's a pretty big dojo, and——"

As she took in the nostalgic scenery and savored the small joys of each day, her gratitude for all those people welled up.

Such scenery would not last forever.

Someday it would surely fade — but that was not a sad thing.

It would simply melt and blur into the gentle haze of memory.

In that soft parting, it would remain, never truly disappearing.

*——I won't last much longer.*

One day, drinking tea in Gallen-sama's room, she heard those words.

"These days, I often think about what will happen after I'm gone."

"Gallen-sama, you mustn't say such——"

"It's not a gloomy subject. People die eventually; I'll simply part from Krische and you all a bit earlier than the rest……that's all."

True to his words, there was nothing dark in his voice — the same gentle tone as always.

The same gentle, softly-laughing smile.

"Grace and Gorka……When Krische lost her parents, I lamented how little time I had left. I wanted to stay at that child's side and watch over her, to take their place……but there was nothing to be done about age. After Krische killed the bandits to protect the village, the villagers feared her, and she had no place to belong……I argued with Galla over and over — she'd been looking after Krische in Grace's stead — about what to do."

He brought the black soybean tea to his lips and looked up at the ceiling.

"That's why I entrusted her to Bogan.……If I'd been younger, I might have left the village and lived with her somewhere else, but given my age, I wouldn't last long. She was clever, talented — she'd have no trouble surviving on her own. But precisely because of that, I wanted her to find someone she could rely on."

And he narrowed his eyes.

"I wanted someone who would love her, watch over her."

To her ears, those words sounded like a prayer.

"Back then, I couldn't have imagined how things are now.……That Krische would always be right here, at my side……I truly began to feel she might be with me to the very end."

His voice caught, slightly hoarse, but continued with warmth.

"But as it turned out, I'm here alone. Krische is at the manor, laughing happily, living surrounded by many people……and I've become just another old man who drops in from time to time to dote on his granddaughter."

"Gallen-sama……"

"Feeling a little wistful, yet……strange as it is, truly happy."

Then, with a wry smile, he turned to her.

"Now I'm just one among many who love her, and she loves me as one among many. The people who love her and are loved by her are too many to count, and I'm no longer someone special to Krische. She'll grieve my passing……but that grief will fade amid so much love, and in time I'll become just one of many memories."

She wanted to say *that's not true* — but couldn't.

Gallen-sama was roughly the same age as her father, but her father was still youthful, and Gallen-sama looked far older.

He was neither noble nor mana-wielder; even if he lived several more years, he wouldn't live for decades.

No words would change anything, and he wasn't seeking comfort.

He had already accepted all of it.

"Her Majesty the Queen, despite her youth, is a wise sovereign. Now that the recent war is over, peaceful days will continue……and as long as they do, Krische is the gentlest of girls, one who loves such days. Not everything will go smoothly, of course, but she is surrounded by many kind people — including you — and what happened in that small village won't happen again.……I can't see the future, but that child's future is quite bright……surrounded by much love."

His voice carried an inexpressible tenderness.

He didn't stand at Krische-sama's side the way Argan-sama did — yet surely he held the same depth of feeling, treasured in his chest even across the distance, and she felt that, and lowered her gaze.

"Do you think parting is a sad thing?"

"……At least, right now, yes."

"Of course, sad partings exist……but so do many that aren't. I've commanded many subordinates. There are many I haven't seen since leaving the military, but quite a few are surely living happy, peaceful lives on different paths. Whether they're alive or dead, I may never know."

*Parting is like that,* he said.

"Life is full of such partings. Not just death. I'll never see those people again. But each walked their own path, and parting as a result is not a sad thing. Surely you have friends like that too?"

"That's……yes."

Karen-sama, who had been like an older sister, had returned home and sent a letter saying she was to marry.

Her partner was a lower-ranking noble but a fine young man — the letter was bubbling with excitement.

They would probably never meet again.

She had felt a little wistful, but it wasn't a sad thing.

"That's how it is. If you live long, there will be many more such partings. Death is just one such parting, and if it turns out to be a good one — that's enough."

Gallen-sama looked at her gently.

"I'm glad you're fond of this old man……but don't feel you need to grieve. The same is true for Krische. I feel death drawing near, and yet I can smile, sit here drinking tea with a kind girl like you, and feel happy.……You may not understand yet, being young, but it's really not a bad thing."

At some point the tears had started falling, and Gallen-sama smiled wryly, stood, and gently stroked her head.

"As many partings blur into shining memories, fading and wavering with time into recollections, each time something catches your eye, you'll find yourself smiling without knowing why.……One day, you too will see that kind of scenery. And when that day comes——"

The hand was rough, yet strangely soft.

"If you remember an old man's idle musings over tea and smile — I'd be glad."

*——As one trivial memory among many.*

Few things have been described with as many words as the beauty of the moon.

A waxing crescent turned the sky to deep black; a full moon painted it deep indigo.

It was always the moon that set the color of the starry sky.

Not as dazzling as the sun, its outline clear — perhaps that modest radiance was what made it so.

Gazing at it every day, the moon held an allure that made one lose oneself.

What it was, she understood in some academic terms — but for her, the moon was a distant vista, a kind of painting.

Something one could never touch — a concept, a beauty that existed only in the imagination.

*——To think she was now standing in such a place.*

Stretched before her, an undistorted horizon of perfect clarity.

It nearly threw off her sense of perspective.

Part of it was the sand and rock, and part of it was the scenery being far too visible in the distance.

Though the sun was visible, the sky held the stars of night.

Far away, a blue sphere — the world where they normally lived — floated against a backdrop of stars and void.

Hanging there as naturally as anything, shining like a jewel.

"Is that black soybean tea?"

"Yes, I was in the mood."

"Maybe I'll have some too. I haven't really had any since coming here……"

At Selene-sama's words, she nodded and poured some into a cup.

Having finished packing up the tent and supplies they'd brought, this was a moment's rest before heading back.

Selene-sama sat on the crate beside her, brought the black soybean tea to her lips — and frowned.

"……Isn't this a bit strong?"

"Oh, shall I dilute it?"

"No, it's fine, but……you do like it strong, don't you."

"Black soybean tea, well……Gallen-sama preferred it about this strong, and I got in the habit."

*I see,* Selene-sama chuckled, and cradled the carved-wood cup in both hands, gazing at the blue star.

Argan-sama was strolling with the two excited ones perched on her shoulders. Lila-sama and Elvena-sama were apparently performing some Kreisharana ritual——

With solemn expressions, they bounced about, stacking moon rocks into a mysterious tower.

Trips usually ended in these groupings.

The energetic duo and the ritual-starting Lila-sama were too much energy, and Selene-sama, looking a bit tired, often spent these moments lounging beside her.

"No matter how many times I see it, it's beautiful. We've seen all kinds of beautiful scenery, but……"

"Truly. I never imagined we'd come to a place like this. I got a little carried away, at my age."

"Fufu, they were talking about how Selene-sama didn't come back for dinner."

Strangely, everything was lighter on the moon.

The heavier the celestial body, the stronger the gravity; the lighter, the weaker — and the light bouncing of what seemed like a short walk had led Selene-sama to get lost for half a day.

"It's a bit lonely, though. The sky is beautiful and the floatiness is fun, but there's nothing else but sand and rock……"

"Yes……it's enjoyable as a trip, but if someone told you to live here, it would be rough."

Normally one couldn't even breathe, and there wasn't a single living creature.

Not a blade of grass grew — apart from the starry sky, the scenery could be called bleak.

"I'm pretty satisfied, and I think I'll be fine for the next twenty or thirty years. It's not like the ocean or mountains where you want to go back every year……I just want to relax at the manor for a while. My body's sense of balance is probably off too."

"I bet it'll feel incredibly heavy."

She laughed wryly, and Selene-sama nodded, sipping her tea.

She too savored the bitter taste, and slowly turned her eyes skyward.

As if cutting out the view, sealing it away into memory.

"Black soybean tea while gazing down at the earth from the moon……after a hundred years of such absurdities, time's just flown by. We're using time so extravagantly, I almost feel guilty about it."

"It's true — these are days I couldn't have imagined when we were over there."

"Same here. I'm appalled at how lazy a person I turned out to be. I was supposed to humor that idiot, and before I knew it, I'd become a fellow idiot. I wonder what Aleha and the others would think if they saw us."

She watched Selene-sama sigh, laughed, thought for a moment, and spoke.

"……When I was dying over there, I felt strangely happy."

"……?"

"Happy, old memories kept surfacing, wrapping around me……and I imagined the scenery I hadn't yet seen. I thought: even if I don't wake up, that might be all right. It was that comfortable."

That must have been what Gallen-sama had spoken of.

Simply comfortable, happy — a gentle time, gradual days.

"When Gallen-sama passed, I was terribly sad at the time……but having stood in that same place myself, I understood that Gallen-sama was truly happy. And it probably wasn't just him……when we went to Albernaria the other day, I felt something similar."

"Ah, you did seem pensive during dinner."

"Yes……fufu, only we are here. We drifted to a different place from all those people, and we parted.……But when we taste that nostalgic scenery, that nostalgic food, somehow……"

She gazed at the distant figures of the two, riding happily on Argan-sama's shoulders.

"I think it's because many people tried to leave something behind. In their final moments, perhaps they pictured us as we are now, and departed smiling……I can't help but feel that."

Albernaria remained as it was — the beautiful White City.

From Kalua-sama and the others' swordsmanship academy came the lively voices of children, and at Koza-sama and the others' restaurant, they savored cooking as delicious as ever.

Surely each had their own reasons.

But among those reasons, she thought, there was surely something like that.

"A blessing for a new world's journey……so that the departure might be a little less lonely."

She saw Gallen-sama's kind face reflected in the dark tea.

"……As a benediction for the long road ahead."

She closed her eyes, and a smile came naturally.

Most of those people were now only memories.

Wistfulness came and went, but more than that, a warmth settled in her chest and colored it.

Diluted by the ever-growing happy memories, much would fade and blur and lose its shape.

But such gentle memories would surely go on casting their faint light within her heart forever.

"You've been staring off into space by yourself, but is that what you were thinking about?"

"Well……drinking black soybean tea just reminds me of Gallen-sama, and I get lost in thought……"

"……Please don't burst into tears out of nowhere. I always think something's happened."

"Th-that's……I'm sorry……"

She let her gaze wander, and Selene-sama smiled wryly and gently stroked her head.

"But, well, I agree with you. Being able to enjoy the moon like idiots with those idiots — that's thanks to people like that. Alberan ended about as ideally as it could, and now it's a new era……we made preparations for that, sure, but that alone doesn't make things go smoothly."

"……Yes."

"It's because so many people thought: let that idiot's selfishness be just a little bit happier. Being here now isn't just because she was amazing, or anything like that……it's because all those people were watching over us."

*We should be grateful,* she said with a gentle smile.

"……Not that you of all people would ever forget that."

The palm was harder than a woman's should be — and strangely soft.

She felt Gallen-sama's hand in it, and shook her head.

"No, I wouldn't be so sure. I'm the type who can't even remember what I ate three days ago……since the first century has passed, I thought I'd write a book — a summary of everything from back there through these hundred years, using my diaries."

"……Didn't you write a similar book about fifty years ago? Those absurdly thick three volumes?"

"Those covered only the days over there, so it's a bit different. This time I want to include that gratitude, everything from coming here through the first hundred years."

"I-I see……"

"This time it will likely be five volumes. Actually, I've already decided on the title——"

# Chapter 329: Koten Suigetsu

*——In the east of the continent lies the nation of Tosho, and at the foot of the imperial capital where the Emperor resides stands a particular dojo.*

Its students number over a thousand — the greatest martial arts dojo in the imperial capital.

The name Koten Suigetsu-ryuu is known to all in Tosho.

Its fame had resounded even across the continent.

*Know all the hundred martial arts and yet know not enough — if you would know the way of the warrior, knock upon the gate of Koten Suigetsu.*

Even the sparrows in the streets repeated this, and the supreme mastery of its head instructor, Kazama Tesshuusai, had long since reached the domain of peerlessness. As the Sword Saint who bore the blade of Fuji, he was revered by countless warriors.

In the broad, wooden-floored dojo illuminated by eternal magic lamps, the old man's mere presence — simply sitting there — emanated a quiet yet indescribable pressure, and the men facing him felt as though they had swallowed lead.

"……I forbid it."

With a slight stirring of his long, snow-white beard, Tesshuusai spoke with grave weight.

He wore nothing but a simple kimono, bore no sword, and yet his words carried a sharpness and heaviness like a blade pressed to the listener's throat.

"I shall keep in my breast the foolish words you have uttered in defiance of the Emperor's will. If you have time for such idiocy, devote it to polishing your own skills. That is all from me."

"But…… are you truly saying this is acceptable, Master!? Groveling before the foreign color-hairs, forbidden even to look skyward! They are mere outsiders beyond the sea — if we band together, we need not submit but rather——"

"I said I forbid it. If you would say more, then face me in combat, Seijuurou. If you intend to walk this foolish path, the responsibility is mine…… cut me down first, and then go."

Over a hundred years old, his flesh wasted away, yet standing six and a half shaku tall.

When Tesshuusai rose and looked down upon them, the men before him stiffened at the force of his presence — his fighting spirit made manifest.

"Twelve senior students — all of you here, and you lack the nerve to kill a single unarmed old man, yet you speak such petty words? There are two choices, Seijuurou. I shall give you one chance to wipe away the disgrace you have spat. ……Choose."

He drifted toward him, seized his collar, and fixed him with a glare.

Just past forty — still young enough to be mistaken for a youth, the man met the killing intent in his master's eyes, wavered briefly, and lowered his gaze.

"I…… do not possess a blade to turn against you, Master."

Hearing this, Tesshuusai exhaled deeply and roughly released his grip.

"Then go. I too shall forget today's events. And you shall never speak of this again. You who cannot even swing a stick properly, yet presume to challenge the Emperor's will — the height of arrogance. ……Before turning your eyes toward the heavens, polish what lies within. Understood?"

"……Yes, sir."

And so the men departed, and in their place came a single man.

He resembled Tesshuusai some twenty years younger, exuding a dense aura of martial prowess.

"Why did you allow this to happen, Kojuurou?"

"I believed that words from you, Father, would serve Seijuurou better than mine…… Of course, should he push the matter further still, I shall fulfill my duty as his father."

Kojuurou leaned against the wall with arms folded.

Then he looked up at the ceiling.

"Nearly thirty years since then…… most alive today know nothing of those events. Please forgive today's incident as youthful folly."

"It is because I think so that I forgave it. But should it go further, there will be no second chance. If this school must be weighed against this nation, I shall show no mercy, even to a grandson. ……That country cannot be defeated."

So saying, Tesshuusai gazed at his own palm and sighed.

"I reached desperately for the lonely moon in the empty sky…… and when at last I grasped its reflection on the water, only then did I comprehend the unfathomable height of the moon shining at the zenith."

Then he gazed at the moonlight streaming into the dojo.

"……To realize that one is an iron boat sunk to the bottom — the present age is far too peaceful for that."

* * *

The sound of warfare from across the sea had reached their shores as well.

The great nation of Orburan, which had conquered everything from the far west to the far east of the continent.

The combined armies of over a dozen nations had been swept aside like dust from a sleeve.

Breaking through like a bursting dam, they had taken control of the eastern continent in the blink of an eye — so it had reached Tesshuusai's ears.

At the time, his name had been Kazama Fujisai.

True to the name — peerless, second to none in swordsmanship.

Recognized as the greatest swordsman under heaven, there was none who did not know his name, and having completed his long warrior's pilgrimage, he had been appointed as the Emperor's chief sword instructor.

The position had briefly satisfied Fujisai, but the role of sword instructor was not what he truly sought.

For Fujisai, who had lived by crossing blades with talented swordsmen, threading the boundary between life and death, the days were intolerably tedious.

It was during such a time that news from the continent reached his ears, and his heart raced with excitement, his chest swelling at the prospect of a battle over a nation's very survival.

Yet while many warrior houses cried for total resistance, the Emperor alone remained calm.

To fight head-on was reckless — for the sake of the people, war must be avoided and friendly relations established, he declared. Against the overbearing Orburan, which looked down upon them as a minor nation, he adopted a conciliatory stance.

He resolved to avoid war at all costs, silently accepting every condescending demand from the other side.

Many warrior houses naturally rebelled.

They declared it an act of abandoning the pride of this nation, which had maintained its independence against the continent's threats for so long.

Fujisai had no interest in such matters of state, but his heart lay on that side all the same.

He yearned for the chance to swing his sword again on the boundary of life and death, and it was around that time.

A proposition was brought to him.

In three months, the envoy of Orburan would arrive, accompanied by the kingdom's supreme commander and hero — Orburina.

They would eliminate them here to demonstrate their martial prowess, and change the Emperor's mind.

A crude plan, certainly not one to be praised.

However, aside from the Emperor, everyone desired war with the continent's barbarians, and if Fujisai declined, someone else would surely carry out a similar plan.

If the dam was going to burst regardless, let it be by his own hand.

Not for the nation, not for pride — simply because it was more interesting.

With that reasoning, Fujisai had agreed.

An imperial exhibition match timed with the arrival of Orburan's envoy.

Before the Emperor, many renowned warriors would compete in sword, spear, and naginata.

Fujisai's task was to draw Orburina — the effective pinnacle of martial prowess in Orburan — into the exhibition match, and kill her while making it appear an accident.

Meanwhile, others would take advantage of the chaos to dispatch the supreme commander who should have been at the Emperor's side.

*Following his victory in the exhibition match, he wished by all means to test his sword against the renowned Orburina* — the Emperor had approved Fujisai's prior proposal to this effect.

The response came with the caveat *provided the other side also agrees*, but likely the Emperor himself wished to demonstrate their martial strength.

While accepting the other side's terms yet refusing to submit — the idea of defeating Orburan's gold standard, Orburina, in a match carried great appeal as a way to show that in the event of war, they would not go down easily. The Emperor had personally clapped him on the shoulder and nodded proudly.

The Emperor's smile, seen after so long.

It had been ages since he had seen a smile on the face of that slender-faced young man he had known since childhood.

In hindsight, it must have been a decision made in anguish.

The Emperor had not been blessed with build or natural talent for the sword, but he was keenly intelligent for his youth.

He must have seen more than anyone, and amid the warrior houses clamoring loudly for war with the continent, he had no allies.

Even so, he had not been swept away — the young Emperor alone had assessed the times with a clear eye.

Seeing that smile, Fujisai had nearly changed his mind — yet the Fujisai of that time was a fool.

In the end, he decided to let events take their course.

* * *

——The arena was spacious.

The inner garden of the Tenpuu Palace in the center of the imperial capital was wide, its white sand meticulously maintained each day.

Fujisai stood calm before the giant facing him, squinting against the sunlight, listening to the wind.

The man wielded a cross-bladed spear — Fujisai did not remember his name.

*Here, I should like us to fight with live blades.*

The man had declared as much, and what he held was not a wooden replica but a real cross-bladed spear.

His stance was respectable, but he was no match for Fujisai.

When two combatants skilled in ki manipulation faced each other, the length of their weapons did not simply determine superiority.

In a fight between two who could not wield ki, the longer weapon conferred a direct advantage, but that was only because they lacked the footwork to crush the distance in a single step.

It was a matter of reach and striking range.

When the first move was missed — or when one was a beat too slow — the length of the weapon exposed the weakness of poor handling.

And in a ki-enhanced single-step strike, that opening was enough to take a life.

The cross-spear wielder was a respectable martial artist, having won his way through the imperial exhibition unperturbed.

Against Fujisai, who held his sword casually over his shoulder, he chose a watching stance.

Caution born of recognizing Fujisai's caliber at a glance.

But it was not a wise choice.

Rather, his only hope of victory lay in pressing the attack with the reach of the cross-spear, seizing the initiative constantly.

By surrendering the initiative — he had already lost.

Fujisai leaned his weight forward in an almost-toppling step.

"——!!"

In that instant, the spearman's eyes flew open, and he unleashed his fastest thrust.

But Fujisai felt no alarm.

This thrust had been *drawn out* by Fujisai.

Sliding the sword that rested on his shoulder smoothly forward, he let the neck-aimed thrust glance off by slightly tilting his body, angling his blade against the cross-guard's branch to deflect its trajectory, then pressed deeper — as the cross-spear's path was diverted upward and the man's torso lay exposed, Fujisai drove his bent elbow into the solar plexus mid-step.

The man folded at the waist. Fujisai seized his left arm, circled behind him, twisted the arm while bearing down his weight, and drove him face-down into the white sand.

Pinning the giant prone and pressing his blade to his throat was as effortless as drawing a breath.

"——Match decided! Victory to the Chief Sword Instructor of the Imperial House, Kazama Fujisai!"

He heard the cries of admiration and applause from the dais, sheathed his blade, rose, and knelt with bowed head.

Having dominated three consecutive bouts against warriors who claimed to be the greatest under heaven — a five-consecutive-championship feat in the imperial exhibition — yet nothing in his heart felt settled.

Victory was a foregone conclusion; he had not experienced a perilous fight in years.

Applause rang, and a voice called to him. He raised his eyes.

One step up from the white sand, the hall of the imperial dais — at the center, the Emperor's seat, enclosed by bamboo blinds.

To the right sat some ten prominent figures, including the warrior houses.

To the left sat four foreigners.

Apart from a large man who radiated unmistakable martial power, they were all women.

He could not be certain which was Orburina.

All of the women, despite their sex, exuded an air of martial capability visible even from a distance, each suggesting they were no easy opponent.

The strongest presence came from the black-haired woman.

But judging by the atmosphere, she was likely a guard.

That left the blonde woman and the silver-haired girl.

The blonde wore an elegant military uniform, while the silver-haired one wore a fluttering outfit beneath her cloak.

The visitors had demanded the right to carry swords, citing religious reasons.

Likely it was out of wariness toward their hosts — and yet, the choice of clothing clearly unsuited for combat raised questions.

He stole a glance, careful not to be rude.

The silver-haired girl's eyes were narrowed, seemingly focused — no, something was different.

Her lips moved in a subtle chewing motion.

The blonde beside her quietly reached toward the girl's thigh as she sat, and the silver-haired girl flinched, eyes widening, pressing her hand down on the other's and turning to stare.

Whispered something by the blonde, she nodded hastily.

He considered seriously whether this might be some kind of signal, but a ridiculous image intruded — a sister pinching a drowsy sibling's thigh to scold her.

In any case, the blonde seemed to hold the superior position.

If so, the silver-haired one was likely Orburina — but she looked impossibly young.

Of course, it was rare to see foreigners at all.

Their appearance and age might differ greatly from those of eastern birth, but at first glance, she looked like a beautiful young girl.

Even assuming she had stopped aging early, it was hard to believe she was over forty.

And yet, he felt an intense sense of wrongness.

In terms of appearance, she was someone who should inspire no wariness at all.

Her demeanor was unmistakably that of someone who did not fight.

And yet, Fujisai *felt the presence of martial arts from her as well.*

Something beyond the five senses was sounding an alarm.

He could see the Emperor communicating something to an attendant behind the bamboo blinds.

The attendant stepped out and went to the foreigners.

This was likely about the match with Fujisai.

Through the interpreter at their side, the three who heard the message looked to the blonde woman.

And she looked to the silver-haired girl.

The blonde seemed briefly uncertain, then nodded, and turned to the silver-haired girl.

Whispered something in her ear, the girl nodded, stood, and conveyed something to the attendant. She had what were presumably her own leather boots brought over, and carefully put them on.

On the girl's feet, they looked crude.

Thick leather boots with steel reinforcement at the toes and heels.

That these were no ordinary footwear was obvious at a glance.

"Fujisai-dono, one more bout, if you would."

"……Yes. As you command."

He was called, stood, and made his way to his position, waiting for her.

The girl, having put on her boots, trotted over in that same childish fashion, and stood before him.

"Thank you for this. Please do your best."

She spoke in fluent local language.

Fujisai, who had not expected her to know their tongue, blurted in surprise:

"A-an honor beyond words, my lady."

"An honor……?"

"The sword arm of Orburina is renowned even in these lands."

"Orburina……"

"……?"

She twisted her head in thought, then clapped her hands.

"I see — the pronunciation changes like that over here, does it?"

"My apologies for any inaccuracy."

"No, Krische's speech might be a little odd too…… this is the first time Krische has actually used it. Shall we use real blades?"

"There are blunted blades and wooden swords available…… what would you prefer?"

It made no difference — in earnest, a single blow would end it regardless.

It was not a meaningful question, but for the sake of appearances before the audience, he needed to ask.

"Hmm…… well, anything is fine. You won't accidentally kill or be killed with real blades, so don't worry. Feel free to go all-out."

The words, delivered with a smile, were the height of arrogance.

She said this having watched the matches that preceded this.

Any underestimation of the girl was now gone.

"Then, as we are."

"Krische should stand over there?"

"……Yes."

She trotted to the opposite position in that childlike way.

Turning to face him, they exchanged bows — and it was the moment Fujisai drew his sword and she drew a curved blade from behind her waist with her right hand.

"……"

As if fair weather had suddenly become a storm — the air changed, the pressure changed.

The wind took on an unpleasant chill across his cheeks, and the bright sunlight seemed to dim.

The call of *begin* echoed and faded like a distant voice.

The conviction that he harbored no complacency, felt just a moment ago — he realized its foolishness in that very instant.

That very conviction was nothing other than complacency.

What the girl held was a notably small curved blade.

The edge barely a shaku long — more the length of a billhook than a sword.

No matter how little the length of a weapon determined the outcome, this was an absurdly short weapon.

And yet, the sinuous curve — like a woman's waist, an obscene line — and the spine-crawling presence it bore made it feel as though the tip were already resting against his throat. The violet eyes that watched him caused his body to stiffen.

The gaze of a serpent. Inorganic. A predator's eyes.

Even the great serpent he had once slain had not possessed an aura so chilling.

As if to sever that invisible pressure, Fujisai brought his drawn sword into a middle guard.

A quiet murmur of surprise rose from around them.

For the past decade, Fujisai had transcended the constraint of fixed stances, residing in the freedom of formlessness.

As water flows, he carved fluid, infinite forms in each fleeting instant, seizing advantage and overwhelming any opponent regardless of their stance.

He had believed this to be the pinnacle of the sword, and had proven it without cease.

But she, too, was formless.

The small curved blade dangled limply, like a drooping willow — and its pressure.

Before that, Fujisai felt terror at placing himself in the anchorless domain of formlessness.

When the heart was muddied, formlessness became finite.

As frozen water cannot flow, stagnation became a binding.

He needed the anchor of a middle guard, the tip directed forward, to cut through her pressure — otherwise he could not even stand before this girl.

At a glance he knew — a formless stance surpassing his own.

So this was Orburina.

Before Fujisai, who had been called peerless under heaven, the solitary moon that he had gazed upon and reached for was right before his eyes.

Even if captured and held in the mirror of water, it was a peak that could never be grasped.

"……Magnificent. I never imagined I would encounter something like this today."

"……?"

"The name Heavenly Sword, bestowed upon you — it is no illusion reflected in a water mirror."

What spilled from his lips was pure admiration.

Orburina stood in a realm beyond the reflection of the moon in water — her stance alone was enough to make Fujisai sense defeat.

He had heard that "Orburina" meant "heavenly sword."

But titles were always inflated.

He had assumed she would be a respectable swordsman, while resigning himself that she would fall far short of his own conception of a heavenly sword.

The heavens were beyond human reach — that was their nature.

He had named his school Suigetsu-ryuu precisely because he was certain he would never arrive there.

Not only himself, but no one in this world — not even those who searched to the end of the future — would ever reach the lonely moon in the sky.

That resignation was overturned by this girl simply standing before him.

"And yet, your small frame must have its limits. ……This is not about superiority in a match."

In technique and spirit, she was decisively superior.

But in that small body, in those arms and legs, there were limits.

The body — the talent one was born with. On that single point, the match could be fought.

All that remained was how close he had come to her realm.

The heart he had thought perfectly still now rippled.

The technique he had thought perfectly polished now showed clouds.

Even so, this body, forged and tempered, bore the weight of all the years behind it.

From the moment he could first remember, he had swung a sword through uncountable seasons.

Eating, sleeping, swinging — gnawing at the path with the instinct of a creature, never doubting that he would reach the summit.

The tenacity engraved in his flesh roused the fighting spirit to challenge this sudden, towering wall.

"……Here I come!"

Fujisai struck first — and the distance between them seemed to collapse, an illusion that left those watching in awe. A transcendent step.

Not fast — simply, purely, *early.*

So smooth and free of waste that human perception failed to track it.

A single step forward.

That act alone was proof of the peerless under heaven.

Even those who knew the concept of a heavenly sword, who knew what Orburina was, would have been moved to awe and admiration.

And yet the silver-haired girl showed no surprise, shifting her weight slightly back — by a hair's breadth.

The horizontal slash aimed at her neck from the left — a blow that would have literally frozen the air — was dodged by a tissue's width.

Fujisai felt no shock. Only unhesitating admiration.

This girl was truly one who had mastered the solitary heavenly sword.

A single swing from the far edge of range, leveraging every inch of his reach.

A divine stroke — not a fraction too much, not a fraction too little — designed solely to cut one inch across the throat.

That she dodged it — literally without a shred of wasted motion — meant Orburina was already beyond human.

A step stripped of every conceivable inefficiency, and the trajectory of the blade.

Her eyes had seen through it, down to the fraction and the hair's-breadth, with total certainty.

She had understood at a single glance — even if Fujisai wielded his body with absolute freedom and delivered the finest single stroke of which he was capable — it would fall short by a hair's-breadth of where she stood.

And having dodged that stroke, the initiative in that instant belonged to Orburina.

The distance had closed.

The girl's strange weapon — the weakness of its shortness negated, the advantage now hers — Fujisai could not win.

Just as Fujisai delivered a stroke of divine quality, she too would deliver one — as effortlessly as breathing.

At theoretical best, he could survive two exchanges.

He was certain that by the third, she would take his head.

Bearing resignation and admiration alike, he committed his all to defense — to bring this match to its rightful conclusion as a fight beyond all precedent.

His intuition told him that, at his best, he could survive two exchanges.

A third exchange, reached by threading the needle — that was where defeat awaited.

If he could not make that real, everything he had devoted to the sword would be meaningless.

The girl's right arm shimmered like a heat haze.

An opening movement that, repeated a thousand times, would sever a thousand throats — and yet it was impossibly gentle.

No tension, no tell.

Though possessing physical form, it was like a breeze — and in the next instant, a gale.

Only in a concentration so deep that even an instant could be distinctly perceived was it possible to perceive this blade.

Even so, by the time he noticed it, it was already halfway through — on the verge of slicing his throat.

Merely dodging would be too late.

Blocking would not suffice either.

And so — the only choice was to attack. To dodge through a strike committed to mutual destruction.

Angling his blade, he unleashed the fastest possible downward diagonal slash.

The girl's reaction was no longer a matter of speed — it bordered on precognition.

The instant he began to angle his blade, she withdrew hers. Ducking beneath his downward slash, she moved to Fujisai's right side.

Without killing her momentum, she planted one weightless foot and pivoted left, spinning behind him.

That footwork was the very incarnation of death in a thousand changing forms.

He sensed that her landing behind him was also a step — a step that, in the next instant, would effortlessly cut his throat.

A wind like a sickle-weasel — an invisible blade seemed ever pressed against his neck.

Even dodging her first strike was a feat one-in-ten-thousand might achieve.

A fleeting exchange invisible to most human eyes.

Even those who could follow it would have offered nothing but lavish praise.

Had anyone but Fujisai accomplished the same, Fujisai himself would have thought so.

But Fujisai was not satisfied.

The true test was the next move.

Only by surviving it would all that he had devoted to the sword be redeemed.

Splitting the instant, in a time that seemed frozen, thoughts bloomed and scattered like sparks——

"——Now! Strike down the foreigners!!"

The shout rang out without warning.

The step that should have cut into Fujisai was instead directed past him.

By the time he looked, she was already on the dais.

Not the same group Fujisai had been in contact with.

Others, who likewise rejected submission to Orburan and sought to sabotage the talks here.

Originally, Fujisai was to have killed Orburina, and the signal would be given.

Whether they had learned of his plan in advance or it was coincidence — regardless, what he felt in that moment was fury.

Everything Fujisai had accumulated to this point.

The decisive bout that should have been the culmination of it all — the supreme defeat — had been defiled.

"Kill the traitors!!"

He roared with fury, and immediately gave chase — and because of that, he saw clearly.

The instant the warrior lord who had given the call drew his sword and stepped toward the foreigners, a silver wind passed through, and a red blossom opened at his throat.

Several who had risen with him stood frozen in disbelief. The comrades who appeared through the sliding screens stared likewise.

In the next instant, she leapt into the air, kicked off the ceiling, and took another man from above.

Fluid and unconstrained.

She had not deliberately exploited an opening.

Despite the acrobatic movement, it registered to the eye as perfectly natural.

As one walks, runs, and steps in — she kicked off the ceiling as a matter of course.

By the time Fujisai had cut down one, Orburina had already cut down two more.

The beauty he glimpsed from the corner of his eye was the very embodiment of natural law.

Each time her blade was swung, it traveled a trajectory that seemed the only possible one, and was delivered exactly as it should be.

By the time Fujisai had cut down two more, she had felled three.

She was not merely killing.

While cutting down men, she was clearly planning the next move, and embodying the ideal in every motion.

It was not only the earlier one-on-one.

The fundamental nature of her swordsmanship — and his — were different, and she overwhelmed him.

Her blade had likely been forged in the chaos of the battlefield.

The remaining three foreigners likewise drew their swords and cut down several, and gathered on the white sand.

In concert, Orburina also alighted on the white sand.

"Silence!! Before my very eyes — how dare you commit such a disgrace……!!"

"Your Majesty……"

The slender-faced young man emerged from behind the bamboo blinds, anger plain on his face, eyes sweeping left and right.

Fujisai immediately moved to shield him.

"Enough, Fujisai! If any among you would add further insult to my guests and mock me, then cut me down first. I shall show you the Emperor's sword……!!"

And taking up a fallen blade, the Emperor glared at all around him.

A voice of piercing resolve.

And yet — whether from excitement or fear — his hand trembled ever so slightly.

Seeing it, Fujisai roared.

"——You are before the Emperor!! ……The matter is decided — lay down your arms!"

Swordsmanship had never been the Emperor's strength.

That was Fujisai's role.

At his shout, the men set down their swords and prostrated themselves, and——

"Seize them!!"

Those who had come running at the commotion took the men into custody.

The Emperor, paying them no mind, descended to the white sand and bowed deeply.

Voices called *Your Majesty*, but the Emperor did not heed them.

"No amount of bowing can atone for this, but I offer my deepest apology for this disgrace."

Hearing this, Orburina spoke to the blonde woman as if asking her to interpret, and the blonde replied.

Turning back to face him, Orburina opened her mouth and spoke.

"Um, please raise your head. Your Majesty's feelings have been well received, but since blood has been spilled, we shall take our leave for today. We would like to arrange a proper meeting on another day, where we can discuss things calmly. ……That is the message."

"……I am grateful."

When the Emperor raised his head, Orburina pressed a fingertip to her lips in thought, tilted her head, and looked at Fujisai.

"Also, our match was interrupted, but — should we continue?"

Fujisai was momentarily dumbfounded, and met the Emperor's eyes.

She was utterly unperturbed, as if everything that had just occurred might as well not have happened.

In truth, for her, it may have been nothing more than that.

"Your gracious words are more than I deserve — however, that bout revealed to me the shallowness of my training. If the opportunity should present itself again someday, I would be most honored."

"Hmm…… is that so? Ehehe, then perhaps some other time."

Seeing her then, he was struck by a realization.

In that brief span of time, she had cut down six men, and he had cut down three at her side.

Fujisai was drenched in blood from head to toe, and yet — on her fluttering cloak, on her beautiful silver hair — not a single drop of blood had fallen.

She remained as she had been during their match — beautiful and untouched.

The instant he noticed this, he understood the height at which she stood, and the strength drained from his body.

What he had been looking at was not even a reflection in a water mirror.

He had merely been gazing up at the wavering moon-shadow from the bottom of the water.

Having fought his way to the surface, how beautiful the radiance above.

From that day, Fujisai changed his presumptuous name.

He was an iron boat sunk to the bottom — the lonely moon in the sky remained far, far beyond.

* * *

Fortunately, Orburan did not demand anything great as a result of the rebellion.

It had been an assassination attempt against the supreme commander and the King's sister.

The Emperor, who had been prepared for war, was instead offered the opportunity for dialogue as promised, and on the condition that submission be pledged henceforth, the incident was treated as largely pardoned — even considering the mountainous, forested terrain and the island's distance from the continent, it was extraordinarily lenient.

The Emperor was overjoyed, relieved, and clapped Tesshuusai on the shoulder.

*I am fortunate to have a loyal retainer like you at my side,* he said.

In the Emperor's unknowing mind, that was how it was.

Though Orburina had not been defeated, neither had Fujisai lost; he had acted swiftly in response to the warriors' treachery and helped stop it — a loyal retainer.

The Emperor believed this had also made a strong impression on the other side.

The truth — that Tesshuusai had himself been among those plotting the treachery — never once crossed the Emperor's mind.

His swift action had been born of fury at having his bout interrupted.

It was neither righteous indignation, nor political calculation, nor devotion to the Emperor — it was, from start to finish, a laughably impulsive act driven by personal outrage.

And yet the Emperor trusted Tesshuusai, praising him as a retainer without equal.

From that day forward, Tesshuusai swore in his heart to never betray the Emperor's trust, to never make those words a lie — to serve faithfully as the loyal retainer he was believed to be.

* * *

Some thirty years had passed — relations between the two nations had been good, thanks in no small part to the Emperor's statesmanship — but the events of that day were becoming a thing of the past, fading from memory.

That Orburina had not been defeated was, perhaps, worse than if she had.

The word was spreading that Tesshuusai was a Sword Saint equal to or surpassing Orburina, and that in a genuine fight, the victor would be Tesshuusai.

The greatest swordsman under heaven was Tesshuusai, they said.

Even the continent's greatest hero could not defeat this island nation's Sword Saint.

And so, they said, Orburan could certainly be beaten by the strength of this nation's warriors.

The current state of groveling before that country must be overturned, and the Emperor's majesty made known.

Such voices were growing, and shamefully, even among Tesshuusai's own students, there were fools drawn to this thinking.

It was then that the Emperor informed him of Orburina's impending visit.

Tesshuusai bowed before the Emperor and petitioned for one final battle in his life — the continuation of that day's bout.

He wished to inscribe upon his life the third exchange — the defeat he had missed — the greatest bout under heaven.

The venue was the dojo — before the eyes of his arrogant, blinkered senior students.

When the announcement of her arrival came, the one who appeared was a girl unchanged from those days.

"It's been a long time, Fujisai-san. Krische heard you wished to continue that bout from a long time ago."

Ignoring those who looked dubiously at her unchanged appearance, Tesshuusai bowed deeply.

"Yes. I wished…… to cross swords with you once more."

At those words, she placed her hand on her sword and rose.

Glancing at the men to either side and then at him, she smiled and asked.

"Yes. Well then…… shall we begin right away?"

That day, it had been three exchanges.

But thirty years had passed — this day, the culmination of his life's swordsmanship.

How close had he managed to come?

"If that is agreeable, by all means."

And today — how much closer could he come?

As when he had first taken up the sword as a child, the instant he drew his blade, the premonition of defeat was there.

And yet, even as she took up her curved blade, his heart was still — a mirror's surface, reflecting the moon on high.

Once, he had called his school Suigetsu-ryuu.

The solitary moon in the empty sky — from his resignation that it was merely a phantom, beyond human reach, he had likened his own sword to the moon reflected on water.

And in truth, what his hand held was but a water-moon.

But what stood before him now was not.

In her hand was the radiant principle of the moon, a true and certain mastery.

Not a phantom, not an illusion — a heavenly sword that laughed away his foolish resignation.

The phantom moon he had once caught in a water mirror was now nothing but foam — and from that day, he had captured only the outline of that moon.

Both alike, the swords they held were the formless, infinite drooping willow.

That he could stand in that form before her filled him with pride.

"Whenever you are ready, Fujisai-san."

"I changed that name, on that day. ……It was presumptuous, before one such as you."

"……?"

He sank down as if crumbling, and she sank with him.

Even a single step — at the uttermost extreme of principle.

The solitary moon remained far above — he gazed at it, smiled, and narrowed his eyes.

What he was about to deliver was the culmination of his sword.

But it was not a sword wielded to win.

——It was a sword to reclaim the defeat of that day.

# Chapter 330: Simulation-Game-Style Country Descriptions

■——Simulation-game-style country descriptions (as of Chapter 1's start point)

A bit of fun in simulation-game style; if you don't like that sort of thing, beware.

○ Kingdom of Alberan — Difficulty: ★★★ (Difficulty: the canonical historical route is a tutorial)

Economy: 4 — Military: 5 — Tech: 4 — Population: 4 — Personnel: 5+

Located comparatively to the northwest of the continent, a once-superpower.

In the past it controlled half the continent; having concluded the Sacred Spirit Pact with the dragon and spread it as international law, there is no nation on the continent that does not know Alberan. After splitting from Elsren it lost much territory, but it has not lost all of its former power; it remains one of the strong nations, with a strong army, high technology, and economic strength.

However, it is surrounded by strong neighbors — Elsren to the east, Elderant to the west, Galshan to the south — and given the hostile relations, caution is needed. Furthermore, in the main scenario "The Two Cursed Children," the current king has fallen ill from an unknown cause, and beneath the surface a struggle is being waged between the princess and the royal brother over succession. The royal palace harbors a foreboding atmosphere, and a misstep can plunge you into crisis even early. Although strong, this nation requires consideration in many points.

Also, this country at times produces personnel of strangely high ability — but caution there too. They are hard to distinguish, but most have great problems with their humanity, and mishandling them can become a double-edged sword.

Originally this country has many talented persons, so eliminating these early is one option. In post-Elsren-split scenarios the policy "Removal of the Cursed Children" is implemented, eliminating these talents at birth, but it is not perfect, and the policy has many disadvantages — keep in mind their excellence may bare its fangs.

Whether to use them or remove them. That alone changes the play feel — a fascinating country.

When the royal-brother faction's split is prevented, the strength of the resulting "Perfect Alberan" is something to behold. "Gildanstein Alberan" reforms via the interaction event "Uncle and Niece" with "Krische Christand." First, dissolve the conflict with "Kreschenta Alberan" through interaction. In the fourteen-years-prior scenario "The Babe Who Did Not Cry," choosing "End the Bad Custom" can produce "Krischeria Alberan," which makes things smoother. With her interaction, "Gildanstein Alberan" reforms.

☆ Recommended Unit Type

· Light infantry

As a former superpower, the average military level is high overall.

The vast knowledge and know-how accumulated by those who once dominated half the continent. The military structure built from it remains worthy of a king's army, with high redundancy.

Above-standard in every aspect — that is Alberan's appeal.

Of these, picking one would be light infantry.

Alberan's terrain has many forests and mountains. The light infantry trained in this environment is highly capable, and combined with the aforementioned redundancy enables stable, highly sophisticated tactical coordination.

But defensive capability is no different from other nations' light infantry; handle them with care.

· Jaleia Gashea

Alberan-exclusive top-tier heavy infantry. Following the canonical route from main scenario "The Two Cursed Children," triggering Alberan late events "The Birth of Alberinea" and "Mass Production of Magical Weaponry" leads relatively smoothly to availability.

Both breakthrough and attack power are off the charts; you will not lose head-on combat. But the unit is very expensive, so beware of surprise attacks and sabotage.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

The basics against Alberan are how to avoid Alberinea-related events. Once "Krische Christand" achieves merit and gains rank, she becomes unstoppable; once Alberinea events progress she gains "Alberinea" and automatically becomes a top-tier commander unit.

Especially Elsren and Elderant should avoid combat in the kingdom's north as much as possible to avoid "Alberinea Awakening" triggered by her first battle. Although you can avoid it by overwhelmingly defeating the Christand army quickly, on Difficulty: Hard or above the Christand army has abnormally high commander stats, top-tier morale and discipline, and short-term defeat is effectively impossible — avoid combat instead.

Even if you avoid these historical events, or have them turned off, this country remains very strong, and given enough time "Krische Christand" automatically becomes the northern army's commander unit, so when conquering, again avoid combat with the northern army and aim for swift capital fall.

Particularly on Difficulty: Historical, "Krische Christand" has the trait "Heavenly Sword," which dramatically increases tactic-success rate; she will frequently force one-on-one duels on your commander units. As time passes, or by event, when she organizes her private soldiers — the "Black Century" or the "Black Flag Special Service Battalion" — her tactic-success rate effectively becomes 100%, making her unkillable; the key is sealing the northern army. Also, when she holds a fortress, large siege engines are destroyed by the same "Heavenly Sword" effect, so siege-and-seal with massed forces is the answer.

Only Galshan can ignore the north and aim for capital-fall by all-out attack from the start. Before northern reinforcements arrive, you must defeat "Dagren Garkha," "Gildanstein Alberan," and "Felwors Keesliton," which is very hard, but afterward the difficulty drops greatly.

However, "Kreschenta Alberan" has an extremely high probability of joining the northern army, triggering Alberinea-related event "The Two Cursed Children." After that, with the event "Princess as Banner," remnants will rise and oppose, so swiftly raise affinity through interaction to prevent and contain her — that is the prerequisite.

Thereafter, protecting the north and keeping the two's happiness gauge (※) high allows stable territorial expansion — an ideal start. Some call it player-cheat.

(※) A trigger for rebellion and so on, one of the personnel-unit parameters. It increases or decreases by personality traits and events. But for these two, isolating them in the same place and leaving them automatically raises happiness above max — a mysterious specification (possibly a bug); unless triggered to hostile action they are completely neutralized. Reports say that when she shows up in random events in this state, she is wearing an apron dress (verification needed).

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Krische Christand"

Even at low difficulty she pulverizes you with crushing stats and skills; on Difficulty: Historical the Heavenly Sword Krische is unmanageable. She forcibly raises tactic-success rate, and endlessly performs the normally low-success "Head-Hunt" (※ forces one-on-one, dramatically lowers morale). The basics of the Alberan war are how not to fight her. Except for some nations, on Difficulty: Historical, drop any plan but allied victory.

· "Kreschenta Alberan"

If given time after the succession event, she will, with overwhelming strategy and politics, take diplomatic supremacy and force a one-sided game. Because of "Trait: Cursed Child of Alberan," giving her combat experience turns her in a flash into a commander unit on par with her elder sister, so beware.

· "Bogan Christand"

A commander with high stats in everything except politics — command, force, strategy, charisma. Since he holds far too many continent-top personnel, defeating him head-on is extremely tough even ignoring "Krische Christand." Conversely, if you can seal him, you can suppress many talents at once.

· "Norzan Velraigh"

Top-class commander surpassing even "Bogan Christand." Skilled in politics; over time he becomes independent. Heading numerous continent-leading personnel, Alberan is dangerous even setting aside the two cursed children.

· "Dagren Garkha"

A commander who looks like a brute but has high strategy. Like "Bogan Christand," charisma and esteem (※ unlike charisma, this varies sharply with battle history and actions, raising soldier capability) are very high; defeating his army of high morale is hard. Strategic detour is the orthodox approach.

· "Gildanstein Alberan"

The greatest threat when isolating and sealing the two cursed children. Top-class commander, but for all his charisma he has high notoriety; weaken him with indirect plays. If reformed by succession events, he becomes flawless and dreaded.

In the main scenario, one of the hardest nations to keep alive. Either side you take in the unification war, you'll be the first crushed. To play, recommended is the era of Wise King Wetharius, e.g. "The Two Reines" scenario. With good location, continental supremacy is potentially open.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Large-scale weapons

A nation of skilled engineers; weapon quality and quantity are far above others. Mass-produced during fortification with focus on range, power, and rate of fire — very powerful in defense. They convert easily to siege weapons; if turned outward early, they are a great force.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

With Galgain's Wall and other fortified cities, head-on conquest is hard. To target Karent, the eastern nations turn to defense; if from the west, prepare accordingly. In the east, first rule the surroundings. Karent has no excellent commander units in main-scenario, so given decent forces, you can take it.

· None in particular.

○ Kingdom of Gelganik — Difficulty: ★★★

Economy: 4 — Military: 5 — Tech: 4 — Population: 4 — Personnel: 4

A great power dominating the northwest of the continent's east; national scale near Alberan's. Descendants of the Lusheran Empire which once ruled the entire east, sharing roots with Futaria, the small northwesternmost state, which it protects. Their imperial capital was turned into Lake Lusheran by the magical-dragon disaster and most was burned, but several regions were unhurt; they renamed and rebuilt.

The Lusheran Empire originated from horse-riders centered on plains; Gelganik likewise. With multiple sources of fine horses and cavalry-centered military, known as the Country of Knights.

Crops, ores, livestock — all blessed; self-sufficient internally; the most stable east-side nation. Stability and slack provide the foundation for a strong military; for east-unification, the foremost candidate. Provided you don't slacken, you can go ahead in nation-vs-nation battles.

Excellent personnel; especially commander quantity and quality are continent-top. Distance from Alberan makes Difficulty: Historical continental unification impossible, but other difficulties are easiest in the east. With aggressive policy, on Difficulty: Historical, allied victory with Alberan is very hard.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Heavy cavalry, light cavalry

Two main cavalry types in Gelganik. Light, fast horses bred in the south; sturdy heavy ones in the north. Used in turn, by rear, flank cavalry charges and rear-disruption. All highly disciplined; heavy cavalry equipment alone overwhelms other nations' cavalry.

Young nobles, regardless of upper-or-lower-class, are required to make their first battle in cavalry; high mana-holder ratio; elite. Almost all are strategy-trained elites; combat power in solo action is world-top. Wielding such cavalry, in plain-field battle, one of the continent's top five.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Strongest east-nation; aside from personnel, national strength and military rival Alberan. Personnel too far surpass average national levels; expect very tough battles.

So long as the field is Gelganik's choice, you cannot win in eastern battles; lure them, deal blows, sap forces. In forests their power is more easily dulled; basically luring into forests.

But while their cavalry is especially strong, the army is faultless; head-on, you will surely struggle. Particularly, "Alfmarz Gelganik"-led "King's Army" boasts the east's strongest, in equal numbers a sure loss. Pay close attention. Don't pick a single decisive head-on battle.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Alfmarz Gelganik"

Number-one overall in the east; commander unit. Top-class commander ability, on par with Alberan's monstrous top commanders, plus high political ability — also a king, taking diplomatic supremacy. Eliminate as soon as possible.

· "Olivars Gelganik"

A scaled-down version of his father "Alfmarz." High growth; eliminate before he grows. The other princes are average; dispatching him is highly valuable.

· "Triskes Savak"

A famed Gelganik general. Force is not high, but command and strategy exceed Alfmarz; tactic-success rate is very high. Even at low troop counts, do not slack.

○ Toran Tribal Confederation — Difficulty: ★★★★

Economy: 2 — Military: 4 — Tech: 2 — Population: 3 — Personnel: 3

One of the east's three great nations, in the south. A nomad confederation ruling the south's vast plains; subjugating numerous farming-based small states. Holding farmer-life would weaken them; for centuries their center has remained nomadic.

Inferior in economy and tech, but as a tribal nation where most men are warriors, fearsome; especially nomadic cavalry as army backbone. Mobility and information speed inconceivable for normal nations grant strategic supremacy. Few troops, but tens of thousands of nomadic cavalry, used well, easily break double their force.

Forces from subject states draw on infantry, with not many siege engines but enough; for invasion, more than sufficient. Subjugation-friendship with surroundings; tribute is the main income.

Strongly hostile to Gelganik, but in direct contact with the Sui Empire to the east, they cannot commit fully; skirmishes continue. The other side has good cavalry too; from national-strength gap, slightly disadvantaged. Conversely on Sui's side they hold an edge.

A complete nomadic confederation with very high potential.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Nomadic cavalry

Their horsemanship — said to ride before standing — outstrips others; light cavalry and light bow-cavalry discipline overwhelming. On plains, hit-and-run alone wins most. Only Gelganik approaches them in the east; if you can deal with their cavalry, in plain-rich east no rivals.

The advantage of having cavalry — over 10,000 — as main force, not infantry, is great; outside forests and sieges, large advantages, but how to preserve main force in such fields and win is key.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Free hand to nomadic cavalry means likely loss. How to constrain them, hold key points, is vital. Settlements scattered as their movement points are vital; attacking with sacrifices reduces their options. Captive interrogation for info is crucial. Confederation-natured: heavy blows lose "authority" (※ tied to subjects' loyalty, especially impactful in confederations) and they split — "let flesh be cut to cut bone" mindset is key.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Sard Toran"

Toran great chieftain with high command and strategy; commanding nomadic cavalry, very troublesome. Charisma not low but notoriety high; espionage-sourced rifts among vassals work.

· "Rafar Ziga"

King of a long-subordinate small state. Force, command, strategy combined; "Castle-Toppler of a Hundred" — undefeated in sieges. "Trait: Castle-Toppler" buffs tactic-success in siege/defense; if fighting, do so on field. Take strategic advantage and fell him.

○ Sui Empire — Difficulty: ★★★★

Economy: 4 — Military: 5 — Tech: 4 — Population: 5+ — Personnel: 4

One of the east's three great nations, easternmost. Rich soil and long coast; the east's largest land area and population. Not as much as Elsren but very high national strength; in pure power the east's top nation, but plagued by recurring civil wars; once every few years troubled by civil war. A misjudgment leaves them exposed to outside threats and they collapse.

Conversely, military know-how is built up; soldier-training and military tech are notable. Commander quality is high; if united, eastern unification is not hard.

Main-scenario emperor isn't witless but is mediocre; if aiming to unify, replace him early.

On Difficulty: Historical, like Gelganik, distance from Alberan rules out continental unification; unlike them, allied victory is comparatively easier — try it.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Crossbowmen — Heavy crossbowmen

How to efficiently use conscripts as army backbone — improving for that point, their crossbows turn amateurs immediately into combat power. Inferior in range and rate, but at mid-range power rivals bows; fast-train and quantity are strengths.

Trained bowmen do not appear endlessly; even if defeated today, in a year or ten years you whittle them down — eventually you outshoot. The king's tactic, born of vast manpower, eventually wins against any opponent.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Provoking can unite them; espionage-driven splitting is the orthodox. Against the inexhaustible Sui in head-on, you'll lose endurance first. If fighting, aim for short-term decisive victory by overwhelming force, like Alberan in the unification war.

If split, abundant personnel and strengths are lost; nothing to fear.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "La Simpha"

Top-class continent force; commander unit. Average command and strategy, but with strong private unit Shūrai (Roaring Thunder) and "Trait: Head-Hunter," forces "Head-Hunt." In forests where tactic-success rises, beware.

· "Lu Zen"

Sui's foremost strategist; very high command and strategy. Other stats above average — continent-leading commander unit; potentially more dangerous than Simpha.

· "Riu Roulai"

Sui's first prince; young at main-scenario start, but grows into an all-rounder model king. United Sui can be the east's strongest; eliminate early.

○ Kingdom of Skrif — Difficulty: ××

Economy: 2 — Military: 3 — Tech: 3 — Population: 1+ — Personnel: 3

In the southwest of the continent, adjacent to Karent; surrounded east by Toran and north by Gelganik, but maintains sovereignty long without subjugation. All cities in the great forest are fortified; despite small national scale, blessed in personnel; commander quality consistent across eras.

All commanders carry "Trait: Forest-Bred"; in forests they fight beyond stats. At home they fight even great powers favorably, but eventually endurance fails. Reading the times, avoiding waste battles, in battle, how efficiently to cause minimal damage and force enemy retreat — Skrif demands more delicate play than other nations.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Light infantry

Light infantry specialized for forest combat. In forests they overwhelm normal light infantry, with greatly reduced fatigue. But Skrif has very few heavy infantry, so light infantry forms the line. Not in melee but head-on, the strengths are lost; against equipment-superior opponents in formation, easily overrun. Use with care.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

So long as you don't attack first, they stay in their territory; leaving alone is fine. Attack when finishing eastern unification, advancing west. Especially "Belzadia Nitrias" is a top-class continent commander; forest-combat capability is overwhelming. Esteem is extreme, raising soldier ability greatly. If you don't mind notoriety, burn the forest.

But a relatively dialogable nation; non-aggression proposals are accepted; not fighting and bypassing is one option. Resists invading Karent; pre-stoke the discord.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Belzadia Nitrias"

The "war-god"; top-class continent commander unit. Effectively defending Skrif's land and sovereignty alone in this strife; in forest combat, even doubled forces fall easily. To fight, prepare a comparable commander and ample forces.

○ Holy Spirit Nation of Futaria — Difficulty: ×

Economy: 1 — Military: 3 — Tech: 2 — Population: 2 — Personnel: 3

A nation built by descendants of the once-flourishing great empire Lusheran. Capital built at the foot of the mountain leading to Dogaruaz where the magical dragon sleeps; declared strict defense-only. High mana-holder ratio; tame the giant blue eagles inhabiting the mountains as mounts — nature close to Kreisharana but not as extreme; closer to a normal nation.

Few in population, but strong soldiers; with many sky-conqueror Blue Eagle cavalry, known in the east.

Legends say past pride summoned the dragon's wrath; faith forbids extravagance, and despite a fine location with quality magical crystal, economy is not vibrant; processing tech is behind.

For continental supremacy, many issues including these to resolve; a hard country, but mere survival is not so hard.

★ Recommended Unit Type

○ Blue Eagle cavalry

Once feared as messengers of death, these giant blue-feathered eagles. Their wing-beats whip up gusts; arrows do not pass; mounts of theirs cannot be weak.

Like griffin-riders, with sky-bypass, formations are meaningless; the elite-mounted ones raise "Head-Hunt" success greatly. Even doubled force, used well, can take the commander's head and turn the tide.

△ Countermeasures in the Main Scenario

Like Kreisharana in the "Founding King Bazarishe" scenario — a brute nation with strong soldiers and air cavalry. They declare strict defense; if you don't touch them, they don't strike. Leave alone is best. Decent relations leads to alliance via the Wetharius Pact.

If you must destroy them, ensure high-force units as commanders/escorts to counter "Head-Hunt." Basically no fortified cities, so defense itself is not great; destruction itself is not hard.

But never touch Dogaruaz. The magical dragon is literal; mere attempts at interaction can ruin a country, in some cases lay the entire continent to waste.

○ Notable Units in the Main Scenario

· "Kotrūz"

Continent-leading force; commander unit. As commander he lacks strategy and charisma, but his force and "Heavenly-Wing Knight" feature aim only for "Head-Hunt" — a foul brute; beware.

· Other "Heavenly-Wing Knight" units

Inferior to Kreisharana's in force and Head-Hunt success but more numerous. "Rear Descent" and similar lets them take rear, get pinch-trapped; commanders should have force and strategy together.

○ Tōshō Country — Difficulty: ★★★★★

Economy: 3 — Military: 4 — Tech: 4 — Population: 3 — Personnel: 4

A nation governing the island east of the continent. Basically free of the continent's strife, but inside the island, civil wars endlessly; unique tech and cultural evolution.

Mostly forested but rich land, especially blessed in mineral seams; prone to disasters — storms, eruptions, earthquakes.

Mountain-and-forest land; high mana-holder ratio; soldier quality is favored; despite the continent's eyes on its rich resources, sovereignty kept; long history.

For continental advance, the unique tech is a hindrance: hard to adapt to wide-open plains; if developed via coast and island lines, allied victory with Alberan is possible. So long as you cede continental dominion, drawing Alberan's alliance is not hard.

Heavily armored warriors on sturdy horses, but main weapon is long-bow — special cavalry unit. Riders are mostly warrior-class mana-holders; close-range long-bow direct fire is effectively unavoidable. Compared to continent-nations, specialized in mountain and forest fights; horse-based strategic-detour avoidance is hard; on home ground big advantages.

Impassable obstacles everywhere; distant from the continent so terrain info is hard to obtain. Fortunately, they barely advance to the continent, but invading them is very tough.

Tribal-confederation-like, but with strong wariness/hostility toward continent-people; hard to split; under invasion they unite. Sacred Spirit Pact does not work; even captives are not safe; if missed they will ambush endlessly. Culturally, technically advanced unbroken tribes — not shy about cruelty; losses devastate your morale. Literally exterminating one by one — gains aren't small but expect corresponding hardship.

After negotiation with the Tei (emperor), high probability of revolt; nation overthrow forces restart; basically remove dissidents first before negotiating.

Strategy, politics, charisma — Tōshō Country's ruler. Better as administrator than predecessor, but force is low; in warrior-strong Tōshō, esteem is low.
