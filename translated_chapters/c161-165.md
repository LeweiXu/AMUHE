# Chapter 161: Cookies

"I-I've finished here……Bery-sama, Selene-sama's room is done."

"Thank you, Elvena-sama. Hehe, you've been getting ahead of things so quickly since you came that I'm running out of work myself."

Elvena had gone searching for Bery, who was supposed to be doing laundry, only to find her already in the kitchen preparing a meal.

From the look of it, the soup was already finished.

Elvena froze, mentally reviewing the division of tasks.

After the two of them had gathered undergarments, sleepwear, and laundry together, Bery was to handle the washing while Elvena took care of room cleaning.

She had cleaned her own assigned room, then Selene's room.

When she went to clean the room that was nominally Krische's — the room shared by Bery, Kreschenta, and Krische — the cleaning was already done.

The bed, neatly made since morning — that was where the confusion began.

Bery should have been doing laundry.

Yesterday's rain had prevented hanging anything out, so there should have been a considerable amount — even accounting for Elvena working quickly, Bery could not possibly have finished in the time it took to clean two rooms.

Yet when Elvena rushed to the courtyard, the laundry was already hanging on the line, drying.

The residual moisture in the clothes clearly suggested they had been there for some time.

They must have been hung before Elvena had even started on the second room.

What was going on — Elvena grew more bewildered.

She decided to at least clean the front entrance and service entrance she had noticed earlier, and picked up a mop.

It had rained until nighttime yesterday, and the entrances had been a bit dirty.

——But the dirt was already gone, without a trace.

It should still have been there moments ago, before she had gone to the courtyard — yet the floor was spotlessly polished.

Where had Bery disappeared to?

She rushed outside to the jade tiger — Gururun's area.

The sleepy, contented Gururun was relaxing in the purpose-built shelter.

It had obviously already been fed.

The horse was also munching happily on hay.

At this point, what she felt was no longer bewilderment but a chill down her spine.

It was not even noon yet. Glancing at the kitchen window from outside, she noticed steam rising, and—

She ran into the kitchen to find the soup already complete.

"Is something the matter, Elvena-sama?"

"N-no……"

She could have passed for younger than Elvena.

Silky red hair.

Her head was slightly below Elvena's, and her large eyes were those of a girl.

She looked young, yet her bosom was full and her waist slender — combined with her manner, she seemed like a composed, mature lady. A most enigmatic woman.

Wrapped in an apron dress, the way she tilted her head held a refined allure, both girlish and elegant.

——But one must not be deceived by appearances.

Beneath that exterior, she was nearly as superhuman as Krische.

Since coming here, Elvena had primarily assisted Krische.

The household work had been an afterthought — it was only after Bery was stabbed that she had begun taking it seriously.

Tending to the Queen and maintaining the estate — sharing duties with Anne as Bery's replacement, she had thrown herself into the work. But that was where the first hints of something off had begun.

This estate was simply too large.

When she thought about it, every time Elvena returned, the estate was in near-perfect condition.

No different from the era of Roland's mansion, which had employed a large staff.

Moreover, Bery had also been diligently serving as the Queen's personal attendant, so she could not possibly have devoted all her time to estate management.

She, too, should have been doing it on the side.

On top of that, Bery had been entrusted with managing the Christand ledgers, and calmly assisted Krische and Selene with their paperwork as well.

Ask her any number and the answer came without pause — the contents of nearly all of Christand were likely recorded in that red-haired head.

Krische's seemingly tall tale about Bery managing the Gurgain estate entirely alone — the moment she realized it was no tale at all, she should have understood.

This woman was the servant among servants — Bery Argan.

As Elvena stood now, she could not even step on her shadow.

"……Um, is something wrong?"

Bery blushed faintly, discomfited by Elvena's intent gaze, her eyes darting over herself.

Elvena caught herself and shook her head.

"Oh, Bery-sama. There's some hay in your hair……"

"Hm? Oh, so there is. How embarrassing……"

She plucked the straw and pressed a hand to her cheek, looking abashed.

It was charming, but Elvena could not afford to be charmed.

So the horse and Gururun had been fed by her after all.

The faint possibility that Krische or Selene had fed them before leaving was now eliminated.

"When I actually watch your work, Bery-sama……I realize how far I still have to go."

"Um……that isn't so at all. Elvena-sama, you're perfectly——"

"No, truly. ……Compared to your workload, mine is perhaps a third……no, a quarter, a fifth at most. It's made me realize just how much I've been relying on others."

She sighed, sounding genuinely pathetic.

Bery smiled wryly and approached, gently stroking her head.

"I simply know this place well, and I've learned where to cut corners. My cleaning is just the bare minimum, and the effort I put into each task is quite different from yours. You're pushing yourself a bit too hard, Elvena-sama — perhaps you should relax a little."

She was slightly shorter.

Yet the gentle smile on her lips was refined and full of feminine grace.

Being patted on the head brought not discomfort, but a sense of reassurance.

She was simply beautiful, through and through.

"Do what's needed where it's needed. Handle time-consuming tasks in parallel……the reasoning is simple. If you relax, get more comfortable with the work, and learn to see the whole picture as a flow, I'm sure you'll pick it up quickly, Elvena-sama."

"……Yes. Thank you. Um……starting tomorrow, would it be all right if I shadow you for a while?"

"Hmm……yes, let's try working together for a bit."

Bery said, moving away to ladle soup into a small dish.

"Would you mind taste-testing for me?"

"Of course."

Elvena smiled, nodded, and accepted the dish.

The aroma that tickled her nose was the rich fragrance of vegetables melted into the broth.

The ingredients were meltingly soft, and when she tasted it, a deep, full flavor delighted her tongue.

How had she managed to make this while doing all those other chores?

It was a work of art, crafted with an attentiveness that defied belief.

"……The ingredients have dissolved right in, and today's is wonderful too."

"I see."

*I'm glad it turned out well*, Bery said, looking somehow relieved.

Elvena gave a wry smile.

Could she still feel uncertain, even producing cooking of this caliber?

Every dish she made was more delicious than anything Elvena had tasted at Roland's mansion.

Each vegetable, each piece of meat.

Whether it was the meticulous preparation, the perfect balance of seasoning, or the combinations — even a simple grilled dish became a work of art.

With a deeply layered soup like this, even Elvena's palate struggled to articulate exactly what made it so good.

Yet despite possessing such skill, Bery was never satisfied.

She was likely perceiving things on a level Elvena could not fathom.

"……Had you already started this morning?"

"Well, I only did the prep and put it on the heat. I soaked the laundry in soapy water first, and went from there."

Elvena finally understood the difference between herself and Bery.

Not sequential, one-task-at-a-time work — but parallel, simultaneous.

Bery surveyed the whole picture and processed work as a surface, not a line.

Her way of seeing things was fundamentally different.

Just as one would finish prep work before the water boiled, she applied that thinking not just to cooking, but to everything.

"See? When you hear it explained, it's all quite simple. Get used to it, build your routine, put together your schedule for what you'll finish that day……it's just a repetition of small things, like cleaning while the laundry dries."

"……I see."

The words were easier said than done, yet when she said them, they sounded like the simplest thing in the world.

"So please don't push yourself. Think of it like a little game of building blocks — keep it light."

"……Right now, you look to me like a fairy-tale sorceress, Bery-sama."

"Oh, you flatter me. Hehe, but I'm sure you'll soon realize that the sorceress was just a sleight-of-hand artist all along. I'm still far from where I should be."

She looked a touch embarrassed, chiding gently with a playful smile.

In moments like these, she was remarkably similar to Krische.

Both of them would say, *anyone should be able to do this much*.

Surely they both set impossibly high standards for themselves.

Blind to how others saw them, always fixing their eyes on perfection — hopelessly oblivious to praise.

Whenever compliments came, both would look troubled and laugh ambiguously.

Perhaps it was precisely these qualities in each other that drew them together.

They could afford to be proud of their abilities, yet they remained endlessly modest.

Krische praised Bery, and Bery praised Krische — and surely neither would ever be satisfied with herself.

Their way of being was that of ascetics, and come to think of it, Selene and Kreschenta shared something of that quality.

Christand was, it seemed, a gathering of such seekers.

Understanding at last what the insurmountable wall was, Elvena sighed.

When the wall kept retreating, what could one do?

Half-resigned yet resolute, she smiled.

"Then I shall begin by becoming a sleight-of-hand artist. Please continue to guide me, Bery-sama."

"Um……please, take it easy……"

"Yes. I must reach that state as quickly as possible."

She could not surpass her, but she could follow in her wake.

That much was surely possible, and that meant there was nothing for it but effort.

Before Bery's troubled face, Elvena was simply, utterly earnest.

* * *

Everyone returned for the midday meal.

Soup and bread — a light lunch, followed by cleanup.

Everything proceeded as usual today. No issues with the cooking.

Krische seemed slightly less energetic, as though she was not feeling well — that worried Bery a bit, but she could not stop Krische if she insisted on going to work.

Krische had said she would finish early, so perhaps something lighter for dinner — easy to eat, nutritious. Maybe less meat tonight.

After lunch, Bery finished the household tasks with Elvena, then sat in her room working on the ledgers while mulling over such thoughts.

"Would you like a refill?"

"Thank you. Um……you may take a break if you like?"

"No, I'd like to observe your work today, Bery-sama."

Elvena sat beside her, posture impeccable.

Her well-formed features were sharp and alert, her hair neatly trimmed at the shoulders and glossy.

Not a single gap in her bearing — the very picture of a proper servant.

Bery gave a wry smile and scratched her cheek.

Anne was also a hardworking and diligent woman, but Elvena was the same — and lately, even more intensely so. It was almost enough to make Bery feel embarrassed.

It was her nature, no doubt, but there was something about pushing too hard that concerned Bery.

Enthusiasm itself was wonderful.

But she worried about Elvena pushing herself to the point of illness, and being gazed at with such sparkling, admiring eyes was rather ticklish.

She sipped the tea Elvena had poured and let out a breath.

Even without being able to taste it, the warmth and aroma provided some small comfort.

"Elvena-sama, please, sit here."

"Um……yes."

Bery stood, took out a teacup for Elvena, placed it on the desk.

Then she poured tea, invited her to sit, and closed the ledger.

She had thought to distract herself with some idle chat — but that, in fact, was Bery's greatest weakness.

"H-have you been settling in here?"

"Yes. Everything is so new……I was a bit bewildered at first, but I've gotten quite used to daily life."

"I see. That's good……"

The conversation died, and her gaze drifted.

Work talk, cooking, or the kind of polite greetings that qualified as courtesy — those were manageable, but casual conversation was genuinely difficult when she stopped to think about it.

A conversation without purpose or agenda — what was she supposed to talk about?

The weather might be safe.

——*Nice weather today, isn't it?*

——*Yes, it's lovely.*

No, that would not do. The conversation would end right there.

Then again, Elvena's past was not a happy one.

She certainly could not ask about old memories — that was off-limits.

Today's work was done.

Then perhaps tomorrow's tasks — no, that would only make Elvena tense up more.

Elvena was watching Bery's face curiously.

A thought struck her, and she nodded.

"C-could you, um……when you have a chance, please pass along my thanks to Kalua-sama?"

"To Nee-san……?"

"Yes, for the, well, the gift she brought when she visited the other day……"

About two weeks ago, Kalua had picked out some fruit on her way bringing Krische home, presenting them to Bery as a get-well gift.

Starting from there, perhaps the conversation might branch out — so Bery reasoned.

But Elvena gazed at Bery, thought for a moment, then smiled.

"Of course. I'll pass it along."

"Y-yes……thank you……"

Contrary to Bery's intentions, silence returned.

Elvena sipped her tea and studied Bery, while Bery's gaze wandered left and right, searching for something.

For Bery, this was an excruciatingly awkward silence.

But it lasted only a moment.

What broke the air was the sound of Elvena's laughter, soft and overflowing.

"Hehe, I know it's rude to say this, but……"

"Um……yes?"

"You're a very charming person, Bery-sama. ……Forgive me. I was being a little mean."

Elvena laughed delightedly, and upon seeing Bery's blank expression, wiped a tear from the corner of her eye.

"You don't have to force yourself to make conversation on my account. Your thoughtfulness makes me very happy, truly."

Only then did Bery realize she was being teased, and her cheeks flushed.

Which only made Elvena more amused.

She gazed at Bery with a gentle smile, and Bery, eyes downcast and embarrassed, spoke.

"I-I'm sorry……this kind of conversation is a bit beyond me."

"No, no. If anything, I'm relieved……so even you have things you're not good at, Bery-sama."

"That is simply because you overestimate me, Elvena-sama. I'm mostly appearance — I have far more shortcomings than strengths."

"Hehe, if your appearance is mere appearance, then what should I do, falling so far short even of that?"

"Um, well……"

When Bery fumbled for a reply, Elvena's smile deepened and another laugh escaped.

Again she said, *Forgive me*, with a rueful grin.

"But truly, in that regard you and Krische-sama are very much alike. You're both, how should I say……too modest by half."

"Krische-sama aside, I am, well……"

"I suspect if I asked Krische-sama the same question, she'd say exactly what you just did."

"Ugh……"

Bery's embarrassed face was, in a word, age-appropriate — maiden-like.

Calmly observing this expression, so at odds with her usual composure, Elvena committed the sight to memory.

Master and servant both — a pair that endlessly provoked one's mischievous side.

"Forgive me. Watching the two of you together, I feel a certain……envy."

"Envy?"

"Yes. You both look so very happy."

The words made Bery blush even deeper.

It was no secret how she and Krische spent their days together.

Elvena witnessed it daily, and Bery had grown somewhat numb to it — but hearing it stated aloud brought a particular kind of embarrassment.

"This isn't teasing — I truly mean it. It's not about romantic feelings per se, but the relationship itself is something beautiful, and, well……"

Elvena herself grew bashful as she spoke, smiling awkwardly.

"I get a little embarrassed saying this, but a relationship where two people complete each other so perfectly……it's enviable, or, how should I put it. ……Simply that, in this vast world, to have found someone who fits so perfectly — it feels like something fated."

*I wouldn't make much of a bard*, Elvena said, looking abashed.

Even within the flow of the conversation, these were awkward words to say aloud.

Yet Bery fell into thought, and when she looked up, there was something uneasy in her expression.

"……Does it really look that way?"

"Wh—— do you mean to say you're not confident?"

The unexpected response bewildered Elvena.

Bery's gaze wavered, and she opened her mouth.

"No, that's not……of course, I love Krische-sama. More than anyone."

It was a declaration.

Having witnessed their daily life, Elvena had no reason to doubt it, but hearing it stated so plainly still carried a certain impact.

Her own face reddening from the topic she herself had raised, Elvena's eyes swam, and she recalled the time she had once asked Krische about Bery.

"And Krische-sama, too, loves someone like me……I do not doubt that feeling for a moment. I have sworn to devote my entire life to Krische-sama."

"Y-yes……"

They were an absurdly similar pair.

"But the real me is……a terribly pathetic person, and I wonder whether someone like me is truly worthy of being first in Krische-sama's heart……whether someday, she might become disillusioned……there's just a small part of me that worries……"

Elvena had heard nearly identical words from someone else, not long ago.

Frighteningly pure.

That was what made them so alike, through and through.

"W-well, you see, Bery-sama."

"……?"

"As it happens, I was just——"

Before Elvena could finish, a knock sounded at the door.

Bery waved Elvena back into her seat and stood to answer it.

"Welcome home, Krische-sama."

"I'm home, Bery. Elvena too……a break?"

"Yes. We were having a little chat."

Krische pressed her face close, leaning against Bery, and Bery embraced her.

Elvena marveled at what Bery could possibly have to feel uneasy about, then noticed the bag in Krische's hand and tilted her head.

"Krische-sama, what's in the bag?"

"Um……well, these are cookies Krische made as snacks over there. Leftover from the ones Krische made for Neigal and the others……Krische brought some for you two."

"Hehe, thank you. Elvena-sama, shall we have some together?"

Bery smiled, released Krische, and reached for her hand——

But before that, Krische took a small, bite-sized cookie from the bag, and without raising her face, still pressed close, lifted it to Bery's lips.

"……Bery."

Bery looked bewildered, but——

"Um, well, yes……"

Glancing sideways at Elvena with some embarrassment, she resignedly opened her mouth.

She ate the cookie, chewed, swallowed.

"Today's are very crispy cookies."

"……Are they good?"

"……? Yes, very——"

The instant Bery said it, Krische's body went rigid, and the bag slipped from her hand.

The sound of cookies shattering inside the bag echoed.

"……Krische-sama?"

Bery, too, froze at Krische's reaction.

Krische backed away, looking at Bery as though frightened.

Her beautiful face twisted as if in anguish, her purple eyes wet and trembling like the surface of water.

She shook her head again and again, staggering.

She simply stared at Bery.

"Krische-sama!"

——And then she turned and fled down the corridor.

Bery moved to chase after her, but——

"……Ah."

She stopped almost immediately, sank to her knees on the spot, and covered her face.

Her slender shoulders trembled and shook.

"Um……Bery-sama……?"

Not understanding what had happened.

Elvena approached in confusion and watched Bery, who did not respond to her voice.

And then, noticing the fallen bag of cookies on the floor, Elvena picked it up.

She took one of the broken cookies from inside, looked at it, and put it in her mouth.

"——"

She nearly choked and pressed a hand over her mouth.

The honeyed sweetness she had expected was almost entirely absent.

It was simply — just salty.

In that instant, she understood everything.

And she could only stand there, staring at the bowed figure of Bery.

# Chapter 162: The Celestial Maiden in Her Palm

"……You fool. Where exactly was any of that 'nothing serious at all'?"

"I'm sorry……"

Some time later, as the sun went down.

Selene returned to the estate with Kreschenta and Anne in tow.

She promptly heard the story from Elvena, then listened directly from Bery — sitting in a chair with her head bowed — and let out a deep sigh.

Anne, unable to bear what she had heard, left the room to search for Krische.

Kreschenta creased her brow, had Elvena pour her tea loaded with honey, and watched the two of them with an exasperated sigh, chin propped on one hand.

"Haa, Argan-sama, in a way you're an even bigger nuisance than ojii-sama. Right now, the one putting the kingdom at greatest risk isn't the three nations — it's you. Do you understand that?"

"……I'm sorry."

That was all Bery said in reply.

She did not raise her face. Her shoulders trembled quietly.

Seeing that, Kreschenta's eyes shifted uneasily.

"You shouldn't be apologizing, that's not what I'm trying to say——"

"Kreschenta, be quiet for a while or go to another room."

"……A-are you ordering me?"

"If you don't, I'll be genuinely angry."

Glared at sideways by Selene, Kreschenta looked displeased but fell silent.

She took a big gulp of tea, found it too hot, and set the cup down somewhat roughly.

*Honestly——* Selene looked away from Kreschenta and turned her gaze to Bery.

Sitting in the chair with her face lowered.

Her small frame looked even smaller, her thin shoulders even more delicate.

None of her usual composure was anywhere to be found.

But this was who Bery really was.

A small woman who was always forcing herself, merely wearing the mask of a guardian.

Selene circled behind her and wrapped her arms around her from behind.

The body that flinched at her touch — she extended her arms to enfold it, pressing her cheek to Bery's.

Golden hair slipped and fell, sliding down Bery's neck.

Watching it, Selene spoke quietly.

"Why are you always like this? I told you to lean on me once in a while, to let me go to some trouble. Am I still that unreliable?"

It had been the same when her mother died.

Selene had cried endlessly, and Bery was at her side the whole time, comforting her, carrying on as usual, arranging the funeral.

The consummate servant, always.

Even though Bery — who had spent the most time with her mother — must have been the saddest of all.

When Bery was with her mother, she would be just a little indulgent. Look just a little younger.

Long ago, she must have left all of that behind.

*'If you keep being stubborn and putting on airs like that, you'll end up like Bery — a girl who can't be honest and let herself be spoiled. Children should act like children and lean on their mother, honestly.'*

*'Nee-sama, I…… have no intention of interfering with the young lady's upbringing, but would you please stop using me as a bad example? It makes things very difficult……'*

*'Well, it's the truth, isn't it? Fufu, you're welcome to prove me wrong, you know?'*

*'……Absolutely not.'*

Remembering the two of them, Selene narrowed her eyes.

"I can't know how painful it is for someone who loves cooking as much as you do to lose her sense of taste. ……But I know the reason you hid it wasn't anything like that."

If it were simply painful, she probably would have spoken about it without fuss.

As if to say it wasn't painful at all — *I'm fine,* like that.

Smiling that troubled smile, same as always.

That was the kind of person she was.

The reason she hadn't done that must have been something else.

"You were afraid Krische would be disappointed in you, weren't you?"

At those words, Bery's shoulders jumped.

Selene sighed, as though exasperated.

"I can see right through you, Bery. Did you know? I've been watching you since the day I was born."

If the person she respected most was her mother, the person she admired most was surely Bery.

Talented enough to do anything, hardworking, and yet never arrogant, never presumptuous. She always handled everything effortlessly, as a matter of course.

She was the same as Krische.

Never once had Selene beaten her at anything, and problems that made Selene rack her brain, Bery dispatched without a thought.

Unlike her mother, who had her occasional lapses, Bery was the consummate superhuman.

Ask and she would answer anything; wish and she would make it come true.

She was a different breed of being. She must never worry about anything. And even if she did, it would be on a plane Selene could never understand — there was a time she had believed all of that, and it was only after Krische arrived that she learned otherwise.

After Krische came, it was as if things kept peeling away from Bery, one after another.

*'If you would…… continue to stay with me like this, always, from now on.'*

The desperately earnest tone of those words, heard on the road to the capital, was something she remembered well.

She appeared to have everything, yet at the same time appeared to have nothing.

*'— At Krische-sama's side. I realized my meaning lies there. And if so, then my name belongs to Krische-sama, I thought. ……That it exists for that purpose — that is what I wished.'*

In certain respects, she and Krische were very much alike.

Tormented by something for which there was no answer, as though drowning in it.

That was surely because they both possessed the kind of power that could obtain anything, if only they reached out their hand.

"If you ask me, you're two of a kind, Bery. You both go around thinking you can solve everything on your own, that there's nothing you can't do — and that's exactly why some trivial flaw becomes unforgivable, you fools."

"That's not——"

"Yes it is. A matched pair of hopeless egotists, the both of you."

Without even a small stumble, without ever knowing their own limits.

And precisely because of that, every tiny crack in themselves loomed large, unforgivable, frightening — cowards, both of them.

They should have been the most dexterous of anyone, yet they were the most clumsy.

"Does losing your sense of taste erase your worth? Does it make Krische disappointed in you, make her stop loving you — is that what you think?"

"That's——"

"You really are a fool. If that's your argument, then what does that make me — someone who can't cook, can't do housework, and even when I try my hardest at my own specialties, Krische calls me 'a so-so klutz'? What am I, exactly?"

Selene unleashed a torrent of words, then glared at Kreschenta.

"Don't you dare say anything unnecessary."

"I-I wasn't going to…… unlike you, onee-sama, I can read the room."

Kreschenta, who had been about to interject, hastily looked away.

Selene sighed in irritation and squeezed Bery tight.

"You're always so quick to lecture everyone else, and yet when it comes to yourself, how can you be so faint-hearted? You're the one who says Krische's flaws don't bother you, Kreschenta's flaws don't bother you, even my flaws don't bother you — yet your own flaws are the only ones you can't accept? No one else will ever acknowledge them, ever forgive them — is that it?"

"……!"

Kreschenta looked displeased, as though to say *I certainly don't have any flaws* — but Selene's glare silenced her.

Bery kept her face down and listened to Selene's words in silence.

"Are all those things you always say just lies?"

"They're not lies——"

"Then trust us a little more. I think your cooking is wonderful, but your worth to me isn't just that. No matter how many or how few flaws you have, you're my precious family, the person I look up to like an older sister…… that's what I believe. Can't you believe it?"

Bery shook her head faintly and gripped Selene's arm.

"Krische feels the same way, you fool. Do you really think she was disappointed in you and went off somewhere? When you were stabbed, she risked her life and tore herself apart to save you — she even challenged a dragon, that's how much you mean to her — and you think something like this would make her lose faith? ……If you honestly believe that, then — even with you — I really will slap your face, Bery."

Bery's hand clung to Selene's arm like that of a small child.

*Honestly——* Selene smiled, closed her eyes, and pressed her cheek close.

"Once you've calmed down a bit, let's go look for her together. If you say you won't go, I'll go instead and gently comfort Krische myself — I'll say all sorts of things about you, true and otherwise, and steal her away. Is that what you want?"

"……That's not fair."

"That's what you get for not being honest. Do you realize I'm a little angry here?"

Selene said this, released her arms, and moved in front of Bery.

She crouched down, and gently wiped the tears that had traced down Bery's lowered cheek.

"Is it so humiliating to be comforted by your unreliable ojou-sama?"

"……It might be."

"Oh, come on……"

"But, just a little…… ojou-sama, you remind me of nee-sama."

Selene pressed her forehead to Bery's with a satisfied look, peering into her tear-dampened light-brown eyes.

"Hmpf. And whose daughter do you think I am?"

"Nee-sama's and the Master's…… fufu, you really are just like nee-sama in so many ways. Like being terribly clumsy……"

Bery smiled and pulled back, wiping her eyes.

"And the way you're somehow always so bossy despite that — that's exactly like her too."

"The one who's always bossy toward her mistress is you, Bery. ……What a sharp-tongued servant."

Her rosy lips formed a soft, girlish smile.

"Well, it's not as though I'm being rude. ……All the ways you're alike — they're the things I loved most about nee-sama."

Her red hair swayed softly.

And she kissed Selene's forehead.

Her youthful face somehow looked grown-up — and what was there was the usual Bery.

Kreschenta, who had been watching with her chin rudely propped on her hand, sighed.

"Slightly better than I thought, but you're just as much of a crybaby as Nora. Aren't you embarrassed?"

"I apologize, Kreschenta-sama. You've had to see an unsightly display."

Only Krische and Kreschenta laughed with delight.

"I'll send an escort later, then. Let's save the deeper conversation for over dinner. Until then, please rest and recover from the journey."

When their eyes met, they exchanged quiet smiles, and Lila bowed to her as well.
