# Chapter 365: Alberan I

It should have been a battle they could never lose.

That tribe was a people whose only merit was their dexterity with their hands.

Flatterers who survived by fawning and prostrating themselves before other tribes — spineless weaklings without a shred of pride.

Enreine — the people of the shadows, just as the contemptuous name once given to them declared.

Before the great tribe Aldos, led by Bernaik, they should never have been permitted to even raise their heads.

And yet, their conduct over this past year had been beyond intolerable.

Whatever had possessed them to such arrogance, they had gone so far as to utter the madness of refusing to supply weapons without payment in gold, had cut down his envoy, and sent the head back lashed to a horse.

It was an insult sufficient to warrant the annihilation of their entire clan.

That such a thing had occurred under Bernaik's reign as great chieftain of Aldos was a stain that would endure for generations.

It had smeared mud across the radiant future Bernaik had envisioned — to make Aldos the greatest tribe in all of history, in his own generation.

His elegant golden hair glimmering in the dappled light filtering through the trees, Bernaik led his valiant warriors toward the lands of Enreine.

The battlefield was a somewhat open space within the forest.

The craven of Enreine who had foolishly shown themselves numbered a hundred at most — clutching ungainly, excessively long spears, standing in orderly lines as though huddled together.

*——They can't fight properly, so they mean to win by the length of their weapons.*

Seeing this, everyone laughed, and Bernaik was no different.

Deciding there was no need for him to step in, he yielded the glory to his warriors and remained on horseback in the rear.

As he watched the three hundred warriors surge forward at his command, something felt wrong.

It would immediately become a melee, and a massacre — that was what he had expected.

The reality was the exact opposite. The Enreine men bearing their long spears, the moment Bernaik's warriors closed to near range, lowered their spears in unison at the sound of a horn, presenting their points toward the attackers without a single gap.

The warriors who had charged in as a group could not kill their momentum, and impaled upon that wall of spears, unified without the slightest break in discipline, they screamed in agony.

In an instant, dozens of skilled fighters who had been at the very front were dead, and those behind them met the same fate.

When those who had faltered heard the horn sound again, the Enreine men began to march forward in orderly formation, spears still leveled.

Their stance thrust spears both forward and overhead.

Even warriors renowned as champions could not easily leap over that to close in.

"What is this……"

The warriors were slowly pushed back, and seeing this, Bernaik bellowed.

"Fall back and regroup! Spears that long can't be swung easily. Enter the forest on both flanks and attack from multiple——"

He tried to issue orders on the spot, but the sound of bowstrings cut him off.

Men who had appeared from the forests on both sides at some point were loosing volley after volley of arrows at the warriors whose attention had been fixed on the spears ahead.

The men, focused entirely on the spears to their front, failed to notice the arrows and were pierced through one after another, toppling as they fell.

Only then did he realize this had been a scheme laid out in advance.

Draw their attention to the spears ahead, then use bowmen hidden on the flanks to annihilate whatever warriors survived — they had understood that Enreine's weak men could never defeat Aldos warriors in a straight fight, and had deliberately baited their overconfidence to claim victory.

The provocation using the heads of the dead was almost certainly part of it.

A way of fighting utterly without warrior's pride — the height of cowardice.

"Cowards……! I'll go myself — the rest of you, follow me!"

He gnashed his teeth, leaped from his horse, and drew his sword, thrusting it skyward.

Many had died, but it was still enough.

If a hundred warriors had fallen, then he would simply cut down a hundred in return.

That was all there was to it.

He stepped forward to do just that — and an instinctive thought flashed through his mind.

Would this cowardly opponent truly want a melee from here?

*——They'll come to finish it.* The instant he thought that, he whirled and swung his blade at the presence behind him.

A large man who had closed in without a sound dodged the slash, evading it cleanly.

A single sweep that would have cleaved any one of a hundred men — and the man had avoided it effortlessly. That level of skill.

"……You're the chief of Enreine, Mitskronia."

Six shaku and three sun tall, with sharp features beneath black hair.

Gazing into those keen, raptor-like eyes, Bernaik smiled.

"I'll praise you for dodging that stroke. I thought you were some petty schemer who relied on cowardly tricks, but as rumored, you're quite the skilled fighter."

"Regrettably, I handed the position of chief to someone more fitting just the other day, Great Chieftain Bernaik."

"……Handed it over?"

"Indeed. ……To the Child of God we have long awaited."

Rather than from behind where Mitskronia had appeared, screams now rose from the warriors deployed to the front.

Bernaik furrowed his brow and looked — what had appeared was a beautiful girl with silver hair.

She was drenched in so much blood that the color of her hair had already turned to red.

"How do you do. You are Bernaik, I take it?"

She was a child.

Likely just past ten years of age.

Too small for even an ordinary longsword — her body, clad in nothing more than a single robe and a necklace, showed no sign of womanly development.

Amid all of that, only her large, violet eyes gleamed with a spine-chilling brilliance.

"I am Bazarishe. ……As Alberan, I demand the submission of you and Aldos."

"……Alberan, you say?"

"Yes. From today, I am your sovereign."

Child of God — one who descends from the realm of the gods to walk the earth, to lead and guide the people.

A legend, a nonsensical fairy tale.

And he had heard there were many in the past who had claimed such a title and spoken such madness.

"Prattle…… But since you invoke the name of Alberan, I will not show mercy even to a child."

"How unfortunate."

Her body sank in an instant, and Bernaik slashed reflexively.

But closing the distance in a blink, the girl slipped past Bernaik's sword as though threading a needle, arriving at his feet — her blade's point pressed against his throat.

"I am the stronger one, and you are the weaker. Will you acknowledge me now?"

"Kh……"

"If you wish to call for a formal duel, I will accept as many times as you like. ……But for today, shall we say it's my victory?"

He was speechless.

In swordsmanship, at the very least, there was no one in the surrounding world who could match him.

True to his epithet of 'the Radiant One,' blessed with prodigious talent, Bernaik boasted a sword arm that overwhelmed all others — and the girl had defeated him with ease.

"I despise pointless things. You will enter my service and assist in pacifying the surrounding lands. Follow me and you will be rewarded accordingly; resist, and you die. Those are the two choices I give you…… Personally, I prefer the former. The more capable people I have, the better. ……Isn't that right, Mitskronia?"

"Indeed. Losing Bernaik here would be a great waste, I feel. His presence will be a tremendous asset to us going forward."

"See? Mitskronia agrees."

An innocent smile as she withdrew her blade, tilting her head and gazing up with upturned eyes.

"What do you say? ……I'd like to get along with you too, you know."

Covered in blood, she wore a smile like a little girl.

Hearing the childlike ring of her words, gazing into that violet, his whole body trembled.

Bernaik yielded to the impulse, dropped to one knee, and bowed his head.

"……I swear my allegiance to the great Alberan upon my sword and my name. Please forgive my discourtesy, Bazarishe-sama."

"Ufufu, I'll forgive you."

The girl stepped closer and stroked Bernaik's head.

"……Let's get along from now on, shall we? Bernaik."

Arrogant, and utterly innocent — the voice of a child.

Bazarishe was, in appearance, truly just a child.

The mentality appropriate for someone of twelve years.

Including her bearing and manner, if one set aside her beautiful appearance, she seemed at first glance to be nothing more than an ordinary girl.

However, her mind was abnormal — one could only call it that — and she was blessed with every conceivable talent.

Enreine had always been home to skilled artisans, but the one who had caused their capabilities to advance by leaps and bounds over these past few years was Bazarishe, still a child at the time.

From architecture to furnace efficiency to smelting, everything on the inside — invisible from the outside — was different.

The gap in quality between the swords forged there and the ones given as tribute was staggering.

Enreine's swords had always been known for their quality, but in both durability and sharpness, they were now incomparable to what they had been before — forged from the same iron ore, yet one would not believe it.

Clashed against them, ordinary swords shattered as though they were made of bronze.

The young chief Mitskronia had noticed her extraordinary gifts when she was two, and while concealing her existence from outsiders, had involved her in every field and had her refine their internal workings.

Convinced that she was the Child of God, he had done so to break free from their exploited existence.

"Hard at work, I see, Bernaik."

When he was swinging his sword at the edge of the settlement, the one who appeared was Mitskronia.

"……Alone?"

"Bazarishe-sama is napping. She usually is at this hour…… Perhaps as the price for a mind that works incomparably faster than any human's, she has always slept twice as much as others."

"Telling me her weakness is foolish. What makes you think I won't slit her throat in her sleep?"

"You are the noblest of warriors. You would never do such a thing."

Bernaik snorted in displeasure and sheathed his sword.

"Stop with that manner of speech. Regardless of circumstances, I'm the one who submitted. You're the war leader here. Are you mocking me?"

"You were defeated by Bazarishe-sama, not by me. You are far more suited to the role of war leader than I am. Apart from Bazarishe-sama, you are the only person I know who accomplished the feat of slaying a roukai at the age of fifteen. I have respected you ever since I heard of it."

"You're the older one."

"Anyone can grow older. But those who accomplish great deeds are born different. When I see someone worthy, the matter of a few years' difference becomes trivial."

Bernaik was twenty-five.

Mitskronia was past thirty, but he was a man impossible to get a read on.

Not a small man, but lacking the resistance one expected from a great one.

"I don't understand your aim. What do you want?"

"That's quite direct."

"I don't trust anyone who claims to have no desires. That girl is probably just as she appears. She doesn't seem to be thinking much of anything in particular — but the one pulling the strings is you. What you're thinking determines my future as well."

When Bernaik fixed him with a glare, Mitskronia smiled wryly.

Then he set his sword on the ground before him and sat down.

"To one such as you, Bernaik, I shall answer upon my true name and my sword."

At this gesture, Bernaik also sheathed his own blade.

He set it down beside Mitskronia's and sat.

"I wish to build a nation here."

"……A nation?"

"Yes. What do you think of how things are? Of the sight of people, divided into countless tribes, consumed by nothing but territorial squabbles?"

Mitskronia spoke as though lamenting.

"Strength alone has value. We are beasts that merely walk on two legs. No matter how many tribes you unite with your strength going forward, you will eventually grow old and die. When a weak successor takes your place, Aldos too will splinter. ……Prospering and declining, splitting apart — we have repeated this cycle for thousands upon thousands of years. Do you not think we have made no progress?"

"It is inevitable. Nothing lasts forever. The same would be true of your so-called nation."

"Yet we can take one step forward."

*For example, technology,* he said, indicating his sword.

"The power of one person is not eternal. But the forging techniques born here will be carried on through the generations. What did you think of the recent battle?"

"A cowardly way of fighting, shameful for any warrior."

"Indeed, a cowardly way of fighting. Yet the men of this settlement, who could hardly be called warriors, cast aside their pride and defeated even the valiant warriors of Aldos."

When Bernaik glared at him with open displeasure, Mitskronia smiled wryly and shook his head.

"Of course, without Bazarishe-sama, it would still have been anyone's fight from that point. However, it is a fact that by abandoning reliance on individual valor and becoming one as a group, they gained strength. They proved that even the weakest of people, when united, can overcome warriors. ……More than anyone, I wanted you to witness that."

"……What?"

"That is precisely why I sought battle at that location. Bazarishe-sama is, as you saw, Alberan…… A simple duel would have settled things just as well."

It was unpleasant, but it was fact.

The girl had used her small frame to sink low — he had been caught off guard, true, but Bernaik's sword was not so forgiving that that alone would have let her slip beneath it.

Yet Bazarishe's blade had found his throat as though drawn there by gravity.

A sword that inverted cause and effect.

Just as whether an arrow hits or misses is decided the instant the archer draws, the moment she stepped in, Bazarishe's blade was already at Bernaik's throat.

Subduing without killing is far more difficult than killing.

Having been defeated while she held back, he had no grounds to complain.

"Just as one trains the body to wield a skillful blade, a group too can be trained as one to become a single strength. And this strength can be inherited through the generations without being lost. ……Not strength dependent on individual brawn, but value placed in inherited technique and wisdom, shared and carried forward. Leaving new footprints of humanity upon this land of beasts. ……That is what I mean by a nation."

"I see. A considerable ambition, but……"

He trailed off and looked up at the sky.

"The dragon destroys those who grow arrogant. Surely you know that."

Nation — it was a word from a distant age.

In the legends and myths of the far, far past — some humans, it was said, had left the forest and lived upon the open plains.

Gradually their numbers grew, and they who built the nation of Lusheran upon the vast flatlands ruled the land as though it were their own, but they caught the eye of the dragons that soared through the sky, drew their wrath, and in a single breath, every human living there was erased.

It would have been well if it were mere myth, but in truth, dragons existed.

He personally knew two elder sages who claimed to have had an audience with the dragon of Albyagel.

A mountainous body slumbering in a hollow of mana crystal cliffs.

Its heat altered the climate of the mountains to the north, so that not a flake of snow accumulated even in the depths of winter.

He had never seen the legendary sight of it soaring through the sky, or the roar said to reshape the very terrain, but neither was it so uncertain an existence that one could doubt it.

"That girl is indeed Alberan. But against a dragon, equal to the gods themselves, she is scarcely different from any ordinary person."

"It has been long since the great one entered its deep slumber. When things take shape, it would be wise to pay a respectful visit, I think, but…… The last time it flew the skies was hundreds of years ago, I've heard. It is unlikely to take an interest in us at this late stage."

Mitskronia looked up at the sky and squinted against the dazzling sun.

"Seen from the heavens above, we must look like insects crawling upon the earth. Tell me, Bernaik — do you grow angry at insects crawling on the ground?"

"……So long as they aren't an eyesore, you wouldn't bother with them? You are an optimistic man."

"One could also say that everyone else is too pessimistic. Perhaps it is inevitable after cowering beneath the canopy of the trees, living in fear of the sky for thousands, tens of thousands of years, but……"

He said this and smiled.

"Be that as it may — how about it? Living in fear is not your way, is it? You are the hero who faced roukai and jade tigers with nothing but your own body…… What befits the Radiant One, Bernaik, is not the shade of the trees but the light of the sun."

Gazing into Bernaik's eyes as he spoke.

"We have but one life, in the end. ……If I must die, I would rather die looking up at the sky with a grand ambition in my heart, laughed at as a fool, than trembling in fear and waiting for death…… That is simply what I believe."

"……I see. You are, as far as I know, the greatest fool I have ever met."

Bernaik laughed, and then he answered.

"Very well. I'll ride your silver tongue. This stain of defeat — at any rate, I had thought nothing short of the preposterous achievements you describe would ever wash it away."

And he drew his sword, pointing it at Mitskronia's neck.

"But you will stand at my side. If ever I judge you unworthy of that place, I will cut your head from your shoulders at once. ……Never forget that."

"……Upon my sword and my true name — Rokrut, I swear."

At those words, Bernaik furrowed his brow.

"Should I fall midway through, I ask that you bring Bazarishe-sama into the sunlight."

Mitskronia pressed a hand to his chest as he spoke, and Bernaik replied with exasperation.

"My true name is not something I give as lightly as yours."

"Yes. I am well aware."

Bernaik rose to his feet and spoke.

"Prove to me that every word you spoke was the unvarnished truth. If you prove yourself a man worthy of being this Bernaik's friend, then I shall answer your oath with one of my own."

Mitskronia smiled and nodded, then took his sword in hand and stood.

"I've had enough of practice swings. Spar with me, Mitskronia."

"Gladly, Bernaik."

* * *

Enreine — the meeting hall of Alberan.

At the head sat the silver-haired Bazarishe, and at her sides, Mitskronia and Bernaik.

Here, it seemed, the warriors often shared meals together in this manner.

Seated intermixed with the warriors Bernaik had brought from Aldos, it was meant to foster goodwill.

A sort of informal revelry had been the norm since his arrival here.

"Roughly one or two out of ten, I'd say. I truly cannot match you with the sword."

"Mm…… Bernaik, you're quite impressive. And Mitskronia is the strongest warrior here."

Bazarishe was nibbling on venison with her cheeks relaxed, speaking of the sparring match just moments ago.

"So long as it's not a total victory, there's nothing to boast about."

"I see, so you're a hard worker. You should keep at it. It makes me happy when you get stronger — it means less work for me."

Sipping from a cup of juice, Bazarishe smiled.

"But don't just focus on swordplay — from now on, you'll study tactics under Mitskronia. If it were only opponents a duel could settle, then it would be enough for me to step in…… What I need from you is the ability to handle the tedious ones who can't be dealt with that way."

Bazarishe said this as though it were self-evident, then tilted her head back dramatically and looked at the large map hanging behind her.

"Between Enreine and Aldos, our current fighting strength is less than a thousand. Including those who had submitted to Aldos, perhaps two thousand at most…… In the next year, I intend to gather ten times that."

When Bernaik furrowed his brow, Bazarishe laughed.

"Within ten years, I'd like to make it a hundred times that. Bernaik, what I need from you is to gather forces — to assemble ten thousand soldiers who will rally under the banner of Alberan. First, though — I will make the Garkha to the south submit to me."

Garkha was a great tribe of the south that surpassed even Aldos in military strength.

Defeating them had been something Bernaik himself had been considering.

"……However, there is one condition for you."

"A condition?"

"Yes, it's simple. ……You must not draw your sword."

At those words, the crease between his brows deepened further.

"Not through reckless valor but through intellect — I want you to subdue and overwhelm them…… What I desire is not a strong blade, but the arm that wields it."

*I already have a strong blade,* Bazarishe said, indicating herself.

"To command an army that exceeds ten thousand, I need limbs to move it. Mitskronia as my right arm, and Bernaik as my left…… Would you be willing to become one of Alberan's arms?"

Despite her childish frame, her gaze seemed to look up and yet look down upon him.

The jewel-like violet eyes were fixed upon Bernaik.

One who descends from the realm of the gods to walk the earth, to lead and guide the people — a foundling of the divine.

Those transcendent eyes declared that the legend was truth.

"……As you command, Alberan."

*——To build a nation of the Child of God upon this land.*

That was, indeed, not a bad proposition.

# Chapter 366: Alberan II

The vast training ground, cleared from the forest, was filled with men standing in formation.

*Mae e susume, tomare, migi wo muke* — voices rang out, and the men moved in accordance. (T/N: "Forward march, halt, face right" — drill commands given in deliberately stilted/formal phrasing in the original, reflecting the novelty of organized military drill in this era.)

"What a pointless exercise."

"Yet a necessary one."

Bernaik watched the scene with evident boredom, and Mitskronia offered his reply.

The warriors of Aldos had been voicing their discontent as well, but between the recent battle and the existence of Bazarishe, their complaints had not yet escalated to outright defiance.

"Aldos warriors are strong. In a ten-against-ten fight, they'd beat most opponents. But assembling a thousand — or ten thousand — of nothing but strong warriors is a difficult thing. One must use not just the peak of the mountain, but its slopes as well to muster such numbers."

"I understand the logic. But they won't be able to show their true ability like that."

The idea of standardization was not entirely incomprehensible.

It was true that there was strength in orderly formations and disciplined coordination.

Even women and children incapable of fighting, if a hundred of them thrust long spears forward in unison, would present a wall that even warriors would struggle to breach head-on.

Against opponents one could not match individually, the battle line compensated for skill.

As a method of warfare for the weak, it was rational.

But for those being forced to match the level of the weak, individual strength was sacrificed.

"Quite right…… In truth, I was hoping to have the Aldos warriors serve as the hammer."

"The hammer?"

"If I were to use the present situation as an analogy, I would be the anvil."

Mitskronia turned to face Bernaik, and—

"Bazarishe-sama would be the hammer."

"Kh……!"

At the same moment as those words, someone leaped onto his back without a trace of presence — Bazarishe.

Her silver hair swaying, the girl perched atop his shoulder with a delighted smile.

"Hmhm, are you studying hard?"

"We are currently working through the fundamentals."

When Bernaik grimaced, Mitskronia laughed.

"Drawing the enemy's eye with a battle line standing in orderly formation, then flanking them with mobile forces — hammers — that strike from behind. In ancient times, this was called the anvil tactic. The battle line serves as the anvil, and the mobile cavalry as the hammer."

"You lost, Bernaik. Be my horse."

Bazarishe was a childish child.

He sighed wearily but let her have her way.

He ignored Bazarishe, who had vaulted onto his back.

"Then why are you making my warriors do this pointless exercise?"

"To help them understand the strengths and weaknesses of the battle line. Why is the battle line strong? How should one break it? ……Being inside it lets them grasp much of this through instinct."

"If everyone were idiots it would be one thing, but these are tactics that once existed…… Anyone with a bit of sense will learn them and copy them soon enough. When that happens, you'll need to study to be prepared — that's what Mitskronia said."

Hmhm, Bazarishe asked.

"What determines victory or defeat — superiority or inferiority — in group combat?"

"The side whose individuals kill more wins."

"A correct interpretation. With equal forces, the side where each individual can kill two is stronger. So the question is: how do you make one person kill two? ……Mitskronia."

*Sir,* Mitskronia crouched and used the handle of a knife to draw lines on the ground.

Rectangles representing battle lines, and arrows showing direction.

"Say a hundred men in five ranks, carrying long spears, and warriors like the Aldos ones from before come charging in — do you understand what's happening at the front?"

"……?"

"Aldos warriors rushing in pell-mell…… We have twenty men abreast in the front, and their vanguard is at most ten or so. But our side has the spears of the front three ranks as well — effectively sixty against their ten."

Small stones were placed in front of the rectangles.

"Because our weapons are longer, our attacks reach them first. If one man tries to break through the spear wall, he must dodge and cut down six spears. There aren't many people who can break through at six-to-one odds."

*This is a simplified version, of course,* Bazarishe said.

"The important things are always seizing the initiative and creating situations of many against few. A person who could dodge one or two spears cannot dodge five or six. If we can seize the initiative with our weapon reach, we take no casualties while only the enemy's numbers decrease — making the situation ever more favorable."

In fact, in the recent battle, Enreine's casualties had been zero.

There had been only a handful of wounded, he'd been told.

"The flanking archers work the same way. Attacking one-sidedly from a position where the enemy's attacks cannot reach. In the recent battle, Aldos warriors could not close to sword range even once — they only received attacks. ……Unable to decide whether to break the frontal spears or hunt the flanking archers, each acted independently and scattered, and were picked off in many-against-one engagements, and then the finishing blow from behind."

*A thoroughly satisfying complete victory,* Bazarishe said with a smile.

He had no argument on that point.

"Well, I was there, after all. There wasn't a chance in ten thousand that you could have beaten me. But assuming I weren't present — from that point, there were still openings for you to win. What would they have been?"

"……Break through the archers on one flank and regroup."

"That's right. If everyone had acted as one under your command and broken through the archers on one side, you could have regrouped. But even then, your movements would have been scattered…… You would have taken heavy losses and had to retreat to the settlement to reassemble your warriors."

Aldos warriors were strong.

But just as Bazarishe said, looking at it objectively, that was likely how it would have played out.

Turn toward one side, and the other shoots you in the back.

"Combat by numbers is arithmetic. It is not individual killing power but collective killing power that determines the victor. Create as many local numerical advantages as possible, and kill the enemy without giving them the chance to do anything. Train so you can move your forces freely to achieve this. Of course, there are people like you and Mitskronia who can overturn a modest advantage, but those can be dealt with individually."

Giggling in a thoroughly pleased manner, Bazarishe laughed in his ear.

"The more the numbers grow, the more individual strength is diluted. By the time we've assembled an army of ten thousand, the power of a single person becomes a small thing. ……If you wish to serve as my arm and lead ten thousand soldiers, you must learn the art of wielding them like your own hands and feet."

"……What comes after gathering an army of more than ten thousand?"

"We will destroy the Kreisharana to the north."

He furrowed his brow.

For centuries, they had been the great tribe dominating the entire northern region.

A collective of warriors who rode griffins soaring through the sky, ruling dozens of surrounding tribes from on high.

Their dominion did not extend to this area, but there was no one who did not know their name.

It had been something Bernaik himself had been considering as a goal — decades in the future.

But Bazarishe was almost certainly naming it as a goal for the next few years.

"The griffins number at most around a thousand, and the Kreisharana themselves don't even have five thousand fighters. It will take some effort and labor, but it's not that difficult."

"You have…… a plan?"

"Several, yes. But first, I'd like you to prove that you are worthy of being my arm…… Something along those lines."

With a light hop, she finally jumped down, and looked up at the sky as if it were dazzling.

"A place free from worry and fear, liberated from everything cramped and tiresome — that's what I'll build here. Where I can sleep as much as I want every day, eat as much as I want, play as much as I want……"

Then she drew the sword from Bernaik's hip, and with a flourish of her hem—

"……People, trees, griffins — and if need be, even dragons."

The small girl raised the keen steel toward the sun.

"I will cut through everything that stands in the way, and lead the people of the shadows into the sunlight."

——She thrust that point toward the high heavens.

Her childlike voice rang out, insolent beyond measure.

One could call it the pinnacle of ignorant immaturity, and yet no one could dismiss it as mere childishness — Bazarishe's figure as she stood there was far too beautiful, far too commanding.

She spun back around and turned her jewel-like eyes toward him with a smile.

"Next month, we march on Garkha. ……You will command. Mitskronia will observe. There, prove yourself worthy of being my arm."

A gaze that seemed to look up, and yet looked down.

Violet filled with absolute conviction and confidence.

She was, without question, the genuine Alberan.

* * *

"——Aldos has attacked, you say?"

At the words of the messenger, Godeus — the young chieftain of Garkha, who had been lying on a sofa receiving a lecture from a priest — sat up.

His coarse black hair was grown long and gathered at the back, his beard left to grow as it would.

On both cheeks, symmetrical geometric tattoos marked him as chieftain.

Just past twenty — it had been only about half a year since his predecessor, his father, had died suddenly in battle with a magical beast, and the seat of great chieftain had passed to him.

A note of delight was mixed into his words.

"How many?"

"The exact number is uncertain…… but it seems to be around a thousand."

"Ha, quite the procession. Day after day I've been stuck listening to the old man's tedious lessons — things are about to get interesting."

Rasel, the elderly priest with a long white beard braided into three plaits, frowned at those words from beneath his robe.

"These are the records and laws you must learn as great chieftain."

"Your lot can memorize that stuff. A great chieftain's job is to lead warriors on the battlefield. Warriors want a great chieftain who takes a hundred heads, not one who just remembers a hundred words."

"If I may retort — a great chieftain who takes a hundred heads and knows a hundred words would earn even greater respect. Your predecessor was precisely that."

He shook his head in exasperation.

Leading scores of wolves, a giant wolf that exceeded a man's height — a roukai. The prodigy who had slain one single-handedly.

Because the previous great chieftain viewed the brilliance of Bernaik — the son of the great chieftain before him — as a threat, it was an order that amounted to a legal execution: refuse, and be branded a coward and lose all honor.

What set Bernaik apart from the ordinary began here.

He refused all offers to accompany him, challenged the roukai's pack alone, fought for a full day and night, and returned having accomplished it.

Illuminated by the dazzling light of dawn, he strode back bearing the great fang of the roukai, his gait confident and proud.

And so his epithet was the Radiant One.

Bernaik had earned the absolute respect of the warriors within Aldos.

Thereafter, at the young age of twenty-two, he had challenged the former chieftain to a duel, won decisively, and seized the seat of great chieftain, and many tribes were on sharp guard against the Aldos he now led.

Double the numbers, true, but against Bernaik, that was somewhat less than reassuring.

The thousand he had brought were undoubtedly Aldos's finest — renowned as formidable fighters — and Godeus, so recently come to power, was far too young.

Rasel had judged that in time he would become a great chieftain no less capable than his predecessor, but there were warriors who looked down on Godeus, who had yet to prove himself with any great achievement.

Godeus craved a feat that would earn him recognition from those around him.

"……Godeus-sama, what do you think is necessary to slay a roukai single-handedly?"

"Hah? Worried old man. Even I'm not fool enough to underestimate Bernaik — I'm not about to call for some duel."

"It is certain that he is a swordsman of extraordinary skill…… but that alone is not enough."

Rasel looked at Godeus with a serious expression.

"To slay a roukai that commands a pack, alone — one needs not only strength but great cunning and caution as well. "You're saying the other side has some kind of plan?"

"That I cannot say. But it would be wise to give careful thought to the fact that they have come to win. That Aldos has marched out means they have anticipated that we will gather more than double their numbers and still judged that they can win."

Had Bernaik been nothing more than a brute, the roukai would have devoured him.

The duel too had been the same.

Seven years after slaying the roukai, he had carefully consolidated his position before challenging the former great chieftain, seizing the seat in a manner that left no one room to object.

Not merely strong, but a man with the composure to know when and how to use his power.

He must never be underestimated.

"……I had expected it would be a few more years, but perhaps he judged this was a chance not to be missed — so soon after the transition."

"If, on the contrary, he's underestimating me, so much the better. But it seems the rumors were just rumors after all."

That a vassal tribe had shown defiance toward Aldos, and Bernaik himself had gone to discipline them only to suffer a humiliating defeat.

There were plenty of stories of things falling apart because an incompetent became great chieftain.

Still, this was Bernaik's Aldos.

Marching against another power while leaving internal troubles unresolved was something only the most colossal of fools would do.

"In any case, it's not a bad situation for me either. If I beat that Bernaik, they won't be able to touch us for the next ten years. I'll be recognized by the warriors, and can expand Garkha's territory with confidence."

"Worrying is an old man's nature. My ears are going to get calluses — honestly."

Godeus stood with an exasperated air and spoke.

"I'm leaving the settlement in your hands for a while, old man."

"Yes, I wish you good fortune in battle…… I pray for your safe return."

As Rasel bowed his head, Godeus departed the room.

This was a young man he had looked after since infancy, like a grandson — rough around the edges, his mouth included, and with many flaws, but gifted and with a bright future ahead.

He prayed to the god of valor that his misgivings would not manifest in the worst possible way.

# Chapter 367: Alberan III

The battlefield was the forest of Arendol.

This area was dominated by algana trees that grew straight and branched high.

Algana produced quality timber, and ease of felling was one further advantage.

The algana's shallow, spreading root system prevented dense underbrush from growing around it — only ground cover carpeted the earth — making transportation and various other tasks convenient.

Spears, shields, bows, arrows, buildings — wood was used for everything.

A forest with quality algana was extremely valuable, and it was not uncommon for such forests to be fought over in wars.

Visibility was not too poor, but a forest was still a forest.

Scattered thickets of undergrowth obstructed the distant view, and clear sightlines could not be taken for granted.

"If they've been pushing the pace, they could appear any time now. Send word to the rear to be on guard — the last thing I want is to be bitten in the flank."

"Understood."

At the words of Godeus, clad in leather armor with a great shield strapped to his back, one of the men beside him relayed the order.

Godeus and his forces were traveling one of the paths cut through the forest — a road cleared to allow the passage of wagons.

The terrain was generally firm, but with mild undulations like small hills.

To advance the main body, this road was the only real option.

Garkha's warriors wore almost no armor, instead wielding large round shields.

The timber was algana, reinforced with iron at the rim and at the central boss cut for the fist — sturdy constructions, each painted with a colorful symbol.

Among their people, the shield was regarded as something almost more sacred than the sword, and being granted a shield was the proof of a warrior.

There was a rule that a shield engraved with one's own symbol must never be released on the battlefield — a man might be killed for the offense of stepping over another's shield.

Consequently, their skill with shields was universally high, and this was one of the reasons Garkha ruled so much territory.

Fundamentally, they did not form ranks but charged as a mass, much like most tribes — however, they coordinated with those around them on the fly, constructing improvised walls of shields.

Even when surrounded by strangers, they assessed the situation instantly, locked shields into a defensive formation, and calmly worked toward a solution — this flexibility was their weapon.

Many a tribe that had mocked their disciplined wall as the spectacle of comical turtles found itself overwhelmed by the implacable advance, then cut down the moment cracks appeared.

Battle was all about flow — especially in forest combat, where situational awareness was difficult.

Victory and defeat were often decided by the mood of the moment, and the impenetrable wall of shields was, for most warrior rabble, an invincible barrier against which they had no recourse.

When one man flinched, those who saw him flinched as well, and no matter how many formidable warriors numbered among them, once the tide had turned, the mood could not be reversed.

And their other weapon was the throwing axe.

Most Garkha warriors, in lieu of bows, carried two throwing axes on their belts — the first used at the start of battle, the second once the enemy line broke.

Kill the enemy's momentum with the first. If that was enough to crush them, well and good.

If not, weather the storm behind the shield wall, apply pressure, then shatter them completely with the second axe to claim victory.

This was the basic tactic known as 'Garkha's Two Throws.'

It was more a simple set of agreements than a proper tactic, but against the uncoordinated hordes that most opponents fielded, it gave them a decisive edge.

However, nothing was without flaws.

Their equipment was, for this era, heavy — and they were slower than lighter opponents.

Against lightly equipped enemies carrying simple armor and a single sword or bow, they lost in flexibility, and were particularly vulnerable in chance engagements.

Though the warriors carried minimum provisions, their equipment limited their capacity, and for major battles they needed wagons, restricting their movement.

When Garkha suffered major defeats, it was invariably because these weaknesses had been exploited.

Aldos was especially dangerous.

In stark contrast to Garkha, their culture held the longsword as deeply sacred.

Their ferocity — swinging two-handed swords with no fear of death — was well known, and if they were allowed to close to a melee, the battle could turn in an instant.

Berserkers who blocked thrown axes head-on with their swords.

Being drawn into a melee was the one thing that had to be avoided at all costs.

Every great tribe that ruled multiple others had its strengths.

Differences born of ancestral cultures and faiths — the tribes that had continually proven the value of theirs subjugated their neighbors and came to be called great tribes.

What had been mustered was just over two thousand.

Not a bad number, but no reason for complacency.

Fear was an invisible monster.

The instant they believed they were losing, cowardice would spread like a contagion.

And the moment that happened, no matter how great their numbers, the group would disintegrate.

Garkha prided itself on being the rock, but Aldos was a thunderstorm.

The moment they ceased to be rock, even for an instant, they would be overwhelmed.

"Great Chieftain — an Aldos force sighted ten ri ahead."

"Ha! Coming at Garkha head-on, are they? Halt the advance! Transition from column to battle formation!"

He roared at the messenger's words, donned his helmet, and moved the shield from his back to his left arm.

Two throwing axes hung at his right hip, a five-shaku short spear rode his back, and a large axe sat at his left hip.

Standard equipment for a warrior of Garkha's warrior class.

Atop his iron helm with its cheekguards rose a spike and a horsetail crest.

A well-worn piece — the helm passed down among Garkha's great chieftains.

His leather armor protected his trunk and limbs.

Shield and arrow-ward — quite a few men, trusting in their tattoo charms, fought bare-chested, but Godeus was a coolheaded realist.

It somewhat restricted movement and was inconvenient, but he understood the utility of armor.

He found it tiresome, the trend of glorifying as heroes those who faced the enemy with only a shield and bare skin.

A single arrow that happened to find a gap in the shields, a single stone — any of these could prove fatal.

In his own tenure he wanted to at minimum equip them with gauntlets and greaves, and ideally helmets.

If all the warriors donned full armor over their shields, nothing could be more reassuring.

The enemy would need to penetrate both sturdy shield and armor to inflict a lethal wound.

Warriors marching forward behind shields, fearless of falling arrows, would crush enemy formations like a rolling boulder.

If they were going to bear the weaknesses of heavy equipment, they might as well lean into it entirely — that was his philosophy. But the warriors lived in the space between reality and fantasy.

A man who fought without a single piece of protective gear, who slew many and returned alive, was indeed a hero.

The more needless risk one took, the higher the estimation as a warrior.

Their ideals of what a warrior should be — held for reasons of culture and belief — could not be so easily stripped away simply because rationality said otherwise.

What a vexing thing, he thought, eyeing the nearly half of his men who were bare-chested with tattoos, and drew a throwing axe from his hip, spinning it in a casual warm-up toss.

In fine form — if he could crush Aldos decisively, the warriors, many of whom still looked down on him, would recognize him as great chieftain.

That the enemy had come at them head-on, whatever their reasoning, was a stroke of fortune.

"Pass the word: the first throw must be in unison. From first to last, we never yield the flow — we crush them."

* * *

On the opposing side — the Aldos-Enreine force — a battle line had been formed.

"Almas, Roddia. As I told you, the center is yours."

"Understood! Heh, thank you, Great Chieftain!"

"But no reckless moves. You will match your pace with both flanks without fail."

At those words, Almas and Roddia — vassal chiefs under Aldos — grimaced.

"We've told you time and again — if we have to match the pace of those Enreine cowards, we'll lose a battle we should win."

"……This is a battle to experience Alberan's way of fighting. I've placed you at the crucial center, so stop complaining and obey. Your role is the shield — the sword lies elsewhere."

The two displayed their discontent but answered by slapping the swords at their hips.

Then they turned their backs on Bernaik and headed toward the warriors assigned to them, and sighed as they walked.

"Seems even the Radiant One lost his fangs after that great defeat."

"Lamentable. I thought the age of Aldos was upon us."

The great defeat at the hands of Enreine.

These two had not been present.

From what they'd heard, it seemed Bernaik had fallen into a clever trap and been wiped out — and then had acknowledged a child called Bazarishe as Alberan and sworn fealty.

They had doubted their ears when told, and doubted their eyes when they saw for themselves.

Bernaik, who had never bent the knee to anyone since childhood, kneeling before a slip of a girl like Bazarishe.

Perhaps he secretly fancied young girls — such jokes might pass, but if this was going to continue, it would be no laughing matter.

They had been lectured at painful length about the new way of fighting.

But however they listened, it was nothing more than clever scheming and underhanded tactics.

It was not the battle of warriors, competing with martial honor.

At the very least, it was not the figure they had wanted in their great chieftain of Aldos.

No one in Aldos disrespected Bernaik.

Extraordinary gifts displayed since childhood, the solo slaying of a roukai.

And at the duel with the predecessor — witnessed by every chief present — he had overwhelmed his opponent utterly, not allowing even a single blow to land.

Aldos valued the sword and martial valor above all.

At over forty, these men could well call Bernaik a youngster, yet as warriors of Aldos they had recognized him, and they had genuinely looked forward to his leadership.

Head trampled upon by a girl like Bazarishe, forced to impose the same upon Almas and the others — all they did day after day was practice walking in a line.

Bernaik had been humiliated, lost his temper, and suffered a crushing defeat.

Fine.

But that was merely a result.

One wins sometimes and loses sometimes — that was the nature of battle.

Challenge again, seize the victory — that was how warriors did things.

Aldos warriors did not use the shields favored by Garkha's fighters; instead, leather armor and helms protected their heads, torsos, and limbs.

Arms and thighs were left unarmored to preserve freedom of movement — their first priority was the sword.

A single sword was both the weapon that cut the enemy and the shield that guarded the self.

Just as a Garkha warrior was granted a shield upon becoming a warrior, an Aldos warrior was given a single sword.

Branches and foliage blocked lines of fire, and situations like the recent coordinated volley were not especially common.

Close-quarters fighting, chance encounters — these were the typical beginnings, and arrow attacks were mostly sporadic.

Rather, praising the skill of the archer who struck the blow — that was the warrior's way.

Their seemingly reckless style of individual charges was not pure heedless bravery either; it was the result of dispersing to avoid arrows and projectiles — prioritizing mobility.

In most cases, this had served them well and struck terror into countless tribes.

Their skill with the sword, honed through total devotion, was without equal.

If even one or two managed to cut into an enemy group, the foe would be shocked by the disparity in skill, fall into disorder, and once a melee began, the rout became one-sided.

And precisely because individual prowess was emphasized to such an extreme, a handful of transcendent warriors could dominate the entire field.

Like Bernaik — a warrior beyond human limits who cut down even flying arrows and bisected multiple men in an instant — a single person could seize control of the battle itself.

In this era, where a hundred or two hundred constituted a skirmish and a thousand on either side was called a great battle, an approach weighted toward individual strength was by no means wrong.

The only thing that could stop a warrior who cut down a hundred was another warrior — one of the rare few.

The idea that military strength meant pursuing individual excellence was correct in a certain sense, and the resentment Almas and Roddia bore toward Bernaik was perfectly reasonable.

"Rejoice. We're right in the center of the front — at the very tip of the spear."

At Almas's words, the warriors all showed delight.

To cut down the enemy on the field of battle.

Every one of them believed without question that they had been born for this.

"But the great chieftain seems a bit gun-shy after the recent defeat. He says not to charge in as usual, but to match the Enreine cowards' pace."

At those words, the warriors exchanged glances.

Like the two chiefs before them, many had grown thoroughly sick of the recent 'training.'

"The Radiant One is still young — even the sun can be hidden by clouds. But when clouds veil the sun, it is the warriors of Aldos who blow them away."

"The defeat happened because we weren't there. First — wait for the signal."

"Let us be the great wind that sweeps away the clouds, and clear the skies for our great chieftain!"

In answer, the warriors drew their swords and thrust them high like a battle cry returned.

"A trying role. Aldos's warriors are all valiant…… I knew there was considerable resistance, but."

"That's very detached of you, Mitskronia. Don't tell me you're acting humble just so you can dump the trouble on me."

He was a man who gave way when pushed, offered no resistance, and yet clung like a vine.

A collective of warriors who had honed themselves to the utmost without resorting to tricks, whose skill was their only weapon.

"But it is only one logic. One completed form. In the end, it is like rock-paper-scissors…… Everything has advantages and disadvantages; it is a matter of matchups. I wish to make Aldos into Bazarishe-sama's hand and foot — without changing what they are."

"One could say Bazarishe-sama does not understand why Aldos is strong. She views warriors as numbers, and sometimes sees a warrior with a strength of one as simply — one."

Moving groups like one's own hands and feet.

A system that eliminated individual initiative treated people as tools.

Bazarishe looked at human beings the way one might regard chess pieces.

"Indeed. It is on precisely that one point that I feel uneasy about Bazarishe-sama. The warriors of Aldos are not like the men of Enreine. Aldos warriors are strong because of their environment, their beliefs — everything cultivated over generations."

"For a little while longer."

At that answer, Bernaik sighed.

"Fine. I decided to go along with you. I still can't see the full picture you envision, but once we reach ten thousand, it'll become clear — is that right?"

"Without a doubt."

"Then what must be done, must be done."

A roar like a war cry echoed from where Almas and Roddia had gone, and he narrowed his eyes.

"……A trying role indeed."

"To die or to live is their choice…… I conveyed my demands. If they fail to obey, that is the end of it."

Bazarishe was innocent as a child.

At the root of things, she saw herself and everyone else as different creatures, tending to them the way one might tend to one's livestock.

Or perhaps more like tending to a flower bed.

*'I'd like you to cull the Almas and Roddia group early. Preferably in the next battle — dispose of them however you see fit.'*

Without malice, smiling — she was Alberan.

She was decidedly not a god — she was, as the word said, a child of the gods.

# Chapter 368: Alberan IV

The enemy force was to the right.

To the left, where Garkha was advancing, rose a small hill — the enemy had likely avoided the uneven terrain.

At some distance from the road — the area was notably free of undergrowth, well suited for battle.

They wheeled to face that direction.

Not lined up with the precision of a proper formation, but they had spread out and extended both wings.

Proper theory-based tactics did not yet exist, but the absence of theory did not mean the absence of tactics — these people were by no means fools.

They understood what formations were advantageous and which were not.

Extend your wings wider than the enemy's, and you could surround and fight the warriors at their flanks.

A principle anyone could grasp.

Being surrounded was worse than surrounding — anyone could see that.

The fundamentals of tactics were straightforward.

A human can only fight the opponent in front of them, so if someone else hits them from the side or behind, you win — even a child could understand such logic.

Precisely because it was logic anyone could understand, it was inevitable that everyone tried to prevent it.

Being flanked or having your back taken in battle was the mark of a fool, and a skilled warrior always maintained awareness of the whole battlefield, maneuvering to avoid exposing flanks and rear.

But their thinking stopped there.

Gather enough numbers to avoid disadvantage, and once the fighting begins, the rest is up to each individual.

In the chaos of a melee, grasping everything accurately was impossible, and even if one could issue accurate orders, they would only reach the men within ten ken.

Amid the screams and war cries — the battlefield music played at the border of life and death — there was no means of making one's voice heard accurately across all the warriors.

Even if it could be heard, whether they understood the intent was another matter, and even if the intent was perfectly understood, there was no guarantee they would follow orders.

The man receiving orders also had his own priority — killing the enemy before him, not being killed himself.

Unreasonable orders would be ignored, and if obeying them meant death, they would be defied.

A chieftain did not have the right to order warriors to die.

He could order them to fight, to not flee — but how each warrior used his single life in that fight was the warrior's domain.

It was left to each individual.

Everyone valued their own life.

That they risked that life on the battlefield was entirely for the sake of honor.

Where one chose to die was a warrior's decision, and any chieftain who robbed them of that right and forced them into a dog's death would find his head on a different set of shoulders by the next morning.

They were by no means fools — rather, they were simply, genuinely human.

The military structures that would become standard in later eras were mechanisms of brainwashing, transforming people into organizational components by constraining individual free will.

Because of that, commanders could give orders at will and move armies freely — but the notion that one had the right to freely use and control something as irreplaceable as a person's life was supremely arrogant and cold-blooded.

Even if someone conceived such a notion, the question of whether it would be readily accepted was a firm no, and so there was no point in devising plans on paper.

Mustering numbers, strengthening individuals, improving coordination — that was their best.

In the sense of letting people fight as people, their current state was by no means wrong.

* * *

"……Incredible."

The word slipped from Godeus inside the shield wall.

What filled the air was the sound of countless arrows striking shields.

Against the advancing Garkha forces, Bernaik had adopted a strange formation.

Large plank shields were set up at intervals across the front line, warriors standing in formation behind them — and from the gaps between, hunters were loosing volley after volley of arrows.

Caught off guard by the fierce arrow storm right at the opening, they had taken some casualties before the men could cluster together and form their shield wall — but the dominant impression was one of surprise.

This was not the Aldos way of fighting — sword raised, charging headlong.

The large stationary shields were presumably a precaution against their throwing axes.

Since no man held them, they could be made thick enough to stop an axe.

Their opening move had been completely neutralized.

The throwing axe was not merely a weapon.

At the start of battle, when everyone was tense, it served a role in loosening stiff bodies and minds — Godeus considered that its most important function.

None of the warriors wanted to be seen as cowards.

Many claimed that killing enemies and being drenched in their blood from head to toe was a warrior's joy — but in truth, there was always the fear that one might die.

Brave words, loudly proclaimed, served to cut off their own escape, telling themselves that fighting like warriors and surviving was their only option.

Once the killing began, excitement would drive the fear away, but in the moments before battle, fear swelled the most.

The roaring war cries also served to crush the clinging dread.

The throwing axe was another such release.

A volley that caused even minor disruption put them at an advantage.

If one's own axe happened to fell a foe, the surge of *got the first one* was exhilarating, and even a miss that made the enemy flinch let a man tell himself the tide was in his favor.

A hundred men throwing might only kill a handful.

If the enemy took cover, sometimes as few as ten at the front went down.

But at that most tense moment — the charge — seeing brave enemies in the front rank fall to death gave the heart a tremendous respite.

Fear flipped to elation, and once the killing frenzy took hold, all was well.

Just before blades crossed, they could seize a great psychological advantage.

Battle — group battle especially — was not a contest of arms.

It was not simply a matter of which side was stronger, but a contest of the heart, a battle against fear.

The side that imposed fear won; the side that flinched lost.

Just as a single person wielding a sword could dominate a field through terror, what must be seized was not situational advantage but psychological advantage.

The side that made the enemy believe *we're losing* — was the side that won.

# Chapter 369: Alberan V

By contrast, the weapon the Garkha warriors carried was the axe.

Wave-push — this was Garkha's guaranteed pattern for victory.

For that man, the heavy sword and the armor — all of it was part of his body.

They were roughly the same age, both young enough to be called as much. And yet, at the most fundamental level, they were nothing alike.

True to his epithet, The Radiant, Bernaik was a warrior favored by Koreis from birth.

A warrior a cut above the rest — one could tell at a single glance. One life to take one, and be taken in turn. Both being human, that was the norm.

But humans were not equal. There were those whose single life could kill four, five, ten, twenty — and Godevus had that pride in himself.

But above the high there was always higher, and there existed those who could cut down ten or twenty such warriors with ease. The man before him was, without question, one of them.

That single stroke would undoubtedly cleave through the sturdy shield and sever the arm behind it. Catching the blade in the shield, twisting to wrench away the weapon — that was basic technique, but Godevus did not believe he could react to this man's true killing stroke.

This great shield and his left arm existed for one purpose: to steal just that fraction of time — the span of a single stroke from this man. Let the body hold, and in that instant, slam the right arm's axe home.

Whether it would land, he could not say.

But that was fine. If it landed, he would laugh; if it was dodged, he would laugh and say *Well done*.

*— Simply execute, without guile, what you have decided for yourself.* In the end, that was all a warrior needed.

And yet —

"……Are you mocking me, Bernaik?"

Bernaik continued his unhurried approach. His sword still hung at his hip — six ken, five ken — unmistakably entering Godevus's striking range.

"I said exactly what I meant, Godevus. …… I already told you — you are a fine warrior."

"Kh……!"

And the distance was already four ken.

He discarded the luxury of thought and surged forward. Still leading with his left side, posture low, he thrust the great shield diagonally upward as he closed. A draw-cut alone would not cleave this great shield in two.

And the great shield was also a weapon. A direct hit could shatter bone and take a life with ease.

As expected, there was no impact, and no figure to left or right. For an instant, a shadow blocked the sun.

Godevus spun his body on the spot. Joints screaming in protest, he hurled a right-handed axe stroke behind him.

Bernaik had vaulted over the great shield on his back, landing with breathtaking grace, and drove his gauntleted fist into the haft — at the base of the axe-head — timing it with his forward step.

The axe stopped dead. The impact reverberated through Godevus's grip as though he had struck a boulder, and in the instant before his body was entangled along with the axe, he released it and spun. The great shield, held horizontal, swung at the man's neck — but Bernaik caught up the axe with a flick of his wrist and leapt. He landed on the great shield, pressing Godevus flat to the earth, and leveled the axe's edge at his throat.

"……It is too soon for you to go to the halls. You will come with me."

His breath was not even slightly quickened. A voice of singular allure — cool, yet clinging, heavy.

Then he thrust the axe into the ground before Godevus, turned his back with perfect composure, and addressed the warriors who had been watching the duel.

"Alberan has set foot upon this land, and we of Aldos have chosen to become her sword."

At those words, Godevus and his men stared wide-eyed.

"In time, a nation of the divine child shall be built upon this land. To us who cower in the shadows, fearing the dragon — Alberan cleaves the gloom and shows us the light."

Bernaik indicated the sword hanging at his hip.

"……Gather beneath Alberan. If the sword of Aldos is fit to be offered to the divine child's right hand, then the shield of Garkha is fit to be offered to her left."

Then Bernaik turned to face the rear. Not toward Godevus, who had pushed himself upright to look at him, but toward the trees behind.

Suddenly, from one of those trees, a single girl alighted. Leaping from a high branch, she descended as though a feather were falling — soundless. Her knees absorbed the landing softly, and she stood.

The hem of the long robe that fluttered around her was out of place on a battlefield; long silver hair and purple eyes that held light like jewels surveyed the warriors in silence.

To those watching, her movements were so light — as though she possessed not weight, nor even substance — and so out of place in their beauty. Descending with the word *Alberan*, she was cloaked in illusion and mystery.

Bazarishe directed a mischievous smile at Bernaik, mouthing only *You noticed me, well done* — and an unamused Bernaik paid her no heed.

For her, this was the first step — the one she could not afford to fail. She was not the kind of person to leave it to others and wait in the rear. She would come to observe Bernaik's fight, doubling as an assessment.

A moment's thought was all it took to realize.

As though following a scripted performance, Bernaik drew his sword, knelt on one knee, and offered it to her. Bazarishe walked forward at her leisure, took the sword in hand, and raised its point toward the heavens.

"As Alberan, let me pose a question to you."

Her voice, too, was clear and cool. A child's voice that reached the ears as though it could pierce them — and yet possessed an indescribable bewitching quality, as intimate as a whisper even at a distance.

"Will you walk the sunlit path with me — or rot and crumble in the shadows?"

Then, slowly, the point leveled forward.

"Choose now, in this very moment. There are only two paths before you."

*Choose*, she continued, and one warrior dropped to his knee.

Seeing the man bow his head, the one beside him knelt as well, and the wave spread — friend and foe alike.

Watching this, Bazarishe narrowed her eyes, then spun around and smiled at Godevus.

"Great chieftain of Garkha — Godevus."

"……"

The girl said nothing more, and simply extended her left hand — the one that held no sword.

Godevus hesitated for a moment, then knelt before her, bowed his head, and raised his great shield in both hands as though presenting it.

Bazarishe accepted the great shield, and her smile deepened.

"Let us be on good terms from now on, shall we? As those who walk together."

In response, Godevus lowered his head another degree.

And so the battle ended, and the dead were buried.

The dead were overwhelmingly on Garkha's side, but by Bazarishe's order, both sides cooperated — digging pits and laying the warriors' remains within them.

Bernaik watched from a distance, and beside him, Bazarishe laughed.

"Hmhm, I've grown even fonder of you, Bernaik. You fulfilled my every requirement — eliminating the ones we didn't need exactly as requested, with minimal casualties on both sides…… You kept your promise not to draw your sword, and met all my demands."

The ones *we didn't need* — her gaze fell on the warriors who had held the center. Having lost their two leaders, Almas and Rottia, and suffered no small casualties, some among them shed tears, mourning the loss.

Even so, she wore her smile throughout.

"I acknowledge they needed to be dealt with. But whatever the case, they were warriors. I won't tell you to grieve, but know this: laugh at their deaths and you will make enemies."

"It's all so complicated, isn't it……"

"The word *Alberan* that serves you so well is built upon a foundation of faith. A sharp sword, mishandled, takes its wielder's life…… If you claim to be a god yourself, that's another matter — but if not, make the effort to learn."

When no other eyes were on them, Bernaik dispensed with formal speech. Bazarishe was undeniably a genius, stronger than anyone — but she was no warrior. She did not even know what reverence for a warrior's faith meant, and naturally, she could not understand the reverence directed at her by others. She was still a child.

Showing deference to Bazarishe was like giving jewels to a dog. Bernaik despised such meaningless gestures.

Bazarishe glared at Bernaik with some displeasure and pouted — *But I don't understand these things*.

"I can see that a good person dying is indeed a shame. But showing pity to one's enemies like that — it's incomprehensible to me."

"That's because, at root, you think of nothing but yourself."

"……?"

"A person is an individual, but also a member of a group. The higher one's perspective rises, the more one thinks of the group's wellbeing."

Individual likes and dislikes are just personal, he said.

"They may have disagreed with me, but both were decent men as warriors of the group. They swung their swords not for their own desires, but for Aldos. The deaths of two men who defied their chieftain were inevitable…… but that does not change the fact that they were fine warriors."

Bazarishe's petulant expression went blank, and she simply stared up at him. Those large purple eyes gazed with empty, inorganic light.

"The same applies to enemies. It is only natural to kill someone who became your foe and raised a blade against you. But that does not diminish their worth. A warrior is still a warrior, and as long as that is so, their value does not decrease. …… Even if they were my enemy as the leader of Aldos, seen from the heavens above — within the great mass of humanity — they were still warriors who deserved to be honored."

Bazarishe considered for a moment and said, *I see*.

"I think I understand — a little. And you explain things quite well, Bernaik."

"Raising you is not my job, but……"

"I'll have you know, I'm well aware that *raising* is a terribly rude word."

She glared at him with displeasure, but he continued, unperturbed.

"Look at what aligns with what you're aiming for. If you want to become a god, you need learn nothing at all. ……But if you wish to join the mass of humanity, much learning is required."

"Muu……"

"I said it because I took you for the latter — but how you think about it is up to you. For the rest, go ask that man who's supposed to be raising you."

He jerked his chin toward Mitskronia, approaching from behind.

His armor was drenched in blood. It had been Mitskronia and Aldos warriors loyal to Bernaik who had charged into the Garkha rear.

Allowing the weakened center to be pushed in, advancing the spear-equipped flanks forward — forming a double envelopment, with Mitskronia lying in wait at a distance, moving in on the horn signal to deliver the finishing blow. That was the shape of this battle.

The one surprise had been that the archers alone had not been enough — but credit was due to Godevus's quick thinking.

Seeing Mitskronia return from overseeing the burial rites, and similarly watching Godevus return from keeping watch over the Garkha warriors, Bernaik began to walk.

"Bernaik."

"……Ask Mitskronia for the rest."

"What would you have done if the dueling opponent had been someone stronger?"

He furrowed his brow and turned back. Bazarishe's face was blank. Like a doll, she simply stared up at him.

"You're the one who told me not to draw my sword."

"Then, even if it meant being killed, you would not have drawn it?"

"Then I would simply have been that kind of man."

Bernaik said it as though it were the most natural thing in the world.

"……Whatever you may be, I have acknowledged you as Alberan and sworn upon my sword and my name. A man who would bend that — I would not acknowledge as a warrior."

"You really are a fool, aren't you."

"Say what you like. Whatever you might be, I am who I am."

Bazarishe stared at Bernaik for a long moment, then laughed — like a child.

Then — *In that case, it's an order* — she said.

"From now on, even if I command it, you will not die without permission. If you are about to die, you will break my orders and prioritize staying alive."

Bazarishe held up a finger and smiled.

"……I want to talk with you more, Bernaik."

To those words, Bernaik answered with exasperation — *I am deeply honored* — and turned his back.

"Therefore it is an order. You will not try to wander off somewhere on your own, but stay here and talk to me about all manner of things."

"…………"

"Surely you wouldn't bend your oath upon sword and name over something like this, Bernaik?"

He turned to look at the delighted Bazarishe once more, expression sour, then shifted his gaze to the approaching Mitskronia.

"What seems to be the matter, Lord Bernaik?"

"……You are this girl's handler, Mitskronia. Do something about her before my patience reaches its limit."
