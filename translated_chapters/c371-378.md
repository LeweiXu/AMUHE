# Chapter 371: Alberan VII

The great tribes of Aldos and Garka.

Then, having brought countless lesser and middling tribes under her sway in the blink of an eye, the name of Bazarishe of Enreine had spread for a little over two years.

Just as that whole upheaval had begun to settle and was fading from memory, a single message was relayed across the surrounding world.

——*Children of the shadows, hidden among the trees: gather to Alberan.*

Many took those words as a provocation.

For those who held the trees of the forest sacred in particular, this was tantamount to trampling on their faith.

A number of tribes joined together and, with weapons in hand, made for that domain — intent on putting the impudent slip of a girl who claimed Alberan's name on display.

"……What is this."

Eidenahl — the chieftain of the great tribe Rodet, a giant old warrior with a long white braided beard — stared in stupefaction, his eyes (which usually looked half-asleep) opened wide.

He had ridden through the forest at the head of four thousand warriors and stepped into Alberan's domain.

What had been built there was a colossal wall.

It was not particularly rare to construct a wall by lining up plain logs.

But the wall before his eyes was made of white stone, laid in regular courses to a height of twenty *shaku*, and atop it stood archers — hunters with bows who looked silently down at them.

He had lived a hundred years and accumulated much knowledge and experience.

He had thought now was the time to entrust the seat of chieftain to a promising youth, support him while imparting what he had learned, and wait to return to the earth.

Such had been Eidenahl's thinking when the words of that disrespectful little girl reached his ears.

He was not the only one who had felt anger; he had called out to many tribes and bound them together, declaring that this battle must be the very meaning of his birth.

That this duty fell to no one but himself.

He was a great chieftain, and one who had lived long.

Warriors paid especially deep respect to those who had reached old age.

It was proof of having survived countless battles on a single life — all the more so when there was no shameful blot upon him, no record of fleeing from an enemy.

A valorous warrior, and at the same time a sage rich in knowledge and experience: Eidenahl.

Even tribes that habitually opposed one another had agreed it was good to stand shoulder-to-shoulder when *he* called, and they followed him to strike down their common enemy, Bazarishe.

The great tribes Aldos and Garka.

Since these tribes — known for their martial prowess — had submitted to Bazarishe, all had braced themselves for a hard fight; but a battle on this scale came perhaps once in a lifetime, if at all.

Even if a man should die in it, no warrior could ask for a better place to fall.

Stirred by the spirited warriors who laughed and lifted their fighting spirit, even the inexperienced young men were brimming with morale.

Yet the moment they saw the gigantic stone wall before them, every one of them was struck dumb.

Working logs to build a simple wall to defend a settlement was already considerable, gruelling labour.

Only a handful of comparatively wealthy tribes ever managed it.

Since there were warriors who could vault over a small wall easily, such things tended to be more a display of authority than a practical defense.

And precisely because they understood the labour involved in a log wall, the fact that this one was built of stone was beyond belief.

The work of quarrying and dressing stone was not on the same scale as woodwork.

Moreover, he had never even heard of any quality stone quarry in this vicinity.

Looking at the staggering quantity of stonework used here, it almost seemed as though something had been conjured from nothing.

The dressing of the stone, course upon course, was uniform as if shaped to specification.

Could even a hundred craftsmen, given several decades, build a thing like this?

Precisely because Eidenahl had the breadth of knowledge and experience, and the wit to apply them, he could not suppress the trembling that rose from the marrow of his bones at the sight.

Directly before them stood a great gate.

Vast enough that one wondered if it were built to admit giants, reinforced with iron and weighty.

He could not believe it would be easily broken with the axes they carried.

A glance was enough to tell that the wall went all the way around.

But of his four thousand soldiers, fewer than a hundred warriors were capable of scaling such a wall.

The vast majority of them stood helpless before this twenty-*shaku* stone face.

Naturally one would think to bring up ladders — but it was plain to the eye that arrows would rain down on them throughout the carrying and the climbing.

In some places, in apparent anticipation of just that, timber-framed platforms jutted out from the wall in positions where ladders could be shot at from the side.

Of course, his side had brought hunters as well.

But even with equal numbers, between firing arrows from below and shooting down from above — which side was at the advantage was obvious at a glance.

Looking at the breastworks for cover, it was clear that an exchange of fire had been fully anticipated.

He was a fine sage.

Stunned though he was, his head churned out countless plans for how this wall might be cracked.

Yet the more he thought, the more he understood one fact: *it had been built with all of those measures already accounted for.*

The great gate was set somewhat back — beneath a stone arch, with gaps above and to either side.

Any attempt to destroy the gate would draw arrows, or other things, from those gaps.

Given the ditch in front of the wall, the ladders would have to be very long.

Building strong ones in such lengths was no easy matter.

And to prepare a great number of ladders required time — which they had no provisions to support.

Even were they to find some way, the morale of so many warriors would not hold.

No man would gladly help to build ladders, knowing he would then be ordered to that suicidal task: climbing under arrow fire to take the wall.

Provisions or no, by the next morning half their number might well have melted away.

Yet if they turned back even once, this coalition would crumble.

Tribes would inevitably appear to declare submission to Bazarishe.

Lose this moment, and the strength needed to stand against Aldos and Garka would be utterly gone.

He understood, and yet there was nothing he could do.

The reinforcements who emerged from the forest one after another were rendered speechless at the sight of the great wall before them.

The unease became a murmur.

*This is no work of mortal hands*, he heard.

And along with it, voices saying perhaps she truly was the genuine Alberan.

Some no doubt spoke from knowing they had no chance, and from fear of death.

But because Eidenahl, of all people, could grasp how vast the spectacle before him was, he could not lightly utter words of denial.

*Perhaps Bazarishe truly is the genuine Alberan.*

The words dancing in his own heart were the same as theirs.

"……"

A blast of horns rolled high through the sky, layering one upon another.

Innumerable haughty crimson banners — driving sword-points into the heavens — rippled into view, and atop the gate, directly before Eidenahl and the others, appeared a girl.

Backlit, her form was hard to make out clearly: only her beautiful silver hair glittering in the sun.

She was clad in silken robes and a graceful white dress, and merely looked down upon them, saying nothing.

Understanding what was being asked of him, Eidenahl moved forward.

That he did not dismount was the chieftain's last point of pride.

Several others followed in his wake.

They were the chieftains who led each tribe.

In the strangely heavy tension, no one spoke as they moved forward.

Whatever they felt, they were the ones who led their tribes.

They surely understood what manner of spectacle this was.

That she had built this wall here through her power as a child of the gods.

The sight was such that even that explanation would be accepted without protest.

It had been only two years at most since this region had come under Bazarishe's banner.

To say that a wall like this had been raised in that span — one would more readily accept that it was a ruin from the age of myth, from the time of the gods.

When they reached the foot of the stone bridge that led to the gate, its splendid construction stole their gaze.

It was smoothed to a finish, and he could not even find the seams.

It was as if a stone in the shape of a bridge had suddenly fallen from the sky.

He looked up to the gate's parapet, where a figure in silver armour with elegant golden hair — likely the Shining One, Belnaich, great chieftain of Aldos — held out a sword to her, and she drew it with a smooth motion.

"……Eidenahl, great chieftain of Rodet."

A voice like the chime of a bell, and at the same time sweet.

The arc of the sword was smooth; she pointed its tip toward Eidenahl's face.

The point of the blade swayed slightly, indicating the ground.

Cold violet eyes, framed in silver, played a chill, inhuman light into Eidenahl's own.

He could no longer think of this girl as a haughty fool falsely calling herself Alberan.

"Y-, Lord Eidenahl……"

Eidenahl dismounted, removed his helm, and knelt on one knee, bowing his head.

"……Forgive my discourtesy, Alberan. I, Eidenahl, as great chieftain of Rodet, swear my submission to your person — by sword and name, by ancestors and gods."

Cries of dismay ran through the warriors at his back.

But not one of them hurled a curse at the bearing Eidenahl had taken.

Every man there had been overawed by the situation.

The other chieftains exchanged glances, and only one barked aloud.

"……What a contemptible sight, Eidenahl! Have you truly cowered before some little girl?!"

A great man, like Eidenahl, with a long pure-white beard.

"Boral……"

"It seems even your soul has aged. Frightened by a single wall, dropping to your knee…… how pitiful."

The great chieftain of the great tribe Selbus — a man with whom Eidenahl had crossed swords countless times.

Eidenahl's greatest enemy, and at the same time his greatest friend.

Eidenahl glared at him as if doubting his sanity, then quickly understood.

He did not in the least believe there was any chance of victory in this situation, nor that the girl who had built this wall was an ordinary person.

He was simply taking on the role of barker, in order to test her true mettle and convey it to the warriors at their backs.

So that none of the warriors would walk away thinking the chieftains had cowered before a little girl and bowed without a fight.

Boral drew his long sword from his hip and pointed its tip at the girl.

"Boral, great chieftain of Selbus — do not lump me in with these spineless wretches, little girl. If you are truly Alberan, then come down here and prove it!"

Boral laughed and addressed the man beside him — the man with golden hair.

"That said, fool though she is for putting on airs, I do not relish turning my blade on a woman or child. ……I would have no objection if it were *you*, Belnaich."

Belnaich narrowed his eyes and said something to Bazarishe.

Bazarishe smiled and answered him with a few words, then took the hem of her skirt in hand and lightly leapt down onto the bridge.

There was no other way to describe the soundless landing.

Even the splendid landings of Belnaich and the other large man who followed her looked clumsy and heavy beside hers.

Captivated though he was by the elegance of her descent, Boral also dismounted, and shouldered his long sword.

"Boral, great chieftain of Selbus. ……As Alberan, I accept your duel."

Bazarishe stepped forward with a faint smile, her long sword hanging at her side.

Up close, she was a girl of remarkable beauty.

Long hair like smooth silver thread.

A nose drawn cleanly down her face, and cherry-blossom-coloured lips that softly formed a smile.

Her great violet eyes, like jewels, glittered fringed in silver as her hair was fringed.

She was small in stature, but the slimness of her limbs and the smallness of her head made one not feel that smallness; with the soft, womanly lines of her body, she seemed a sculpture wrought by the gods.

Merely by being there she captured the gaze, and embodied a world of gods.

Alberan — Bazarishe — by her presence alone dragged everything around her into the dream sung of in the old legends.

"Whenever, however you please."

So saying, she stopped one step *inside* Boral's reach.

Strangely, neither Boral nor any of the watchers took it as mockery.

"Hah……!"

Boral sank down — his body betraying no sign of age — and propelled himself forward with explosive force.

He had personally hunted three magical beasts and dedicated nearly a hundred years to the way of arms; he was one of the foremost warriors in this part of the world.

His charge was a thing the average man could not even follow with his eyes; one would lie cleaved in half before having understood what had happened.

A swift, savage, beautiful, ultimate stroke.

That single flash was a perfect embodiment of his epithet *Boral the Body-Splitter*.

But the girl slipped past him, beneath his arm, as though sliding through.

Without the slightest sign of fear, her smooth movement was almost a casual walk.

The reverse stroke meant to take her head split only the air above her.

Only a very few could see her brushing the cut aside with the flat of her own blade and turning it away.

To most onlookers, it appeared that the sword itself was afraid, of its own will avoiding cutting her.

The blade does not reach the gods.

That seemed entirely natural.

Boral's tremendous swordsmanship was visible even from afar.

The shriek of the air, far past anything that could be called a wind-cut.

It was precisely because they could grasp the fearsome speed of his motion that the girl no longer looked human in their eyes.

Her body, swaying as if in a dance, must surely be defended by an unseen power of the gods, they grew certain.

She floated back a step, lightly, putting distance between them, then with one hand behind her back and her head tilted, she smiled.

"Has that been proof enough, Boral. ……Your sword is one fit to be wielded under me."

Breathing hard, his shoulders heaving, Boral laughed at the girl.

"……Truly, the genuine Alberan. I, Boral……, now know it was for this very day that I lived on into old age."

She narrowed her eyes; her mouth softened.

"Grateful words. Then let your honour as a warrior be fulfilled here. May my still-young blades gather under the child of the gods."

A voice meant only for those nearby.

Hearing it, Eidenahl closed his eyes for a moment, and called Boral's name.

"Yes. ……What a shame."

She slipped past Boral as he stepped in, laughing, and came around behind him.

"Hah……"

Holding her sword in a reverse grip, easily —

She drove the point through his armour into his heart and pulled it free.

Boral, with a smile on his face, dropped to his knees, fell, —

— and rolled onto his back to look up at the sky.

Bazarishe knelt by his side, placed his fallen sword back into his hand, and smiled.

"Depart on your journey, as a valorous warrior."

"……My thanks."

Boral smiled happily, closed his eyes, and Bazarishe quietly stood up after watching that.

A glance from her — and Eidenahl knelt and bowed his head once more, the other chieftains likewise.

The warriors watching from a distance, too, knelt and bowed their heads at the sight of this maiden bestowing her grace upon a great warrior.

Catching that out of the corner of her eye, she walked past Eidenahl and the others, who knelt in a row.

"He is to be buried as a noble hero. ……The greetings can come after that."

"……Yes."

Then, before the giant and the tall man — Mitskronia and Belnaich:

"Mitskronia, if there's no problem, see that the warriors outside are fed, later."

"Yes. Splendidly done, Lady Bazarishe."

She smiled at Mitskronia's words, and as she passed beneath the gate she glanced at the visibly displeased Belnaich and murmured to him.

"How was it? It was the proper Alberan-like Alberan you wanted, was it not. *Splendidly done, Lady Bazarishe*; you might say that to me sometimes too."

"If you'd kept it that way until we got to the room I might have considered it…… that's exactly the sort of stupidity I'm talking about."

"……Mitskronia, Belnaich is sulking because his duel was taken away. Do something about him."

"That's *my* line. Hand back my sword, now."

Mitskronia gave a wry, *good grief* sort of laugh at the two of them.

Then he barked out toward the soldiers around them, who were still tense.

"New warriors have come running to gather under Alberan! Tonight we shall hold a feast to celebrate! Raise your blades, and let the heavens hear whose are the hands that uphold us! That voice will surely reach the gods, and bring still greater blessings upon the child of the gods!!"

At those words the men finally let out their breath, and let out a battle-cry that seemed it would split the air.

# Chapter 372: Alberan VIII

When a certain kind of stone is crushed and mixed with water and ash and worked into a paste, it hardens with time.

It was when she was still five that Bazarishe learned about this thing called *forged stone*.

It was when she was looking over an old ruin together with Mitskronia.

"In ancient times, it seems there was an era when there were many stone-built temples like this one…… though when one considers the labour involved, it is hard to believe."

"I wonder if they used up all the easy stone in that era."

Cradled in Mitskronia's arms, Bazarishe spoke through a yawn.

There remained the bases of huge columns and a few fragments that seemed to have been the roof.

The taller pieces stood about eight *shaku*, two *shaku* across.

"It seems there were once stone roofs and splendid pedestals as well…… those bits and pieces were carried off and are now used in temples and the like across the land."

Mitskronia related to her the stories that had been handed down.

The child of the gods — Bazarishe — was an exceedingly singular genius.

By the age of three she had learned to handle mana, was able to inscribe formulae upon mana crystals with ease, and could recite the priests' words verbatim having heard them only once.

A talent that "having talent" did not begin to describe.

Mitskronia's father, the previous chieftain, on his sick-bed, had strictly enjoined the village folk never to allow others to learn what Bazarishe was, and had appointed Mitskronia as her companion-attendant.

Bazarishe was the very child of the gods who would bring light to the people of the shadows.

Ever since, Mitskronia had shown her everything and shared all his arts with her without stinting.

Such breadth of knowledge and experience would surely be what made the child of the gods what she was.

It was like pouring water into a great jar with no visible bottom: pour as he might, she made all of it her own without effort.

That day was no different.

"……This is not just plain stone."

"……?"

"There are no seams."

She hopped down out of Mitskronia's arms, easily lifted up a stone, and with surprising force hurled it at the upper part of a column, smashing it.

"L-, Lady Bazarishe……!"

He was stunned that she would damage a precious ruin like that without hesitation, but unconcerned, she picked up a broken piece and looked at it.

The violet eyes catching the fragment took on an inhuman light, and Mitskronia stayed the hand he had begun to extend.

Bazarishe was usually childlike, yet from time to time she would put on a transcendent look.

It was, no doubt, the moment when her thoughts were turning at unimaginable speed.

In that moment her usual childlike expression vanished, and she became blank.

The villagers said it was as if she were possessed by the divine.

A figure that one hesitated even to address.

It was an unspoken understanding that, in such moments, one did not speak to Bazarishe unless asked, and did not interrupt her.

Bazarishe gazed at the broken cross-section of the pillar, looked over the remaining rubble, and glanced about her surroundings.

Then, without saying anything, she began to walk, and Mitskronia followed.

Beyond, there was a cliff face.

"……Sword."

When asked, he handed her his sword, and she thrust it into the cliff face and gouged out earth.

Tossing the sword aside, she examined the gouged earth.

He had told her many times that the sword was a sacred thing and asked her to handle it with care, but she did not really seem to take it in; she usually managed, but at moments like these she handled it carelessly.

There was nothing more important than her learning, and so he simply picked the sword up.

By now Mitskronia had half given up.

For a while Bazarishe poked at the soil, looked over the cliff face, and then said:

"That is closer to brick."

"……Brick?"

"Something shaped and hardened…… this cliff face has been cut by hand. This finely-grained clay — it would be natural to see it as one of the materials. Although hardening it would only make it clay again…… so either there is some special technique involved, or some special ingredient."

Mitskronia furrowed his brow.

"If it were a technique, it would be natural for it to be passed down…… so we should consider it a problem of the ingredients. But if we can pinpoint what that ingredient is, we ought to be able to make the same thing. Then we wouldn't need to cut stone — we could just shape it as we needed."

"You are saying that this kind of construction would also be possible for us?"

"If we can find the materials."

Bazarishe turned her gaze to Mitskronia.

Her gracious silver hair swayed.

"It is not impossible to neatly cut out a column like that earlier one, but there's no good quarry around here. Counting the time and labour as well, it isn't realistic. That's what was puzzling me."

"……I see."

"I have gained useful information. A whole week of all-the-honey-I-want, then?"

"Three days, please."

"Mn……"

She knit her brows and glared at Mitskronia.

"Five."

"Understood — let us split the difference and make it four. If you can pinpoint the technique, I will guarantee you a week."

Bazarishe folded her arms and pondered for a while, then nodded that she would have to put up with that.

Mitskronia narrowed his eyes at her honest acceptance.

"I cannot imagine that *you* of all people would, Lady Bazarishe…… but if you should resort to the cunning trick of doling out information in pieces — say, having already pinpointed the technique — then I should have to resort to a cunning trick myself."

Her shoulders jumped, and Bazarishe's eyes wandered.

"I-I was just about to say it. I can make a rough guess from the composition, that's all……"

"……Composition?"

"There are tiny fragments of white stone mixed in. I thought at first they might be merely mixed in, or differences of concentration in the blend…… but it would be most reasonable to think that the white stone itself is crushed and mixed in."

Bazarishe came over to him and asked to be picked up, and Mitskronia lifted her into his arms as before.

"The white stone itself is sometimes mixed into the local rock, but most likely there is a mass of rock somewhere made up entirely of the white part. Looking at how few ruins there are like this one, I suspect it is not in this vicinity."

"……I see."

"Either way, considerable quantities are needed. Even if we found some, it would only be enough to use experimentally. That includes whether or not this clay is really a material…… it might just be that *something* has been quarried out of it."

Mitskronia nodded.

"I shall set people to looking."

"Please do. ……I think that the various things in this world are dissolved into one another."

"……Dissolved?"

Bazarishe nodded and pointed at the sword Mitskronia carried.

"You extract iron from iron ore to make iron. But probably the stones lying around contain something like iron *elements* mixed into them. If you gathered them up they would probably yield the same iron. The things that get knocked out as impurities when iron ore is heated are also elements that compose *something* — they just aren't iron."

"Elements……"

"Yes. What matters isn't the white stone itself, it's the elements inside it. The elements contained in this clay…… if they happen to bind in some particular way, they can become like that pillar."

So saying, Bazarishe pointed at the ruined column with her finger.

"Water becomes ice when it is cooled. When it is hot it becomes water. Gold is solid, but in great heat it melts. Iron, which softens when heated, will probably also melt if you apply still more heat. ……Long, long ago, I expect that all sorts of elements were dissolved together like that."

"In the age of the gods, it is said that the earth was simply a sphere."

It was a story from the creation myth.

It was said that the wrath of the chief god Ordoris twisted the smooth sphere and gave rise to land with hills and valleys.

"I do not believe that, but never mind. ……At any rate, heat is one of the conditions that can dissolve elements together and separate them. The fact that fertile soil is faintly warmer than wasteland is probably the same reason. Things we can't see dissolve and change."

Bazarishe's words were not the prattle of a child.

In whatever she looked at, no one could see as deep, or read as much, as she did.

"It is the same with people…… moving things take on heat, things at rest grow cold. Bury them and bones remain while the rest passes back into the soil; but a frozen corpse does not rot. Food, in winter, does not spoil or change. Why do we rub two pieces of wood to start a fire? To create heat…… and heat brings about change, turning the wood into flame, and into ash and charcoal."

Bazarishe smiled.

"All things change. The human body, in the end, is the result of food being changed…… into movement, into growth. And one of the most important elements in that is heat. The right heat dissolves elements together, and separates them out."

*How about that?* she said happily.

"It is still only a hypothesis, but probably it isn't very wrong. From that angle, what matters is the proportion of necessary elements in the materials…… if we can learn to spot the stones and soils that contain a high proportion of the elements that turn earth into a pillar like that, we could make as much as we wanted."

"……A truly intriguing line of thought."

Everything she saw, she saw differently.

Just a column, or just a stone — yet from it she drew tens, hundreds, perhaps far more times the knowledge Mitskronia could.

And the vast knowledge she gained, she calmly stitched together inside her own head, transforming it into wisdom.

"That was a very useful conversation. Combined with the earlier topic, how about a week?"

"……So you come at it that way."

Mitskronia gave a wry smile and considered briefly.

"And that the human body, too, is the result of various things dissolving together?"

"That is what I think."

"……Then a thought, if I may. If you eat nothing but honey because it is sweet and delicious, would not the balance of those *elements* dissolved into your body shift, and might it not throw you out of order?"

*Nh*, Bazarishe scrunched up her face in displeasure, and Mitskronia shook his head exaggeratedly.

"I should certainly love to let you taste honey to your heart's content, Lady Bazarishe…… but if it should harm you, I cannot stand by. The body that has yet to grow…… surely bone and flesh and blood cannot be made of honey alone."

"Th-, that's not necessarily so. Everyone says honey is full of nutrients."

"But to lean on it alone is a serious problem…… that said, having none at all would be a pity. ……Five days, then. You agree?"

Bazarishe groaned, and Mitskronia stroked her head with a wry smile.

"Hey. I told you I didn't want any. Don't put honey into someone else's tea on your own."

"I am simply, kindly, teaching the unrefined Belnaich, who knows nothing of the adult sophistication that is honey-tea, how good honey-tea is."

A room in the manor of the walled city.

Just as expected, she had absorbed great tribes with hardly any deaths, and Bazarishe was in a good mood.

Saying, *I shall pour the tea myself*, she dropped spoonful after spoonful of honey into the cup.

Belnaich, sitting on the sofa across from her, looked exasperated.

Mitskronia, watching from the side as Bazarishe poured in mountains of honey, gave a wry smile.

She had once clung to a small jar of honey and refused to let it go.

After Mitskronia had spent a great deal of time talking her round, occasions of eating it straight had grown rare; instead, she now drank herb tea with great quantities of honey poured into it.

Bazarishe stirred the tea, now thick with honey, and held it out to Belnaich with a delighted smile.

"Here, Belnaich. There is no possibility, surely, that the proud warrior Belnaich would refuse Alberan's tea."

"……Mitskronia, swap the cup."

"Nothing of the sort. Whenever I drink honey-tea happily, you always insult it. Try it at least once before you say anything."

"Now, now, Lord Belnaich. For my sake…—"

"There. Mitskronia is saying so as well."

Belnaich sighed.

*First, just one mouthful*, said Bazarishe, and reluctantly Belnaich brought the cup to his lips.

Seeing his eyebrow twitch, Bazarishe's face lit up happily.

"*Hmph*, that was completely an *unexpectedly not bad* reaction. When you do that, Belnaich, your eyebrow twitches. I know about it."

"……I only thought it wasn't bad with a *little* honey in it. Either way, this is too sweet."

"Then if cutting it down would be to your taste, you must concede that honey-tea itself is acceptable. If you stop complaining, I shall reduce the amount next time."

"I'm telling you not to put it in without permission."

Bazarishe looked at the displeased Belnaich and laughed merrily.

She seemed quite fond of him, and seeing it Mitskronia was always relieved.

The child of the gods — she was a genius, slightly twisted in places, yet artless and bright.

The villagers all liked her, but they kept a small distance from her.

She was the symbol of hope in a settlement that had been oppressed by the strong and could only cower.

Out of respect, they drew a line between themselves and her, and revered her from the heart.

Even in the battle against the renowned Belnaich, few had imagined a victory of that magnitude — yet they had taken up their blades with trembling hands for the sake of the hope that was Bazarishe.

Such respect that, for her sake, they would gamble their lives.

It was faith itself: no one would dream of standing beside her, and they treated her as one looks up at a god.

But Belnaich talked back to her plainly, sometimes mocked her, treated her as a child.

The presence of such a man was, for her, surely the greatest thing of all.

She was, indeed, Alberan, but the very person herself believed less than anyone that she was Alberan, nor did she wish to be.

It was only that all the village wished it of her, and so she was.

Perhaps for that reason, in front of Belnaich, Bazarishe became conspicuously talkative.

A knock came at the door, and the muscular young man who entered was Goudeus, great chieftain of Garka.

"My apologies, the situation was difficult to read and I am late."

"It is fine. The conversation begins now."

Bazarishe had had Goudeus lead troops in reserve in a place outside the walls, in case the men outside should choose to attack.

Even if the enemy mounted an offensive, it would surely not last long.

She had been certain that a night-attack at the moment their morale collapsed would shatter them.

"As I thought, smoke signals are hard to read in the wind."

"Yes…… I did make out the *all clear* signal, but for that very reason I felt no need to rush, and I observed the enemy as I came."

"That is fine. Caution is one of your virtues. Sit."

# Chapter 374: Alberan X

The Kreisharana were the people who had taken root at the foot of Albyagel, where the divine being slumbered.

Long ago, they had been the gatekeepers who barred the entry of fools who would disturb the divine being's sleep.

The divine being was an absolute one, a god with a body.

The roar of its wrath could pierce a high mountain with ease, and could rewrite the very shape of the land.

A single beat of its wings raised storms; trees were torn up by the roots; a single descent set the earth ringing.

When had they become its gatekeepers?

No one knew the precise history.

But it was preserved as legend.

『*Crush you as I might, you keep boiling up endlessly — and dealing with you is a nuisance. If you wish my permission, do not disturb my contemplation. So long as that is kept, I shall tolerate your existence.*』

In the days when all feared the divine being, men of courage prostrated themselves before it and begged forgiveness.

Many heroes lost their lives without ceremony.

But it was the words spoken to Almantis — the hero who later became the founding chieftain of Kreisharana — when he begged that forgiveness, that became the beginning of all things.

Thereafter, they were charged with the role of gatekeepers.

In those days there were many fools who, thinking to test their nerve, set out to challenge and slay the divine being; Kreisharana, as gatekeepers, dispatched all of them.

And, in order to dispatch fools, they trained themselves with desperate diligence, and dedicated their lives to the office of gatekeeper.

All of it to protect countless innocents from the divine being's wrath.

But not everyone was such a fool.

The first to be allowed an audience with the divine being was Zeklit, an old sage held in high regard throughout the surrounding region.

He conveyed deep gratitude to the Kreisharana who served as gatekeepers, and bowed his head, saying he wished to convey his heartfelt thanks to the divine being who had pardoned them.

He had spent long years travelling among many tribes, preaching that the divine being's wrath must never be drawn upon them, and his character lived up to the stories told of him.

Judging that he would cause no problems, they permitted the audience, and joined him in conveying deep gratitude to the divine being.

『*The demand was conveyed, and is observed — though only in passing moments. I ask no more, and have neither concern nor real interest in you. So long as you keep that word, I shall close my eyes to such trifles as your crawling along the ground before me.*』

A glance of red-violet that conveyed the gulf in their respective stations.

At those words, all were inwardly relieved from the heart.

The origin of the name *Kreisharana* lay in a word for a moon shaped like a blade.

It had been taken, they said, from the moon that hung directly over Albyagel on the very night Almantis received the divine being's pardon and descended to the foot of the mountain.

——*We shall be like the moon hanging in that sky, a blade without cloud.*

Each time he saw that moon, he would recall his vow to the divine being.

Almantis died early, having shielded a young warrior from a Jade Tiger.

Lamenting how he would have rejoiced to see this, they prayed to him from the heart at the bladed moon that hung in the sky that night.

So that they would never forget his noble vow and reverence for the divine being, they came to favour curved swords bent like that crescent — and in time it took root as a part of the culture.

Together with the sword bestowed when they became warriors, they were given a duty.

To devote one's life as gatekeeper to the sanctuary where the divine being dwelt.

For those who knew by tradition the age of the dragon — when calamities of heaven and earth had been wrought — there was no role more honourable than that.

They held that role as the entirety of their existence, and asked nothing in return from the people.

But those around them were not the same.

It began as goodwill: harvests were sent to them from the surrounding settlements.

In those days they conveyed their respect and gratitude to Kreisharana, who bore so weighty a duty, and from the heart spoke of wanting to assist their service even a little.

The people of Kreisharana, in their turn, accepted those gifts together with thanks.

To live at the foot of monster-haunted Albyagel was no light matter, and on top of that they were truly grateful that men should bear the duty of gatekeepers; the people of Kreisharana, for their part, were glad of those who spoke of cooperating to the limits of their ability.

When they obtained the means to tame and rear the griffins that lived on Albyagel — using them as a way of safely patrolling the divine being's environs — they began to interact with various settlements to make their role and the divine being's covenant widely known.

They were warmly welcomed by many settlements who knew the divine being's terror, and many harvests were brought to Kreisharana's settlements.

They never accepted precious metals and the like, and conveyed that there was no need to strain to provide harvests; precisely *because* of that, however, even more harvests and respect gathered to them.

In small return, the people of Kreisharana would readily take on dangerous tasks like exterminating monsters; in the beginning, all of it was good will.

But over the long years, it became custom.

In time, an upper-and-lower relationship became fixed between them and the various settlements, and tribute came to be taken for granted; the fatal crack came in a particularly cold year.

In many regions the harvests failed, and people starved.

Within Albyagel, perhaps thanks to the divine being's tremendous mana-blessing, there had been some harvest — but the population had grown, and it was barely enough to stave off hunger.

Feed for the griffins also had to be secured.

Kreisharana, as in years past, demanded tribute from the settlements.

As gatekeepers of the sanctuary, neither griffins nor warriors could be allowed to starve.

If a fool used such an opening to set foot inside, and drew down the divine being's wrath, the matter would not stop at hunger and suffering.

To them, the demand seemed a legitimate one.

But the gratitude of settlements that had lost many to starvation in consequence turned to hatred.

Several years later, many settlements united and sent several thousand warriors against Kreisharana.

To strike down Kreisharana, who under the cover of the divine being's authority had taken their harvests.

There were not a few among Kreisharana who grieved at the fact that so many had died in their settlements; some demanded that the chieftains correct the situation so it could never happen again, and some ran themselves ragged trying to repair the relationship — but they were too late.

And the fight, too, was over in moments.

For the sake of the gatekeeper's duty, the warriors of Kreisharana were permitted, by virtue of the abundant tributes they received, to dedicate their lives wholly to martial pursuits, and they rode griffins.

On the other side, those who had been ceaselessly preyed upon by Kreisharana.

Against twice their number, Kreisharana's casualties came to a dozen or so.

The gulf between the two sides was absolute.

The chieftains of that day, in fury that this was the result of having forgotten the vow to the divine being, had their men ride round all the settlements and tear apart the chieftains and elders as a public example.

If they were going to forget gratitude and the vow, they had to be bound by fear so that nothing like this could happen again.

In place of the divine being, *we* shall manage them.

Custom thus became domination.

The warriors of Kreisharana were beings entrusted by the great divine being with the role of gatekeeper.

Settlements that defied them were swarmed by countless griffin cavalry and burned to the ground as warnings.

Power gradually drove many warriors mad.

In those early days there were many warriors of conscience who grew angry with younger ones drunk on power and committing lawlessness, and who punished them; but as the years went on, even that became a thing taken for granted.

Outwardly they were taught to refrain from luxury and esteem honest poverty, and within Kreisharana's settlements life was austere; once one stepped outside, however, it was not so.

Travel on the outside, where one could wield power as one pleased, was the greatest of pleasures.

When word spread that he who could ride a griffin could roam many settlements and exercise power as he liked, the number of those who sought to be sky-cavalry for that very reason only grew.

The noble crescent moon raised by the sublime tribe born of courage and good will was clouded, and at some point became wholly tainted.

Precisely because they stood face to face with the divine being, the awe of it alone was something they did not lose.

But once it was understood through long association that the divine being was indeed, as it had said, uninterested in human affairs, even that role became a mere pretext.

They trained their bodies in order to abuse the weak.

Their swords were not for remembering their forefathers' vow, but for display, for showing off authority.

A single sight of the curved sword hanging at one's hip would have anyone prostrating themselves and fawning, offering up even a beloved wife or daughter.

All knew that disrespect to a warrior meant the destruction of a settlement.

A warrior who could ride a griffin would receive at most a verbal scolding even if he beat a chieftain because he disliked the look in his eyes, or in some cases killed him outright.

Killing one or two children who threw stones was a joke.

Killing a settler simply because he had been impudent was sufficient justification.

Disciplining them through fear so that they would never defy them again was very nearly a duty for them.

The instant they saw him, those who had been outside — old, young, men, women alike — abandoned even what they were holding, threw themselves down, and pressed their foreheads to the earth.

The young man with the keen face — Coutos — closed his eyes at the sight, thinking what a terrible state of affairs this was.

"Y-, yes……! At present he is at the chieftain's house…… I believe."

At Coutos's descent everyone again threw themselves down, and saying *wait*, he tied his griffin's reins to the fence.

He called out a single word to the prostrate people, and the chieftain came hurrying out.

A bearded, brave-looking giant of a man, but at the sight of Coutos he immediately dropped to one knee.

"Stand. Chieftain Doruss, I have come to summon the great warrior Fanaroh — guide me."

"……Yes."

There was relief visible at the words.

They had met several times.

Doruss understood, at the level of *this one is one of the better ones*, what kind of person Coutos was.

He at once stood and led him into the house — to the bedroom.

——It was the master bedroom.

Coutos guessed the situation, told Doruss he could withdraw, and knocked sharply at the door.

"It is Coutos, Lord Fanaroh! A summons from the great chieftain!"

『*Aaah……? Wait a bit.*』

"You are to return at once."

A click of the tongue came from within, and a *get out of the way*.

There was a scream from a woman — most likely Doruss's wife.

Then a tall, brown-haired man with a goatee showed his face from within the room, naked.

He laughed and shut the door, and Coutos went outside with a sigh.

He fetched Fanaroh's beloved bird, tied up at the rear, and brought it to Levu's side.

Unlike their owners, the two birds were brothers and got on very well, rubbing their foreheads together.

The chieftain came out attendant-like to see him off, and prostrated himself.

"Doruss, tell the others your wife is to my taste."

"……Yes."

The chieftain's hand trembled faintly, with humiliation, fury, or grief.

Unable to bear it, Coutos called to Fanaroh and rose into the sky.

"Hey! Don't leave me behind!"

Fanaroh too quickly lifted off and chased Coutos from behind.

"Hah, that face will leave permanent furrows between your brows, Coutos. Why don't you have a bit of fun once in a while? Putting them in their place is our work…… and you know I'm one of the gentler sort."

The words were a jest, but the fact that they were *true* was what was unsalvageable.

He was scum, content to have other men's wives offered up for his amusement — yet even this Fanaroh was, indeed, considerably one of the *better* ones.

There were men like that in droves, not only in Kreisharana.

But Kreisharana's problem was that they were *too strong*.

In other tribes, even when one acted high-handedly, one did not cross the minimum boundary one was supposed to keep.

Provoke too much resentment, and the tribes would unite and someday march in to destroy you.

A devotion to family, friends, acquaintances — and not only them, but utter strangers too.

Whatever sort of opponent she might be, in the end she lacked griffins.

If only she were truly the child of the gods.

Coutos, in his heart, prayed it.

# Chapter 375: Alberan XI

One of the many who had been swallowed by power, desire, and pride.

# Chapter 376: Alberan XII

"Hey now, that's quite a thing. What is that……"

Fifteen griffin-cavalry had come to scout.

Looking down from above, the great warrior Fanaroh said as if surprised.

About a dozen *ri* from the Dragon's Maw — in cleared forest, a stone structure had been erected.

A stone wall close to half a *ri* in length.

Within it, a great many buildings were under construction.

The wall, too, still looked half-finished, but the side facing the Dragon's Maw was probably complete, and quite tall.

It had been only two weeks since they had heard the rumour of Alberan hauling materials, and there was no time, in that span, for so vast a fortress to have been built.

On top of which, the sheer mass of stone — that anyone could have moved that much stone without being noticed was strange to begin with.

Even if it had been gathered from across the land, a movement on that scale would surely have stirred up rumours.

"This is Alberan's magic, then?"

"Magic……?"

Coutos addressed Fanaroh, who had turned to him.

"There is a story I recall hearing — that Bazarishe can wield magic that turns plain earth into stone. ……If that rumour is true, then that staggering pile of stone must be the earth here, transformed."

"What are you on about? You think the princess Bazarishe really is a child of the gods?"

"No. It is a trick. If she truly had that power, the construction would already be complete. Why she would not be done if she possessed the power to turn earth into stone — that is the puzzle."

So saying, Coutos turned his eyes to Fanaroh.

Fanaroh was by no means stupid.

Even the great warrior could not hide his shock at the fortress taking shape below.

He understood that, by any reasoning, moving that much stone was impossible.

By ordinary calculations it would have taken decades of time, and quarrying that much stone was no ordinary task.

Coutos did not particularly want to dispel Fanaroh's anxiety, but a man with even a moderately quick head would eventually notice.

"It is certain they have some technique for turning earth into stone…… that is how they obtained the stone, but she is not, as is being said, an opponent who wields the power of a god."

"Right. It's a bit of a startling sight, but……"

"Even so, that they have bound and put to work that many men is in itself terrifying……"

Within the forest, as numerous as the stones, there were tents in great number.

Those at work were not on the order of one or two hundred.

Several thousand at minimum had gathered in that area, engaged in various tasks of construction, woodcutting, and so on.

Even from far above one could pick out countless warriors among them.

Men ordered to do this kind of work would not gladly take to it.

Yet the men were divided into countless work-gangs, and every one of them was working without complaint.

To grasp several thousand warriors and bend them to one's will.

If Bazarishe could be said to wield magic, that was the magic.

It was not fear.

There were no figures being driven by the whip.

In fact some of them were even looking up and laughing at them.

"Are they making light of us, just because they have the numbers? Irritating……"

"Lord Fanaroh, it is the warrior chief's strict order."

"A *greeting* should be allowed. They're laughing at us, you know? Are you saying I should go home insulted by warrior-leftovers below me?"

"If we frighten them and scatter them, we may lose track of Bazarishe. ……Even Lord Fanaroh would not get off without consequences for that."

Fanaroh glared at Coutos and clicked his tongue, then said, *fine*.

"As thanks for laughing at us — give them one spear. We're not giving them a second, so aim well."

The cavalry around them laughed, and followed Fanaroh as he descended.

Coutos sighed wearily and watched it from above.

A glide-spear-throw, carried by the speed of the dive.

There was no man who could throw a spear straight up overhead, and there were almost none with bows down there.

A horn blew, and he could see many of the men down there looking at some kind of cart on the wall under construction.

Come to think of it, ever since they had appeared, men had been gathered around that cart.

Looking carefully, the gaps in it were too large to be a cart, and the shape was odd.

Below the puzzling Coutos he saw Fanaroh and the others sweep into their glide, and the moment they slipped into the fortress proper, a bell rang.

"——!?"

What he had thought were carts moved as one, and hurled something.

What was loosed spread quickly and flew toward the cavalry who had levelled their spears.

A few of them, including Fanaroh, noticed in time and turned their griffins, fleeing upward; but five who failed to escape were tangled and fell.

Coutos likewise descended at last, going to Fanaroh.

In the fortress, men swarmed the fallen griffin-cavalry, hammering them with axes or driving in swords.

The screams of griffins and men reached even Coutos and the others above.

"Damn it! What was that…… !?"

Fanaroh's face was twisted, sweat bursting from his whole body in that single instant.

The men who had escaped were pale-faced too.

The griffins fully bound up in the net — perhaps they meant to capture rather than kill them.

The men below were forcing the thrashing griffins down by main strength.

Many of the men were pointing at them and laughing in great peals.

To Fanaroh, who was openly furious at the mockery, Coutos almost shouted:

"Lord Fanaroh! ……We have lost five — do you intend to press on past this?"

"You bastard——"

"Calm yourself. ……First, the report. We have learned they have a guard against griffins. The loss of five is a heavy blow, but if we stop here, we may at least answer to the warrior chief as a scouting mission."

Fanaroh kept his anger plain, and clicked his tongue.

*Going*, he said, and flew off toward the Dragon's Maw, while cheers rose from below.

To have lost five griffins, the symbol of Kreisharana, and on top of that two captured — even Fanaroh would surely be raged at.

He would not be killed, but punishment was a certainty.

While debating whether to plead for him a little or simply look on, Coutos gazed below.

That stone wall, and that strange wooden contraption that had picked off griffins gliding at full speed.

And that Bazarishe, who bound so many warriors and made them follow her.

These were not the same as the tribal coalitions Kreisharana had once crushed.

The warriors of Kreisharana, who ruled the sky.

And the crimson banners raised by the warriors opposite them, in the device of swords driven into heaven.

Not ignorant fools puffed up with pride.

Knowing, understanding, with all that — they meant to turn their swords on the lords of the sky.

Watching the blades shining in the sunlight, he felt complicated emotions dancing in his chest.

In the end Fanaroh was abused by the warrior chief Dotrias before all the warriors, and was assigned for the next year to the care of the Dragon's Maw.

Leaving the Dragon's Maw without permission was forbidden — effectively house arrest.

Naturally, he was also forbidden from joining in raids on settlements and the like.

Inwardly displeased, Fanaroh nonetheless had no choice but to obey, since the order came from the warrior chief.

Defiance toward a higher-ranked warrior was effectively a sentence of execution.

If one defied a man who wielded authority and earned his displeasure, what was ordered was a duel.

To be killed outright would be the lesser fate.

There were cases in which, during a duel, the victor would cut off the loser's hands and feet so that he could never wield a sword again.

In Kreisharana, no value is recognised in a man who cannot fight.

If it had been done in war that would be one thing, but to spare a man's life in a duel and instead deliberately cut off his limbs is essentially making an example of him.

And the warrior chief Dotrias had several such precedents.

All but one of those men had chosen suicide within half a year, having had both hands and one foot cut off; the one who had been forbidden even that was kept alive years on.

For a warrior who had once tasted the heights, the suffering of being cast down to the lowest place is beyond words.

The one survivor was a man once known as one of the great warriors, robust and strong, but he was no longer a shadow of his former self, and could only beg Dotrias for the mercy of being killed.

His pride had long since broken; men said he would couple even with beasts on command — and watching him, Dotrias always laughed pleasantly.

『*Even with his limbs gone, I cannot bring myself to put my own hand on a warrior who once met me as a fighter.*』

So he would say, all show and no mercy.

Overwhelming strength and cruelty.

The reason for the duel had been a small back-talk to Dotrias.

That having dovetailed with his own desire to make his power absolute, the result was that he had reduced one of the great warriors to livestock kept alive.

No one would think to defy a man like that.

It was the unspoken understanding that the great chieftain too would say nothing of such *discipline* by the warrior chief.

The warrior chief was the next great chieftain — for the sake of his old age, the great chieftain favoured the warrior chief, and the warrior chief, too, feared the great chieftain's enormous power, and whatever he thought, he kept up appearances.

But that the warrior chief was the kind of person who could calmly kill one or two great warriors was certain, and the present holder of the office, Dotrias, was outstanding even among them.

Even the great warrior Fanaroh, faced with a single furious glance from him, could only tremble from head to toe and grovel.

For Kreisharana, which had known only skirmishes since most settlements had sworn submission, this was the first great war in long years to be carved into history.

And to lose five warriors and have two griffins captured on a mere scouting mission was a blunder that smeared mud on the face of the Dotrias who had been entrusted with this campaign.

By rights, death would have been unavoidable.

But the information that the enemy possessed strange contraptions for downing griffins was substantial — the loss was, ultimately, a cheap one — and Fanaroh had escaped with his life.

That was the result of Coutos lending him a passing word in his defence.

It was not that Coutos felt any particular pity for Fanaroh's life.

He simply thought he wanted to see what the other side was after.

Together with the voices that grew stronger by the day, she returned to the fortress with a smile.

With two attendants, she headed to her work-room.

* * *

"Hmph, Alberan-special honey-tea is delicious, isn't it, Belnaich."

On the sofa, peering from the side at the face of Belnaich, who was sourly sipping his tea — Bazarishe.

Mature for one called a girl, yet still too young to be called a woman.

Her body of five *shaku* and two *sun* was slender and yet drew curves; the limbs that swung a sword were thin.

The face that wore a smile was, beyond any doubt, a face beloved of the goddess of beauty — to a degree that left no room to question her status as the child of the gods.

"I thought you might come today, but my prediction was off."

"Do not ignore me."

She wrapped her jewel-like violet eyes with long lashes and pursed her lips at him as if glaring.

The fine man across from them — Mitskronia, with his cultivated moustache — smiled wryly.

"That well-trained one…… reinforcements seem to have arrived, but if she is not coming today, it looks as though they will offer up sacrifices for several more days."

"The great warrior Fanaroh, was it? — the present commander seems even more spineless than I imagined. He has not the spirit even to make a decision. If the great chieftain himself comes out at this rate, all the better."

From the Dragon's Maw to Kreisharana's main settlements, by griffin one could arrive by sunset.

Depending, the great chieftain might fly out tomorrow and be in command the day after.

"From what we hear, he is a very impatient man. Won't he come and offer us a field battle?"

"He cannot possibly be that dull. Rotten as he is, he is the great chieftain who binds many settlements; a mere strong-arm fool could not fill the office."

"Stop looking at me as though *I* were a fool. I am a thousand times wiser than Belnaich."

Bazarishe spoke discontentedly, and Mitskronia hurried to soothe her.

They had left three thousand to the rear and were marching with twenty-two thousand.

According to information drawn from captured warriors, the force originally stationed at the Dragon's Maw was about six hundred griffin-cavalry and roughly two thousand from subordinate tribes.

From there they would have scrambled to gather warriors, but not enough to reach twenty thousand.

And the enemy did not have logistics established to the degree of supplying that many men gathered all at once.

Before this battle they had built many wagons and made preparations to mobilise carriages and ox-carts.

Their action had begun after laying in sufficient provisions in advance.

The opposing side had taken griffin-cavalry to be sufficient and made no provision for large-scale lines.

Even squeezing what they could from each settlement, they would be short of everything.

At the most, they were perhaps a little over ten thousand at present.

To press a field battle against twice their number would be folly.

"Even so, it is not absolutely impossible."

"……?"

"If the great chieftain, who has not seen the developments thus far, comes out, then even after hearing the tale he will tend to underestimate. In a fight wholly inside the mountains, he cannot fully use the griffin-cavalry. Bringing forces down to the foothills and fighting at the edge of the forest is within possibility. He may want to deploy his griffin-cavalry to their fullest."

Belnaich folded his arms; Bazarishe tilted her head and considered.

"If we were them, we would lure us into terrain where tribal cooperation is possible, and slip the griffin-cavalry in through gaps in the trees. Poor visibility, mountains they know — the geographic advantage is theirs. They would think of arrangements to use it to the full and aim for piecemeal annihilation."

The line formation was a hastily-acquired skill. On flat ground he was confident in employing it, but on rough mountain terrain matching pace and maintaining control would be hard.

He could give detailed instructions and adjust, but it would not be perfect.

There would inevitably be disorder.

If the Kreisharana warriors, who could just barely band together, were going to be used, then setting up positions on that assumption would be sound.

Arrangements such that, when one group clashed with the enemy, another could strike from flank and rear.

If the griffin-cavalry were properly deployed and given orders from the air, they could easily seize the heights, the flanks, and the rear, and would have an overwhelming advantage.

If used in concentrated form, throwing in several hundred griffin-cavalry could aim at collapse.

The other side waiting; ours advancing.

The slope's momentum could not be used.

Numbers aside, far from being to our advantage, it was rather a disadvantage.

The griffin-cavalry — elite who could descend anywhere — was a major presence.

"But it is doubtful whether an amateur in army warfare would think of that. To restore the prestige of the griffin-cavalry, the chieftain would normally think to show their results in a form many can see. ……The griffin-cavalry are without doubt being looked down on by the subordinate-tribe warriors defending the Dragon's Maw."

"……I see. Psychology, then."

"Yes. I think the two of you, being what you are, think of things too rationally. But human beings are foolish; even when they know which is the right path, they do not easily walk it."

Predicting the enemy's actions was, of course, important.

To enumerate every possibility and weigh measures against all of them is the ideal.

But depending on the case, it was a wasted preparation.

"Against an amateur, feints are unnecessary; just cut and be done. Rather than trying to be clever, plain methods are sometimes the right answer. ……From their movements so far, I do not think this enemy is sharp enough to play any backhand."

"Well, we know they are quite stupid…… counting reinforcements, we have confirmed eight hundred and twelve griffins. Three hundred and thirty-two have already been processed. If they come at us in the way you describe, I should like to shave off at least another two hundred."

"Just so. If we whittle away the griffin-cavalry, Kreisharana is no more than a band of fallen warriors. From what we hear and have seen, replacements cannot easily be made."

The griffins had a fairly fierce temperament.

And they prized strongly the bond with their owners.

While double-riding might be tolerated, they hated letting another warrior take their reins, and indeed they thrashed about furiously.

Twelve had been captured alive and were being fed, but no one had yet managed to tame any.

Belnaich drained his tea and stood up.

"There's nothing in particular left to discuss. I'll go check on the griffins."

"I shall come too. Hurry up and tame them. I want to fly through the sky soon as well."

"Tame them yourself."

He said it in an exasperated tone, and behind him, *toto-to-to*, Bazarishe trotted along.

Mitskronia smiled wryly, watching the merry retreating figure, and closed his eyes.

"……Your child laughs happily today as well."

*The day I lay down my sword is not far*, he continued quietly, and brought the honey-fragrant tea to his lips.
