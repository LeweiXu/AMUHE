# Chapter 166: Hospitality
 
The dining event was held not in the dining room but in the drawing room.
 
The sofas and other furniture inside had been temporarily moved to another room, and the cold floorboards were covered wall-to-wall with carpets.
 
Today, in deference to the Kreisharana, the meal would be taken seated directly on the floor.
 
It was Bery who had suggested that the first step in showing welcome should be to accommodate their guests' culture.
 
Kreschenta had said anything was fine, and so the proposal was adopted in full; the food, too, leaned more toward rustic country fare than delicate cuisine.
 
The pumpkin soup was, of course, non-negotiable. Rich beef stew simmered until meltingly tender, a light salt-based seafood soup using fish and shellfish procured from Arna.
 
Three whole roast chickens.
 
Brined overnight with herbs and salt, basted with butter during roasting.
 
The amber-glossed chickens were like jewels――indeed, like amber itself.
 
Their bellies were stuffed with vivid, colorful ingredients, and a rich aroma wafted from within.
 
Fat-laced bone-in thighs were arranged in a circle on platters as if adorning them.
 
Cheese, bacon, and other fillings were piled high on flat plates for making sandwiches, and skewered meats――chicken, pork, lamb, and beef――bristled like a mountain of pins.
 
――Truly, this was a warrior's feast.
 
So primal it was almost raw, an absolute parade of meat.
 
Vegetables were relegated to the soups, with salads serving as a modest garnish at best; the undisputed stars were the meats.
 
"Wow……amazing……"
 
Lila, guided by Elvena, couldn't help but exclaim, then covered her mouth in embarrassment.
 
Vinsreel, who followed, and the warriors too showed astonishment in their eyes.
 
A dinner party at the queen's invitation. And the queen herself had taken up the kitchen knife, they were told.
 
Their anxiety about what excessively refined dishes might appear vanished in an instant, and the heat of meat filling the room made saliva well up and stomachs growl.
 
There was an overwhelming gap in culinary technique, but the food was close to what would be served at a Kreisharana feast.
 
Carpets covered every inch of the floor, and the tables were low――clearly consideration for guests who sat directly on the ground rather than using chairs.
 
The queen herself was already seated on the floor.
 
They were well aware that the people of the kingdom used chairs, and they understood that sitting directly on the floor was considered 'uncouth' in their culture.
 
For the leader of a great nation to accommodate them in this way.
 
In terms of welcome, it was utterly unlike what they had received from any of the nobles they had visited along the way; it was, truly, heartfelt hospitality.
 
The journey here had not been unpleasant, thanks to the efforts of their guide, but it hadn't been entirely free of unpleasantness either.
 
Their appearance was likely part of it.
 
Few had looked kindly on the Kreisharana walking about in furs, and some nobles had paid lip service to welcome while regarding them with the contempt one might show the unclean.
 
To the kingdom, the Kreisharana were mere barbarians.
 
Mountain-dwelling, uncouth savages from the countryside.
 
Of course, they hadn't expected to be sincerely welcomed from the start, and had even accepted that it might be the case.
 
The Kreisharana's loyalty was directed at the dragon's covenant-bearer――Krische, not the kingdom.
 
They should end the state of severed relations because she lived in this country; whether or not to form friendship with the kingdom itself wasn't strictly necessary.
 
It would be perfectly fine if the relationship lasted only for Krische's lifetime.
 
Many thought so, and naturally, some among those present here felt the same.
 
Even so, the events in the audience chamber and this reception were shifting their impressions somewhat.
 
At the very least, the queen's words in the audience hall had been honest; this consideration and thoughtfulness proved it.
 
This country's queen was different from those they had met.
 
The way she sat shoulder to shoulder with Krische on her left, looking affectionate, was also different from her appearance in the audience chamber――she looked like nothing more than a sweet, innocent girl.
 
That softness in atmosphere was surely deliberate.
 
She was tacitly conveying that this was nothing more than a casual dinner party, nothing stiff or formal.
 
Seated to her right was another girl-like figure with brilliant golden hair and striking beauty.
 
She was conversing with the sinister-faced old man and another long-haired old man, and noticing them, gave a light nod.
 
Further to Krische's left sat a large-framed man――clearly a seasoned warrior.
 
A face like a crag, his military uniform stretched taut; his mere presence, just sitting there, was imposing.
 
No ordinary warrior, surely.
 
The man observed them with a scrutinizing, almost glaring look, and――
 
"Nyan-nyan, your expression is scary. Why are you glaring at the guests?"
 
"N-no, it's just……warrior's instinct, so to speak."
 
――was scolded by Krische.
 
The warriors were guided to their seats one by one from the back, with Lila and Vinsreel at the head, and once everyone was seated, Kreschenta clapped her hands together.
 
She rose and began with a brief toast.
 
After confirming that everyone had been served drinks, she kept the formalities short, then raised her goblet.
 
Though the customs of toasting differed in the details, the basics were the same.
 
They exchanged the gesture of the cups, and the queen drained what could scarcely be called wine――mostly fruit juice――and if the host of the feast did so, the guests could hardly do otherwise.
 
The warriors gulped their drinks heartily, while Krische alone sipped at hers, watching the scene.
 
The beginning of the feast was understandably cautious.
 
Exchanging impressions of the food, sharing stories of the journey to the capital.
 
Surrounded by warriors and looking thoroughly uncomfortable at the far end was the guide Argoush Giterns, inwardly trembling while drinking wine that tasted of nothing.
 
He was their guide and Anne's father.
 
It was Krische who had suggested inviting him while they were at it, and so he too had been called to the queen's feast. Outwardly wearing a dignified noble's smile, he was drenched in cold sweat behind it.
 
He was a marquis, yes.
 
His noble rank was by no means low, but in practical terms, he was simply a noble managing a rural district――in other words, a provincial administrator.
 
Fundamentally different from the capital nobles who shaped the nation and its politics; if possible, he would have preferred to live quietly in his assigned territory.
 
The Giterns family had long maintained peace and continued its bloodline precisely because they stayed uninvolved with the royal court and remained detached from politics.
 
For such an Argoush, being invited to the queen's feast was, in itself, a stomach-churning affair.
 
"The climate and daily life were different, and it would be a lie to say there were no inconveniences……but thanks to Lord Giterns, we wanted for nothing. He arranged blankets for us when we underestimated the cold, prepared thick tents for us……he saw to our meals, our daily needs, everything. Without him, it would not have been such an enjoyable journey."
 
Lila and Vinsreel praised Argoush to the skies.
 
Their gratitude seemed genuine.
 
They were heaping accolades on his service before his liege, Queen Kreschenta, all but saying *please reward him*.
 
Kreschenta tilted her golden hair, gleaming red, and turned her violet eyes toward Argoush.
 
"Is that so? I'm glad to hear he performed well despite not being a formal guide, Marquis Giterns. I shall have to give him a reward later."
 
"Y-yes, Your Majesty! I merely did what was expected of me as a subject of Your Majesty. I only fulfilled my duty; such special consideration is more than I deserve."
 
"The Kreisharana are to become friends of the kingdom alongside Arna. For them to heap such praise on you, it would reflect poorly on me to give nothing in return. Please accept graciously."
 
It was a truly beautiful smile.
 
Some kind of commanding presence――what might be called authority――emanated from that small body.
 
Or perhaps it was what one would call the bearing of a ruler.
 
Something formless, possessed only by those born to reign above others.
 
Though beautiful in appearance, she was little more than a child――yet no one who heard she ruled the kingdom could doubt it.
 
She had that sort of mysterious air about her.
 
"There may not be much glamour in the kingdom's northwest, but I am aware that you are faithfully watching over your territory. Tax revenues have been gradually increasing since your tenure, improving markedly after the road improvements……You seem to be doing quite well. ……I understand you're now looking to develop livestock farming. How is that progressing?"
 
Argoush froze.
 
The queen spoke as though she were reading from a report.
 
The northwest was rural even by the kingdom's standards, with nothing in the way of notable specialties.
 
He had indeed turned his attention to livestock farming as a new industry, but they had only reached the stage of bringing in experts and surveying suitable land――nothing practical had actually begun.
 
He submitted reports to the kingdom each season, but they were mostly columns of numbers.
 
They were certainly not the sort of thing the queen would pore over, and unless someone analyzed the numbers with such a purpose in mind, there should be no way to guess what Argoush was attempting.
 
It was also unlikely she had looked into him just because he would be at the feast.
 
His attendance had been decided practically moments ago.
 
Argoush glanced at the person beside the queen――the silver-haired girl gnawing on a bone-in thigh, watching him.
 
The dragon's covenant-bearer, the hero of the civil war.
 
What they shared in common was ability beyond their years and jewel-like violet eyes.
 
*――Could it be that this queen has a grasp of the fiscal situation and every minute movement across the entire kingdom?*
 
His suspicion was already near certainty.
 
"S-so far, everything is proceeding smoothly……we've found several suitable candidate sites and are in the process of recruiting livestock farmers. We plan to start from there and expand gradually."
 
While answering, he desperately searched his mind for anything problematic in the reports sent to the kingdom.
 
He didn't think there was, but this opponent made his stomach ache regardless.
 
"I see. I hope it goes well."
 
She was Queen Kreschenta――a being on an entirely different level.
 
Considering that a single whim could cost him his head, there could be nothing but tension.
 
"The northwest will also serve as a gateway to the Kreisharana going forward, so shall I send some nobles to assist you? You seem short on personnel for what you're trying to accomplish……Please don't overwork yourself."
 
"Y-Your Majesty, I am deeply grateful for such consideration……"
 
"It would be troubling if a capable person like you collapsed from overwork. That would be a loss for the kingdom."
 
Argoush bowed deeply from his seated position to express his gratitude, and Anne, who had been behind Kreschenta, begged her permission and then ran to her father's side, beaming, moved by the sight of him being praised so highly.
 
She stationed herself beside him with a wine pitcher at the ready.
 
"Here, please, Marquis Giterns."
 
"……You, honestly."
 
Sighing at the sight of his daughter's radiant smile, he accepted the wine.
 
He had mountains of things to say to her, but she appeared to be doing well here, at least.
 
Relieved by her healthy appearance, Argoush smiled.
 
The dinner party, though laden with diplomatic intent, avoided the main topic for a while.
 
The food being so excellent was likely part of the reason.
 
Then, once the wine had loosened tongues just slightly, it was Kokys――who had been speaking with Vinsreel under the watchful gazes of Gallen and Elrouga――who broached the subject.
 
"Still, even for a nation of warriors, that's a remarkable number of seasoned fighters. They appear to be the finest elite even by Kreisharana standards……Lord Vinsreel, what is the meaning of this?"
 
For a mere envoy, the numbers were far too large.
 
Surely they hadn't worried about being waylaid by bandits while mounted on griffins that could fly.
 
At Kokys's words, the atmosphere shifted.
 
The warriors lowered their voices, and Vinsreel wore a faint smile.
 
"It somehow feels itchy to be spoken to so formally by a warrior as evidently formidable as yourself, Agrand-dono. Please, use your normal speech."
 
Kokys looked to Kreschenta.
 
"Today is merely a dinner party. If Vinsreel-sama says so, I don't mind either. Matters between warriors I leave to you."
 
"My thanks, Your Majesty."
 
He bowed deeply from his seat, then turned his gaze back to Vinsreel.
 
"Truth be told, this stiff way of talking doesn't sit well with me. I appreciate it. But if that's how it is, then it should go both ways. It'd be odd if only I spoke casually to a guest, Vinsreel-dono."
 
"That's my line. ……Unlike Lila, I'm not so confident with the lowland tongue. When it comes to serious talk like this, I'm always afraid of getting the wrong nuance. You've saved me."
 
"Good to hear, but……no, I'm genuinely impressed at how fluent you are. I can barely manage a greeting in another language, so I honestly respect it."
 
Kokys spoke with a sigh, poured wine into Vinsreel's cup, and filled his own as well.
 
They each took their cups, clinked them, and Kokys prompted with his eyes.
 
"We heard from Krische-sama that the kingdom faces an unavoidable invasion by other nations……what's the real situation?"
 
"What you heard is accurate. We're being targeted from the east, west, and south by three great powers of comparable strength."
 
Kokys spoke, moistening his tongue with wine.
 
"They'll likely come at us in coordination. Even conservatively, the situation is precarious. I have no doubt we'll win, but……"
 
His gaze drifted to Krische.
 
Krische's cheeks were flushed from the wine, utterly indifferent to such talk.
 
She was tugging at Bery's hand, trying to seat her between herself and Kreschenta.
 
The beautiful red-haired servant wore a troubled expression, mindful of the atmosphere, and was whispering admonishments like *you mustn't, really*, though ultimately she obeyed her beloved mistress and sat down.
 
Krische entwined her arm with Bery's, the very picture of contentment. Alberinea, in high spirits.
 
It was hardly a convincing sight for claiming *with her here, there's nothing to worry about*.
 
Kokys rubbed the bridge of his nose.
 
"Hah, pay it no mind. I understand Krische-sama's abilities."
 
"Ha……I'm grateful for that. Still……it's true that some in the military are losing their nerve. Ordinarily, the situation is bad enough that one would think there's no chance of winning."
 
He drained his wine and sighed, and Vinsreel, seeing this, refilled his cup.
 
Kokys thanked him and continued.
 
"Diplomatically speaking, we expect hostilities to begin in about half a year, but there's a risk it may come sooner. From the thaw of spring onward, we're preparing our forces on the assumption that the enemy could move at any time."
 
In fact, the odds of that were quite high, though he didn't state the reason aloud here.
 
The dragon had visited the capital――that rumor was spreading and had surely reached the three nations already.
 
It could be used as a pretext for war in any number of ways.
 
"I see, so it was indeed wise to come early……"
 
Vinsreel rested a hand on his chin, narrowing his keen eyes.
 
"I understand the kingdom's situation in broad terms. However, we cannot simply pledge our full cooperation all at once. The rift between the kingdom and the Kreisharana runs deep……many on our side do not wish for friendship with the kingdom."
 
He drained his cup and gazed at the empty vessel.
 
Though his words were addressed to Kokys, they were really meant for Kreschenta and the others to hear.
 
Kokys was simply there to start the conversation.
 
Both parties were aware of this.
 
"At the very least, the majority opinion on our side is that we should avoid a situation where the Kreisharana themselves are exposed to hostility. That we should not put ourselves on the front lines."
 
"Naturally. There's no benefit for the Kreisharana in it."
 
Kreschenta spoke while having Bery pour her fruit juice.
 
She already understood the gist from those words alone.
 
"But you cannot simply stand by while Onee-sama is in dire straits. ……The pretense would be military exchange and observation, I take it?"
 
Vinsreel's eyes widened, then he smiled wryly.
 
Krische tilted her head, looking between her sister and Vinsreel, and Kreschenta smiled.
 
"What you're after is a just cause for entering the war. You'd be dealing with ill-mannered foreigners who invaded the kingdom without a thought for the Kreisharana who were there for an exchange――is that it?"
 
"Precisely. What we need is a situation that leaves our people with no choice but to accept, Your Majesty."
 
Making the Kreisharana warriors shed blood for Alberan.
 
Naturally, many would object to that.
 
Prudent judgment would dictate staying uninvolved――even for Krische's sake, plunging headlong into this war and staking the Kreisharana's very survival was foolish.
 
But not everything should be decided by profit and loss alone.
 
"There are those who object to lending aid to the kingdom, but if the Kreisharana themselves were to be slighted, that is another matter entirely. Our presence here is, in essence, to create that justification."
 
"How grateful……but are you sure? It's not my wish to drag the Kreisharana, who have been living in peace, into this."
 
She had heard the Kreisharana's population was roughly that of a town.
 
While she could certainly use every hand she could get, it would be somewhat troublesome if the Kreisharana were destroyed.
 
Not only mining rights for the mana crystals, but she eventually wanted to breed their griffins on a large scale within the kingdom.
 
If that expertise were lost, it would be a significant setback.
 
If many of them died in the war, the Kreisharana would have no choice but to rely on the kingdom, which would eliminate much of the friction regarding mining in the future――but that wasn't an urgent matter.
 
Their willingness to join the fight was a welcome development in the current situation.
 
But if they chose to stay out of it entirely, that too would be perfectly acceptable at heart.
 
"Peace and tranquility are to be cherished, but before all else, we are warriors. Those who worship martial prowess and devote their lives to nothing but the refinement of their bodies. ……And the brave figure of Krische-sama, who demonstrated her martial might single-handedly before the holy spirit, is still burned into these eyes."
 
Vinsreel said, looking at Krische.
 
"To fight on equal terms with the holy spirit despite our interference, and to become its covenant-bearer. A figure truly worthy of respect, the very image of a warrior to aspire to――having witnessed such a thing, to sit back in fear of losing our peace and tranquility would be contrary to our very way of being. To overlook it would mean losing our pride as warriors."
 
Krische looked at Vinsreel with a troubled expression, then glanced toward Selene.
 
Selene returned a look that said, exasperated, *just give up*.
 
"Though our strength is humble, we stake it on this pride. Our numbers are few, and I won't claim each of us is worth a thousand, but each will do the work of ten, perhaps a hundred. If it would not be a burden, please allow us to fight alongside the kingdom. Let this be a token of enduring friendship hereafter――Kreisharana Chieftain Alkirens, too, wishes for this."
 
Vinsreel bowed his head, and the warriors watched.
 
Kreschenta looked at him with inward exasperation.
 
No political maneuvering, no games.
 
Straightforward and simple――when a warrior with muscles where his brain should be was given the helm, nations surely ended up like this.
 
"Please raise your head, Vinsreel-sama. It is we who should be making the request. Conservatively speaking, this is a crisis of national survival――for you to offer your strength to an Alberan in such dire straits, we could never refuse. ……I swear on my name that I will never forget this kindness."
 
That said, having an easy-to-manage group on her side was perhaps welcome.
 
At the very least, they could serve as a deterrent against the west――Elderant, and the uninformed masses, unaware that the Kreisharana's national strength was that of a single town, would surely celebrate the support enthusiastically.
 
A whole nation lending its strength, even in name only, produced an effect beyond its actual value.
 
She couldn't expect much from them as a fighting force, but just the morale impact was meaningful enough, and in terms of easing the general public's anxiety, there was value.
 
Kreschenta did not doubt her sister's victory, but if it could ease her sister's burden even slightly and reduce the postwar strain, weighing the risks, it wasn't a bad idea.
 
"Thank you. ……That said, the Kreisharana are now scarcely a small nation. We cannot act on a large scale, and our cooperation will essentially be limited to the kingdom's west――against Elderant."
 
"That is more than enough. Simply diverting Elderant's attention would be a great boon."
 
The mountain range extending from the Albyagel stretched far to the west, bordering the northern reaches of Elderant.
 
If the Kreisharana entered the war, Elderant would have to consider the possibility of their rear being threatened. Concentrating solely on a main offensive under those conditions would be difficult.
 
For their national strength, even that modest contribution was more than enough.
 
Vinsreel nodded.
 
"Our purpose is as I stated: creating a pretext."
 
He looked back at Kokys.
 
"――But as you suspected, Agrand-dono. What we have here are the Kreisharana's handpicked elite. Their purpose is to serve as Krische-sama's escort throughout the war, attending her and fighting as her blade."
 
Every Kreisharana warrior present had volunteered.
 
Some revered Krische as a warrior who could stand against even a holy spirit; others were interested in warfare on the plains. Their reasons varied, but naturally, every one of them was among the very finest the Kreisharana had to offer.
 
Few as they were, Vinsreel had confidence in them precisely because of that.
 
"Against a battlefield of tens of thousands, thirty riders may seem trivial, but every one here is an experienced griffin rider. We have no intention of falling behind even a thousand men. ……I am confident we will be of service."
 
Vinsreel stated flatly, and Kokys laughed with amusement.
 
"Bold words. Yet the fact that they don't sound like empty boasts is what makes them most reassuring."
 
"It's an honor to be so assessed by a warrior like you. If you're willing, I'd like to request a sparring match sometime for the sake of exchange."
 
"By all means. I was just thinking of asking the same."
 
Kokys poured wine into Vinsreel's empty cup as he spoke.
 
They clinked cups――and watching this, Krische, still clinging to Bery's arm, spoke up.
 
"Hmm……Is it really alright? Krische still thinks peace is best, and you don't have to force yourselves to help……Krische feels a bit bad about dragging you in."
 
"At this point, it is no longer solely for Krische-sama's sake. It could be called somewhat drastic, but fighting side by side is not a bad way to bridge the gap with the kingdom. ……Having experienced the journey here, at least I believe that. The others feel the same."
 
Vinsreel looked toward the tense Argoush and smiled.
 
The relationship between the two nations was not a good one.
 
But looking at individuals, one by one, it wasn't so.
 
Small courtesies, hospitality like this――if they took each of those things in turn, such ill feelings would surely dissipate in time.
 
"Outward beauty alone does not reach even a holy spirit. ……Strip away the garment of nationhood, look without preconceptions, and we are the same people, able to drink and laugh together. As Her Majesty said, Krische-sama is the catalyst. ……We have simply decided to take up our spears for the future of both the kingdom and the Kreisharana. There is no need for Krische-sama to feel troubled."
 
Krische looked at Lila.
 
Lila smiled and nodded, echoing Vinsreel's words.
 
"It is as my brother says. ……Hospitality like this carries the heart within it――we were truly, sincerely grateful."
 
Every dish laid out was easy to eat for Kreisharana people unaccustomed to knives and forks; they had even removed chairs and covered the floor with carpets so everyone could sit directly.
 
The truth lay in the care given so naturally, and it held great value.
 
"This is not being dragged in, but our own wish to stand alongside the kingdom in the future. From the beginning, we were given the discretion to decide. ……It simply took this form as a result, and whatever the outcome, there is truly no reason for Krische-sama to worry."
 
"……Is that how it is?"
 
"Yes, that is how it is."
 
Krische nodded ambiguously, looking half-convinced, and turned to Bery beside her.
 
Bery, too, said nothing, nodding quietly and smiling at her with obvious happiness.

# Chapter 167: Cross-Cultural Exchange
 
On the thin blanket of snow.
 
Before the watching eyes of several hundred soldiers, two men fought in the center.
 
Over six shaku tall and powerfully built.
 
In military uniform――the large man wielded his spear with a finesse surprising for his frame.
 
With the slightest motion, he deflected and evaded his opponent's spear, launched strikes that would be lethal against any ordinary fighter, and sometimes deliberately created openings to invite attacks and counter.
 
A heavy white-ash battle spear of eight shaku, made from argana wood.
 
The sound of it cutting the wind was like a club being swung, and his thrusts punched holes through the air like arrows――and when swung broadly, even the shaft became a weapon.
 
Behind it all lay solid theoretical construction; everything existed within a fully calculated framework of technique.
 
Yet the tall, handsome man facing him was a warrior of no lesser caliber.
 
Wielding a spear of the same eight shaku with equal mastery, his movements were flowing, dreamlike.
 
Like water tracing its way through rock crevices, like wind racing through gaps in the trees.
 
His spear, stripped of all waste, slipped past blocks and through his opponent's guard.
 
He continued evading thrusts that would have been killing blows against any other, and still had room to return attacks.
 
On a windless day, this spot alone raged like a storm.
 
Powder snow whirled up, and the trails of both spears were traced in silver.
 
The kingdom's finest warrior, Kokys Agrand.
 
Kreisharana's finest warrior, Vinsreel Sharana.
 
Both displaying their superhuman technique without reserve, for over half a quarter-hour.
 
It was the larger man――Kokys――whose spear first shed its fighting intent.
 
"Well now, I had an unconscious contempt, thinking it was ultimately just a spear that hadn't known real battle. At this rate, a match between us would never reach a conclusion. We'd just exhaust ourselves trading blows."
 
"So it seems. ……A match is still a match; I meant to win. But to break through your defense, I clearly need more training."
 
Vinsreel likewise lowered his stance, shouldered his spear, and spoke with a note of exasperation.
 
Against anyone other than an outlier like Krische, he should have been able to win with room to spare.
 
But that thinking, too, had been arrogance.
 
Kokys Agrand's spearwork evoked the seasoned mastery of Vinsreel's own father, Chieftain Alkirens, yet coupled it with a raw, surging ferocity.
 
Vinsreel had been fighting at full capacity within the bounds of a sparring match, but he could not claim to have pressed Kokys into a corner.
 
"You call it contempt, but those words are mine to say, Kokys-dono. ……I thought of the people of the plains, accustomed to comfort――that Krische-sama alone was special. But to find a warrior like you in the kingdom."
 
Vinsreel addressed Kokys by his given name.
 
In Kreisharana custom, when addressing a warrior worthy of respect, it was proper to use the personal name rather than the family name.
 
"Had you been born among the Kreisharana, it would not be strange for you to hold the rank of warrior chief. It makes one appreciate just how wide the world is."
 
"Being praised so directly even I feel a bit bashful."
 
Kokys scratched his head with a wry smile.
 
"Heh, though I must say, standing around praising each other like this feels a bit awkward. Let's leave it here."
 
"Indeed. More than words can say has already been conveyed."
 
Vinsreel showed a masculine smile on his handsome face and approached.
 
Kokys strode toward him and extended his right hand.
 
"Kokys-dono, your prowess speaks for itself. As a friend to fight alongside under Krische-sama, I could ask for no better."
 
"The feeling is mutual. Meeting a warrior of your caliber is a true joy, Vinsreel-dono. I look forward to a long friendship, honing our spears together."
 
Their hands clasped, and cheers erupted around them.
 
Among the kingdom's central nobility――the court nobles――the Kreisharana warriors were poorly received. But in the military, where martial prowess and honor were revered, it was different.
 
From ancient times, a warrior's strength commanded respect that transcended rank and station.
 
When it came to warriors worthy of the name "worth a thousand," few would be foolish enough to deride them as barbarians.
 
A warrior who had matched Kokys, arguably the kingdom's finest after Krische.
 
Vinsreel was, at the very least, someone many soldiers felt deserving of respect.
 
Kokys clasped Vinsreel's shoulder and bellowed for all to hear.
 
"Kreisharana's foremost warrior, Vinsreel Sharana, is every bit the equal of me, Kokys Agrand, as you have all seen! To insult this man and his warriors is to insult me, who crossed spears with him! Bear that in mind!"
 
Then, as usual, he turned toward the VIP seats where Krische was sipping tea and watching――or rather, toward Selene, seated between Krische and Lila.
 
Seeing Selene nod, Kokys invited Vinsreel toward those seats and addressed the soldiers.
 
"From here on, it's an exchange session. Anyone who fancies themselves skilled, step forward. From what I've seen, the Kreisharana warriors are all formidable even apart from Lord Vinsreel. If you underestimate them, you'll be the ones left red-faced. Give it your all."
 
At his voice, a kingdom warrior with confidence in his arms stepped forward.
 
A man from Kokys's Second Corps.
 
A Kreisharana warrior also came forward――and watching the scene, Vinsreel sat in the prepared chair and exchanged cups with Kokys, taking the offered wine.
 
"Thanks to you, we got to see a fine match, Lord Vinsreel."
 
"Thank you, Marshal Christand."
 
"Exceptions aside, Kokys is the kingdom's finest by any measure. For a warrior's nation, yes, but to find someone in those mountains who can match him……"
 
Selene spoke with an air of amazement, spreading her hands.
 
In terms of population, the Kreisharana were likely below one-thousandth.
 
No matter how much of a warrior nation the Kreisharana might be, it was still surprising that their warrior chief was close to Kokys, who stood at the kingdom's apex.
 
It wasn't as though Kokys had gone easy to let him shine.
 
As a matter of fact, Vinsreel stood in a domain extremely close to Kokys.
 
Vinsreel laughed and shook his head.
 
"It was only a sparring match. A true duel to the death would be a different story."
 
He looked past Kokys to the great war-spear of solid steel held by his adjutant standing behind him.
 
Kokys was in light military uniform, using the same standard-issue spear as the soldiers.
 
For a sparring match, he hadn't been in his optimal state.
 
"If you donned full armor and wielded that spear, no matter how I look at it, the loss would be mine. Even winning the earlier match, I'd only barely have a chance at victory――in a straight-up fight, it wouldn't be even, but my defeat. I still have much to work on."
 
"If we're talking about a real fight, your side fights in light gear meant for griffin-back combat. This setup favors me, since I fight on foot from the start. If we went all out for real, the outcome would still be uncertain."
 
If fighting in full armor, Kokys would win.
 
He could force in aggressively using the armor as a shield and brute-force the decision.
 
A horse was merely transportation.
 
In the kingdom, mana users fought primarily on foot; their heavy armament and martial techniques were designed around that premise.
 
Whether the opponent were Norzan or Gildanstein instead of Kokys, the outcome likely wouldn't change, but Kreisharana warriors fought with an emphasis on mounted combat from griffinback.

From the moment of Anne's birth.

How she had come to walk the path of a servant, and how she had arrived at the present day.

It was as though a bard were narrating a heroic saga — oddly grandiose, with needlessly convoluted phrasing.

Bery felt a twinge of regret, but offered appropriate responses as she listened.

"——Her beauty aside, Argan-sama was a woman who embodied every quality a servant should possess, the very image of my ideal. I felt as though I had been struck by lightning on a cheerful, sunny day——"

"Th-thank you……I-I understand how you feel, so perhaps you could stop there……"

"No! This carries extremely important significance. Please, hear me out."

The sections in which Anne heaped beautiful praise upon Bery and detoured to extol her were something she would rather have done without, but apparently these were quite important within Anne's narrative.

What amounted to a half-torture session — 'Anne's Greatest Hits: The Argan Collection' — lasted a full quarter-hour.

By the time Bery could no longer lift her face from sheer embarrassment, Anne finally passed through that section and reached the main topic.

Krische had begun softly snoring near the start and was sleeping happily in Bery's lap.

"……I see, marriage."

"Yes. Tou-sama told me I should start thinking about such things."

*Had all that preamble truly been necessary?*

She thought it but kept it from her face.

Bery was at least capable of that kind of tact, and she very much wanted to avoid another recitation of the earlier material.

"It's a difficult question for a noble. As a member of the Argan family myself, the proper course would be to marry into some house, bear children, and work toward restoring the family."

The Argan family had fallen, but it had not been formally dissolved by the crown; it remained a noble house in name, empty of substance.

The small parcel of land they had managed as nobles had been voluntarily returned to the kingdom, deemed beyond their capacity to administer. The debts, however, had been shouldered as the Argan family's own.

Since no damage had been done to the kingdom, there was no grounds for punishment.

Their father had likely hoped to rebuild from there.

He had collapsed partway through, but with the debt problem resolved, a restoration would have been relatively straightforward.

If, for instance, Bogan and her sister Lazura had had another child, and that child had inherited the Argan name and achieved some distinction, restoring the family would have been simple.

Or Bery could have done the same, or taken in a husband.

Considered solely from the standpoint of carrying on their father's will, that should have been the first choice — but her sister Lazura would not have wanted it either.

Naturally, if their child had wished to restore the Argan name, Lazura would have been delighted and would have left it to them — but she had never wanted a second child for that reason.

It was a matter of temperament.

Put simply, both Bery and Lazura were, as nobles, irresponsible.

"Well, that's only the general argument, I suppose. I'm not really in a position to say much about it. What about you, Anne-sama?"

"……I've never given it much thought. I've never met a gentleman who struck me as, well, *the one*——"

Anne said, and stole a quick glance at Krische, her cheeks coloring faintly.

When Bery looked at her curiously, Anne hurriedly shook her head.

"No……it's, well — with someone like Gallen-sama, I do feel a certain……mature appeal, you might say."

"Ah……fufu, Gallen-sama is a wonderful man indeed."

Bery giggled softly.

She knew that Anne was quite attached to Gallen.

If someone called him attractive, she could certainly see why.

He was a stern, imposing presence, but far gentler than his appearance would suggest — a kind old man.

But he was an old man.

Anne was hardly suggesting *that.*

"……I'm fond of love stories myself, but when I try to imagine it for my own life, it doesn't quite feel real. Entering into a marriage with a gentleman, bearing children……it is, as you say, a perfectly natural thing. And if they say a woman's happiness lies there, then perhaps it does."

Anne said that much and sighed.

"Still, whether it would make me happier than I am now — that I'm not so sure. I'm more than content with my life here, serving Her Majesty, Selene-sama, and Krische-sama, and learning at Argan-sama's side."

Bery could say nothing.

She listened quietly, waiting for Anne's words.

"……I am aware of how fortunate I am, when I consider that many people end up in arranged matches and marriages they never chose. ……The Giterns family has both my brothers married with children, so there's no issue of duty to the house — and Tou-sama said as much, that I needn't worry about the family."

Hmm, Anne tilted her head with a troubled expression.

"Even so, Tou-sama told me to think carefully about how I wish to live from here on."

Anne fell into thought, and Bery alongside her.

But there was one thing she understood.

"He sounds like a wonderful father. He's thinking of what's best for you, Anne-sama."

"……Yes. But I'm sure what he wants is for me to marry and show him his grandchildren. As a dutiful daughter, the right thing would be to do exactly that, but……"

Anne raised her head and looked straight at Bery.

"If I were to marry into some household, it would become difficult to continue working here, at the side of Argan-sama — the person I have made my lifelong model."

"I-is that so……"

The words made Bery squirm, but she felt that interjecting would only derail the main topic further.

She appreciated the sentiment, but Anne could be a touch too direct at times, leaving her at a loss for how to respond.

"Moreover, given the recent incident, Her Majesty's circle of trusted people is limited — and finding a replacement at a moment's notice would be difficult. ……Looking at the situation, the answer seems obvious enough, but."

"……I see."

In her own way, Anne was giving thought to the circumstances of the Christands and Kreschenta.

Anne had her share of flaws, to be sure, but she was a thoughtful woman.

It was only that her thoughtfulness occasionally went too deep and came out the wrong way. Bery herself viewed her as a hardworking, good-natured, caring colleague.

She was hardly so admirable a person herself as to tell Anne what she should do.

If she were to offer something nonetheless, what would it be?

Bery thought for a moment, then spoke.

"……I think of it as something like a river."

"A river?"

"Yes. We are perhaps leaves floating upon it. Drifting as it flows, carried as it wills, not knowing where we'll end up……struggling, worrying, and yet, eventually reaching the sea."

*Fate,* her sister had called it.

No amount of worry could return one to scenery already passed; all things were irreversible, carried by the current.

"In life, the things one can truly choose are small. Whether to surrender to a gentle current, to throw oneself into the rapids, or to drift into some forgotten tributary."

In the everyday, in glory.

Ways of living were many, but what people sought was the same.

"Or perhaps……to draw alongside a petal drifting near, and let yourself float together."

Bery smiled wryly and tickled the nose of the peacefully sleeping Krische.

Krische squirmed and scrunched her face, and Bery's shoulders shook with silent laughter.

"In the end, what matters is whether the scenery you see is beautiful — that is what I believe. Just because everyone follows a current does not make that current the right one……I may simply think so because I'm a rather hopeless contrarian."

She chuckled softly and continued.

"What Anne-sama should value most is her own happiness, before anyone else's. The care you show for myself and Her Majesty is truly touching, and I'm grateful. But to let yourself be swept along by duty and position would be a waste."

"……A waste."

"Yes. You have this freedom — rather than drifting on by inertia, wouldn't it be far happier to choose where you flow? Even if you come to regret it, as long as you chose for yourself, there is at least a baseline of acceptance."

Choosing something would never change the world dramatically.

If anything changed, it was only how the world appeared.

And choosing existed for exactly that purpose.

"Perhaps what Marquis Giterns meant by 'think it over' is something along those lines."

Bery said, and looked at her.

Black hair tied back in a simple bun, without adornment.

And yet that unadorned simplicity was, after all, her charm.

"Whatever choice you make, I believe someone like you will find happiness, Anne-sama. You have a way of naturally bringing smiles to the people around you."

*You were surely born with it,* she said, lowering her eyes with a smile.

It was something Bery, at least, did not possess.

"Whatever ties you think bind you — if you shift your perspective, they turn out to be trivial things, easily managed. Please don't worry. Whatever choice you decide is best for yourself, I will support it — whatever it may be. And so will Ojou-sama, and Krische-sama."

She raised her eyes.

"So please, make the choice that you yourself desi——Anne-sama……?"

Anne, who had been listening with a serious expression, suddenly began shedding tears.

Bery stared at her in astonishment.

Anne covered her face with both hands and shook her head.

"I-I'm so sorry. I just……I was so moved……for even Argan-sama to say such things to me, I-I just……"

"I-I didn't say anything all that remarkable……"

"Hearing it from Tou-sama, and now this — when I think about how truly fortunate I am, it just——"

It was then.

Without so much as a knock, the door opened with stately grace, and in swept red-tinged golden hair.

"Her Majesty has returned, Argan-sama. Shall I prepare tea——"

Elvena in tow, Kreschenta entered — then caught sight of Anne, head bowed and tears streaming, and shifted her gaze to Bery with a furrowed brow.

"What happened? Has Argan-sama finally reached her limit with Anne-sama and delivered an ultimatum?"

"N-no she hasn't. Kreschenta-sama, you mustn't say things like that."

"Giving orders to me — just who do you think you are? But I'm in a good mood today, so I'll let it slide. Elvena-sama, would you kindly make some tea?"

"Y-yes, Your Majesty."

Elvena glanced worriedly at the weeping Anne, then hurried to the table to prepare a fresh pot of tea.

"Um……has something happy happened?"

"Elvena-sama is so wonderfully attentive. Unlike Argan-sama, she doesn't run her mouth unnecessarily — it reminds me of the comfort of Nora's days. I believe we ought to assign Elvena-sama as my personal attendant from now on."

"K-Kreschenta-sama……"

What dreadful timing.

Sure enough, Anne flinched, and Bery rushed to smooth things over——

"Th-that's not happening, Kreschenta. Anne is assigned to your side, and that's decided."

The commotion had apparently woken her.

Krische, still in Bery's arms, spoke to Kreschenta with a drowsy yawn.

"K-Krische-sama……!"

Anne looked up, tears still glistening, her eyes shining with emotion.

"Uuu……I don't want that, Onee-sama. I want to trade Anne-sama for Elvena-sama."

She froze again at Kreschenta's words.

"Kreschenta, there is such a thing in this world as fate. ……Having Anne at your side — that too is a kind of fate. Fwah……Kreschenta should be more grateful for the fate that brought Anne to you as a servant……right, Bery?"

"Y-……yes……"

A rather convenient use of 'fate.'

Wriggling to adjust herself in Bery's lap, the sleep-addled Krische kissed her upon waking, looking supremely disinterested in the matter.

Bery wore a troubled, ambiguous smile.

"Krische-sama……thank you so much……!"

"……?"

Regardless, seeing how deeply Anne was moved by those words, even Bery could say nothing.

Krische tilted her head at the gratitude, and while clinging to Bery, addressed Kreschenta.

"……You see? Now, no pouting. Come here, come here."

"Uu……"

She beckoned the displeased Kreschenta over, embraced her, and stroked her head.

"Anne, you are Kreschenta's personal attendant. Krische gave you the day off today, so starting tomorrow, you'll work even harder."

"Yes!"

Anne wiped her eyes once more at Krische's words, and raised her face, filled with resolve.

Bery watched her with an indescribable expression, and sighed, as if giving up.
