# Chapter 311: Reine XII

A clear afternoon.

People gazed upon the many paintings lined up in the great cathedral of the church, unable to conceal their astonishment.

Even an amateur could not question how true to life these paintings were.

The gradations of color, the detail of the rendering, the beauty of the light.

Though each depicted an otherworldly paradise, every one was painted as though a piece of reality itself had been cut away and captured — their force transcended the realm of mere paintings.

Paintings that could not have been drawn by human hands.

Even a genius who spent their entire life trying could not, one felt, have produced even a single one of them.

If one were told that the artist — the Holy Empress Elsreine — was an existence equal to a god, not a soul would think to object. They were, truly, paintings of divine creation.

More than a dozen 'paradises' were lined up, and visitors formed a line, offering donations as they went, uttering exclamations of wonder as they gazed upon each one.

*How beautiful, these cannot be mere paintings* — such words filled the space, and combined with the solemn music performed by the orchestra, there were even those who wept tears of emotion.

"……Elsreine-sama descended from the heavenly realm to this world, our guide. To deliver us from this land filled with suffering, she thus reveals through her paintings the path ahead. Paradise is a place only the pure of heart may enter——"

The priests repeated such words to them, again and again.

"Unadorned angels who cherish modest feasts in paradise. One need only look upon their smiles to understand. Free of attachment to the worldly things that fill this mortal plane, wearing only thin cloth, simply laughing alongside one another……filled with the pure innocence of an unwanting newborn, unblemished in form. Elsreine-sama shows this to you not through words, but through paintings. ……The true form one must take to be welcomed as an angel of paradise."

Elsreine's paintings mostly depicted feasts.

Scenes of people enjoying meals and tea in meadows and forests, surrounded by nature.

There were no dazzling decorations — people clad only in thin cloth shared fruit among themselves, spending their time in smiles.

Paradise was said to be a place free from want, where gentle angels shared all things with one another.

All were free of desire and overflowing with kindness, creatures who had cast aside the heart of conflict, and thus the world was without impurity.

Paradise was a world more beautiful than any they could imagine, the priests told them.

Those who might have scoffed at words alone found themselves speechless before the beauty of the paintings.

Perhaps such a place truly did exist.

So convincing were the paintings called 'Elsren' that they could make one believe it.

The 'Gathering of the Angels,' in which multiple paintings were periodically assembled and exhibited, drew enormous crowds and spread the teachings far and wide.

The church distributed bread to the poor — and with the sermons the priests gave alongside it, the number of believers in 'Reine's Heaven' in Elsren had grown at an astonishing rate, centered around the lower classes.

By now, people had begun gathering at the churches not for the bread, but solely to hear the sermons, and there were even those standing outside the cathedral, straining to catch the priests' words.

A man tossed a gold coin into the offering bowl, where copper coins were the norm.

After making a circuit of the paintings, he was ushered not toward the exit, but to the parlor in the back of the church.

Before long, a priest appeared.

After exchanging light pleasantries, the merchant broached the subject of donations.

His attendant placed a heavy box on the table and opened it, revealing a mass of gold coins.

The great merchant said he wished for the church to accept this as a donation, and then, as though it were an entirely separate matter, began to speak of the paintings.

What beautiful paintings, they were.

If he could view such paintings every day, his faith would surely deepen.

However, he was a busy man — it was a shame that he could not visit the church more often.

The priest listened with a smile and, glancing at the gold coins, said that the depth of his faith was more than clear from this alone, then casually asked which painting he had liked best.

When the merchant named one of the displayed works, the priest replied that it was indeed beautiful, one of the most popular — and the merchant said, *I see*.

Then, as if he had forgotten, he said *Please accept this as well*, and handed over yet more gold coins in a pouch.

The priest tested the weight in his hand and nodded, saying that for a man such as himself, surely even Elsreine-sama would grant her blessing.

*This, too, is faith* — smiling as he spoke.

Reine's room, where half-finished paintings stood in rows.

"As always, wonderful paintings……the people will surely rejoice."

She saw off Pope Naruko Ribenilas, who held a painting in his hands, and lowered her eyes.

The Papal Court visited regularly, taking paintings away to display in the churches.

They said that to guide the people, to spread the teachings far and wide, many paintings were needed.

They said the people saw these and rejoiced.

Was that really so? Was the message truly getting through?

Lately, doubt was all that seeped in — and yet, as though clinging to something, she painted paradise.

A hundred, two hundred. Past a thousand and approaching two thousand.

Gradually losing her voice in parliament, Reine could do nothing but nod at their words.

Even when she thought they were wrong, she could not say so — she could only have Gorzarius and the others relay Reine's opinions on her behalf.

Reine had not uttered a single word in parliament for several years now.

She understood almost nothing of the nation's internal affairs.

At the very least, on the road to the orphanage — this Renaria appeared prosperous, but what of the other cities?

Even Gorzarius and the others told Reine nothing.

They merely offered harmless words: *There are no issues at present.*

There was a part of her that felt she should believe them — and yet, she could not help but feel she could not.

Many of the nobles were not particularly clever, and she often found their proposals incomprehensible.

They would regularly offer solutions to problems like *what is one plus one* that amounted to *three* or *one*.

There was no way the country could be free of problems under such management.

Some regions surely suffered from poverty.

Unemployment must be rising, public order deteriorating.

It was easy enough to imagine — and yet, she was told there were no problems.

Perhaps, as Naruko said, the common people were living together more peacefully and harmoniously than she imagined.

If that were so, then perhaps this form was the correct one, Reine thought.

And yet, the opposite seemed far more likely.

"……Would you care for more tea?"

She nodded, and Fenera poured the tea with a smile.

As if looking after Reine, whose words had grown so few, Fenera spoke in a bright voice, chattering about this and that.

She could spend a good quarter-hour just talking about how nice the weather was.

She laughed and talked happily about the most trivial things.

"……Fenera, do you think this country is becoming a good one?"

"Um……"

"Do you think Ribenilas is telling the truth? That Reine's feelings are getting through via the paintings, and that……the country is becoming a good one? Even though the policies are inconsistent and full of things that make no sense?"

She spoke while gazing into the poured tea, and Fenera fell silent for a while.

"Everyone says there are no problems, even though there clearly should be. Is Reine the one who is wrong to doubt those words? Is Reine——"

And then Fenera knelt before her and gently embraced Reine.

"……Fenera, too, thinks things are a little strange, to be honest. To tell the truth……she doesn't feel the Pope is particularly trustworthy, either……"

Glancing at the door, she whispered in a low voice.

"It rather seems as though Elsreine-sama is being kept from moving freely, doesn't it? And if that is so, then Rukazan-sama as well……Fenera doesn't want to imagine it, but."

"Rukazan……too?"

"Fenera has……always been told by Rukazan-sama to inform him if there is any change in Elsreine-sama's condition. At first……she thought he was simply concerned for Elsreine-sama's well-being, but given the current state of things, she has begun to feel that may not be the case."

Reine kept her eyes lowered, still gazing at the tea.

"Every time Elsreine-sama voiced something, the nobles raised their voices in opposition, and time and again Rukazan-sama and the others would intervene, saying they would calm the disrupted proceedings……but might it not be that, outside of Elsreine-sama's knowledge, that was how things had been arranged?"

"Arranged……"

"An agreement that any proposal from Elsreine-sama is to be met with opposition, regardless of its merit. If such things are repeated, it would become difficult for Elsreine-sama to speak at all……and that difficulty might well have been the very purpose."

Fenera's words aligned perfectly with what she had been trying not to think about.

*Perhaps* — *no, surely not* — words she had repeated to herself again and again.

If Gorzarius were deceiving Reine, then everything seemed to fall neatly into place.

Led by Gorzarius, the Three Grand Dukes.

Naruko, too, had been chosen by them, and their connections ran deep.

Gorzarius held dominion over the western territories, and if the Three Grand Dukes cooperated, none in this country could stand against them — the true rulers of this nation were not Reine, but the Three Grand Dukes.

If that was their purpose, then the current situation made perfect sense.

*'——I bear responsibility for those who died in that war. Those who grieve for them need someone upon whom to direct their hatred.'*

But she wondered, since when?

She did not want to doubt Gorzarius, and she wanted to believe in the Gorzarius who had been so good to her.

Perhaps he had simply forgotten such feelings along the way.

If so, perhaps if Reine scolded him, he might have a change of heart, and they could start over.

More than that — perhaps Reine simply did not understand.

Perhaps, truly, this country was moving in a good direction in this very form.

Right now, Reine knew nothing of the actual state of affairs.

"……I think I will try to investigate."

"Investigate……?"

"Yes. ……I cannot say anything without looking into it first……and I admit, the thought of doubting him does not sit well……but, if it becomes clear, if I find that it was merely Reine overthinking things, then Reine can put her mind at ease."

——Having decided this, Reine began slipping out of her room every night.

She started by scrutinizing the information that gathered in Renaria.

Slipping out through her window, from window to window.

She examined the documents of various departments with her own eyes, committed them to memory, gathered information, and increased the frequency of her visits to the orphanage — then slipped out after dark to survey the city herself.

Even in Renaria, which on the surface bustled with activity, she found several slums, and it was all too easy to confirm the sight of homeless children gathering and passing their days together.

Problems existed everywhere, and Reine had not heard of a single one.

Homeless children stealing, gaunt girls standing on street corners, being bought by soldiers who should have been on patrol — coupling in alleyways.

When the girl demanded payment, she was beaten; she begged for forgiveness through her tears, and the soldier spat and walked away.

And before long, the girl was back on the corner.

Twenty years had passed since the founding of Elsren.

Yet even Renaria harbored lawless zones, and even the patrols did not uphold their own regulations.

Such scenes played out outside the designated pleasure districts as well.

A distant sight from the nation she had imagined twenty years ago.

At the very least, this was not 'Elsren.'

Something nauseating churned within her chest.

The anxiety that she was being deceived, being used, gradually took on the weight of truth.

In contrast to that chaos, the churches were beautiful.

Large churches stood in areas with good public order — where land was by no means cheap.

They were neither noble estates nor merchant residences, and yet what were they guarding?

The churches had sentries even at night.

Sneaking in took some effort, and the ledgers were not easily found.

Still, after visiting several churches, she managed to find, separate from the public ledgers, at least two sets of hidden books.

Revenue from 'donations' that was enormous by any measure.

Most was recorded under the pretext of lending out Reine's paintings, but one set showed a peculiar correlation with orphanage records — matching the 'departure' of orphan girls with entries reading *one fine article*.

Transactions with merchants were being conducted.

The amounts were exactly what one would expect for a single illegally sold slave.

A single entry was enough only for suspicion, but after finding several similar records, she was certain.

They were selling orphans.

Remembering the smiles of the children at the orphanage, the revulsion inside her chest deepened further.

She took the evidence and, the following day, went straight from the orphanage to the Papal Court.

She entered Naruko's private residence within its grounds and, no longer bothering to conceal her actions, searched for ledgers.

In the Pope's study, where Reine's paintings hung on the walls, several bookcases stood empty, their contents thrown out.

Like the books scattered across the floor.

On the desk, shoved carelessly to one side, several discovered ledgers lay open, and bound to the chair in the center was the Pope, Naruko Ribenilas.

When Reine entered the residence without permission, Naruko had rushed over in a panic — by then, she had already found the hidden ledgers.

Unlike Reine's mother or Reine herself, who could process everything by memory, most people managed their finances through ledgers.

Unlike the public ledgers, which had been balanced to conceal anything suspicious, the hidden ledgers showed the true state of affairs.

Transactions that violated the law — the dark inflows and outflows of money were recorded there, and it was these ledgers that were used for actual calculations.

However, these were not meant for anyone else's eyes.

Simple codes were used so that only the writer could understand them, and these were no exception.

To extract that information, all the fingers of Naruko's left hand had already been broken.

At first, Naruko had claimed it was all a misunderstanding, but as his left fingers were broken one after another, and his left arm was bound in preparation for severance, he must have realized Reine would not hesitate.

He spoke honestly about the codes.

What arrived at that moment was——

"……Rukazan, explain yourself."

"Please calm yourself, Elsreine-sama."

Gorzarius Rukazan.

She immediately pressed the sword she had seized from a soldier to his throat, but Gorzarius, hands raised, appeared perfectly composed.

His gaze briefly acknowledged the ledgers spread open on the desk off to the side.

Reine frowned.

"Reine is calm. However, you, along with the other two and the nobles recorded here, have been involved in corruption and have committed crimes. ……Is it true that you received donations from Naruko and provided favors in return?"

"Yes, I'll admit it. ……That said, it hardly seems appropriate to discuss such matters with the door open. Might I come inside first? I have no intention of fleeing or hiding."

Reine maintained her distance warily, and Gorzarius unfastened his sword belt and placed it on the shelf beside the entrance, followed by his knife.

Then, hands still raised, he walked to the back, rearranged a sofa that had been shoved aside along with the desk, and sat down upon it.

Then he looked at Naruko and gave a wry smile.

"……What is so amusing?"

"No, merely that you show no restraint whatsoever."

"Depending on circumstances, Reine may have no choice but to do the same to you."

"I would prefer to avoid that. Still……might you lower your sword? Unarmed — and aged as I am — there is no risk of Elsreine-sama being caught off guard."

His black hair had lost its color and turned gray.

His large frame carried somewhat less muscle than twenty years ago.

What had not changed was that single eye, staring squarely back at her.

"You, however, are unchanged — as beautiful as ever. I hear the Queen still appears the same……perhaps the children of gods know nothing of aging. Mind and body both remain beautiful, pure. Including the fact that you would hear me out like this."

"……Reine remembers the words you once spoke to her. Have you……forgotten them?"

"Hardly. I remember them well."

"……Then why would you do such a thing?"

Gorzarius rested his hand on his chin and thought for a moment.

*A difficult question*, he said with a wry smile, and then turned his gaze to a painting by Reine that hung on the wall.

"No matter how I phrase it……each reason is true, yet any single one alone could also be called false. That I was genuinely captivated by Elsreine-sama's paintings is one reason, I suppose."

"……The paintings?"

"Yes, they are truly beautiful paintings. I had never felt any appeal in paintings, in art……but after seeing Elsreine-sama's work, I believe I understand something of that appeal."

*And then*, he continued.

"That I still revere Elsreine-sama as much as ever is also a reason……and that I enjoy a good gamble may be another. Even this current situation is not a losing hand for me."

"……?"

"Consider this calmly. No matter what truths may exist, no matter what legitimacy Elsreine-sama may have, to say you will judge me by law is a difficult proposition. You would simply be seen as having gone mad, and in parliament, you would be treated accordingly."

She gripped the sword tighter, and Reine asked.

"……Did you engineer the parliament as well?"

"I merely fulfilled Elsreine-sama's ideals."

"Reine's……ideals?"

"Yes. That after listening to everyone's opinions, this was the inevitable outcome. For the nobles who wished to fatten themselves, the peaceful, beautiful paradise Elsreine-sama wished for was nothing but an obstruction. ……I merely organized a simple majority vote to that effect."

Unable to grasp his meaning, Reine furrowed her brow in confusion.

"You wished to build a good country together with everyone — and so, this country is good for 'everyone.' Everyone satisfies their own desires freely, unpunished……an Elsren different in form from the one Elsreine-sama dreamt of. A paradise — for them."

"……What are you saying? This is——"

"As you can see from those ledgers and from seeing reality with your own eyes……at the very least, this is what was desired in this country. Using the other child of the gods as a figurehead, a shield against the absolute power of Alberan and the Queen — a paradise in which to suckle at the sweet nectar……"

*Indeed*, Gorzarius said, and laughed.

"It is difficult for someone as pure as Elsreine-sama to understand, I'm sure. But……this is what humans are. Using others, each and every one putting their own desires first — and it is the accumulation of such motives that creates the collective we call a nation. ……And this is the Elsren that everyone built together."

"This is……Elsren?"

"Indeed. I merely listened to their opinions, as Elsreine-sama wished. It would seem that their vision of a paradise differed from Elsreine-sama's. In fact, I hear the common people, too, believe that Elsreine-sama's Elsren is not of this world, but a place somewhere above, in the heavens, to which one is called after death……that if they endure these days of suffering in this reality, they will be rewarded."

*What a shame*, he said, glancing at Naruko, who sat silently beside them, cowering in fear, and laughed.

Boldly, as though proclaiming that he was in the right.

"……Is this, for Rukazan as well, Elsren?"

"A difficult question. I have no particular dissatisfaction or complaint, but……"

"Rukazan, you said that if Reine could create the kind of place she wished for — where everyone could live happily and in peace — it would reach kaa-sama too. That you would help for that purpose. ……Was that a lie?"

"As I said, this is a nation formed by each person receiving and interpreting the Elsren that Elsreine-sama tried to convey……I, as the first among your subjects, continue to serve Elsreine-sama and strive only to build a better Elsren."

She felt sick, and her head ached.

It seemed as though her words were not reaching him at all. She pressed a hand to her forehead and let her gaze wander.

*'All of it was born from a misunderstanding……It was not mother who killed Fiiri. It was Rukazan, standing right there.'*

And then, she remembered those words.

"……Did Rukazan kill Fiiri? Were you deceiving Reine from the very beginning?"

Gorzarius spoke as though exasperated.

"I believe I addressed that previously as well. ……One can dress up anything in words, and excuses come easily. Words are meaningless things. If I say yes, will you run me through with that sword here and now? If I say it was the Queen's doing, will you believe me?"

"That's——"

"Either way, it is in the past. Elsreine-sama doubted the Queen, and chose not to read that letter……Elsreine-sama chose of her own will, and has continued to choose of her own will to this day."

With that single eye, straight and unflinching, without a trace of shame.

Gorzarius had always stood before Reine with that same commanding presence.

"I have always entrusted the choice to you, Elsreine-sama. If parliament was under our control, you had the option of forcing compliance through sheer power. You could have ignored our words entirely, never relinquished your authority, and simply done as you wished. Could you not have built a nation like the Queen's? It should have been easy for you."

"……!"

"But you feared the nobles' criticism, closed your mouth, ceased making decisions yourself, and entrusted the choice to us. And now that this has come to be……this is, beyond any doubt, a nation born of your choices, Elsreine-sama."

*With that in mind, there are three things you can do from here*, he said.

"You have time. You may continue to hope that someday they will understand, and live your days as before. Or you may crush the corrupt nobles by force, slaughter them, and through civil war, build your nation anew. Or, you may choose to trust the Queen you once doubted, return to Alberan, and apologize for everything."

Counting down three raised fingers, Gorzarius laughed.

She did not understand why he was laughing.

She could not understand.

"Anything beyond the first will bring misfortune to many. Many will die. Including, of course, the children who have finally found happiness at the orphanage. If you flee to Alberan, they will resent you. They will say you abandoned and betrayed them."

Her shoulders flinched, and she lowered her eyes.

She could not look at Gorzarius's face.

"Many among the common people will think the same. All those who placed their hopes in the Elsren you painted, different though it may have been, will feel the same. With that understood, I suggest you make your decision. At the very least, every option is a simple one for you……rather like a certain time before."

She felt Gorzarius rise to his feet. Reine drew back, and her back struck the door.

Gorzarius came to stand before Reine.

He knelt, grasped the tip of Reine's sword, and pressed it against his own throat.

The hand that held the sword trembled.

"You need only apply a little force, Elsreine-sama. I will not resist. If you command me to take my own life, I shall press my own throat upon this point. ……I am, now as ever, your subject — as I swore upon my name, for as long as you have need of me."

"……!"

"What is there to hesitate over? ……If you believe I deceived you, there is only one thing to do, is there not? If you are angry, then thrust it forward. If you cannot bring yourself to do it, then give the command……I will obey."

She did not understand how he could do this. Reine could not understand.

Though a situation where the slightest pressure would mean his death, there was not a fragment of fear in him.

As though he believed he would not be killed.

As though he believed he was in the right.

She could neither pull the sword back, nor push it forward, nor utter a word.

She merely let the strength leave her grip, and released it.

"……You are astonishingly kind, truly."

He took the sword and set it atop the shelf.

Then Gorzarius stepped back, and turned his gaze to a painting by Reine.

"From the bottom of my heart, these are beautiful paintings. Without blemish, pure……the more I age, the more I think so. I am truly grateful to Fiiri, who taught you so many things and prompted you to paint such works. ……I never imagined that in a world where the only pleasure is a wager or two, something so magnificent could exist."

He took the framed painting in his hands and gazed upon the figures of smiling, happy people.

"The inner self conveyed through a painting……this painting is Elsreine-sama herself. Ever since winning the gamble of my own advancement, these paintings are all I ever think of. I have seen nearly every one of your paintings……and each time, I wonder with anticipation what you will show me next."

*We are likely broken in a similar way*, he said with a wry smile.

"I felt as though people and objects were all the same — tools, or something like them. I studied how to use them deeply, used them as they were meant to be used, and felt not a moment's hesitation. Naturally, I possess nothing of the intellect you or the Queen possess……but from the moment I was born, I, too, was broken."

There was no malice in that smile.

No guilt — he was simply smiling.

"……One might even say I feel a kind of kinship, an admiration. You, who could kill so dispassionately on the battlefield, should by rights be far more broken than I — and yet you can paint such a paradise. I find it wondrous, that you could be so kind. Perhaps that is why I wish to understand."

*You are truly foolish*, he said, in a tone that held no reproach.

Smiling, unchanged.

"And yet, I find that foolishness beautiful. Of everyone I have ever seen, you are the most beautiful. The Queen, before I met her, must surely have been such a person as well. What paintings she might have drawn……well, that is beside the point."

He said this, hung the painting back upon the wall, and——

"Regardless, this too is your choice. Your answer. ……I look forward to what follows, Elsreine-sama. My sole joy in life now is these paintings you paint."

*——By all means, please do not put down your brush.*

Gorzarius said this, and fell silent.

The Elsren Holy Empire. First Empress Elsreine — a beautiful and wise ruler.

She who built Elsren through near-miraculous ability seldom appeared before the public, however, and never gave speeches or the like.

What she showed them instead were paintings of paradise.

Through such fantastical paradises — of men and women, young and old, sharing fruit and laughing together — she was said to have guided the people.

A technique of precision unsurpassed across past and future.

A heaven on earth woven from obsessive detail and layers upon layers of pigment.

Yet from those paintings of Elsreine, the figures of adults vanished, and gradually, only children came to be depicted.

All but one — the final work she left behind.

"Elsreine-sama……"

Fenera, who had been waiting before the carriage, spotted Reine and hurried to her side.

*Are you all right?* she asked, her face full of concern, and Reine quietly nodded.

She had told Fenera the situation.

But how should she convey this outcome?

What should she say? Would Fenera even understand?

She considered this, and decided to at least reassure her — to say *I'm fine* — and,

"……?"

The words would not come, and she froze.

Her eyes darted about in confusion; she opened her lips to speak, then closed them, pressing a hand to her throat.

"……Elsreine-sama, did someone do something to you?"

She shook her head and tried to answer, but no voice came.

She opened her mouth in a desperate attempt, but only air escaped.

Bewildered, she tried again and again, trembling with anxiety, and seeing this, Fenera asked.

"Could it be that……your voice won't come out?"

She nodded at those words, her gaze still wavering in agitation.

Fenera stared blankly at Reine, then twisted her face and shook her head.

Then she pulled Reine's body close, and——

"……It's all right……"

In a trembling voice, she said, *Please don't push yourself.*

# Chapter 312: Reine — End

*——I have adored you from the very first moment I laid eyes upon you, Reine-sama.*

To that voice whispering with tender affection, Reine answered as though exasperated.

*'……I finally understand. You're broken, aren't you.'*

*'If being broken means you will accept me, then it would be most convenient for me to answer: yes, I am broken.'*

With a soft, amused laugh, shoulders shaking, Fiiri laughed like a young girl.

*'But is such a distinction necessary? One could say that everyone is broken, or that everyone is perfectly sane. We are all imperfect creatures, Reine-sama. You included, of course.'*

And then she pointed to her own head.

*'A perfect being would want for nothing, and desire nothing. No matter how especially beautiful you may be, Reine-sama, you are ultimately the same as the rest of us — a single, imperfect animal. Eating, sleeping, coupling — no different from a dog or a cat, just one more human creature……'*

Reine frowned, and Fiiri smiled.

*'……You look down on this worthless world, insisting you are different, and what you truly are is simply afraid — afraid that if you do not believe yourself special. You use the word "special" as self-justification for your own alienation, nothing more. You bear a child in search of a reason to exist, and not a single thing you do differs from any other human being.'*

*'……Would you like me to kill you?'*

*'Goodness, no. Just a little instruction from your new tutor, stepping into Benuure-sama's role.'*

Coolly sipping her tea, narrowing her eyes.

Never dropping her composed air, showing neither fear nor timidity.

Nothing but an infuriating woman.

*'We are merely a herd of beasts, in the end. Each one freely pursuing their own interests, as they please. Money, the opposite sex, power……everyone is free to do as they wish. You are the same — by loving Elsreine-sama, you merely seek the return of being loved yourself, using her for that purpose. Even pure altruism exists only because of the return of self-satisfaction. Whether consciously or unconsciously varies from person to person, but……'*

Mockingly, Fiiri continued.

*'This is the world we live in. If you call it worthless, then you should die. There is no human being who can love another without any return at all. ……And you should know this already, and yet you go on living — is it not because you hoped that someone in this worthless world would give you a reason to live?'*

*'…………'*

*'But the world will not reshape itself just to suit you. It is you who must change, Reine-sama. If only you would open those closed eyes, you would find no shortage of people who seek your love, who wish to love you, who wish to give you a reason to live.'*

*——Including me, of course.*

She reached out her hand, touched Reine's cheek, and traced her skin with tender affection.

*'Won't you start by accepting my love? I promise you will not regret it. So that Reine-sama need never close her eyes again……I will grant your every wish.'*

Then she withdrew her hand, and with a soft laugh, covered her mouth and let the laughter spill.

She drained her tea, and stood.

*'It is late, so I shall take my leave, Your Majesty. ……Please think it over at your leisure. Tomorrow morning, I shall come to tidy and clean——'*

Reine seized her hand and threw her onto the bed.

She fell without resistance, and Fiiri stared up at Reine in blank astonishment.

*'……You can make that sort of face, too.'*

*'Well, naturally……I am human, after all.'*

Saying so, her gaze wavered and her cheeks flushed; she let out a sigh laced with a deep breath.

She loosened the tension from her body and gave an embarrassed, wry smile.

His younger brother, a year his junior, unlike him, possessed talents that people envied.

His brother rebelled and went to the battlefield seeking fame and fortune.

*"Brother, I've already decided. Don't worry, I'll achieve great deeds and return safely. When I do, let's have a good drink together here again."*

However, his brother seemed determined.

Argoush gave up trying to persuade him and reluctantly sent him off because his brother had talent.

He thought that if it was his brother, he would achieve great deeds as he said and eventually make a name for himself as a great general.

That's what he thought.

But his brother lost his life easily in his first battle.

His brother, whom Argoush couldn't help, had unluckily clashed with the enemy's main force in the war against Elderant and was cut down by a fierce enemy warrior.

*"...You must not dream, Argoush. In the time you spend dreaming, there are endless things you should do and are expected to do. There are many obligations within your reach that will not end even if you spend your entire life on them."*

*"Obligation..."*

*"Yes, don't misunderstand. Your life is not for yourself alone."*

Witnessing the effortless collapse of the talent he admired had a significant impact on Argoush thereafter.

There were those above, and then even higher above them.

The world was vast, and he was too small.

He would simply and earnestly do what he had to do.

Within his reach, to the best of his ability.

Simply to fulfill his obligation as a noble.

"—No, but, I was truly impressed by Marquis Giterns back then. That back, walking so confidently alone towards the dragon..."

Argoush's virtue lay in his earnestness; he was a timid, ordinary man.
