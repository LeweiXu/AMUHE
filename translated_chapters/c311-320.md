# Chapter 311: Reine XII

A clear afternoon.

Looking at the many paintings arrayed in the cathedral of the church, the people wore expressions of astonishment.

Paintings so true to life that even an amateur could not doubt them.

The gradations of color, the depiction of fine detail, the beauty of the light.

Though they depicted a paradise removed from reality, every one of them had been rendered as though reality itself had been cut out and laid down — a force that already exceeded the realm of mere painting.

Paintings that did not seem made by human hands.

Even one called a genius, spending an entire lifetime, would not have been able to produce a single one of them.

That the painter — the Holy Empress Elsreine — was an existence equal to a god, even when stated thus, no one would think to argue; truly, these were paintings of divine make.

A dozen and more *Paradises* were arrayed, and visitors offered donations as they formed lines, voicing their admiration as they gazed upon them.

How beautiful, words like *they cannot be paintings* echoed across the hall, and combined with the solemn music the orchestra resounded, there were even some who wept in awe.

"……Elsreine-sama is our guide, descended from the kingdom of heaven into this world. To save us from this land filled with suffering, she shows us through her paintings the way forward. To paradise, only those of pure heart may travel——"

The priests repeated such words to them again and again.

"The angels of paradise honor a humble, modest feast — without ornament. Look upon their smiles and you shall understand. Without attachment to the things that fill this profane world, clad only in thin cloth, simply laughing together with those beside them……filled with the innocence of unselfish infants, a form without taint. Elsreine-sama shows it to you not in words, but through her paintings……the rightful form, that you may be welcomed as angels of paradise."

Elsreine's paintings most often depicted feasts.

Figures enjoying meals and tea in places surrounded by nature — meadows, forests.

Without dazzling ornament, people clad only in thin cloth shared fruit and passed the time smiling.

In paradise, none lacked food, and only gentle angels who shared all things resided there.

All were unselfish, overflowing with consideration, beings who had abandoned the heart of conflict, and so the world was without taint.

Paradise was a beautiful world beyond what one could imagine, the priests said.

If only their words, some might have laughed them off — but seeing the beauty of those paintings, no words would come.

Perhaps such a place truly did exist.

So true to life were the paintings of *Elsren* that they made one think such a thing.

The "Gathering of Angels," where multiple paintings were periodically collected and exhibited, drew enormous crowds and spread the teachings.

Together with the church's bread-charity for the poor — and the sermons given by the priests there — *Reine's Heaven* found its believers, surprisingly, growing chiefly among the poor of Elsren.

Now there were even those who came to the church not for the bread but solely to hear the sermons, and some who would stand outside the great hall, hoping to listen to the priests' words.

A man appeared who tossed gold coins into a bowl that mostly held copper.

After viewing the paintings in their entirety, he was led not to the exit but to a parlor in the church's interior.

After a while, a priest appeared.

After a brief exchange of small talk, the merchant brought up the subject of donation.

A retainer set a heavy box upon the table and opened it: inside, a great quantity of gold coins.

The great merchant said he wished it received as a donation to the church, and then, as though it were an entirely separate matter, spoke of the paintings.

Such beautiful paintings.

If he could see paintings like that every day, his own faith would surely deepen.

But, being a busy man — that he could not visit the church often was a shame.

The priest listened with a smile, and saying *the depth of your faith I can well understand from this*, with his eyes upon the gold, casually inquired which painting had pleased him most.

When the merchant named one of the displayed pieces, the priest answered *that one is among the most beautiful, very popular* — and the merchant said, *I see.*

Then, *as though I had forgotten,* he handed over a further pouch of gold coins.

The priest weighed it, and nodded, *for one such as you, surely Elsreine-sama would permit it.*

——*This too is faith,* he said, smiling.

Reine's chamber, where many half-finished paintings stood lined up.

"As ever, a splendid painting……the people will surely rejoice."

She saw off the Pope, Naruko Ribenilas, painting in hand, and lowered her eyes.

The Papal Court came regularly, taking paintings to display in churches.

To guide the populace, to spread the teachings widely, many paintings were necessary.

The people would see them and rejoice, he said.

Was that truly so. Was it being conveyed.

Lately only doubt had begun to seep in, and yet, as if clinging, she painted paradise.

A hundred. Two hundred. Past a thousand, nearing two thousand.

Gradually losing her voice in the council, Reine merely nodded at their words.

Even when she thought something wrong, she could not say it aloud, and could only have Gorzarius and the others speak her opinion in her stead.

For some years now, Reine had not spoken in the council.

She knew almost nothing of the country's internal state.

At least on the road to the orphanage——this Renaria looked prosperous, and yet, what of the other cities.

Even Gorzarius and the rest told Reine nothing.

They only spoke harmless words: *there is no problem at present.*

She wanted to believe she ought to trust those words, and yet she could not.

Many of the nobles were not particularly clever, and many of their opinions she could not understand.

To the question of what one and one made, they would frequently propose solutions like *three* or *one.*

There was no way the country could be without problems.

In some regions there must surely be problems of poverty.

The unemployed must be increasing, public order must be unraveling.

It was easily imagined; and yet she was told, *there is no problem.*

Perhaps, as Naruko said, the populace lived more peacefully than she imagined, helping one another tranquilly.

If that were so, perhaps this was the right form, Reine thought.

But the possibility that it was not felt far stronger.

"……Tea, would you like another cup?"

When she nodded, Fenera poured the black tea with a smile.

As though concerned for Reine, who had grown markedly silent, Fenera kept up a bright voice and chatted about anything and everything.

Even speaking of how today was fine weather took her a small quarter-hour.

She laughed and told happily of trifling things.

"……Fenera. Is this country becoming a good country?"

"Um……"

"Do you think Ribenilas is telling the truth? That Reine's feelings are conveyed through the paintings, and through that……it has become a good country? Even though the policies are inconsistent, full of strange things."

Gazing into the poured tea as she said this, Fenera fell silent for a while.

"Everyone says there is no problem, when surely there must be problems. Is it Reine, who doubts those words, who has become strange? Reine——"

And then Fenera knelt and gently embraced Reine.

"……Fenera too, thinks it is a little strange. Honestly……I don't think His Holiness the Pope is a person to be trusted……"

Glancing a little toward the door, in a voice low as a whisper.

"It seems, somehow, that they are arranging things so that Elsreine-sama cannot move freely, a little. And if so, that means Lukazan-sama as well……Fenera does not want to imagine such a thing, but."

"Lukazan……too?"

"Fenera is……always told by Lukazan-sama to inform him if there is any change in Elsreine-sama's condition. At first……I thought he was being considerate of Elsreine-sama. But seeing the present state, it feels as though it is not so."

Reine, gazing into the tea, lowered her eyes.

"Whenever Elsreine-sama said anything, the nobles would raise their voices, and Lukazan-sama and the others would frequently rebuke them and calm the disorder, they said……but might it not have been that, in places Elsreine-sama did not know, such an arrangement had been made?"

"Such an arrangement……"

"An understanding that they would rebel against any proposal Elsreine-sama might make, no matter what. If that were repeated, it would become hard for Elsreine-sama to speak as well……might that not in fact have been the purpose?"

Fenera's words matched the very content Reine had been trying her best not to consider.

Perhaps——*there's no way that could be,* she had repeated to herself many times.

If Gorzarius were deceiving Reine, then everything would fit together cleanly.

Gorzarius foremost, and the Three Grand Dukes.

Naruko, too, had been chosen by them, and the connections ran deep.

The west's authority was held by Gorzarius, and if the Three Grand Dukes cooperated, none in the country could oppose them; the actual ruler of this country was not Reine but the Three Grand Dukes.

If that were the purpose, the present state made sense.

*"——I bear a responsibility to those who died in this war. Those who grieve their deaths require an enemy upon whom to turn that hatred."*

Yet from when had it been, she wondered.

She had not wanted to doubt Gorzarius. She had wanted to believe in the Gorzarius who had been so good to her.

Perhaps somewhere along the way, he had simply forgotten that heart.

If that were so, then if Reine reproached him, he might once again turn his heart back, and they could begin again.

Or before that — perhaps Reine simply did not understand.

Perhaps in truth, in this form, this country was moving toward becoming a good country.

Reine, at present, knew nothing.

"……I think I will look into things a little."

"Look into……?"

"Yes……unless I look into it, I cannot say anything……it does feel like doubting, and I am not eager to……but if it becomes clear, if it turns out to be Reine overthinking, then Reine can be at ease."

——Having decided so, Reine slipped from her chamber every night.

First she sifted through the information that gathered in Renaria.

From window to window she slipped from her chamber.

She confirmed with her own eyes the documents of various offices, memorized them, gathered information; and as she increased her trips to the orphanage, she would slip out after sundown and walk about the city.

Even in the outwardly bustling Renaria, several slums existed; she could verify any number of times the sight of houseless children gathered together.

Problems existed in every place, and Reine had heard of none of them.

Houseless children stole, and on street corners thin girls stood; while on duty — on patrol, supposedly — soldiers bought them, and lay with them in alleys.

When the girls demanded payment they were beaten, and weeping, the girl begged forgiveness as the soldier spat and walked away.

And after a time, the girl returned to the corner.

Twenty years had passed since the founding of Elsren.

And yet even in Renaria there were lawless quarters, and even the patrols did not keep discipline.

Such scenes spread even outside the designated entertainment district.

Far indeed from the country twenty years on that she had imagined twenty years before.

At the very least, this place was not *Elsren.*

Something foul churned within her chest.

The unease that she was being deceived and used gradually took on the weight of truth.

Contrasting that chaos, the churches were beautiful.

In a peaceful — and far from cheap — district of land, a great church stood.

Though it was no noble's manor or merchant's residence, what was it that they were guarding.

The churches kept watchmen even at night.

Slipping in took effort, and ledgers were not the sort of thing easily found.

Even so, after she had visited several churches, she found, alongside the public ledger, two sets of hidden ledgers.

A monetary income too vast to be called "donation."

Most was under the pretext of *lending* Reine's paintings; but in one of them was a strange match with the orphanage records — coinciding with the departure of an orphan girl, "one fine item."

Transactions with merchants.

The amount neatly resembled the price of one illegal slave.

By itself, it remained suspicion; but with several similar entries it became conviction.

It was certain that orphans were being sold.

Recalling the smiles of the children at the orphanage, the discomfort within her grew stronger.

Taking it as evidence, the next day, on her way back from the orphanage, she went directly to the Papal Court.

She entered Naruko's private residence within the grounds, no longer hiding herself, and searched for the ledger.

In the Pope's study, where Reine's paintings were displayed, the contents had been thrown out, and many empty bookcases stood lined up.

The same with the books strewn about.

Atop the desk, shoved roughly to one side, several discovered ledgers were spread open; bound to a chair at the center was the Pope, Naruko Ribenilas.

Because Reine had entered his private residence without permission, by the time Naruko hurried in she had already discovered the hidden ledgers.

Unless one could process everything by memory, like her mother or Reine, most people managed the flow of money through ledgers.

Unlike the public ledger, where the sums were balanced so that no irregularity might appear, the hidden ledgers held the truth.

Transactions in violation of law, and other shadowy comings and goings of money, were recorded there, and it was these ledgers that were used for actual reckoning.

But — they were things one could not afford to have others see.

They used simple ciphers known only to the writer; this was no exception.

To draw it out of him, the fingers of Naruko's left hand had already all been broken.

At first Naruko had said it was some mistake; but as his left fingers were broken one by one, when his arm was being bound to be cut off, he realized Reine would not hesitate.

He spoke honestly of the cipher.

It was then that —

"……Lukazan, what is the meaning of this."

"Please, calm yourself a little, Elsreine-sama."

— Gorzarius Lukazan arrived.

Snatching the sword from the soldier she had just disarmed, she immediately put it to his throat; but Gorzarius, even with both hands raised, looked entirely calm.

His gaze caught faintly the ledgers spread open on the desk in the corner.

Reine knit her brow.

"Reine is calm. But you, like the other two, and like the nobles recorded here, have been involved in corruption — you have committed crimes. ……Is it true that you received contributions from Naruko and arranged some kind of favor in return?"

"Yes, I shall acknowledge it. ……That said, holding such a conversation with the door open seems unwise. May I, for the moment, enter the room? I will not flee or hide."

Reine stepped back warily, and Gorzarius removed his sword belt and placed it on the shelf beside the entrance, and his knife as well.

Then, hands still raised, he proceeded to the back of the room, moved aside a sofa that had likewise been roughly shoved to the corner, and seated himself there.

He looked at Naruko and gave a wry smile.

"……What is amusing?"

"Nothing — only that there is no reserve at all."

"Should it come to that, Reine will have no choice but to do the same to you."

"That is something I would not wish for. However……might you lower the sword? Empty-handed — and old — I, surely Elsreine-sama could not be caught off guard by me."

His black hair had now lost its color and turned gray.

His large frame had lost a little of its muscle compared to twenty years before.

What had not changed was that single eye, looking forthrightly upon her.

"You remain unchanged, beautiful. Her Majesty the Queen is said to be the same; perhaps the children of god do not know the meaning of growing old. Heart and body alike beautiful, pure. Including the way you would still hear me out, like this."

"……Reine remembers the words you once told her. Have you……forgotten them?"

"Hardly. I remember them."

"……Then why such things?"

Gorzarius placed a hand against his chin, and thought for a moment.

*Now I am troubled,* he said, smiling wryly, and then turned his eyes to the displayed painting of Reine's.

"Whatever way I phrase it……each is correct, and yet each, taken alone, could be called an error. That I was drawn from the heart to Elsreine-sama's paintings — that would be one reason."

"……Paintings?"

"Yes — truly beautiful paintings. I had never felt drawn to paintings, to art……but since I saw your paintings, I feel I have come to understand their charm."

Then, he continued.

"That I still revere Elsreine-sama, unchanged, is also one reason……and that I am fond of gambling, perhaps. The present situation is not, for me, a losing roll."

"……?"

"Consider it calmly. Whatever the truth might be, however much legitimacy Elsreine-sama might possess, it is difficult to judge me by law. You will only be thought to have lost your mind, and in council you will be treated as such."

Gripping the sword, Reine asked.

"……The council too — was that something you arranged?"

"I only granted Elsreine-sama's ideal."

"Reine's……ideal?"

"Yes. It is what was inevitable as a result of listening to everyone's opinions. To the nobles who wished to grow fat, Elsreine-sama's wished-for paradise without conflict, beautiful, was an obstacle. ……I only assembled that simple matter of majority."

Unable to grasp the meaning, Reine knit her brows and stood at a loss.

"To build a good country together with everyone — as you said. This country is, for *everyone,* a good country. None are judged for satisfying their own desires, and……a different shape of Elsren from the Elsren Elsreine-sama dreams of. For them, this is paradise."

"……What are you saying? Such a thing——"

"Looking upon those ledgers, looking at reality, you should see……at least in this country, that is what was wanted. A paradise of feeding upon sweet juices, with another child of god as its symbol — held up as a shield against the absolute Alberan, against Her Majesty the Queen, child of god……"

*Indeed,* Gorzarius laughed.

"It will be hard for the pure-hearted Elsreine-sama. But……this is what man is. Using one another, each placing his own desire foremost; the conjunction of such schemes is what produces the collective entity called a country. ……And this is the Elsren we all built together."

"This is……Elsren?"

"Yes. I only listened to their opinions, just as Elsreine-sama wished. The paradises they imagined, and the paradise Elsreine-sama imagined, were different. In fact, the populace too believes Elsreine-sama's Elsren is not in this world but in the heavens — somewhere they will be summoned to after death……that if they endure these days of suffering with nothing else, they shall be rewarded."

*A shame,* he said, glancing at Naruko, who had fallen silent in fear at his side, and laughed.

Forthright, as if to declare he was doing what was right.

"……Is this place, for Lukazan as well, *Elsren*?"

"That is difficult. There is no inconvenience or dissatisfaction in particular……"

"Lukazan said, that if a place could be shown where everyone lived happy and at peace, as Reine wished, it would reach kaa-sama as well……that for that reason, you would cooperate. ……Was that a lie?"

"As I said, this is the Elsren that Elsreine-sama tried to convey, formed by each receiving it in his own way……as your foremost retainer, I serve Elsreine-sama still, only striving to build a better Elsren."

She felt ill, and her head ached.

Words seemed not to reach him at all; she pressed her forehead, her gaze unsteady.

*"All has been a misunderstanding……the one who killed Fiiri was not Mother. It is the Lukazan there."*

And then she remembered such words.

"……Did Lukazan kill Fiiri? Were you deceiving Reine from the start?"

Gorzarius answered as though exasperated.

"That, too, I believe I said before. ……Words can be dressed up in any way; explanation is easy. Words are without meaning. If I answer yes, will Elsreine-sama stab me dead here and now? If I answer that it was Her Majesty the Queen's doing, will you believe me?"

"That, is……"

"Either way, it is past. Elsreine-sama doubted Her Majesty the Queen, and without reading that letter……Elsreine-sama chose so, and in the end, by your own will, you have made every choice."

With that single eye, straight, without shame.

Gorzarius had always stood before Reine with a forthright bearing.

"I have always left the choice to you, Elsreine-sama. If the council had been seized, you had the option of forcing them to comply. Why not refuse to listen to our words, refuse to release power, and do as you wished? Building a country like Her Majesty the Queen's would have been easy for you."

"……"

"But you feared the nobles' criticism, closed your mouth, ceased to decide anything yourself, and entrusted the choices to us. As what was built from that……this place is, undoubtedly, a country born of your choices, Elsreine-sama."

*And now what you can do is three things,* he said.

"You have time. Hoping that one day they may yet understand, you may continue passing your days as you have. Or you may crush the corrupt nobles by force, kill them all, and through civil war build your country anew. Or, you may believe in Her Majesty the Queen whom you doubted, return to Alberan, and apologize for all that has passed."

He folded down three raised fingers as he spoke, and Gorzarius laughed.

Why he was laughing, Reine did not know.

She could not understand.

"After the first, much misery will follow. Many will die. Including, of course, the children who have only just found happiness at the orphanage……if you flee to Alberan, they will resent you. They will say you abandoned them, that you betrayed them."

Her shoulders flinched, and she lowered her eyes.

She could not look at Gorzarius's face.

"Many of the populace will think the same. Different though it may be, all who placed their hopes in the Elsren you painted will think so. Knowing that, you should make your decision. At least no choice is, for you, easy……it is rather like a certain time before."

Sensing Gorzarius rise, Reine drew back, and her back struck the door.

Just so, Gorzarius came before Reine.

He knelt, took the tip of Reine's sword in his hand, and pressed it to his own throat.

The hand gripping the sword trembled.

"Only a little force is needed, Elsreine-sama. I will not resist. If you say I should die by my own hand, I shall press my own throat against this point. ……I am still your retainer, for as long as you require me — as I swore upon my name."

"……"

"Why do you hesitate? ……If you believe I deceived you, there is only one thing to do, is there not? If you have anger, thrust it home as it stands. If you cannot bring yourself, then give the order……I shall obey you."

How he could do this, Reine did not know.

She could not understand.

As he said, the smallest force would be his death; yet there was not a fragment of fear in him.

As though, he would not be killed.

As though he believed himself to be in the right.

She could neither pull the sword back, nor push it home, nor speak.

She only released the strength from her hand, and let it fall.

"……Truly, exasperatingly kind."

He took up the sword and placed it on the shelf.

Then Gorzarius drew a little away and turned his eyes to Reine's painting.

"From the heart, a beautiful painting. Without taint, pure……the older I grow, the more I think so. To Fiiri, who taught you many things and made you paint such a painting, I am truly grateful. ……That in this world, where the only pleasure is gambling, there should be such splendid things — that I had not thought."

He took up the frame and gazed at the figures of the people laughing in happiness.

"The inner self conveyed through painting……this painting is exactly Elsreine-sama. Since I won the gamble of advancement, what comes to mind is always this painting. Most of your paintings I have seen……always, you give me such anticipation, of what you will show me next."

*Surely we are broken in similar ways,* he said with a wry smile.

"People and things alike, no distinction — all felt as though they were tools or some such. I learned deeply how to use them, used as I should, with no hesitation at all. Of course, I do not possess the mind of you or Her Majesty the Queen……but I, too, have been broken since I was born."

In that smile, there was no malice.

Without guilt — only a smile upon his face.

"……I would even say I feel a kind of kinship, or admiration. That you, who can kill on the battlefield with such absence of feeling, who must by rights be far more broken than I, can paint such a paradise — that is a wonder; that you can be so kind is a wonder. Perhaps that is why I wish to know."

*You are truly foolish,* he said, with no tone of reproach.

Wearing that unchanging smile.

"And yet that foolishness, I think beautiful. More than anyone I have seen, you are beautiful. Her Majesty the Queen, before I met her, must surely have been such a person. Were she to paint, what would it look like……well, that is no matter."

Continuing thus, he placed the painting back upon the wall.

"Whatever the case, this too is your choice. Your answer. ……I look forward to what comes next, Elsreine-sama. The reason I live now is the paintings you paint."

——From here on too, please do not put down the brush.

Gorzarius continued so.

The first emperor of the Elsren Holy Empire, Elsreine, was a beautiful and sage ruler.

She who had built up Elsren by her supernatural ability — yet she scarcely appeared before the people, and did not give speeches.

Instead, what she presented was paintings of paradise.

By such fantastical paradises — in which old and young, men and women, shared fruit and laughed together — she is said to have guided the people.

A precise technique unmatched past or future.

A heavenly paradise woven by an obsessive level of detail and many-layered paint.

And from her paintings, the figures of adults faded, and there were depicted, in time, only the small.

Save for the one work she painted at the very end.

"Elsreine-sama……"

Fenera, who had been waiting before the carriage, ran up at the sight of Reine.

Worrying for her, she asked *Are you all right?* — and Reine quietly nodded.

The circumstances had been told to her.

But how should this result be conveyed.

What should she say. Could it be understood.

Thinking such things, intending for now to set her at ease, she tried to answer *I am all right* —

"……?"

The words would not come, and she froze.

Her eyes wandered, she was at a loss; she opened her lips to speak, closed them, and pressed her throat.

"……! Elsreine-sama, was something done to you?"

Even shaking her head to answer, no voice came.

She opened her mouth somehow trying to answer, but only breath came out.

In confusion she repeated again and again, trembling with unease, and Fenera, watching, asked.

"Could it be that……no voice will come?"

To those words she nodded, her eyes still wavering in her agitation.

In stunned silence Fenera looked upon Reine, her face contorting, and shook her head.

Then she drew Reine's body close, and held her.

"……It's all right……"

In a trembling voice, *please do not strain yourself,* she said.

# Chapter 312: Reine — End

——*From the moment I first set eyes on you, I have adored you, Reine-sama.*

To the voice that whispered with affection, Reine answered as though exasperated.

*"……I see now. You're broken, aren't you."*

*"If broken means you would accept me, then it would be convenient to answer that I am broken, would it not."*

Her shoulders shaking with mirth, Fiiri laughed like a girl.

*"But, is such a distinction necessary? All can be called broken, all can be called normal. We are all incomplete creatures, Reine-sama. Including you, of course."*

And she pointed at her own head.

*"Perfection is a thing that wants nothing, that desires nothing. Reine-sama, you may be especially beautiful, but in the end you are the same as us — a single, incomplete animal. Eating, sleeping, copulating, no different from a dog or cat — one specimen of the human species……"*

When Reine knit her brow, Fiiri smiled.

*"……Despising this trifling world, holding stubbornly to being different from others, you are only afraid unless you can believe yourself special. You only use the word 'special' as self-justification for your sense of estrangement. Bearing children to seek meaning in your existence — there is nothing in what you do that differs from any other human."*

*"……Do you want to be killed?"*

*"Hardly. In place of Benuure-sama, this is a small lesson from your tutor."*

She breezily put her lips to her tea, narrowing her eyes.

She did not break her composure; she showed no fear, no fright.

A woman, only and always, of an irritating manner.

*"In the end, a herd of beasts. Each living as he pleases, pursuing his own interest. Money, the opposite sex, power……all doing as they will. You are the same — by loving Elsreine-sama, you are simply seeking the return of being loved, using her. Even pure goodwill exists only because of the return of self-satisfaction. Whether conscious or unconscious, that varies from person to person……"*

Mockingly, Fiiri continued.

*"This world is such a world. If you say that is trifling, then you ought to die. There is no human who can love another with no return at all. ……You should know this — and yet you go on living, do you not? Is it not because you hold out hope for someone who will give you a reason to live in this trifling world?"*

*"……"*

*"But the world will not change to suit you alone. What must change is you, Reine-sama. Just by opening those closed eyes, there are any number of beings who seek your love, who wish to love you, who will give you a reason to live."*

——Of course, including myself.

She extended her hand and touched Reine's cheek, tracing her skin with affection.

*"Will you not, first, accept my love? You will not regret it, surely. So that Reine-sama need never close her eyes again……I shall grant your every wish."*

And then, withdrawing her hand, she let a laugh escape from behind her hand.

She drank down her tea and rose.

*"Today is late, so I shall take my leave for now, my queen. ……Please consider it carefully. Tomorrow morning, by way of tidying and cleaning up——! ?"*

With force she pulled at that hand and threw her onto the bed.

When she pinned the wrists of the woman who toppled without resistance, Fiiri stared at Reine in stunned silence.

*"……So you make such a face too."*

*"That is, well……I am also a human."*

So saying, her eyes wavered, her cheeks flushed, and she let out a sigh-mixed deep breath.

She loosened her tense body and gave a bashful, wry smile.

*"Does this mean, then, that you will accept my words from earlier?"*

*"Hardly. ……I simply became curious to see what manner of trifling thing the love you speak of is."*

*"Fufu, as expected of my queen. By joining bodies, you can even tell whether your partner's love is true. What a splendid talent——! "*

She kissed her, slipped in her tongue, and licked her about.

For a moment Fiiri stiffened, her great eyes wide open, but she did not resist.

*"……Be quiet."*

When she said so, the woman fell silent as commanded, eyes wavering, and nodded.

Most things in this world were like tools that spoke words.

Beings unlike Reine and her kind — out of mere caprice, she had simply tried using one in such a way.

There was no particular meaning, no acceptance of the unpleasant woman's words, no change of mind, no love.

# Chapter 316: Duty II

The Kreisharana settlements were all in the mountains.

Steep terrain, many magical beasts.

Naturally, no large land suitable for living could be secured, and the settlements were scattered into five large groups.

At the top in authority was the chieftain, who held strong power; institutionally, however, the system was a council.

There was a council of elders composed of former chieftains and retired senior warriors, and on matters concerning the entire Kreisharana, they were summoned, and conclusions were always to be reached by their discussion.

Even when opinion split, votes were not held; basically, the chieftain's view took precedence.

However, if it was not accepted, the council of elders could initiate the selection of a new chieftain, and everything was committed to a duel between the new candidate and the current chieftain.

But under a chieftain commanding respect, no one would seek to displace him.

This was rarely actually invoked; this time, in resuming relations, opinion had split somewhat, but in the end, Alkirens's opinion had passed as it stood.

A simple but not bad system.

For a great power like Alberan it might present difficulties, but for a small group like the Kreisharana, where the relationship between people and authority was close, it was easy to grasp intuitively.

Though there was risk of split from disagreement, in the Kreisharana, who valued not being captured by any desire, this system showed even stronger stability.

Below the chieftain and elders were the chief warrior and chief gatekeeper, and the great warriors.

At each turnover, twelve selected warriors were so called; their head was the chief warrior, the next, gatekeeper of the path to the divine being, was the chief gatekeeper.

Beeg, who had guided them, was one of these — an extraordinary warrior.

Basically he was the leader who ran the griffin-riders — the sky-warriors — and in emergencies they took command of the warriors under the chieftain or chief warrior.

For them, the sky-warriors who rode griffins were objects of respect and admiration.

The great warriors who became chief warrior or chief gatekeeper were always chosen from sky-warriors; otherwise there were no rank distinctions among warriors, apparently.

If one had the requisite ability as a warrior, the criterion for sky-warrior selection was skill at riding griffins.

There were warriors with superhuman strength but poor at riding, which was likely the reason.

Generally adults valued pure individual strength more than sky-warrior status, accepting the difference as a matter of role.

Below them were the levied warriors — those whose ability had not yet reached warrior level — but their quality and training were beyond comparison with regular soldiers in the kingdom.

More than that, even the trainees who had not yet been recognized as levied warriors compared individually to the children of military noble houses, born and raised in arms.

The astonishing population ratio, in which roughly nine in ten people had mana, was sufficient for the Kreisharana — small as a town, in scale — to be recognized as an independent state.

Even counted among the great powers around it, anyone who knew this would have to admit it was a state of remarkable military strength.

At least taking geography into account, it was not hard to imagine its small force, less than two battalions, easily turning back a corps.

It was thought even among them that the dense mana the divine being emitted strongly affected the birth rate of mana-holders, and the ratio had grown larger year on year.

Eventually scholars specializing in research on this should be brought — at the very least, here many insights unobtainable in Alberan would be gained.

Even in academic terms, this place was a treasure mountain.

Exchange with the Kreisharana would no doubt bring great fruit to the kingdom, and considering this, his role was important indeed.

Wielding a hoe, breaking up the earth, he wiped his sweat with a towel.

"You needn't do field work, Marquis……"

"More hands are better. At least in this place I am the most ignorant and useless, so I must at least help with manual labor."

Argoush, never haughty or affected, was a noble unlike a noble; they knew this and from the heart loved that aspect of their lord.

The captain of the escorts, who had served Argoush for many years.

Sharp-eyed, first-class with the sword.

Here he was in charge of the field, directing it.

The other three around him were also of farming villages.

This time, unlike the usual escort, even though small in number, he had carefully chosen people with specific knowledge of mountains and fields.

The remaining escorts were foraging and hunting in the forest.

Water-drinking potato — a kind of potato called gurish.

Not particularly delicious, but on barren land too, with only water it grew, hence "water-drinking potato."

Originally a southern variety, it was weak to deep snow, but Albyagel's winters were astonishingly mild.

The corner where craftsmen gathered.

The well at its edge — but already there were over a dozen men there.

# Chapter 319: Duty V

The Jade Tiger spends most of its time sleeping.

When it senses something, it wakes from the shallow beast-sleep, but normally, only when very hungry is it active during sunlit hours.

They have keen night-vision, frighteningly fine hearing and smell, and even on a cloudy new moon they hunt as a matter of course.

They are most active for about two *koku* (four hours) after dusk turns to night.

Just after the diurnal beasts begin to sleep.

Then they walk for several *koku* searching for prey, satisfied, and sleep again on the spot.

Disliking rain, they often sleep beneath trees, but it does not seem they hide.

Jade Tigers are kings of the forest.

In the forest, almost the only beings that attack them are their own kind.

Long ago, many magical beasts had been here, but because of them, most had fled to other lands or been devoured.

What remains are about indigo deer and storm-wings.

If we set the dragon aside, the storm-wing stands at the apex of this range; in any age there is always one in flight.

Probably because of the breadth of its territory — even if two existed at once, one would be devoured in conflict, or the other would flee.

Therefore, they were like a natural calamity not directly affecting the power balance of this land.

The indigo deer was an opponent even Jade Tigers risked their lives to hunt.

They often kept distance even when they recognized one another, guarding each other's territory.

Magical beasts' territories were broad — these two species ruled and filled the mountain, and in their gaps the Kreisharana built settlements.

Trouble always arose when that balance broke.

If, for instance, a Jade Tiger from the next mountain, hungry, attacked an indigo deer, the resulting distortion would chain through to the village's surroundings.

A single distortion grew into a great wave, affecting all of the mountains.

The most affected were the people of the Kreisharana, dwelling in the gaps of those territories.

Three days ago, news came that an indigo deer had rampaged a little to the west.

Griffin-riders search for Jade Tigers near the village, and indigo deer when farther.

The indigo deer, said to have specimens twenty shaku at the shoulder in history, had bodies nearly twice the size of a great Jade Tiger.

Their territories were perfect for marking; if they fought a Tiger, the surrounding trees would be felled, recognizable at a glance.

Further west — many beasts had fled the storm-wing dancing in the sky and had begun invading other territories.

Magical beasts are especially fast.

The propagation of the wave is proportional.

The whole village was tense, and the women had taken to sleeping in the warehouse.

They did not forage or hunt; cooking and meals too were done together at the village center.

Lila and some women, accustomed to caring for griffins and to riding, also went up to the sky during the day, so always someone was airborne.

But even this was daily life for the Kreisharana.

Neither men nor women trembled in fear; they laughed and lived as usual.

*Once we get through this, there's the feast,* and the women in spare time were making festival clothes and ornaments; the men too were excited, talking about *who I'll confess my feelings to at the next feast.*

It meant each individual accepted even the danger of death as something near.

Lila.

In this situation with magical beasts approaching.

In Lila's place, another lent his shoulder.

Leaning on him, he said.

"I only fulfilled my duty. ……But the situation does not allow lowering one's guard yet. The rest, please, Lord Doukaran."

"……Hardly need saying. The wounded are a hindrance, go."

"……Doukaran."

Argoush smiled wryly and left the plaza.

The excitement of battle had perhaps dulled the pain.

For about three days, just light movement caused intense pain.

Among the warriors there were several similar wounded; they were laid together in the women's meeting hall and tended.

He did not lack conversation, fortunately.

The women applying ointment for bruises, giving painkillers, tending the wound said *we are deeply grateful* and told him this and that of outside; his subordinates also visited when free and spoke of the field.

As fellow wounded, he conversed with the resting warriors as a pastime.

Talking of how each had gotten hurt, time passed gently.

He was the kind not given to staying still, but thanks to them feeling the same, he was able to be patient.

Fortunately, this time no one died.

The man flung from atop the tower, whom he had seen, was there too.

Like Argoush, in addition to chest bruises, his neck was fixed, his right arm broken, his left leg sprained.

His bed was right next.

Seeing him, he didn't have to lament *it hurts, it hurts,* and the man, still young, was amusing.

Hearing Argoush had a wife and children, he even asked for romance advice, which the other men laughed at.

The one tending him at that moment was apparently the object of his affection; being tended to by her, he froze stiff like stone, even Argoush could not help but laugh.

*He must love her truly,* he recalled how much trouble his oblivious wife had given him.

Gentle and unadorned, when she came as a servant he had been smitten at once, but it took five years for her to notice his feelings.

In his wife's mind, *we became close naturally and grew aware of each other while spending time together,* but she still does not understand how much effort Argoush put into making her notice his feelings.

Recalling such a wife, he thought of Anne.

Anne had inherited her mother's good points and difficult points alike, but she had also inherited Argoush's bad points; he was worried for her future.

He was glad she was happy now beside the queen, but as a father he worried for what came after.

Sharing such family talk, days passed.

He was permitted to be up and about after about a week.

Right around then Vinsreel and the others returned; perhaps having heard, they thanked Argoush deeply.

In emergencies, the warriors did not generally pay sick visits, the rule.

Lila visited often, but no warrior did; the warriors were faithful to their duty.

But going outside, from young to old, many men greeted Argoush with smiles and slapped his shoulder.

Those who had earlier eyed Argoush suspiciously had reconsidered.

That he had nearly died and been wounded was a small thing compared to being accepted by them.

The pain too was even proud — but he did not say it.

In other settlements, of course there had been deaths.

To them, that was daily life.

In Alberan, even soldiers facing down Jade Tigers were lauded as brave.

Even just thrusting a spear was praised as heroic, with rewards from generals.

For a kill, the queen herself would reward; one would even be granted the title of Rinea.

But among them, that was the minimum.

Standing against the Tiger was something one ought to have as a matter of course.

Recalling the courage he had wrung out in fear, he could not boast it among them.

Argoush too accepted it as a matter of course, and as a matter of course returned to daily life.

Mornings he again lightly swung the sword, dropping by the field to look at the harvest.

Harvesting the well-grown water-drinking potatoes, *please let everyone in the village taste them,* he said to Lila, and was told that with the mountain calm and the trouble passed, a feast to celebrate would be held.

So Argoush too went to the feast.

Men competed in spear and bow, and only at this time the women dressed lightly and danced.

Drinking, eating meat, immersing themselves in feast.

Compared to a festival in an Alberan town, it was modest.

No different from those held in country villages.

But seeing them laugh from the heart, Argoush thought it was no less than a town festival.

Watching that wounded man stiffening with the woman of his affection, he smiled wryly,

"Hey."

A voice came from behind.

When he turned, there was a giant to look up to.

Wearing — surely the recent one — a Jade Tiger pelt.

"Lord Doukaran. I'm taking up the lowest seat."

The subordinates beside him grew slightly tense; seeing Argoush nod and shift slightly aside, they too shifted their seats.

Since then, he had hardly spoken with Doukaran.

When thanked, *I have no business being thanked by you,* he had walked off, never approaching or speaking to Argoush.

That he had come on his own was a relief; he poured the wine he had brought into an empty cup.

The only gift accepted — at every seat, the same was being drunk.

Doukaran sat silently and drained the proffered cup.

And said.

"Plain country wine isn't bad either."

"How about potatoes as a side?"

"Already had them. Not bad, but not good either."

"They take to barren land and grow easily — that's their merit. These were prepared as a side, though."

Thinly sliced water-drinking potatoes lightly fried in oil.

If one masked them with salt and oil, as a side they were not bad.

Doukaran took one and ate it, taking the wine bottle.

Argoush bowed his head slightly and received the pour with his cup.

Doukaran poured wine into his own cup, and glared at Argoush, who held out his cup for a toast.

Then he raised his cup just slightly as in a toast, and put his lips to it.

Argoush smiled wryly and did the same.

"I haven't accepted you. Your skill is third-rate as a warrior; for that you spoke as though above us. A hundred-to-one rescue; thank Lasherna."

Lasherna was the Kreisharana's old hunter-goddess.

A faith from the plains times, separate from the divine being.

Alberan had a similar goddess called Laiserna. The roots are surely the same.

"Yes. Without Lord Doukaran's spear, the Tiger would have been an instant ahead."

"Notice before you step in. And you weren't seeing your surroundings either. If you stayed calm, you'd have seen me readying my spear."

"……My apologies."

"Third-rates mistake recklessness for courage. Just because the others praise you, don't get conceited. Even standing on two feet, an infant standing is praised."

Even spoken to harshly, he was honestly glad.

Because it meant Doukaran was calling him a third-rate *warrior.*

"Leather armor is useless against a Tiger. If you must wear something, use this."

From behind, what he handed over was a steel cuirass.

A thick, heavy thing, unblemished, still new.

Surprised, eyes wide, Doukaran drained his cup again and stood.

Removing the Tiger pelt, he set it down and turned his back.

"Field work is done, the wound healed. From tomorrow, instead of a hoe, take a sword and come to me. I'll teach you a real sword — not for show."

"Yes. This——"

"I just put it on for the rule. I only finished it. The first spear was yours. Do as you like."

Saying so, he walked off, and Argoush, watching him in a daze, picked up the Tiger pelt.

Heavy, the fur like wire.

Strong against bruises and slashes; many warriors liked to wear it.

In the Kreisharana, perhaps because too heavy, light fur was favored, but it was still precious.

Even in Alberan it was very expensive, but it had value beyond that.

Watching Doukaran's back, he smiled wryly and bowed, then carefully folded it.

"Marquis……"

"A new family heirloom. ……From tomorrow, I go to Lord Doukaran's."

"……Yes."

Stacking it together with the cuirass, he set them beside him and quietly smiled.

"……He will teach us a real warrior's sword."

To live up to the noble he had once held in his eyes.

As if to savor the realization that he had fulfilled his duty.
