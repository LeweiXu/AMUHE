# Chapter 361: In a Far, Far Future IV

"……I cannot accept this in the slightest."

"Now, now…… Kreschenta-sama, it is only a piece of recreational entertainment."

The place was the villa called the Manor's Second House.

Although they were ladies who did everything by hand and loved the good old ways, they nevertheless had a respectable interest in the progress of the times.

That world over there, while repeating progress and decline, had still produced all manner of inventions, and it was unwholesome to ignore all of them and continue an old-fashioned life.

Yet, were they to incorporate such inventions indiscriminately, they might end up casting away the old-fashioned life they so loved —— and so what they conceived of was the Manor's Second House.

The Manor's First House, where they ordinarily dwelled, kept its prior way of life intact.

In this Second House, however, they would actively and freely incorporate the inventions of over there, and partake of humanity's progress.

Inventions like washing machines and vacuum cleaners went without saying; the spacious garden had been paved into a circuit course.

Cars and two-wheeled vehicles, drones and air-boards that ran through the sky stood lined up in a row, and the occasionally-held event was the Manor Grand Prix that even Gururun could enjoy.

Within the manor were fitted every kind of recreational arithmetic engine gathered from all over the world; rather than a manor, it was more correct to call it a large entertainment facility.

The Third Recreation Room of the Manor's Second House.

Projected at the center of the room was the game screen of the latest installment in the strategy-simulation series *Records of Alberan*: *Alberan of Forgetfulness 7*.

It was a simulation game set in the period of that time, with simplified yet reality-focused in-game economics.

On the battlefield, every soldier was individually controlled, and from a single soldier on the battlefield to a commander to a ruler, one could enjoy a game experience matching one's playstyle — it was a hardcore game that was much talked about.

The series seemed to have continued for some two thousand years, and this company had released, not just this title, several other games reproducing the world of that period.

Sitting on the sofa, atop the red-haired servant, the ancient queen Kreschenta (the genuine one), wearing a white one-piece dress, gripped the controller in evident displeasure.

What she was glaring at was the ancient queen Kreschenta (the fake) displayed there.

To be precise, she was looking at the stat values displayed there and creasing her brow.

"However one looks at it, my abilities are far too low compared to Ojii-sama's. My political ability has even been chased down to a level approaching Selene-sama's, of all people. What in the world can one be thinking to come up with this."

"……Truly rude, isn't she. Well, certainly, I do feel I'm being overrated in various ways…… and conversely, Father is being slightly underrated."

Selene, sitting beside her, also gripping a controller, said this with a faintly dissatisfied tone.

"It is something from very long ago, so it can't be helped. Like Margrave Agrand and Margrave Velraigh, those of that age who were splendid warriors do seem to have been properly evaluated…… if anything, more than I had imagined. See, even individual anecdotes are compiled here…… if anything, one rather admires it."

"……There's an anecdote here I have absolutely no memory of. Apparently in his late years he cut down assassins from his wheelchair…… just what kind of situation is that supposed to be."

"Fufu, that too is what one calls history."

Bery shook her shoulders happily.

"In olden days I was somewhat resistant to it, but…… after this much time has passed, that feeling has become rather faded as well. The figures depicted here are, after all, the people of those times as imagined by people of the current era, so it is only natural they should differ from the actual figures."

"Well, certainly…… at this point, it does feel a bit late to mind. The drama from sometime back where you were the leader of evil — I couldn't help but laugh…… though in a sense, that wasn't wrong."

"That was a rather novel angle…… with even Krische-sama being a magical doll I had created, and so on."

Smiling wryly, she rubbed her cheek against the head of Krische, who was clinging to her left arm asleep.

"However…… for events of fifteen thousand years ago, this does seem too accurate, somehow."

"Too accurate……?"

At the voice of Elvena, who, like the two of them, was gripping a controller, Bery tilted her head.

"I have, from time to time, looked over historical writings concerning that period…… but ever since the great incident of the crustal upheaval lost so many records, descriptions concerning Alberan have become quite scarce. Of course, those concerning the principal figures remained……"

Once, the inhabitants of this planet had been exposed to the crisis of extinction.

A great crustal upheaval, with simultaneous volcanic eruptions and an ash winter.

For a time people had been driven to such an extremity that they could not survive outside mana shelters, and even here there had been several discussions about whether or not to help them.

If they wished, Krische or Kreschenta could deal with it easily.

Yet there was also a strong view that, as long as it did not threaten this paradise, they themselves ought not to interfere.

Having stepped outside the ring of the world of humans, they ought to draw the minimum line.

Without the resolve to become a true god, they should not interfere lightly — that was the conclusion they reached.

In due course the World Tree, seeming to respond to the unconscious wishes of the people, gradually removed the ash that had covered most of the planet, and they were relieved; but for some decades everyone wore somewhat dark expressions.

*"This planet is my garden. To delight my eyes, and to keep the garden clean so that I may walk in it comfortably, is also a duty of my retainers."*

After that, with a single phrase befitting Her Majesty the Queen that Kreschenta let resound for the first time in several thousand years, the standard was greatly lowered.

For natural disasters beyond the hands of the people of that era —— in short, "rather than fixing on trifles and pulling long faces, just quietly help them in secret," at the great queen's command, the policy was firmed up: thereafter surveillance was strengthened, and great calamities would be prevented before they occurred. But because of such an incident, civilization had once declined dramatically.

Much knowledge had been lost from the people, and especially records of low practical use.

The unified calendar that had once been in use was again split apart, and records from before the division came to be widely treated as separate, unreliable material.

From their standpoint, knowledge had merely been lost; it was, manifestly, one continuous history. But from the standpoint of the people who lived through that era, it was something quite different.

Just as the great empire of Lusheran, which once boasted of its glory and was destroyed by Kushenalars, had been to themselves a fairy-tale legend.

For people who, having declined, struggled even with maintaining simple magical implements, the existence of a magical civilization that could once mass-produce such things must have been a fanciful tale on the level of myth.

The long years from the end of the ash winter until what had been lost was retrieved.

Many primary sources were lost, and they had witnessed firsthand how, as people went through the generations, their own time turned into a myth or legend mingled with fiction.

"It is, I think, due to that discussion, but after the visit of the Minarshi, a sweeping reexamination of materials concerning Alberan and Kleinmeil was carried out, and the materials remaining at that time should mostly have been combed through. It is hard to imagine new historical materials being found at this late date."

"……Yes, indeed; given the situation, the Minarshi-san probably investigated in earnest."

At the time of the Minarshi's visit, there had been a small commotion.

Krische, on a hasty conclusion that took the danger of these aliens destroying the planet seriously, had her existence laid bare and put them on quite high alert —— and as a result, they had ended up mediating a peace between them and the amoebas called Alnia.

In the pouch at Krische's waist, she took up the *unyon* curled and squirming in sleep, and smiled wryly.

The aliens seemed to be honestly observing the conditions — that they would forget their own existence and never again involve themselves — and the manor was at peace even now.

The size of their fear of Krische's outlandish power may also have played a large role —— but in any case, at that time, investigating themselves must have been an urgent task for them.

There was no doubt they had gathered up and studied historical materials concerning Alberan, a state of the far distant past, and that those should have become the basis of present-day historical writings.

"So you mean, what is recorded here is unnaturally accurate."

"Yes. There are several persons who shouldn't, by present-day records, remain…… though I may simply be misremembering."

Selene knit her brow as she gazed at the projected commander units.

"Elvena-sama does have a fairly good memory. If you say so, then that is so. The names of civilian-officer nobility are slapdash, but as for nobles holding military ranks, almost all are real persons…… I had unexpectedly assumed such records had been preserved properly."

*It is somewhat troubling*, said Kreschenta, and turned her eyes to the sleeping Krische.

Bery, Selene, and Elvena, too, somehow turned their eyes toward Krische.

Incidents that occurred at the manor were mostly Krische's fault.

The light of mana into the inside of the recreational arithmetic engine —— Kreschenta etched a crease into her brow.

*My*, Bery said, surprised, and ran her mana through, tracing the path Kreschenta had made.

Far from the description "crude," it was a highly concealed cipher, woven into the system part built for body-experience play through mana operation.

People of today, who knew only the four-symbol two-element inscription, would not even notice it.

Reading the recorded letters, the servant tilted her small head.

In the parlour of Dougru's house, beside Mili scratching her cheek, the black-haired girl was bowing her head deeply.

She was Mili's classmate from childhood and her best friend —— her name was Saria.

She had apparently come to play at Ors's house many times, and her acquaintance with Sena was likewise long; come to think of it, he did remember once seeing her with Mili before she turned herself in.

"……I understand the circumstances. As regards Sena-san being a pure game-development collaborator, I swear that I will never let her origin leak; if necessary, I shall provide a written oath. And of course, if the doctor is still concerned even so, I shall give it up, and bear what I have heard today to my grave."

"I understand your feelings. Now, please raise your head."

*Yes*, said the bespectacled girl, lifting her face, looking extremely tense.

She was apparently quite a game enthusiast, and Mili had just been invited by her to develop a game.

The content was a simulation game set in the ancient Kingdom of Alberan,

Alberan, as the beginning of the magical civilization that came after, was a major period even among history enthusiasts, and so it must have been before humanity was exposed to the threat of extinction.

The land of the children of god, preserved in countless plays, stories, and traditions.

She, too, was an enthusiastic fan of that period.

Mili, too, as the daughter of a great corporate president, apparently had the desire to make something of her own without relying on her parent's power.

She had taken up the project, but, faced with the enormous data production demanded by Saria, she had been at her wit's end. Then, upon learning of Sena's origin the other day, before thinking —— so it had gone.

Mili, her eyes welling with tears, was scolded by an exasperated Ors who asked whether she understood what a delicate matter this was; but Sena herself was as usual.

*"If it is Mili-sama's request, I personally don't mind."*

So she had said.

Sena was generally like that.

Even when being talked about herself, she didn't seem particularly interested, and even now, listening beside them, was a cookie-breaking puppet.

*Just tell me the conclusion, that's all I need*, was her usual stance.

Of course, when asked her opinion she would speak her thoughts, but if not addressed, she stayed silent except for the pronunciation corrections.

Tell her that for various reasons one wished to conceal her origin, and she would meekly comply; about herself and about most things she did not seem interested.

Returning favors, and to that end fulfilling requests — those two things were the whole of her.

But for that very reason, her words just now had been a little surprising.

The number-one target of her "favors-to-return" was probably Dougru, and the second was probably Ors.

Anyone hearing it would call Ors's lecture proper, and Ors's view, considering Sena's future, was the safe-side choice.

Judging mechanically, rationally, Ors's view should take priority; ordinarily it would have been a scene where she would not have inserted herself.

Yet Sena, after glancing once at Mili's tearful face, had said as she did.

The same emotionless flat expression as always, the same face, the same tone of voice.

That Dougru had said, *We could at least listen to what they have to say*, was because that one phrase had felt unexpected.

Strangely so, for some reason.

Because his own heart — feeling it unexpected — felt like a very important thing.

"Sena, what do you wish to do?"

"As I said earlier……if Doctor Igaguri permits it, there is no particular reason to refuse Mili-sama's request."

When asked, Sena tilted her head and answered, and Dougru went on to ask:

"Why did you decide to listen to Mili-chan's request?"

"Why?"

"Ors-san was thinking first of your safety, and the lecture was, I thought, a fitting one. The same was true for Mili-chan, wasn't it?"

"Eh? Ah, yes…… any way you look at it, I was the one in the wrong……"

Mili said this awkwardly, and *I'm not blaming you*, Dougru said, smiling wryly.

"It seemed to me that you said what you did out of consideration for Mili-chan, who was being lectured."

"……?"

"Mili-chan was being scolded and was about to cry, and so it looked to me that you spoke up at that moment. Am I wrong?"

*You are not wrong*, Sena answered, and Mili's eyes went wide.

"For example, suppose Mili-chan had been calm. Would you, in the same way, have inserted yourself into Ors-san's lecture?"

"No. Mili-sama beginning to cry was the deciding factor at that scene."

"Why?"

"If Mili-sama begins to cry, the trouble will increase."

At those words Mili froze, and Dougru smiled wryly.

"Then, very well. What do you think of Ors-san's having scolded Mili-chan? Do you think it was an unjust rebuke?"

"No. I consider it a proper scolding, with the concealment of my origin as its first concern."

"That's right, surely. To put it bluntly, Mili-chan made a slightly troublesome selfish request, and received a natural scolding. Compared to the risk of your origin coming to light, the trouble of Mili-chan crying is a small matter. Yet you tried to shield her."

Dougru nodded.

"Don't you think that's an irrational judgement?"

He smiled at her, and said so.

"……Irrational."

"If your origin were exposed, it would be trouble not just for you, but for many people, including myself and Ors-san. Merely because Mili-chan would be sad — even if only slightly — you accepted increasing that risk."

At those words Sena knit her brow, and seemed to fall into thought.

Saria spoke up in a fluster.

"U, uhm, Doctor…… ours was just talk about game development; if you say it cannot be done, then truly——"

"Ah, I am sorry. I am not blaming you, nor am I blaming Sena. On the contrary, as for me, I'd like to welcome your proposal."

"Eh, what, what do you mean……?"

"I made a single promise with her. The matter just now has to do with that."

It was also one of the reasons he had entrusted Sena to Ors.

For better or worse he was an adult, and currently single, so more might be gained from being around the child Mili, raised in her parents' love, than from him.

Sena was supremely capable, but mentally she was like a child.

He had felt it would be good to once let her see children, see what a family was like.

Probably, that thought had been correct.

"Any objections, Sena?"

"……Within my own priorities and judgement-criteria, I believe it was a proper judgement. But, as you say, I also believe it cannot be called a rational judgement."

*Kote*, tilting her head, Sena said, looking puzzled,

"It's difficult."

"Difficult, isn't it."

Dougru laughed pleasantly.

"But I think that realization is a very precious thing."

"……Precious."

"Humans are far more irrational creatures than you imagine. To avoid a small immediate disadvantage, they accept a greater disadvantage further down the line, or let go of a great gain. To call it an issue of intelligence is easy, but I don't think it's that."

*For example, well*, he picked up two cookies.

"There are others among your kin. Beings born with the same intelligence and abilities. Yet by now, surely, they have entirely different thoughts, different abilities. What do you think causes that difference?"

"The difference in accumulated experience, I would think."

"That's right. The difference in what realizations one has gained from past experiences. For example, supposing there is a you who never fought the red-haired woman, do you think she would have any doubts about the rule that forbade it?"

*No*, Sena answered, and Dougru nodded.

"That doubt is yours alone. And such various realizations of that kind have shaped the you of today. There are surely kin of yours who held the same realizations, yet now you are no longer the same being as those kin, and humans are likewise…… you have, all this while, been drawing closer to the answer you want."

"……Is that so?"

"Yes. I think so."

Looking at the two who seemed unable to follow the conversation, Dougru smiled.

"If you are knowledgeable about Alberan…… surely you know the reason why she who once dominated the battlefield was captured by the allied forces?"

"Eh…… yes."

"Why Alberinea gave herself such a weakness — that, apparently, has long been Sena's question."

The two of them rounded their eyes and looked at each other.

"When I heard that, I thought she should find the reason herself. To know and to understand are similar yet different things."

As he spoke, he handed Sena a cookie.

"That promise concerns this. I told her I would help her in finding it. And on top of that, I also thought that your proposal of making something called a game would be a good thing for her. …… To create something to please someone, that will surely give her many experiences and realizations."

So saying, he put the cookie in his mouth.

She had apparently already mastered various dishes from the start, but what she made daily were cookies.

Old-fashioned, plain, honey cookies.

Alberinea had recorded that they were the most basic and finest cookies, taught by a woman called Bery; she must have loved them dearly.

Within her evaluation criteria, the name Bery was carved everywhere.

The red-haired servant who had served Alberinea.

Jaleia Gashea was a being born of love for that servant.

Sena was, to begin with, not built to be rational.

Her judgement criteria, her principles of action, her oddly weighted "favor-returns."

She already knows, Dougru thought, and is simply not aware of it.

What Dougru had to do was only to provide a trigger.

That alone, sooner or later, she would realize.

So convinced was he, because she had been brought into being far too gently.

"If you don't mind, I'd like you two to help her in her search for the answer too. If that's all right, I will also cooperate to the extent I am able."

When he asked, *How does that sound*, the two looked at each other again, nodded, and bowed their heads deeply.

Thinking about the future is enjoyable.

All the more so when it concerns the young.

Dougru, depending on how one looked at him, could still be called young in years; but probably, when his beloved daughter was born, he had moved over to the watching side.

Not what he would do from now on, but how those girls would live going forward.

He spent his time alongside them, thinking of such futures.

Eventually he would die, and Sena and the rest would walk on through the world that came after.

He had been thinking of various things about Sena's path, but unforeseen things happen — that, too, is life.

Rather than the original plan of using her superb abilities to help with Ors's company, accompanying the impulse-driven enthusiasm of young Mili and the others would be more her, and would be good.

Alberinea was, even now, a person he could not really understand, but somehow, watching Sena, it felt to him that she may have been someone who lived just like that.

To make a deranged combat machine into a servant — it could not be called the act of a sane person.

Cooking recipes and household functions casually plugged in.

A nickname-evaluation criterion of unknown utility, and a strange fixation on demanding accurate names through pronunciation guidance.

*"You see, Sena-san. I understand 'jaragasha' is the proper name, but please don't just correct it on your own, all right? Here it's Jale——"*

*"It's 'jaragasha,' Mili-sama."*

*"Ah, Sena-san? Both Mili and I would dearly love to use the splendid form 'jaragasha' as the accurate pronunciation, but in order to conceal Sena-san's origin, we have to use a different name here, or it will trouble the doctor and the others."*

*"……I see."*

She had probably been a person like an innocent child — that thought fitted neatly.

A little stubborn, fixated on her preferences, free and independent.

The moment those two came home from university, they would lose themselves in *no, not like that* and *yes, like this*, and Sena would go along with the two, making for a lively scene reminiscent of the old days.

As if drawn by that atmosphere, Dougru, too, when he went to Roctana Company's development division, devoted himself to instructing the next-generation researchers.

On weekends he drank at his usual bar, sometimes joined by Ors as well.

As if returned to the old days, they spoke together about the future.

His daughter, taking the springboard of the entirely-unsold first work and pouring even more passion than before into the second, must have moved something in him.

*Why don't we try something new*, Ors invited Dougru into the research and development of working suits for outer space.

A working suit that diverted ether-biological prosthetic-limb technology, supporting movement without obstructing the body, and well-suited to movement and precision work in zero-gravity space.

If that received recognition and gained citizenship, ether-biological prosthetic bodies that replaced the entire human body would be easier to release into the world, and the soil for accepting beings like Sena would gradually be prepared.

In modern times, in every place, there are surveillance networks for crime prevention.

Sena seemed at first glance no different from a human, but, looked at through cameras that examined subjects mechanically, she was a distorted being.

There was almost no human whose entire body was prosthetic, and wherever one went one would stand out.

Even with no malice in it, simply going about an ordinary daily life, one would be looked at with special eyes.

*To hide a tree, a forest is best* — he had long thought so.

Ors's plan was a roundabout one taking absurd numbers of years; but even so, considering the absurd number of years Sena had ahead of her, it was not a bad one.

Whether the seed would sprout as he hoped, he did not know; but if a hundred, two hundred — a thousand years on — such a future was possible, then it was a dream worth dreaming.

Dougru accepted that proposal, and once again, taking his hand, returned to the path of research.

—— Looking back, it had been a life that never grew tiresome.

"Doctor Igaguri, the tea is ready."

"……Ah, thank you."

When he sat up in bed, scratching his head, he turned toward the side table.

His short hair had also gone fully white, and he had grown quite used to it.

After that, every time Dougru's hair grew out, Sena would tell him, *Shall I tidy your hair?* — thanks to which, he had spent his time with the in-between hairstyle of slightly-grown-out shaved hair.

She must have liked the name "Igaguri" very much.

The first time he saw a sad expression on her face was when Dougru said he wanted to grow his hair out a bit.

*"……S, Sena?"*

*"……No. If you say my care is unnecessary, then nothing."*

*"T-that's not what I meant——"*

After that, Dougru never again put in a word about her care of his hair.

Sena, in her usual expressionless face, sat down on the bedside chair, eating a cookie, and watched Dougru.

"It has worsened. You still have no intention of replacing yourself with a prosthetic body?"

"The era hasn't caught up yet. And even if I were to live longer, there are still technical limits to the brain's lifespan…… I wouldn't be able to watch over you all the way."

Fifty years had passed in the blink of an eye.

Development had gone well; he had also developed, further, a skin-suit that allowed even those who could not use ether to handle virtual musculature, and the latest model of that was beginning to enter the market.

*Ah, I have done what I could do*, he thought — and at that point, he collapsed.

Internal ether anomalies, what could be called the lifespan of a long-lived species.

Even in modern times, when so many things have been clarified, ether is still full of things not understood.

The basic palliative treatment was a brain transplant.

The fundamental problem was immune deficiency and organ failure due to losing ether protection.

Transplant the brain into a vat-grown body and one could be saved; with the ether-biological prosthetic body that he had built with her, one could even obtain a body better than before.

There were many who wished to live on in such a fashion, but Dougru's life so far was already half a bonus existence.

On top of that, having reached the point where he felt he had done all he could, he thought it was best to leave it to nature.

"……There is a theory, however."

"……?"

"That we have something called a soul, which we cannot perceive, and the body is its vessel. That is what manipulates ether, so they say."

Among ether scholars, those who supported such soul-hypotheses were not few.

Even after the brain — said to govern thought — was elucidated, mankind had still not been able to elucidate just what influences ether.

By examining the brain's workings while one moved ether, they could elucidate trends to a certain degree; but how it directly influenced ether, they did not understand.

"It hasn't been scientifically proven; it's something like a faith."

"……A faith."

*I, too, was half in doubt*, he laughed.

"In particular, for long-lived species like us, the timing of losing the ability to manipulate ether is often after one accomplishes something, or after one is satisfied…… well, they say it often happens at the moment that soul has finished what it has to do in this world. Now that I'm experiencing it, it does seem so."

"You mean you wish to die?"

"It is not a backwards-leaning thought, but more like, I am at peace."

He smiled wryly at her characteristically direct words, and brought the black tea to his lips.

He was still healthy enough to drink her tea by his own hand.

The game development he had been doing with Mili and the others had become quite a topic with their second work, and the three had founded a company.

There had been some problems, but a while afterward it became a subsidiary of Roctana Company, and now occupied a corner of its entertainment business.

Mili and Saria, by various behind-the-scenes maneuvers, had tried to seat Sena as the next president, and the news that a synthetic intelligence might be appointed company president had stirred the news a little while ago.

There had, as expected, been some commotion; but owing perhaps to the nature of the entertainment business — providing recreation to people — and to Ors and the others' behind-the-scenes work, most of the media reported it favorably, and it did not turn into a great — violent — uproar.

In the interviews along the way, the dishonorable nickname "Doctor Igaguri" came to be known to the world, but if her serious-yet-comically-easygoing side helped to dispel concerns of a "synthetic intelligence rebelling against humans," then it wasn't bad.

In any case, her future was not a dark one, and was lit by bright light.

"You have been accepted by society. There will be various problems from now on, but you will surely overcome them. And on top of that, my research, too, has reached a stopping point…… when I thought, *a good future is waiting for you ahead*, the strength left me."

So said Dougru, smiling.

"What I should call my soul, surely, was satisfied at *this is fine.*"

"……It's difficult."

"Fufu, difficult, isn't it."

The same expressionless face as always.

Yet, just slightly, a face that looked even a little lonely.

"Will it be lonely if I am gone?"

"I will not have been able to return the favor. So it will be inconvenient."

*I see*, Dougru smiled wryly, and stroked her head.

"As a hint to that question of yours from sometime back, well."

To Sena, who tilted her head, Dougru said:

"Probably, Alberinea had many favors-to-return. And to do those favors-to-return was, for Alberinea, the most important thing of all."

"Many favors-to-return……?"

"Yes. So many that they could never be returned."

When he stroked her head, her eyes narrowed; just a little, the corners of her mouth softened.

By such small things, Sena had surely accumulated many "favors-to-return."

In that way, she would go on accumulating, forever, formless "favors-to-return."

Returning and returning again, never feeling she had returned enough — that something called "favors-to-return."

Why did Alberinea not give it a different name, he thought.

There must have been, much more than that, a far more fitting name.

Were that the case, she would not have agonized so, and surely the question would have melted away easily.

In a setting of Alberan, surely she had seen anecdotes of herself many times.

Even so, she did not realize, and remained troubled.

"It's like a nickname you would give it."

"……?"

"Alberinea, I think, left that as homework — what kind of wonderful name you would give it. Just a feeling, though."

Alberinea must have treasured that emotion above anything.

It was not a thing for someone else to teach lightly; it was a thing one decided for oneself.

"At any rate, if Alberinea were to see you now, surely she would be very pleased."

"Is that so?"

"Yes. Because Alberinea must have wished for you, who would agonize so earnestly, to be so."

Dougru closed his eyes and nodded.

"She is a person who passed away long, long ago…… but, watching you, even I can properly understand what she wished for in bringing you forth."

Given much knowledge, yet, like an infant, knowing nothing.

Given only the necessary materials, an innocent child puzzled and bewildered.

Because she had wished to value the process by which such a girl, by her own strength, would arrive at an answer, that was why she was so mismatched.

Probably, she had been someone who treasured the irrational above all.

That alone was something he could understand, Dougru thought, and quietly smiled, and brought the black tea to his lips.

Unpleasant, but factual.

In a depression with mana-crystal cliffs, sleeping, a giant body like a small mountain.

Despite the body of a child, looking up like one looking down.

# Chapter 366: Alberan II

Aldos's warriors are strong.

Face one direction and be shot from behind from the other.

*Pyon*, finally jumping down, she looked dazzled at the sky.

Eyes that look up yet look down.

Were Bernaich a mere tough, he would have been eaten by the Roukai.

Same with the duel.

Seven years from the Roukai subjugation, having firmed his foundation, he challenged the previous lord, and seized the great chieftain's seat in a way that allowed no one to object.

Not just strong, a man with the calmness to know where to use power.

By no means could one be careless.

"If on the contrary he is underestimating me, that is most welcome. But the rumor turned out to be a worthless rumor after all."

"That. Crushed by some small fry."

A subordinate tribe had shown rebellion against Aldos, and Bernaich himself went to chastise but suffered a complete defeat — such content.

There were many tales of collapse because a fool became great chieftain.

That said, the Aldos led by that Bernaich.

He had thought such a story unlikely, and seeing the present situation of intrusion, the rumor was probably just a rumor.

Only utter fools attack outside while leaving worries inside.

A youth like a grandson he had cared for from infancy —— with many faults including his mouth, but a beloved young man with talent and a future.

*May the unease not strike the wrong way*, he offered prayer to the god of valor.

# Chapter 367: Alberan III

The battlefield was the Forest of Arendor.

In this whole area, the *Argana* trees, growing straight and high-branching, were the central tree.

Argana made fine timber, but ease of felling was another advantage.

Around the shallow-and-wide-rooted Argana, thickets did not grow densely; only undergrowth covered the ground —— transport and various tasks were easier.

Spears, shields, bows and arrows, even buildings — wood was used for everything.

Forests rooted with quality Argana had very high value, and fighting over them in war was not rare.

Visibility was not too bad, but a forest is a forest.

Sparse thicket clumps obstructed distant views; one could not call the field of vision good.

"If they're hurrying their feet, it wouldn't be strange if they appeared anytime now. Tell the rear again to watch the surroundings. The flanks are the one place I do not want bitten."

"Understood."

To Godeus's words, in leather armor with a great shield slung on his back, the man at his side issued instructions.

The path Godeus and his men were taking was one of the routes through the forest, opened up to allow wagons.

The footing was secure throughout the area, but with some undulation, as if of small hills.

To advance the main body, this road was the only choice.

Garkha's warriors wore almost no armor; in exchange, they used large round shields.

The wood was Argana, with iron reinforcement at the rim and at the cut-out central grip — a sturdy thing, with their colorful symbols painted upon it.

Among them, the shield was, in a sense, regarded as more sacred than the sword; receiving a shield was the proof of being a warrior.

There was a rule that one must never let go of one's symbol-engraved shield on the battlefield, and people had been killed for the reason of having stepped over another's shield with their foot.

Owing to that, all were skilled in handling shields, one of the reasons Garkha ruled wide land.

Basically, like many tribes, they did not form lines and charged in groups; but they cooperated with those nearby according to circumstance, and built impromptu shield-walls.

Even if those nearby were complete strangers, by quick judgment they hardened their defense and calmly devised a way out — that flexibility was their weapon.

Many tribes who derided that figure as a comical turtle were overwhelmed by them, advancing like a wall without a gap, and cut down once they crumbled.

Battle is flow —— particularly in a forest, situational awareness is hard.

Victory and defeat may be said to be decided by the air of the moment; the unbreakable shield-wall was, for many failed warriors, an invincible wall against which neither hand nor foot could be moved.

If one flinches, those seeing him flinch as well; however many several elite warriors there might be, once the larger trend is decided, the air cannot be won back.

And their other weapon was the throwing axe.

Instead of using bows much, almost all of them carried two throwing axes at the waist; one used at the start of battle, and the second when the opponent's formation was crumbled.

If the first axe killed the opponent's momentum and they could be crushed, good.

Even if not, holding through the shield-wall, applying pressure, and using the second axe to completely shatter the opponent — *Garkha's Two Strokes*, this was called.

It was a basic tactic — more a simple agreed practice than a tactic, but against the many who fought as ungoverned packs, they had a great advantage.

But, completeness does not exist.

Their equipment, for this era, could be called heavy, and they fell behind in mobility.

Against an opponent in light gear with simple armor and a single sword, or carrying a bow, they lost in flexibility, and especially in chance encounters they often fell to disadvantage.

They carried minimum provisions, but for that equipment there were limits, and great battles required wagons, restricting movement.

Garkha's great defeats came always when such weaknesses were targeted.

Especially Aldos was fearsome.

In contrast to Garkha, they had a culture that strongly held the longsword sacred.

Their ferocity, swinging swords two-handed without fearing death, was known; if by mistake it became a melee, the flow would be carried away in an instant.

A group of berserkers who blocked thrown axes head-on with their swords.

That a melee not be entered into had to be avoided at all costs.

Each great clan that ruled multiple tribes had its strengths.

Differences arising from inherited culture and faith —— tribes that continued to prove that value ruled the surroundings and were called great clans.

What had been gathered was a little over two thousand.

Not a bad number, but no reason for carelessness.

Fear is an unseen monster.

The moment one thought *we are at a disadvantage*, the cowardly wind spread.

And the moment that happened, no matter how many, the group would crumble.

Garkha called itself a rock, but Aldos was a storm with thunder and rain.

If for an instant we cease to be a rock, in that moment we will be overwhelmed.

"Great chieftain, an Aldos group at ten ri ahead."

"Heh, head-on against Garkha, eh. Stop. From column to combat formation!"

At the messenger's words he roared, donning his helm, fastening the shield from his back to his left arm.

On his right hip two throwing axes, on his back a five-shaku short spear, on his left hip a large axe.

Standard equipment for one of warrior rank in Garkha.

Atop the iron helm with cheek guards, a horn-like protrusion and a horse's tail.

A long-used item, the helm inherited by the Garkha great chieftain.

The leather armor protected torso and limbs.

Shield and arrow-warding tattoos —— there were many men of faith who fought half-naked, but Godeus was a calm realist.

Movement was somewhat restricted and inconvenient, but he understood the usefulness of armor.

He was exasperated at the fashion that praised those who faced enemies with shield and body alone as heroes.

Even an arrow that, by chance, slipped through the shield's gap, or one stone, could be fatal.

In his generation he wished to have at least gauntlets and greaves, ideally helmets, worn.

If all warriors put on full armor and held shields, nothing could be more reliable.

The opponent had to break through the strong shield, then the armor, to give a fatal wound.

That way, Garkha's warriors, advancing without fearing the rain of arrows, would crush the enemy throng like a great rolling rock.

Rather than carrying the weakness of heavy armor, breakthrough was better — that was his thought, but the warriors lived between reality and fantasy.

Killing many enemies and surviving without wearing a single piece of protection was certainly heroic.

The more uselessly one took risks, the more one was praised as a warrior.

For the reason of being rational, he could not so easily strip away the warriors' image of the ideal.

*How troublesome*, he thought, gazing at the nearly half who were tattooed and half-naked, and drew a throwing axe and twirled it for warm-up.

He was in good condition —— if he could win this battle decisively against Aldos, he, who was still made light of by many, would be acknowledged as great chieftain.

What was the meaning of their head-on charge — it was good fortune.

"Tell them to make sure to coordinate the first axe. From start to finish, do not yield the flow; crush them."

In contrast, Aldos —— on the Enreine side, the line had been arranged.

# Chapter 368: Alberan IV

The enemy group was on the right.

A place a little distant from the road —— that area in particular had few thickets and was suited to combat.

He aligned heads toward there.

Which formation was advantageous and which disadvantageous, they understood to that degree.

If they could extend their left and right wings beyond the opponent's, they could surround the warriors at the ends of the opponent's wings and fight.

A logic anyone could understand.

That surrounding is more advantageous than being surrounded was a logic anyone could understand.

The basics of tactics were simple and clear.

Since a person can only fight the opponent in front, getting another to strike from the side or back wins — even a child could understand that logic.

Precisely because anyone can understand the logic, avoiding it is also inevitable.

Gathering people so as not to be at a disadvantage, once the fighting began, the rest was an individual matter.

It was left to each warrior.

Everyone holds his own life dear.

To take that life and venture onto the battlefield was for honor alone.

The one who decides where to die was the warrior; if a chieftain robbed that right and forced an order to die a dog's death, surely his head would be replaced the next day.

The structure of an army that became natural in later eras was a system that, by brainwashing meant to turn humans into organizational parts, restricted individual free will.

Precisely so, freely giving orders and moving an army group became possible; but the conception of using the irreplaceable thing called human life and life-history freely to control was too haughty, cold and inhuman.

Even if there were those who thought of it, whether the conception was easily accepted was no; if so, building castles in the air was meaningless.

Gathering numbers, raising individual strength and cooperation, was their best.

In the meaning of fighting people while keeping them as people, their present was by no means wrong.

Godeus muttered from inside the shield-wall.

Their initial movement had been completely sealed.

The throwing axe is no mere weapon.

Just at the start of battle when everyone is tense, it had also the meaning of loosening hard body and hard mind, and Godeus believed that aspect particularly large.

The warriors all do not want to be thought cowardly.

They say that killing many enemies and being splashed in blood is a warrior's joy, but in their hearts, of course, was the fear that they too might die there.

By spitting strong words and dignifiedly speaking them around them, they sealed their own escape route, and told themselves they had to fight as warriors and survive.

To raise a roaring war cry was also to suppress that clinging fear.

The throwing axe was likewise.

If they threw all together and disturbed the opponent at all, they were at advantage.

If one's axe took down a foe, one's spirits rose with *first one*; even if it didn't hit, if the opponent flinched, one could persuade oneself the flow was on this side.

If the opponents were lucky, perhaps just the front ten.

Fear flips into elation, and entering frenzy was full power.

Just before truly clashing, one could obtain a great spiritual advantage.

Not simply who is stronger, but a contest of hearts fighting fear.

The side that frightens wins; the side that flinches loses.

Just as a single sword-bearing person sometimes dominates and wins through fear, what should be obtained is not situational advantage but spiritual advantage.

The side that makes the opponent think *we lose* wins.

Aldos's warriors, known for valor, would not honestly comply if told *hide behind these shields to defend against throwing axes.*

The actual effect was knowable.

Making us think so was Aldos's greatest strength.

Some sacrifices unavoidable.

By creating this situation, they intended to one-sidedly establish their initial movement.

While receiving the arrow rain and holding shields, the throwing axe through charge sealed for us; a coordinated attack at the right moment.

Reaction would surely be slow.

Our warriors, with frayed nerves, must respond to it.

Without doubt the side seizing initial advantage was theirs.

The situation was completely in their palm.

Having gotten this close, only head-on approach was possible; one could not utilize numerical advantage to flank around.

In the last ten ken, raise speed at once, on signal hurl axes all together, and charge.

Killing some of their hunters and seeing blood, we could still recover.

But while taking the dense arrows, that was too far.

Godeus clicked his tongue. The distance was too far.

"Don't get ahead! Match pace, Doruka!"

A part of the warriors thrust forward holding shields up.

It was not courage but inability to bear fear.

The great shields covering the front could not block side fire.

The shield-wall is precisely because pace is matched.

It has meaning precisely because the body's left and right, the weak points, are not exposed.

That advancing meant this was inevitable.

The warriors who broke out were not a number that could be ignored.

"Match pace! Warriors, grab throwing axes! Hold up shields and rush forward!!"

To show his figure to the other warriors, deliberately so.

The hunters were in retreat posture.

Fifteen ken —— far for throwing axes.

If we approach, between the stationary shields Aldos warriors will appear, charging in.

Time the throwing axes accurately to that moment.

Fail, and this battle is lost.

Past six ken,

Pointless throwing axes —— not a single scream.

Length nearly three ken.

"The enemy is broken! Warriors, from here is our sword's time!!"

After this, holding the long spears and applying pressure, all that was needed was to wait for us to retreat.

If our retreating backs were attacked by Aldos warriors, there would surely be no chance.

Such a planned battle should have aimed for total victory.

The timing of drawing swords was too early.

Only part of the center responded.

"Forwa——?!"

Attention had drifted from the fallen Godeus.

The axe split the throat of the man raising his voice; blood-flowers scattered.

Drawing the great axe at his left waist, stepping in, knocking up the swung sword, severing the opponent's lower legs in two.

Lowering posture, holding the shield overhead to block all attacks, slicing off feet.

Made possible by flexible sense of balance, and the talent called mana, low-posture striding and a rugged shield stance.

All weapon-arts are honed for the purpose of standing and facing each other.

There were none who trained downward; thus, this axe-art, once one slipped into close range, was always victorious.

Behind Aldos's line —— to Bernaich watching from horseback, a warrior beside him called.

"Great chieftain——"

The demand had been conveyed; if not kept, that's that.

This situation too was within calculation.

The center was beginning melee; the warriors around were beginning to draw swords too.

Whether they realized that originally the outcome would have been decided, or not.

Garkha's great shields were quite troublesome.

Pressure with shields while maintaining group, in face-to-face battle, the side pushed was us.

Once individually divided they were no match for the warriors, but group versus group, in head-on shoving, Aldos's longswords could not be fully wielded.

What benefited was Garkha's short hand-axe.

"Their momentum is good. The center will be broken. Hunters to left and right."

"Y, yes!"

*As expected the line and Aldos warriors are a poor match*, watching calmly, he watched the advancing wings.

The center, become a hole, was pushed; the enemy concentrated there.

If our wings advanced a little, naturally the opponent would receive encirclement from both.

But most warriors fighting did not understand what situation they were in.

Looking down as if from the heights of the sky, moving warriors, making forms.

Holding countless lives at will, even life and death freely.

This was, in a sense, a scene only the gods are permitted to see.

"……As expected, this is not warriors' combat."

*A pitiable thing*, with the literal meaning of pity.

He narrowed his eyes at the figures of warriors falling in the center.

# Chapter 369: Alberan V

The center of each line was in extreme close-packed state.

Garkha's warriors avoided the long-spear pressure on both wings and concentrated, drawn into the disordered enemy center.

Godeus in front pressed his great shield against the body of the opponent in front.

"Tch!"

"Hah, what's wrong! Where's that vaunted sword!?"

The warrior with the great shield pressed against his body could not swing his sword; at Godeus's words, he clicked his tongue.

The longsword's strength is in slashes leveraging length, and in the property of the whole body being a blade.

It loses to spears, but thrust is also possible; it is sturdy. Even when the edge is gone it becomes a heavy blunt weapon, and continued combat ability is very high.

In power it loses to axes, in length to spears, but the breadth of options whereby a single blade handles every situation is a great strength.

In melee within a forest, no weapon was so powerful.

A high-grade item only with abundant mineral resources, and so a fearsome opponent —— that said, in close-packed state where hands and feet cannot be moved freely, it is mere ornament.

Whatever one tries, that half-baked length gets in the way.

For the longsword to exert power, at least space to spread both arms is needed.

Against that, what Garkha's warriors carried were axes.

Returning the great axe to his waist, taking the throwing axe from his right hip.

While crushing the upper body with his great shield, he struck down at the foot peeking out below.

"Gh……!?"

"Your foot's unguarded!"

Sword, spear, axe, bow —— there is no strongest weapon in this world.

What exists is the best weapon.

The throwing axe, far too short to be held and fought with, was, in this extreme close-packed state, the best weapon.

Top-heavy, with weight at the tip, with even slight space, even a light swing could deliver a blow to break a leg.

"Sway him!"

At the signal, the pressure of the shield pressed against Godeus's back lessened, his weight slightly back.

"Push!!"

When he shouted as if roaring, from his back came pressure greater than before.

At the same time, Godeus stepped in too, a step forward as if shoving the broken front.

The opponent, his upper body pressed and falling, broke posture; countless slashes, countless screams.

Those around Godeus split and broke the legs of the opponents in front.

Godeus lifted the corners of his cheeks and laughed, baring his teeth.

For an instant releasing pressure, making slight space, then again striking the shield against the opponent.

The opponent who held firm so as not to be pushed, when pressure thinned, momentarily broke posture; he stepped in matching that timing.

Naturally, the opponent's posture broke even more greatly.

When the upper body broke, the feet remained, and what fell upon them was an axe.

Wave-press —— this situation was Garkha's pattern of certain victory.

The warrior whose leg he had just broken collapsed at his feet.

Twisted in pain, turning hatred, the warrior's head — he stepped on it laughing, and broke his neck.

A short spear is good for the rear ranks, Godeus thought.

A spear held in reverse grip, finishing the half-dead at one's feet.

He was certain — they would absolutely break through.

The number of heads visible through the gaps had decreased considerably.
