# Chapter 351: Krische's Krische — Morning (※Important News Inside)

Krische's morning was the kind of thing that began with Bery.

Early in the morning, the moment Krische felt the change in the flow of Bery's mana, her consciousness, plunged in deep sleep, would float up; and with hazy thoughts she would register that Bery had woken.

Morning had come, she would think vaguely with her eyes still closed, but for a while she stayed just like that.

Her eyelids did not open there.

The actions of the just-woken Bery shifted subtly each day.

Sometimes she would comb Krische's hair sara-sara for a while, sometimes she would sawa-sawa stroke her cheek gently, sometimes funi-funi play with it, sometimes tickle her nose-tip, sometimes sasu-sasu trace her lips —— as far as Krische had observed, including small variations that pattern existed in fifty-seven varieties.

Furthermore, multiple of those being combined was by no means rare, and savouring such skinship from Bery while reading her psychology was Krische's secret enjoyment of the early morning.

Today, however, Bery only kissed her on the head and slipped out of bed at once.

For Krische, this was somewhat insufficient skinship.

Feeling subtly dissatisfied — but the once-childish Krische was now a lady.

Unlike in her childhood days, she now considered the why and the how come.

Why had Bery cut short the precious morning skinship so summarily?

Comparing her internal sensations —— her hunger level given last night's meal —— against current time, an answer reached by her superior mind came at once.

The current time, for Bery's wake-up, was somewhat late.

——That is, today's Bery was a 'Sleepyhead Bery.'

Of course, even being a sleepyhead, this was before sunrise.

It was only the slightest bit late, but Bery was the unrivalled lady whom Krische set as her eternal goal, strict with herself and faithful to her duties as a servant.

To recover that small lateness she would adjust the balance in various places, completing every task without throwing off the manor's clock —— she would erase the sleeping-in entirely.

Failures are something every person must commit; what matters is what one does afterwards (citation: Bery Sayings).

Even if Bery had slept in a little and her wake-up skinship had been somewhat restrained, that was unavoidable.

In a case like this, what was demanded of Krische, her right hand and the deputy head servant, was perfect support to cancel out head servant Bery's small sleep-in.

By what task and how would Bery now make up for the sleep-in —— canvassing in her head every time-shortening route Bery might take, Krische muni-muni-ed her lips to pull her consciousness up.

Lifting her heavy eyelids, Krische slowly raised herself.

Naked as she was, she stretched, and as always reached over and muni-pulled the cheek of the sloppily sleeping Kreschenta.

At her sister's spoiled, petulant voice — *Nooo, please don't* — Krische judged she had no intention of getting up today either, and let go.

When you pull her cheeks like this, the probability that Kreschenta wakes up and helps Bery alongside Krische is roughly one in 1,035, but in 1,034 cases out of 1,035 it goes like this.

As an elder sister Krische felt a need for education in the face of her slovenly little sister, but being too strict ran counter to Bery's educational policy.

She sometimes thought it might be that they were spoiling Kreschenta a little too much, but the manor's educator was not Krische — it was Bery.

To trespass on her duty was something that must not be, so Krische tried not to put it into words.

Besides, things were a matter of how you looked at them (citation: Bery Sayings).

Thanks to Kreschenta being slovenly, Krische got to assist Bery alone in the morning, sticking close.

If Kreschenta had ever awakened to the path of the servant, she would without doubt have become a powerful rival, and they would have ended up fighting over the limited pie that was Bery's time.

Considering that, her sister being slovenly was by no means a bad thing.

A reliable elder sister, unlike her slovenly little sister —— it was no bad thing to be evaluated by Bery in such terms.

Slipping out of bed, she went to Bery's side as Bery finished changing into her apron dress.

"Good morning, Krische-sama."

"Ehehe, good morning……"

When Krische stretched up just a little, Bery gave her a light good-morning kiss on the lips.

After that Bery prepared Krische's underwear and apron dress.

This timing too was important.

To slip out of bed before Bery had finished changing was no good.

Changing was originally something one could do alone —— to wait for Bery to finish changing in order to be specifically dressed by her was, however you looked at it, the behaviour of a spoiled child.

It was not behaviour befitting a lady, and on top of that it would, at least a little, rush Bery.

Krische now was a servant, in a position to assist Bery.

Of course Krische wanted Bery to dress her, but she could not just be spoiled, and at any rate it had to be carried out in a natural flow.

Krische woke up a little late, and the already-changed Bery helped her dress —— that kind of story was necessary.

That said, naturally, busy Bery must not be made to wait.

Not too early, not too late: an exquisite adjustment of timing.

Today, by inserting the step of pulling Kreschenta's cheek, she had managed a too-natural perfect-timing dressing-request.

She put on her underwear, then her apron dress.

"Fufu, isn't it tightening too much?"

"It's just right!"

The moment her waist was kyu-cinched by the apron's sash was her favourite of all.

In that instant the conviction welled up that she had become the same kind of servant as Bery, and looking at her own figure in the dressing mirror brought an indescribable satisfaction.

In an apron dress matching Bery's, she relaxed her cheeks and took her arm.

"For now, shall we go and light the corridor lamps?"

"Yes!"

That was the start of Krische's day, the start of her duties as a servant.

To put what should be into the form it should take.

When asked what it meant to be a servant, Bery had taught Krische in many words, but the deepest while sounding the simplest was that one.

For instance, when someone wakes in the morning and steps out into the corridor, naturally the corridor should be bright.

If so, the corridor lamps must be lit, and one thought it should be that way.

Therefore Bery woke before anyone, and poured mana into the eternal magic lamps.

Just that, she said —— and that "just that" Bery applied to everything.

Cooking, laundry, cleaning of course, and for example today, knowing Kreschenta would be in a sulk, she would prepare rakura pie.

Before Krische had said she wanted tea, the tea preparations naturally began, and by the time Krische felt like a nap, work was finished and her hands were free.

The amount of cookies was neither too many nor too few.

Even when Selene and the others irregularly joined in, there was never a "not enough," nor was there ever a great surplus left over.

As if she could see the future, Bery shaped the "should-be."

If Krische used all of her ability and exhausted possibilities, even she could form some kind of estimate.

But exhausting possibilities was just possibilities.

If one tried to prepare for every possibility, naturally much would be wasted.

But Bery's mind, by adding the element of others' psychology to those countless possibilities, computed the manor's future with what could be called the precision of foresight.

It was high-grade prediction Krische could not imitate.

"Today there's a little more, isn't there."

Watching the mana she was pouring into the eternal magic lamps, Krische said it, and Bery smiled.

"Tonight ojou-sama will likely stay up late. Yesterday she went to sleep quite early, and from the look of it, she's struggling with the pot-making…… today she'll be in the workshop the whole day. It'd probably be good to bring her something quick to eat for lunch as well."

The manor's eternal magic lamps were given the minimum required mana first thing in the morning.

That way, the trouble of going to put out the lamps at night could be saved.

Today she had thought there was a bit more than usual, and the reason, it seemed, was something like that.

Information weighed, thought read, conclusion produced just like that.

The manor's day today was probably already inside Bery's head, and the manor would flow, today as ever, exactly as Bery thought.

Most events in the manor were within the palm of Bery's hand.

Knowing everyone's schedule was a matter of course.

On top of that, to read each one's mood and psychology of the day, providing comfort without their realising —— only when one could do that did one become a fully-fledged servant.

The duties of a servant were such that Bery, who from Krische's view was first-rate and far above, said she herself was only halfway down the road.

Compared to that depth, the battles of the past had been children's play.

Of course, compared to the work of the battlefield, where the result tied directly to someone's life or death, a servant's responsibility was lighter.

She certainly understood why warlike work was prized and household work treated lightly, but if asked which was the greater work, there was no need to think.

The greatness of Bery, the pioneer of that work, was beyond doubt the world's first.

Wrapped in her apron dress, until she slipped it off and slept again —— all the time spent with such a person was, for Krische, a study.

Taking Bery's arm, alternating turns at pouring mana into the eternal magic lamps, the two went together to the kitchen.

The work of a servant was truly demanding work.

If one merely went through it idly, fine; but if one sought high quality, the sky was the limit.

To wake earliest in the morning, begin one's activities and overwork a body and mind still drowsy was no ordinary thing, and consumed great strength.

The body would in no time enter starvation —— concretely, her stomach would grow peko-peko, to the point she could no longer walk by herself.

Over the course of lighting the eternal magic lamps, Krische's stamina had already entered the danger zone.

It was at moments like this that Krische felt her own insufficiency keenly.

To avoid further consumption of stamina, the desire to be spoiled was swelling explosively.

It was a desire too strong for the still-half-fledged Krische to suppress.

Even though the cause was that, due to Bery's slight oversleep, her morning skinship toward Krische had been restrained, to complain over that was unreasonable.

It was behaviour far removed from Krische's ideal, the lady-and-servant figure.

But the unconscious is a fearful thing.

Her arms wrapped around Bery suri-suri-ed, and she could not help leaning her weight in and pulling, at which Bery, turning back, smiled wryly.

"My. You're a spoiled one today."

"Eh, um…… ah."

When she was lifted up just like that, Bery looked at her and narrowed her eyes.

"Fufu, was the morning head-patting not enough?"

"U……"

Bery was a top-class servant.

The inside of foolish Krische's head was something she saw straight through.

——So she was carried into the kitchen.

Once they entered, water cats were created here and there by Bery.

The cats cooperated to warm up yesterday's leftover stew, make scrambled eggs in a frying pan, grill sausages, bake bread.

When she was holding Krische and could not free her hands, Bery used cats like this.

If she was going to use magic, controlling things directly herself would be more efficient and better, Krische thought, but apparently this was more fun, and she usually used cats for cooking.

Playfulness was important, Bery always said, and this too was probably part of that.

Sitting on the lap of Bery — who was on a chair —, watching some dozens of cats progress with the pre-breakfast preparations, several cats came over with a tea set.

They held out a teacup of black tea generously poured with honey and milk to Krische.

It was sweet and delicious, the kind of tea Krische liked.

Receiving it and tasting a sip, she carried it to Bery's mouth and tilted it just a little.

"Sweet things are good in the morning."

"Ehehe…… yes. It's so very delicious."

"That's the best news. What shall we make for breakfast today?"

——Here it comes, Krische tensed slightly.

Not "shall we make X," but "what shall we make."

It was a declaration entrusting the morning's menu to Krische.

The most important task within servant duties —— cooking.

Each meal had its differences, but breakfast was especially the meal that began things.

Whether the day was a good one or not was decided by the success or failure of breakfast (citation: Bery Sayings), and absolutely no failure was permitted.

In breakfast Bery too refrained from new challenges, and constantly aimed to provide gourmet cuisine.

If this was no good, no matter how excellent the connecting lunch and the day's closing dinner, it could not be salvaged.

That said, choosing something too safe was also a mistake.

Bery had naturally already considered such things, and dared to ask Krische.

A servant who answered safely without failure here could not be called first-rate.

A dish that just from imagining it sounded delicious, that whetted the appetite —— what one had to suggest had to be that, something that would satisfy Bery from the heart.

"For Kreschenta's sake, I think rakura pie as one of the mains is fine……, and if so, I think the other main should be something salty."

"That's right."

"Then meat…… but rakura pie and meat from morning is a bit heavy, so here, fish——"

——No good. Just grilling is too safe.

Halfway through her words she choked, and her eyes wandered.

"Fish?"

"H-herb-grilled, with shellfish and tomato, in a light soup…… how about that? There's plenty of stock from when Selene caught a lot of fish before, so about three kinds."

"Ah, that's good. Seafood is light too."

"Yes!"

Her head was patted and her cheeks loosened.

Grill the surface to a nice colour, add water enough to soak it through, with shellfish and tomato.

Apparently the dish was originally a coastal one using seawater, a salt-based light soup.

What fish to use, what to add for ingredients, and the finishing touch — all greatly changed the taste.

But this kind of simple, delicate soup admitted no fudging, and not the slightest failure could be allowed.

It was the sort that went bad over the smallest thing — the Kreschenta of the soup world, so to speak.

Since she had proposed it herself, she had to brace herself even more than usual.

If so, then naturally, being spoiled during cooking would be kept to a minimum —— she had to fully satisfy the urge to be spoiled here.

"Bery, kiss……"

"…… really, today's Krische-sama is truly a spoiled one."

"Ehehe……"

Her cheek was stroked, her lips kissed, and just like that, she squirmed around to turn herself face-front and hugged on tight.

The lips were lightly scented with the tea she had just drunk.

Most of the time, Bery tasted of honey.

After finishing pre-breakfast and making breakfast, all of them had breakfast together.

Then they began laundry and cleaning in earnest.

Before, doing many things in parallel had been frequent, but now there were four servants.

With Bery's policy of fun-and-leisure rather than efficiency, what was demanded was quality rather than speed.

Without rush, without hurry, but with order and care.

Today Anne does this work, today Elvena does this work.

When to go and check on Lila, when Kreschenta will wake.

What Selene's progress on her work might be.

Thinking such things, and spending the day —— that was the work of a servant.

Wiping the staircase clean with a cloth, Bery looked vaguely thoughtful.

Her head was always full of someone, and that distant expression looked, oddly, beautiful.

No matter how many years passed, the inside of her head was still hard to grasp.

It seemed she was thinking of something splendid, but sometimes she was thinking of something completely unrelated, and what one could understand was only that she was thinking of someone.

She seemed to be holding the reins, but she let them go easily, going with the flow, drifting as drifting.

What she put to her mouth was vague pillows and vague blankets — words like "roughly," "about," "the feel of it."

Yet by the end, everything fell into its place, and one realised that had been the best.

Strange, sometimes beyond comprehension.

Within Krische, Bery was that kind of person.

In Karka village there were gods one could not quite understand.

For example the number of deer or boar, fruits, mountain vegetables, the bounty of the mountain were the gods' province; rain continuing, clear weather continuing, storms coming were the gods' deeds.

It was not that the gods were hard to understand — gods were probably hard-to-understand things.

That gods were such, people would, not understanding, accept as if understanding, and many would, not understanding, offer prayers.

If told that's the way it was, one had no choice but to accept it as such, and Krische too, vaguely, recognised it as one of the rules.

The concept of faith was hard for Krische to grasp, and as long as she recognised it that way there was no problem.

Whether one believed or not, if one made the gestures of prayer, it was finished.

That Krische, who had grasped it in words alone, had only thinly understood it much later.

The Bery of those days had probably been Krische's god.

Krische's world was the manor Bery made, and that was everything.

Bery had in fact answered Krische's expectations all along, her words had all seemed correct, and the world changed as she willed.

It felt like Bery alone knew all the unknowns Krische held, and Krische had believed without room for doubt.

Yet what Bery always put to her mouth was not what to do, but how one wanted to be.

There were no words she could not recall, and recalling them, they spread vividly behind her eyelids, anytime.

Every time she hesitated over something, the figure of Bery speaking to Krische came back to her.

It was rare to understand Bery's various words at the moment they were said; mostly, only several years later, or sometimes only after decades, would she at last understand a tiny bit, and it would seep into her head.

Probably Bery, knowing it would be so, had always spoken to Krische like that.

Surely that was best, faintly so.

The wider Krische's view grew, the more she could see, and after Bery was gone, far more so.

The words Bery had spoken became close to her, and her figure, vivid, looked beautiful.

She had not changed from the start.

She had only helped Krische look for the Krische that Krische wanted to be.

For broken-headed Krische, without dislike, smiling, she had simply gone along.

Earnestly, all the while thinking of Krische.

"Ehehe, I've finished wiping the ornaments."

When she clung to Bery, her head was gently patted.

Cleaning, laundry, tidying.

Every day the things to do were the same, and entirely different.

Bery's first teaching was always to think.

"It's clean here too, isn't it. ……It's about time Kreschenta-sama woke up too, perhaps."

She too was surely doing the same, putting a finger to her lips and thinking.

Bery was not the god she had once believed in, and so she always thought of all sorts of things.

From small things to great, she earnestly thought of someone, and spent each day.

Bery was probably someone very strange, and a person whose head was a little broken.

Search the world over, there was probably no second one like her.

But because of that, Krische thought, she looked the most beautiful of all.

Her head always full of someone, and helping that someone.

A servant for the one person in the world she did not show consideration to.

The Krische that Krische wanted to be was that kind of Krische.

"Then Krische, I'll go check on Selene."

Today's task was to play along with the Kreschenta who would, pretending to be in a sulk, act spoiled like a child.

Even if she wanted to look in on Selene who was making a pot, Bery did not have two bodies.

So Krische's job was to be Bery's helper.

"Fufu, yes. Then, please take care of ojou-sama."

She kissed the smiling Bery.

Krische's noon began that way.

# Chapter 352: Krische's Krische — Noon

That place was a hut built right beside the manor —— or what had been one.

At the start it had really just been a hut, but a hut had been built next to the hut, a second floor had been added, expansions had been repeated, and now it was almost like a small mansion.

"Selene, I'm coming in."

After knocking and going in, things were strewn about everywhere.

It was the exact opposite of the perfectly tidy manor — chaos.

Going inside, Selene, in a leather apron and her long hair tied up high, was holding clay on the wheel with a serious face, shaping something.

On every shelf were piles of pre-fired clay pieces, of which one could not really tell which were finished works or which were failures.

On the desk, what looked like detail tools were placed haphazardly, alongside clay-dirtied water and lumps of clay.

On a clay tablet sat what looked like a design diagram, a mysterious picture.

Each time Krische came she thought it ought to be a bit tidier, but apparently for Selene this counted as tidy, and if Krische started cleaning up on her own initiative there were strong objections — *don't move that, that goes there.*

She was only allowed to touch things after getting Selene's permission.

For the past several years, pot-making was Selene's favourite.

Before that, it had been glasswork; before that, woodcarving.

When this hut had been built, the very first thing Selene had begun making had been blades, but for the past two hundred years or so Krische had not seen her at the forge.

Naturally, the eastern kitchen knife she had bought for cooking fish was still a captive.

According to Selene,

『The straightest, shortest path is not always the right answer. There are things in the world you only understand by seeing many things and touching them, and I'm now in the midst of just such a long journey.』

That seemed to be the gist, but Krische could still not find a connection between the kitchen knife and pot-making.

Selene certainly had not been a dexterous person, but probably, if she had spent several hundred years simply forging only knives without detours, she would by now have managed at least one decent knife.

Considering that, to Krische it just seemed like she was on an endlessly drawn-out roundabout.

Even so, Krische now was a servant.

And Selene was the master of this manor.

To pour cold water on a master enjoying her mysterious roundabout was no good thing, and tasting subtle unreasonableness, all Krische could do was watch over her.

Shururu-shururu, the wheel turned, and the face of Selene gazing at it was seriousness itself.

The clay was thinly stretched, drawing a curve, and —— funi, it collapsed.

"Ah…… aaaah……"

Seeing it, Selene covered her face, leaned far back and gazed up at the heavens.

For Krische it was hard to tell, but judging by the reaction, this had probably been a good piece for Selene.

While pouring black tea into a wooden cup, gazing at the collapsed clay, Krische thought.

At times like this, the kind of one-liner that hits the mark is what makes a good servant.

She nodded once and smiled.

"Selene, it's all right. They say everything that has shape eventually breaks…… being broken from the start might in a sense be novel, as art."

"…… that's no comfort at all, you fool."

"Mu……"

It seemed that, more than practicality, novelty and forms or expressions never seen before were what was valued — but art was rather a difficult concept.

Krische could not quite grasp it.

Selene washed her hands in the water bucket, dried them with a hand-cloth, and just like that picked up the cup and took a sip of tea.

Leaning her back against the backrest she put both hands up, kuni-kuni-ed them left and right and stretched her body.

Krische likewise stretched her body kuni-kuni, and, *don't imitate me*, Selene glared.

Every time they met, Selene almost always stretched both her arms and limbered up.

It was practically a greeting at this point.

"Ehehe. What are you making today?"

"A vase. Look — you all brought back some pretty flowers the other day, didn't you?"

"Pretty flowers……"

She tilted her head and thought, and at the words "brought back," clapped her hands.

"The ao-pika grass!"

A pale-blue flower, a new species whose blossoms opened at moonlit night and shone blue and pika-pika.

Bery had taken quite a liking to that flower field, and they had brought some back.

The flower bed in one corner of the garden had been made the other day too.

"…… as ever, your naming sense leaves something to be desired. Well, since they were pretty, I thought of decorating with them on the windowsill of my room."

"Ahh…… but, the vases you've made, there are quite a lot left over in the storehouse, you know? Twelve…… no, since Anne broke two, ten now."

"…… the ones Anne broke are exactly the ones I wanted to use."

Selene sighed as if in exasperation.

"But there are still ten spares……"

"Listen — flowers and vases come as a set. There's such a thing as a combination — these flowers go with this kind of vase."

"A combination……"

"How should I put it…… right, in cooking, too — for this dish, this kind of dishware, isn't there? If it just has to fit, you could pile everything onto a soup plate, but you arrange it considering colour because it looks better and more delicious that way, right?"

"That's…… certainly true."

She nodded thoughtfully.

Selene was usually full of incomprehensible fixations, but on this point today there was reason.

Cooking was cooking, plating included (citation: Bery Sayings).

Told it was the same as that, she nodded and was convinced.

Krische was still in the realm of imitation, and could not say she had perfectly understood the concept, but it was not the sort of simple thing that held together purely on logic.

To look delicious was a super-sensual concept.

Truly truly profound, one of the elements of cooking.

Probably it was something close to that.

Selene undid her apron and patted her knee in invitation.

When Krische sat her bottom on it and kissed her, *honestly*, her cheeks were muni-pinched.

"You really, in cooking analogies, are mostly straightforward, aren't you……"

"If anything Selene has weirder fixations—— uuh."

"They're not weird fixations."

While gui-pulling Krische's cheek, Selene sighed.

"Teaching you the concept of art is rather difficult…… but for example. Bery rearranges and swaps out the manor's furnishings often, doesn't she?"

"……? Yes."

"That kind of thing is also a kind of art. Thinking 'this looks better placed here, that looks better placed there' while shifting positions and combining things. You do it too, don't you?"

"…… well, in a way."

"And when you do it, what are you considering as you adjust?"

At those words Krische thought a little, and answered.

"Hm…… pretty, hm, in a way that Bery would seem pleased, perhaps……"

When she answered, Selene's eyebrow twitched and she sighed.

"…… well, fine. What you're doing too is one form of art. To produce something that makes the viewer think 'beautiful' and feel moved or pleased or surprised — that's what art is."

"That the viewer thinks pretty……"

"That's the basis. There's art that takes the form of objects produced like this, and there's art that creates a space through the arrangement of such things. The flower bed of ao-pika grass is art too, and so is tending the orchard, and the garden too is art. Producing in some form something a viewer can be pleased with, and having it convey, makes it art."

*Cooking is also a kind of art*, Selene said, and Krische nodded with a serious face.

"…… I see. Indeed Krische, when she came to the manor, was very moved by the dishes Bery made."

How clumsy a thing she herself had been at the time.

*Mhm, mhm*, Krische looked back over the past.

Dishes that surpassed the concept of "delicious" she had had until then.

A devotion that made her think *they go this far for cooking?*

Day after day she was moved, and that had not changed even now.

Even with one familiar dish, even a cookie, Bery's hands produced small wonders.

Now, in technique, there were a few — the slightest of — areas where Krische surpassed her.

But Bery's small touches, varying with each day, always carried an emotion.

Bery's hands were magic hands.

With a pinch of salt or herbs or a single shake of the pot she finished a dish into art.

"For Krische, perhaps Bery's cooking is the closest thing to the concept of art Selene speaks of. That, certainly, is art."

"I-is it……"
