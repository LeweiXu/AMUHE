# Chapter 351: Krische's Krische — Morning

Krische's mornings began with Bery.

At daybreak, when she sensed the shift in Bery's mana flow, Krische's consciousness rose from deep sleep. With thoughts still hazy, she recognized that Bery had woken.

*Morning*, she thought vaguely with her eyes still closed, but stayed put for a while.

Her eyelids did not open.

Bery's behavior upon waking changed subtly each day.

Sometimes she would run her fingers through Krische's hair, sometimes gently stroke her cheek, sometimes knead it softly, sometimes tickle the tip of her nose, sometimes rub her lips — and as far as Krische had observed, these patterns, including minor variations, numbered fifty-seven.

Furthermore, it was far from unusual for several to be combined, and savoring these expressions of Bery's affection while deciphering the psychology behind them constituted Krische's secret early-morning pleasure.

Today, however, Bery kissed her head and immediately slipped out of bed.

A slightly unsatisfying amount of affection, as far as Krische was concerned.

Somewhat dissatisfied, but the child-Krische was now a lady.

Unlike in her younger days, she considered the why and the how.

Why had Bery cut short that precious morning affection?

Cross-referencing her body's internal clock — her degree of hunger calibrated against last night's meal — with her superior intellect to calculate the current time, the answer came at once.

The current time was slightly late for Bery's usual waking hour.

——In other words, today's Bery was a "Sleepyhead Bery."

To be sure, "sleepyhead" was relative — it was still before sunrise.

Only slightly later than usual, but Bery was the unsurpassed lady whom Krische aspired to emulate for eternity — strict with herself, devoted to her duties as a servant.

To make up for that slight oversleeping, she would make adjustments throughout the morning, completing every task to keep the estate's clock running precisely — erasing the oversleeping as if it had never happened.

Mistakes are something everyone makes; what matters is what you do afterward (from the Bery Compendium).

Even if Bery overslept a little and the morning affection was a bit restrained, that was simply how it was.

In such cases, what was expected of her right hand, Deputy Head Servant Krische, was flawless support to help Head Servant Bery erase that little bit of oversleeping.

How would Bery make up the time, and through which tasks? — Krische mentally mapped out every possible shortcut Bery would take, and coaxed her consciousness awake by working her lips in a little *muni-muni*.

Lifting heavy eyelids, Krische slowly sat up.

She stretched her bare body, then grabbed the cheek of Kreschenta — who was, as always, sleeping in a slovenly fashion — and pulled. *Muniiii.*

*How dreadful*, came a spoiled voice, and Krische let go, concluding that her sister had no intention of waking today either.

Pulling her cheek like this, Kreschenta would get up and help Bery roughly one time in one thousand and thirty-five. The remaining one thousand and thirty-four times, this was the result.

As an older sister, Krische felt the need to address her sister's slothful behavior, but being too strict would run contrary to Bery's educational philosophy.

She did sometimes wonder whether Bery wasn't spoiling Kreschenta a tad too much, but the estate's educator was Bery, not Krische.

To overstep that role was unthinkable, so she tried to keep her opinions to herself.

Besides, everything is a matter of perspective (from the Bery Compendium).

Thanks to Kreschenta's laziness, Krische got to have Bery all to herself in the mornings, assisting side by side.

Had Kreschenta awakened to the path of the servant, she would have been a formidable rival, and they would have been fighting over Bery's time — a finite pie.

Considering that, a lazy sister was not necessarily a bad thing at all.

A dependable older sister in contrast to her slovenly younger sister — earning such an assessment from Bery was nothing to complain about, either.

Slipping out of bed, she went to Bery, who had already finished dressing in her apron dress.

"Good morning, Krische-sama."

"Ehehe, good morning……"

Rising a little on her toes, Bery gave a light good-morning kiss to Krische's lips.

Then she set about preparing Krische's undergarments and apron dress.

This timing, too, was critical.

Leaving bed before Bery had finished dressing would not do.

Getting dressed was something one could do alone — waiting around for Bery to finish dressing just to be dressed by her was, from any angle, the behavior of a spoiled child.

Unbecoming of a lady, and it would subtly pressure Bery to hurry.

Krische was now a servant, Bery's support.

Of course Krische wanted Bery to dress her, but one must not only cling; it had to happen naturally.

Krische waking up a little after Bery and having the already-dressed Bery help her change — that narrative was necessary.

And naturally, she must not keep busy Bery waiting.

Not too early, not too late — a perfect calibration.

Today, by inserting the step of pulling Kreschenta's cheek, she had achieved a flawlessly natural, perfectly timed dressing request.

Undergarments on, apron dress on.

"Fufu, it's not too tight?"

"It's perfect!"

The moment the apron sash cinched her waist tight was her absolute favorite.

In that instant, the realization that she was a servant just like Bery welled up, and the reflection in the mirror filled her with indescribable satisfaction.

Matching apron dresses with Bery — she let her cheeks relax and took Bery's arm.

"Shall we start by lighting the hall lamps?"

"Yes!"

And that was the beginning of Krische's day — the start of her servant duties.

Arranging what ought to be as it ought to be.

What is a servant? When posed that question, Bery had taught her in many words, but the one that sounded simplest yet proved most profound was that one.

If someone woke in the morning and stepped into the hall, the hall should naturally be lit.

Therefore the lamps must be on, and so they should be — that was the reasoning.

So Bery woke before anyone else and poured mana into the eternal magic lamps.

That was all, she said — but that was all, applied to everything.

Cooking, laundry, cleaning went without saying, but for instance, anticipating that Kreschenta would be in a bad mood today meant preparing a rakura pie.

Before Krische could voice her wish for tea, preparations naturally began, and by the time Krische felt like a nap, her work was done and her hands were free.

The amount of cookies was never too many, never too few.

Even when Selene and the others showed up unannounced, there were never not enough — yet there was never a mountain of leftovers.

As if she could see the future, Bery gave form to "how things ought to be."

Krische, too, could manage rough predictions if she used all her abilities to exhaustively map possibilities.

But mapped possibilities were still only possibilities.

Preparing for every one of them inevitably produced enormous waste.

Bery's mind, however, factored in the psychology of others atop those countless possibilities, arriving at predictions precise enough to be called foreknowledge of the estate's future.

A higher-order prediction Krische could not replicate.

"You're putting in a bit more than usual today."

Watching the mana Bery fed into the eternal lamp, Krische noted this, and Bery smiled.

"Ojou-sama will probably be staying up late tonight. She went to bed quite early yesterday, and she seems to be having trouble with her vase project……she'll likely be in the workshop all day. It might be best to bring her something quick to eat for lunch."

The eternal magic lamps throughout the estate received their minimum charge of mana each morning.

That way, there was no need to go around extinguishing them at night.

Krische had noticed the amount was a touch higher than usual, and the reason was apparently that.

Gathering information, reading intentions, and producing a conclusion with ease.

Today's schedule for the entire estate already existed in Bery's head, and events would unfold precisely as she envisioned.

Nearly everything that happened in the estate lay within Bery's grasp.

Knowing everyone's plans was a given.

On top of that, reading the day's moods and psychology, providing comfort without anyone noticing — only then could one call oneself a fully-fledged servant.

Even someone like Bery — a master of the highest order from Krische's point of view — described herself as still only halfway on the path of servitude.

Compared to its depth, the battles of old were child's play.

Of course, the battlefield, where results were directly tied to someone's life or death, carried heavier responsibility than a servant's work.

Krische understood perfectly well why martial achievements were valued and household work was looked down upon — but which was the more admirable calling was beyond question.

The greatness of Bery, the pioneer of that calling, could be called the finest in the world without a shadow of doubt.

Wrapped in her apron dress, from the moment she put it on until she took it off to sleep — every moment spent with Bery was a lesson for Krische.

Arm in arm with Bery, they took turns charging the eternal lamps, and together made their way to the kitchen.

Being a servant was truly demanding work.

Simply going through the motions was one thing, but striving for higher quality revealed a ceiling that stretched to infinity.

Waking at the crack of dawn, commencing activities, pushing a still-sleepy body and mind — it was no small feat, and consumed an enormous amount of energy.

The body plunged rapidly into starvation — specifically, her tummy went *peko-peko* empty — to the point where she could no longer walk on her own.

It was at times like these that Krische keenly felt her own inadequacy.

The desire to be spoiled had ballooned so explosively that further energy expenditure had to be avoided at all costs.

It was a powerful urge — too powerful for the still-half-fledged Krische to suppress.

The cause, to be fair, was that Bery's slight oversleeping had resulted in somewhat restrained morning affection, but blaming Bery for that would be unreasonable.

It was conduct utterly at odds with the image of the lady-cum-servant that Krische aspired to.

But the unconscious was a frightening thing.

Before she knew it, she was nuzzling the arm she clung to, leaning her weight into it and tugging, and Bery, turning around, smiled wryly.

"My, my. Somebody's a little clingy today."

"U-um……ah."

When Bery scooped her up, she looked at Krische and narrowed her eyes.

"Fufu, did you not get enough petting this morning?"

"U……"

Bery was a servant of the highest order.

The contents of silly Krische's head were an open book to her.

——And so she was carried to the kitchen.

Inside, Bery conjured water-cats in every corner.

The cats cooperated with one another — warming last night's leftovers, making scrambled eggs in a frying pan, frying sausages, toasting bread.

When Bery's hands were occupied with holding Krische, she used the cats like this.

Krische felt it would be more efficient to manipulate things directly with magic, but Bery said this was more fun, and usually cooked with the cats.

She also always said that a sense of play was important — this was probably part of that philosophy.

Sitting on Bery's lap in a chair, Krische watched the dozens of cats prepare pre-breakfast, until a few cats brought over a tea set.

They offered her a teacup of tea generously laced with honey and milk.

Sweet and delicious — tea exactly to Krische's taste.

She took it, sipped once, then brought it to Bery's lips and tilted it slightly.

"Something sweet is nice in the morning."

"Ehehe……yes. It's very delicious!"

"Wonderful. Now, what shall we make for breakfast?"

——*Here it comes*, Krische thought, tensing slightly.

Not *shall we make so-and-so*, but *what shall we make*.

A declaration that the morning's menu was being entrusted to Krische.

The most important duty in the servant's profession — cooking.

The specifics varied, but breakfast was the opening meal in particular.

Whether the day turned out well was determined by breakfast's success or failure (from the Bery Compendium), and failure was absolutely not permitted.

Even Bery avoided experimentation at breakfast, consistently prioritizing the delivery of fine cuisine.

If breakfast faltered, then no matter how good lunch — the bridging meal — or dinner — the day's finale — might be, there was no recovering.

That said, choosing something too safe would also be a mistake.

Bery obviously knew all of that and was deliberately asking Krische.

A servant who gave a safe, failure-proof answer at this juncture could not be called first-rate.

What she should propose was something so appetizing that merely imagining it stirred the appetite — something that would satisfy Bery from the heart.

"First of all, since we'll be making a rakura pie for Kreschenta as one of the mains, Krische thinks the other main should be something savory."

"That's right."

"Then it should be meat……but rakura pie and meat for breakfast would be a bit heavy. Perhaps fish, then——"

——*No, simply grilling it is too safe.*

She stumbled on her own words, eyes darting.

"Fish?"

"H-herb-crusted, in a light broth with shellfish and tomato……how does that sound? We have plenty of the fish Selene caught the other day, so perhaps three varieties."

"Ah, that does sound nice. Seafood is light, after all."

"Yes!"

A pat on the head, and her cheeks relaxed.

Pan-sear the surface until golden, add just enough water to submerge, then shellfish and tomato.

A coastal dish that originally used seawater — a light, salt-based broth.

Depending on the fish, the added ingredients, and the finishing touch, the flavor changed dramatically.

That said, this kind of simple, delicate soup left no room for error — the slightest misstep would ruin it.

It was the Kreschenta of the soup world: fussy about everything, quick to collapse.

Having suggested it herself, she would need to brace herself even more than usual.

Which meant, of course, that clinginess during cooking had to be kept to a bare minimum — making it essential to satisfy her need for affection right now.

"Bery, a kiss……"

"……My, Krische-sama really is a little clingy today."

"Ehehe……"

Her cheek was stroked, a kiss planted, and she wriggled around to face Bery and hugged her tight.

On those lips, the faint taste of the tea they'd just shared.

More often than not, Bery tasted of honey.

* * *

Pre-breakfast finished, breakfast made, then breakfast eaten together.

After that, the real laundry and cleaning work began.

In the past, multiple tasks were often handled simultaneously, but now they had four servants.

Bery's policy of valuing enjoyment and leisurely pace over efficiency meant that quality, not speed, was what mattered.

Without haste, without rush, yet with good organization and care.

Today, Anne would handle this task. Today, Elvena would handle that.

When to check on Lila, when Kreschenta would wake.

How Selene's project was progressing.

Thinking about such things while going about the day — that was the servant's work.

As she wiped the stairs with a cloth, Bery seemed to drift into thought.

Her head was always full of someone else — that absent look on her face was strangely beautiful.

No matter how many years passed, what went on inside her head remained a mystery.

She seemed to be thinking about something grand, yet might be pondering something entirely unrelated — the only certainty was that it concerned somebody.

She appeared to hold the reins, yet let them go with ease, drifting wherever the current carried her.

*Roughly*, *more or less*, *by feel* — her words were pillowed in vagueness, blanketed in ambiguity.

Yet when all was said and done, everything settled exactly where it should, and one realized only then that it had been for the best.

Strange, and sometimes beyond comprehension.

In Krische's mind, that was the kind of person Bery was.

In Karka Village, there had been a god no one quite understood.

The number of deer and boar, the bounty of fruit, wild greens, and mountain harvest — all of that was the god's domain. Whether rain continued or sun blazed or storms came — all acts of divinity.

Perhaps it was not that the god was incomprehensible, but that gods were by nature incomprehensible.

*That's just how it is* — if told as much, one accepted it as such, and without understanding, people offered their prayers.

If that was how things were, there was nothing to do but accept them as such — probably another of society's conventions, Krische had vaguely concluded.

The concept of faith was something Krische never really grasped, and so long as she recognized it that way, there was no issue.

Believing or not, as long as she went through the motions of prayer, that was the end of it.

It was much later that Krische — who until then had understood these things only in words — came to dimly understand.

The Bery of old had probably been Krische's god.

Krische's world was the estate Bery created, and that was everything.

And indeed, Bery had always lived up to that expectation. Her every word had seemed absolutely right, and the world bent to her will.

All the unknowns Krische carried seemed to be known by Bery alone — she had believed in her without a shred of doubt.

Yet what Bery always spoke of was not what should be done, but what one wished to be.

There was no word she could not recall; each memory was vivid, spreading behind her eyelids at will.

Whenever she found herself lost, the image of Bery speaking to her would appear.

It was rare for Krische to understand Bery's words in the moment — most of the time, it took years, or even decades, before the meaning seeped in, just a little.

Bery had probably always known it would be that way, and had spoken to Krische with that understanding.

Believing it was for the best, uncertainly.

As Krische's horizons widened, she saw more — and since Bery's absence, far more still.

The words Bery had spoken grew closer at hand, her figure grew vivid, grew beautiful.

She had never changed, not from the beginning.

All she had done was help Krische find the Krische that Krische wished to be.

She had simply kept that strange Krische company, without complaint, smiling, indulging her.

All the while, thinking earnestly for Krische's sake.

"Ehehe, Krische finished dusting the ornaments."

When she clung to Bery, a gentle hand stroked her head.

Cleaning, laundry, tidying up.

Every single day, the tasks were the same — yet entirely different.

Bery's foremost teaching had always been: to think.

"This area is clean too. ……It might be about time for Kreschenta-sama to wake up."

Surely she was doing the same right now — finger to her lips, thinking.

Bery had never been the god Krische once believed in, and so she always, always thought about things.

Small things, big things — earnestly thinking about someone, spending each day that way.

Bery was probably quite eccentric — quite a strange person.

Search the whole world over, and you would never find another.

But precisely because of that, she looked more beautiful than anyone else, Krische thought.

Always with her head full of someone else — helping that person, that was it.

The servant for the one person in the world whom she did not look after.

The Krische that Krische wished to be was that kind of Krische.

"Well then, Krische will go check on Selene."

Today, the chore of dealing with Kreschenta — who would pretend to be sulky and cling like a child.

Even if she wanted to see how Selene's vase was coming along, Bery only had one body.

So Krische's job was to help Bery in that way.

"Fufu, yes. Then please take care of ojou-sama."

A kiss for the smiling Bery.

Krische's midday began in just that way.

# Chapter 352: Krische's Krische — Midday

It was a small building erected right beside the estate — or rather, what had once been one.

It had originally been nothing more than a hut, but then a hut appeared beside the hut, then a second floor, then another expansion, and now it had grown into something resembling a small manor.

"Selene, Krische is coming in."

She knocked and entered to find objects strewn everywhere.

The polar opposite of the impeccably organized estate — pure chaos.

Inside, Selene was wearing a leather apron with her long hair tied up high, a serious expression on her face as she shaped the clay on the spinning wheel.

Shelves upon shelves held what might have been finished pieces or failures — unfired clay objects of indeterminate status.

On the desk, specialized tools were scattered carelessly among clay-stained water and lumps of clay.

On a clay tablet lay what appeared to be a blueprint — a cryptic drawing.

Each time she visited, Krische thought perhaps things could be tidied up a bit more, but according to Selene, this was already organized. If Krische started cleaning without permission, she'd hear *don't move that* and *that goes there* — Selene was particular.

Touching anything required Selene's explicit permission.

Pottery had been her latest obsession for the past several years.

Before that, it was glasswork. Before that, wood carving.

When she'd first built this shed, what Selene had started making was bladed weapons, but Krische hadn't seen her work iron in over two hundred years.

Naturally, the eastern kitchen knife purchased to prepare fish remained a captive to this day.

According to Selene,

*'The straightest, shortest path isn't always the right answer. There are things in this world you can only learn by seeing and touching a variety of things, and I'm in the middle of that kind of long journey right now.'*

——so she claimed, but Krische still could not find the connection between knife-making and pottery.

Selene was certainly not a dexterous person, but had she simply spent several hundred years single-mindedly forging knives without detours, she would surely have been able to produce a decent knife by now.

That considered, it seemed to Krische like she was simply taking an impossibly long detour.

Be that as it may, Krische was currently a servant.

And Selene was the master of this estate.

Throwing cold water on a master who seemed to be enjoying her mysterious detour would be improper — so despite the faint sense of injustice, she had no choice but to watch over it.

*Shuru-shuru-shuru*, the wheel spun, and Selene's eyes were locked in concentration.

The clay stretched thin, tracing a curve — and *funi*, collapsed.

"Ah……ahhh……"

Selene covered her face, flung her head back, and gazed up at the ceiling in despair.

Krische didn't quite understand, but judging by the reaction, it must have been going well by Selene's standards.

Pouring tea into a carved wooden cup, Krische studied the collapsed clay and considered.

At times like these, offering a well-turned remark was the mark of a good servant.

She nodded once, and smiled.

"Selene, it's all right. They say all things with form will eventually break……so something that's broken from the start could be considered quite avant-garde as a work of art."

"……That's not comforting at all, you fool."

"Muu……"

It was said that novelty and unprecedented forms of expression were more valued than practicality — but art was a truly difficult concept.

Krische still couldn't quite grasp it.

Selene washed her hands in the water basin, dried them with a towel, and picked up the cup for a sip of tea.

She leaned back and raised both arms, swaying side to side in a stretch.

Krische mimicked the stretch, swaying the same way, and Selene glared. *Stop copying me.*

Selene stretched like this virtually every time they met.

It had become something of a greeting.

"Ehehe. What are you making today?"

"A vase. Remember how you all brought back some beautiful flowers the other day?"

"Beautiful flowers……"

She tilted her head and thought, then, prompted by the words *brought back*, clapped her hands together.

"The blue-sparkle grass."

A pale blue flower that opened under moonlight and glowed with a blue sparkle. A newly discovered species.

Bery had taken quite a liking to the field of them and brought some back.

It was only the other day that they'd made a flowerbed for it in a corner of the garden.

"……Your naming sense remains questionable, as always. But, well, it was beautiful, so I thought I'd display some by the window in my room."

"Hmm……but Selene, you've got quite a few vases stored up in the shed. Twelve……no, Anne broke two, so ten now."

"……It was specifically one of the ones Anne broke that I wanted to use."

Selene sighed, looking exasperated.

"But you still have ten spares……"

"Look, flowers and vases are a set. This kind of flower calls for this kind of vase — there are pairings."

"Pairings……"

"How should I put it……right, cooking works the same way, doesn't it? There's a right plate for every dish. If you just needed something to hold food, you could pile everything on a soup plate, but you choose the presentation to make it look good and appetizing, right?"

"That's……certainly true."

*Mm-hmm*, she nodded.

Selene had many puzzling fixations, but today's argument had a point.

Plating is part of the dish (from the Bery Compendium).

If she put it that way — well then, yes, Krische could understand.

Krische herself was still at the stage of mimicry, unable to claim full mastery of the concept, but it was not the sort of thing one could grasp through logic alone.

*Looks appetizing* was a supra-sensory concept.

A deeply, deeply profound element of cooking.

And presumably, the thing Selene described was something close to that.

Selene untied her apron and patted her knee, inviting Krische over.

Krische sat down and kissed her, earning a *mou* and a cheek-pinch. *Muniiii.*

"You really are completely honest when it's framed as cooking, aren't you……"

"If anything, it's Selene who has too many strange fixatio—— *uuu.*"

"They're not strange fixations."

Stretching Krische's cheek out with a *guiii*, Selene sighed.

"Teaching you the concept of art is no easy task, but……let's see. Bery rearranges the furnishings in the estate from time to time, right?"

"……? Yes."

"That's a kind of art, in a way. She thinks about how this looks better here, that looks better there, shifting positions, combining things. You do it too, don't you?"

"……I do, a bit."

"When you're adjusting things, what are you thinking about?"

Krische considered the question for a moment, then answered.

"Hmm……something that's, um, pretty, and……that Bery would like, maybe?"

At that, Selene's eyebrow twitched, followed by a sigh.

"……Fine. But what you're doing there is a kind of art, too. Making something that moves the person who sees it — makes them feel it's beautiful, or fills them with joy, or takes them by surprise — that's what art is."

"Makes them feel it's beautiful……"

"At its core. Art produced as physical objects like these exists, and art that creates spaces through the arrangement of objects — that's art too. The blue-sparkle-grass flowerbed is art. Tending the orchard is art, and the garden is art too. Even food is a kind of art, isn't it?"

Selene said this, and Krische nodded with a serious face.

"……I see. Indeed, when Krische first came to the estate, she was so, so moved by Bery's cooking."

How crude her younger self had been.

*Yes, yes*, Krische reflected on the past.

Dish after dish that transcended any concept of deliciousness she'd known until then.

The lengths to which Bery went for the sake of cooking.

Day after day, wave after wave of wonder — and that had never changed.

Even a single familiar dish, even a cookie, was transformed by Bery's hands into a small marvel.

Now, in matters of pure technique, there were a tiny few areas — just barely, only slightly — where Krische might have the edge.

But in the small touches that Bery adjusted from day to day, there was always something to marvel at.

Bery's hands were magic hands.

A pinch of salt or herb, a single toss of the pan — and she turned cooking into art.

"For Krische, Bery's cooking may be the closest thing to what Selene calls art. It really is art."

"I-I see……"

Once more Selene's eyebrow twitched, and she murmured her acknowledgment.

Come to think of it, Krische realized she hadn't eaten pumpkin in the past month.

A hollowed-out pumpkin filled with pumpkin-rich white sauce.

Blanketed in cheese and baked, herbs scattered over the golden gratin.

Bery's pumpkin art — pumpkin gratin.

In her mouth, the phantom of its flavor, and saliva welled.

She resolved to ask for pumpkin gratin for tonight's dinner.

"W-well, food does have the purpose of eating. The kind of art I do is more simply about conveying emotion — nothing else."

"Muu, simply……"

"Yes. Taking away the deliciousness, the utility as a tool — all of that."

Selene raised a finger and spoke.

Art truly was a difficult concept.

Something akin to the concept of *beauty*, Krische supposed.

Krische herself experienced feelings of *this is beautiful* or *this is wonderful*, and that was probably, if not identical, at least close to what Selene and the others felt.

But when asked what exactly was beautiful, what was wonderful — she struggled to answer.

Something beyond words.

Attaching reasons to the simple fact of *I felt that way* was difficult.

Words always seemed to trace the outline of something, slipping off it, never quite capturing the thing itself — always falling a little short, leaving her frustrated.

*Ah*, she thought, smiling as something occurred to her.

"Maybe it's like words."

"……Words?"

# Chapter 354: Krische's Krische — Evening

Breakfast was made, as a rule, by two.

"As I said…… I honestly have no purpose left in life. And by sheer chance, here you are before me, carrying a question. ……After they died, I thought there couldn't be any gods in this world…… but perhaps this is exactly the kind of providence a god would arrange."

He gave a wry smile to the puzzled-looking Sena.

A face so like his daughter, yet entirely different.

His research, which he had thrown himself into to soothe his own grief — she was its culmination.

"I don't want to give you a cheap answer to your question. But if it's helping you find that answer for yourself, I'll gladly take that on. Of course, this is just me being pushy — if you absolutely insist, I'll tell you my interpretation…… but I really do think it's a question you should resolve on your own."

*What do you want to do?* he asked, and Sena seemed to hesitate briefly before nodding.

"Honestly, I do not fully grasp the meaning of your words, but I assess that your proposal is made with my future benefit in mind. It would not be right to dismiss your goodwill…… I gratefully accept your proposal."

"……Good."

Doguru smiled.

"Well then, to start — let's give you a name, something human."

"……? I am Unit Three."

"That's a serial number. Well, everything starts with form…… think of it as a nickname."

"Nickname……"

Her eyes widened just slightly, and he tilted his head.

"Something wrong?"

"No. It is a name given to foster familiarity. The Master has evaluated it as something excellent. I was previously called by the nickname Red Horn."

"……Red Horn."

"It derives from the red decorative part attached to the head frame for identification purposes. I recognize it as a very clear and good nickname."

Her expression didn't change, yet her voice somehow sounded pleased. He furrowed his brow.

Red Horn — he recalled such a reference from when he had been researching her.

Jaleia Gashea had been the general term for combat magical automatons of that era. The first mage-emperor had developed many outstanding magical automatons, all built upon the originals created by Alberinea.

Moonlight's legacy. True to the name he gave his nation, he had been a man who held Alberinea in the highest regard. Of his own creations, he reportedly said:

*'No matter how far I go, they are only clumsy imitations. These are nothing but crude puppets mimicking a great crystallization of wisdom.'*

Fearless, feeling no pain — the iron war-golems. The mass-produced Jaleia Gashea of the Mage Empire were, even as copies, formidable weapons capable of matching the numerically superior coalition forces. Yet, true to his words, the Alberinea originals had achieved incomparably greater results.

Even among the originals there were individual differences, and particularly outstanding specimens had been fitted with decorative elements for the purpose of intimidation. The first to be captured — by the "Red Goddesses," a unit composed entirely of red-haired women — was one such unit.

An original especially feared by the coalition forces, known as "Red Horn."

"……Ever since you woke up, the phrase 'unbelievable' just won't stop coming."

"……?"

"No, it's just my own musing. I wasn't much of a history enthusiast…… but I'm reminded that the past is a continuum leading to the world we live in now."

Doguru smiled wryly.

Sitting here, conversing with an entity whose name had endured for ten thousand years — the thought was surreal.

"Well, you've got the idea of a nickname right. Unit Three is your serial number and individual identifier, but we humans put all sorts of meanings and wishes into names. Or you could say we give names to meanings and wishes."

"That is complex."

At her words, Doguru laughed and touched the photo frame.

"……My daughter used to say she wanted a little sister. She'd seen a friend's younger sister and gotten envious. We'd smile and say, 'Maybe if you're good, one will come along,' my wife and I."

The ratio of ether in the body determined much of a person's constitution. Both Doguru and his wife were long-lived types with high ether ratios — longer lifespans, etheric manipulation ability, internal waste conversion, enhanced immunity, various advantages — but difficulty conceiving was the drawback.

The cause was generally etheric repulsion between partners. A kind of allergic reaction — the body expelled the embryo, treating it as foreign matter before it could develop. In some cases, the ether the developing child itself possessed triggered the body's rejection.

Manipulating one's own ether within another person's body was itself a fairly advanced technique. Etheric healing was permitted only to those with specialized training, and in the age of frequent wars, etheric interference itself had been a weapon — the development of defenses and the research to penetrate them had gone hand in hand.

It was hard to keep something alive, but killing was always easy. A specialist like Doguru, with his knowledge and skill in etheric prosthetics, could pop an unprotected person's head if he wished. Such incidents still occasionally made the news.

Ether was difficult to handle, and controlling it all the more so. Many long-lived couples used artificial wombs to avoid such fertility issues.

Furthermore, bodies that had long been protected by ether appeared healthy, but were in fact biologically very immunocompromised. Cases of mothers developing high fevers and dying when ether disrupted during childbirth — losing that protection — occurred even in modern times.

He had tried to persuade her to use an artificial womb given these dangers, but his wife had wanted to carry the child in her own body, insisting on a natural birth. Doguru had relented. If a second child came the same way, they'd said, laughing at their daughter's request——

——A nostalgic memory, from happy days.

"She took our instructions to heart, helping around the house, being good, saying 'I want a little sister soon,' and excitedly coming up with all sorts of names to tell us…… we didn't know what to do with her."

He smiled ruefully, closed his eyes for a moment, and turned to look at Sena's face.

Modeled on his daughter, she resembled her — with echoes of his wife too. Had the girl grown up, she might have looked just like this.

"The body you're using is modeled on that daughter of mine. She died, but she had come up with all those names, full of wishes and hopes…… I'd like to give one to you. ……How does 'Sena' sound?"

"I see. It is short, easy to pronounce, and a rationally sound designation. In terms of clarity — a criterion for evaluating nicknames — it is difficult for me to judge."

"Nickname evaluation criteria……?"

Puzzled by the unfamiliar phrase, she — Sena — nodded.

"Brevity, ease of pronunciation, and clarity form the evaluation criteria for nicknames. Key examples include: 'Vulture,' derived from a bald head and aquiline nose; 'Baldy Four-Eyes,' derived from a bald head and glasses; 'Skeleton,' derived from a skull-like face — the point of this evaluation criteria is to succinctly capture an external distinguishing feature."

"That's……"

Were those nicknames, or insults? As the bewildered Doguru looked on, Sena turned her gaze to a mirror placed in the room.

"This is an unremarkable, uninteresting face. However, judging from the photograph, it is understandable that from the Doctor's perspective, this would be a clear nickname. For individuals where external features are not distinctive, relational aspects or equipment shape are considered appropriate — for example, 'Skeleton Jr.' for a child of 'Skeleton.' In terms of clarity, then, it is likely not a bad nickname."

"I-I see……"

Receiving this inexplicable explanation of "nickname evaluation criteria," he nodded for the time being. The fact that such a thing existed meant it had been deliberately built in. There was no need for the hedging qualifier "presumably" — it was, without question, a completely unnecessary evaluation criterion.

Perhaps the creator had had a playful streak, but even so, the very idea of deliberately installing such a standard was not quite — no, entirely — beyond comprehension.

Yet at the same time, the presence of such a pointless element made it all the more clear that she had not been created as a mere combat doll. The same went for the apron dress she currently wore.

The person called Alberinea must have created this being with an era of peace in mind.

The hero who unified the continent, the genius inventor whose achievements still dwarfed those of people in the far future. What kind of person had she been?

"However, I have one question."

"……A question?"

"From what I've heard, the Doctor's daughter who passed away was, at the very least, younger than the Doctor. Is that correct?"

"Y-yes…… she was my daughter, so of course. What about it……?"

"Then it would be inappropriate to give me a name intended for her younger sister."

Sena tilted her head with a face modeled on his daughter, expressionless.

"In terms of age, I am the elder sister."

Nickname evaluation criteria. Before instilling that inexplicable standard, surely there had been far more important things to teach this child.

——Doguru could not fathom Alberinea.

* * *

Doguru's laboratory was located on the outskirts of a rural town, partway up a mountain.

It had started as a small prosthetics factory, built on solid bedrock, that he had been allowed to use. At the time, bio-culture prosthetics overwhelmingly dominated the market, and the traditional prosthetics industry was in decline.

Transplanting biological prostheses indistinguishable from the original body — that was what most amputees naturally wanted. The bio-prosthetics technology that humanity gained from the Minalshi, the interstellar civilization, had been revolutionary.

That said, the Minalshi valued human culture and prioritized its preservation. Rather than overwriting it like slash-and-burn farming, they conducted gradual technological enlightenment. They likely found much that was unknown to them in the etheric technologies native to this planet.

Care was taken not to hinder indigenous industry and development, but when a new wave of technology arrived, the old naturally faded. The bio-prosthetics they provided were what many had longed for, and as their distribution expanded and culture factories multiplied, the market share of mechanical prosthetics was steadily consumed.

Mechanical prosthetics became either a stopgap while awaiting bio-culture, or else specialized mechanical tools for craftsmen and technicians.

The company that had lent Doguru this space manufactured just such mechanical-tool prosthetics. A venerable old firm that had once been a household name, it had been swept up in the wave of technological innovation and drastically downsized. In this rural town, it now quietly produced specialized tools for craftsmen and technicians, keeping the business alive.

When Doguru said he wanted to pursue revolutionary etheric prosthetics research, they had even provided financial support. Even after gaining wealth and fame, he had stayed here for that reason.

He had considered relocating his lab to the city, but his wife had loved this place, and so had he. Research was never a one-person endeavor. It had borne fruit only because of the support of the many people in this community, and when it did, he and the then-bedridden chairman had embraced each other in tears.

His daughter had been born here, and he had thought he would never leave this place — and then the attack came.

He had been cautious enough. Protest groups had descended on him, and he had taken care with his daughter's commute.

The townspeople, whose lives had been enriched by Doguru's research, had gone out of their way to help as well. But one day, during a public lecture in the city, a bombing.

Doguru was injured, but his wife and daughter — who had been listening right beside him — were killed.

An old-model bomb using etheric crystals — the bomber had fumbled the throw.

Not a day passed when he didn't wish it had been him instead.

Half a year after she awoke — the reception room of Roktarna Corporation, now once again a prosthetics maker known the world over. The first time he had visited, it had been a corner of an office divided by partitions, but now it was a proper room with fine furniture and carpeting.

On the wall hung a framed group photo of the employees. Doguru and his wife were in it. Next to Doguru, the chairman sat in his wheelchair, smiling happily.

That was the kind of man he had been — displaying a photo like that in his reception room, rather than one of himself.

"You look…… a bit better."

Smiling softly, with a face much like the chairman's, was the company president, Ols.

He was a little older than Doguru. His strong-featured face carried the faint marks of age.

"Do I?"

"Yes. Ever since that terrible incident…… you've had such a burdened look."

Doguru smiled vaguely. After the incident, he had been unable to do anything for a while. He gave his research staff leave. He stopped visiting Roktarna, which he used to drop by regularly, and spent his days in the lab, living as if dead.

Despite being busy, Ols had visited Doguru time and again, checking in on him.

"I'm sorry. I've caused you so much worry, Ols-san."

"Not at all. It's just…… I'm a little relieved. It wouldn't have been right to offer easy words of comfort. If time has healed even a little of your heart, Doctor, nothing could make me happier."

"……Only because of your kindness. Believe me, I felt it — painfully so."

At times it had felt intrusive, yet more than that, he was grateful. Doguru had been spared from choosing death largely because of Ols.

"Is that bar where we used to drink still around?"

"Oh — yes…… I still go from time to time."

"Would you like to have a drink sometime? My treat, of course."

"In that case, I'd be delighted."

Ols smiled happily and nodded.

It was a nearby bar. Whenever he visited here, the chairman would drag him along, and his wife would scold him for drinking too much. A place full of happy memories, and Ols had often been there too.

Doguru may have had some talent, but more than that, he had been supported by people. From small things to large, he had been sustained by many, and it was only because of them that he had accomplished anything. Whenever things were hard, someone's hand had always been there, reaching out.

She, too — awakening after a long age of war, finding herself in the far future — deserved an outstretched hand.

From his jacket's inner pocket, he produced a physical storage medium and placed it on the table.

"……What's this?"

"Likely the culmination of my research. Not my work alone, though."

"Your culmination…… have you hired an assistant?"

"Not exactly, but something like that. In any case…… this will probably be my final research output."

At those words, Ols furrowed his brow. Doguru smiled ruefully and shook his head.

"Don't worry — it's not for a negative reason, but a positive one. I've simply found something else I want to do, apart from research."

"I see…… for a genius like you, it's a shame, but…… yes, perhaps stepping away for a while might be best for you."

Doguru nodded at Ols's words.

"Indeed. That said, I have no plans to leave here at the moment. ……I have debts I can never fully repay. Please don't hesitate to call on me if there's anything I can do, including in regard to this. I may be stepping back from the front lines, but I'm happy to help whenever needed."

"……Thank you. You can't know how much it means to hear that. But…… what is it you want to do, Doctor?"

"Actually, that's partly why I wanted to consult with you."

Jaleia Gashea Unit Three. When considering Sena's future, the biggest issue was her origins. Unauthorized AI research was not permitted.

That said, the current situation occupied a difficult gray area. It was true that Doguru had been conducting AI research, but there were no results from that work — he had simply developed an advanced etheric prosthetic body and installed a Jaleia Gashea core.

Whether a major legal issue existed, and whether there was a loophole around it — that was the real topic of discussion.

Jaleia Gashea cores had been studied using various approaches over the years. The primary goal was understanding the formulas and structures inscribed by Alberinea in the remote past. No one imagined that what they really were — artificial intelligences far surpassing modern technology, needing only a body to speak — and if it became known that Doguru had revived one, it would be world-shaking news.

Doguru had absolutely no intention of putting her in the public eye. That attention drew both good and bad — he knew that better than anyone.

Still, precautions were needed in case her existence was ever discovered, and given that she showed no signs of deterioration after ten thousand years of operation, plans needed to be made for what would happen after Doguru's death.

When Doguru mentioned his AI research, even Ols's face turned stern, but he quickly accepted it as something that had been unavoidable given Doguru's mental state. And then Doguru told him about her.

When he showed the data he'd handed over — the new prosthetic body design, optimized and completed with her input — Ols's eyes went wide. He had clearly been incredulous at first, but once he understood the circumstances, he agreed to see for himself, and Doguru brought him to the lab.

"Welcome. I am Sena, a servant of this household."

"……Oh, ah, pleased to meet you, Sena. I'm Ols Arowz. The Doctor and I go way back — both professionally and personally."

She bowed with old-fashioned formality in her apron dress. Eyes wide, Ols glanced at Doguru suspiciously.

"I swear, I didn't make her wear it…… I forgot to mention that."

"I-is that so. Well, even if it were otherwise, I wouldn't hold it against you, but……"

He spoke hastily to avoid the unfortunate misunderstanding, and they proceeded to the parlor. Cookies were laid out on the table, and a tea set was prepared. Sena showed them in, asked about milk preferences, poured tea for both, then stood demurely to the side.

Ols watched her, round-eyed, then glanced at Doguru.

"And this, too…… isn't something you taught her?"

"No, the work of a domestic servant was apparently built in as part of her functions from the start. This outfit is also her idea of formal attire…… she sewed it herself."

"……Herself?"

"She has, well, shall we say, many peculiar insistences. The creator, Olbri——"

"Alberinea, Doctor."

"S-sorry…… well, as you can see, she's also a bit strict about pronunciation."

Ols looked between the embarrassed Doguru and the expressionless Sena, and smiled gently.

"……I think I understand, a little, why you seem more at ease, Doctor."

Doguru nodded sheepishly.

"As you can see, she's modeled on my daughter, Meia. Sena, you may sit."

"Yes."

She sat on the sofa with impeccable posture and turned her gaze to the cookies. When Doguru nodded, she began eating the cookies she had made herself. Her cheeks softened ever so slightly, and Ols watched with an astonished look.

"When she first woke up half a year ago, I had designed the power system around liquid etheric recharging for practical purposes…… but around the time she awakened, I made a promise."

"A promise?"

"To help her resolve a question she'd been carrying for many years. I told her that understanding the human heart was probably the best way to do it, and she replied that if so, she wanted an exterior closer to a human's — to improve her resolution. After various modifications, she's now essentially human."

Ols stared at her with an air of disbelief.

"I've refined my etheric organic prosthetic body together with her in various ways. In a sense, you might call her a new form of humanity."

"……New humanity."

"It reminds me of a foolish thesis I wrote in high school. 'Bringing Humanity to a New Stage' — I'd slapped that cliché on as the headline…… the completion of a naive dream from when I knew nothing of the world. She is it. That data is a byproduct."

"So that's why…… your final research output."

"Yes. She completed my research for me."

As if a weight had been lifted, Doguru laughed with a look of bemused surrender.

"A body that surpasses the human yet is indistinguishable from one. The stir it would cause would dwarf the previous controversy over etheric organic prosthetics. I hesitated over whether to hand it to you at all, but I decided it would be better in your hands than sitting unused in mine. I leave the handling to you."

"You do realize you're casually handing me an impossibly heavy responsibility, Doctor."

"You're a kind soul who checked up on a man living like a corpse. I'm afraid I couldn't help but take advantage of that."

"……Honestly."

Ols glared at Doguru with a sigh and sipped his tea. Then he turned to Sena, who had by this point become a cookie-crushing machine.

"……Yes?"

"No, I was just thinking how remarkably human you seem. That Olbri——"

"Alberinea. Ols-sama."

"……My apologies. The Jalei——"

"Jaragasha. Ols-sama."

"……, I'm sorry. That, uh, existence known as Jaragasha is something I know about. Klinme——"

"Kleinmeil. Ols-sama."

"…………I'm sorry."

Ols, attempting to speak, was battered by a storm of corrections and lost his words. Doguru offered a wry "I'm sorry."

"I'm afraid it's because I keep mispronouncing things myself, and she's been getting stricter lately. She doesn't mean any harm……"

"Doctor, is there a problem with correcting incorrect pronunciation?"

"No…… it is, after all, what Alberinea herself wished of you. I can't come down too hard on it…… but for the sake of smooth communication, I do think it might be better to let minor mispronunciations pass for the moment, and wait until the other person finishes speaking before pointing them out."

Sena furrowed her brow in thought, then nodded.

"I see. The Doctor's proposal has sufficient merit to be adopted as a future standard for conversational communication."

"……Good."

As the relieved Doguru was being watched intently by Sena, she asked:

"However, Doctor. You previously stated that names have all sorts of meanings and wishes put into them, and I interpreted this to mean that names are very important. Is that interpretation incorrect?"

"Y-yes…… th-that's right."

"Overlooking incorrect names would mean treating them lightly. You are saying that for smooth communication, this should be sacrificed — is that correct?"

"I-I didn't mean to go that far…… how should I explain this……"

She was extremely capable, yet utterly inflexible. As Doguru wracked his brain, Ols laughed.

"I see. ……This may well be harder than continuing your research, Doctor."

"When it was my daughter, my wife was always good at explaining things to her…… I feel a bit nostalgic."

Doguru smiled, a little sadly.

Seeing that expression, Ols closed his eyes and nodded.

"I'll hold on to the data you've given me. It should not be used lightly, for short-term gain."

"……?"

"I understand this is your way of showing sincerity toward us. You no longer seek wealth or fame…… there's nothing in it for you. And if we handle it carelessly, it could bring turmoil to your doorstep. With the right artificial intelligence, this data could create new humans like her."

Ols held Doguru's gaze.

"Even knowing all that, you've entrusted it as payment for a troublesome favor — as a way to repay a debt. I'm sure that's how you meant it…… but Doctor, your research fulfilled my father's lifelong dream. And I, who succeeded him, achieved my own great ambition of making this company's name known across the world again. ……It is we who owe you a debt we can never repay. And Doctor…… I consider you my dearest friend."

Doguru's eyes widened, and Ols spoke with quiet sadness.

"I thought we had long since moved past a relationship of debts and calculations. All those times I visited you — all of it — was simply because I wanted to do something, anything, to help a suffering friend."

"……Ols-san."

"……If a friend is suffering, you want to help. That's all — a simple feeling and a simple act. If you see that as a debt to repay, I…… it makes me sad."

At those words, Doguru was at a loss, and Ols smiled.

"……Am I just a business partner's president to you, Doctor?"

"No, never…… that's not it at all."

"Being too serious is both your virtue and your flaw. If you consider me a friend, then it's simple. ……Just rely on me. Ever since that terrible incident, all I've wanted is to find some way to help you — my dear friend."

At those words, Doguru bowed his head. He pressed his lips together as though holding back tears.

"……Thank you, Ols-san."

Ols smiled and shook his head.

"Besides, I had already achieved my grand ambition and was wondering what to do next. ……Do you remember the first time you came to our company?"

"……Yes."

Back when he had nothing — no money, no track record.

He had been working as an assistant under a renowned researcher, but left over differences in approach, jobless, with no results to show for himself. Doguru had sent an audacious email pointing out flaws in the company's products, trying to get their attention. The late chairman had laughed at his nerve and welcomed him in.

Talk big, then prove it — improve what you've criticized, and I'll listen to your pitch. He had started as a technician. The title "Doctor" wasn't one of respect back then, but a nickname — half wry amusement at a young man whose dreams outpaced his station.

"At the time, I thought it was just another of Father's whims. We were in a twilight industry — even as an old name, we were a small company barely keeping our employees fed. Father was the only one who insisted we could become a world-renowned giant again…… and then he suddenly brings in this strange young man who says he's going to revolutionize etheric prosthetics."

As Ols spoke with exasperation, Doguru smiled wryly.

Every time he got drunk in those days, Doguru had spouted exactly those kinds of grand claims.

"But your passion was genuine. I was swept up in it too, along with the whole staff — you showed us a dream…… and when Father said he wanted to bet the company's future on you, not a single person objected."

Ols met Doguru's eyes squarely.

"You showed us a dream and led us to a wonderful future. I want to show you a wonderful dream in return, and lead you to that future. For the next ten, twenty years…… or perhaps the rest of my life, I will help you, Doctor."

"Ols-san……"

"It has never sat right with me — that you, of all people, who deserve to be happiest, were the one cast into misery. If you say you want to give her a future, then by all means. I'll consult with trustworthy people. We'll need to think about more than just the law — public opinion, too."

Doguru nodded, said "Yes," and then, unable to hold back any longer, pressed his hand to his eyes.

"……'Bringing humanity to a new stage.' You called it a childish dream, but it is grand and wonderful. You say this is the endpoint, Doctor, but for this research to be truly complete, what's needed is for her to walk a new life as a person, in this body. Please — let me help."

Ols smiled at him, then said:

"……The fact that you and Sena met is, in a way, Olbrinah's guidance. I'm in the prosthetics business myself — when I think of all the wonderful inscriptions we take for granted, helping you is also a way of giving back to Olbrinah."

He stood and extended his right hand.

"Let's share the same dream again, Doctor — like we used to."

A brief pause, and Doguru rose too, clasping the offered hand.

Doguru's eyes glistened faintly, and he smiled.

"Yes."

Ols, too, smiled gently, and gripped his hand firmly.

"Ols-sama."

Sena, who had been watching the two of them while nibbling cookies, spoke up.

"What is it, Sena?"

"Alberinea."

The chill in her voice left the room in silence.

For a while, only the sound of cookies cracking filled the room.

# Chapter 360: In a Far, Far Future III

The Albernaria Arsenal, where countless magical armaments had been produced, was located in the western quarter of the royal capital.

An extensive railway network threaded through its vast grounds, and workers and mages bustled about ceaselessly.

There were any number of enormous warehouses, and inside one where the Jaleia Gashea — the weapons that would dominate the world for a thousand years to come, three thousand units in total — stood in orderly rows, a man and a girl were present.

"Neigal, so this is where you've been?"

"Krische-sama……"

The Jaleia Gashea slept as though dead.

The man with the prosthetic arm — Neigal, the director of the Arsenal — had been gazing at the core storage compartments housed within them. He turned and immediately saluted.

"Krische heard the maintenance on all units has been completed. Thank you for your hard work."

"……It was nothing."

The silver-haired girl smiled as she spoke, then touched one of the cores at random and channeled mana into it.

As if in resonance, every core in the area glowed, and she nodded.

"In the Elsren war, the total was 27,543 people. Ehehe, with the learning function and data-sharing they've stopped being so silly, and their performance seems to have improved a fair bit too."

The girl could review the vast records stored within simply by touching them.

Hearing that number, Neigal lowered his eyes.

After the trial deployment in the Five-Nations War and the failures that resulted, what had been installed was a learning function.

*"Krische isn't perfect either, so oversights will still happen. If the Jaragasha themselves can review their combat records and learn from each one as they go, Krische thinks those kinds of silly mistakes can be reduced."*

After that, the Jaleia Gashea's performance had improved dramatically.

Under the pretext of learning trials and military training, several units were placed in training grounds, and soldiers competed eagerly to spar with them.

At first the Jaleia Gashea had been overwhelmed by the coordinated tactics of the elite soldiers, but gradually they learned.

Breaking out of encirclements on their own, defeating enemies one by one.

The mechanical dolls, which had once wielded nothing but brute force, acquired tactics; when pitted against the renowned warriors gathered in the army, they learned technique.

Improved by the girl's — by Alberinea's — hand, they gained more precise sensory capabilities, and in the war against Vezrea they produced terrifying results in mopping-up operations.

*——The age of warriors is over.*

So said the man regarded as the kingdom's finest spearman after Alberinea — Kokys Agrand.

*"One-on-one, I don't feel like I'd lose. But if ten or twenty of them come at you, your weapon won't hold up. And it's only because of who I am now that I wouldn't lose — back when I was young, I'd have been turned to mince in no time. Thinking about the young ones who'll have to fight these things from now on, it's a pitiful thing."*

*"Corps Commander Agrand……"*

*"For us, war's been something like a second daily life. It's a matter of life and death, sure, but when you pit the training you've both honed against each other, you come to understand each other's convictions, how the other lives, what's in their hearts, their very souls…… more than words ever could. It's rough, but it's a kind of dialogue too. Between human beings."*

His eyes held just a trace of loneliness.

*"But these things have nothing of the sort. No dialogue, no feeling — they simply cut down whatever enemy is in front of them. ……From here on, warriors like us, who live for the battlefield, will never sprout again."*

*——A peace-loving mechanical doll, just like Krische-sama, who hates war.*

Somewhere between exasperated and wistful, the great warrior's words lingered in his ears.

The Jaleia Gashea slaughtered to the last man the soldiers who had barricaded themselves inside a fortress, screaming of fighting to the death for their homeland.

The scene had been so ghastly it made Neigal, who had given the order, shudder.

No matter how many times he repeated in his mind that this was war, that it was to protect the lives of his comrades, the inside of his chest grew heavy as lead.

In the war against Elsren, such scenes must have played out everywhere.

Neigal too had gripped a sword in the civil war and killed any number of people, but there had been a sense of mutuality.

Both sides had things they couldn't yield on, and he too was laying his one and only life on the line to fight.

How easy and how comforting it had been to have that excuse — that was what he thought now.

Just making a ludicrously named mana crystal glow a little, and this iron doll would mercilessly cut down and kill the enemy.

The heat of the battlefield he had raced through alongside his comrades was a thing of the ever-more-distant past.

The more results he achieved, the more his body trembled, the more he felt cold, the more nauseous he became.

*——Krische-sama created these weapons for our sake.*

Neigal understood that, and he knew her kindness and her purity.

He adored her as a lord more deserving of devotion than any other, someone to whom he would dedicate his life in service.

And precisely because of that, he could not bring himself to resent anyone.

Neigal took one of the cores in hand.

By production number, it was Unit Three — the Jaleia Gashea he operated.

"What will become of these, once everything is over?"

"……?"

"For example, in the future…… when this power is no longer needed."

"Krische doesn't think anything in particular would happen to them, but……"

The girl cocked her head in thought with a genuinely adorable gesture, exactly befitting her appearance.

"Ah, but now that you mention it, that's true. It would be a bit of a waste to just throw them away after all the trouble of making them, or to leave them sleeping in a warehouse. It would be nice if they could do some kind of work besides the battlefield."

At those words, Neigal nodded happily.

Though she possessed a mind more brilliant than anyone's, she sometimes failed to notice the most obvious things, or said something off the mark — his lord was a little childlike and eccentric.

But when you asked her like this, she would properly consider the words of a person like Neigal, worry over them, and give an answer in her own gentle way.

The Jaleia Gashea were terrifying weapons.

Iron monsters created by a naïveté that could be called cruelty — the naïveté she possessed.

Nauseating in their sheer unfairness, chilling just to look at.

And yet Neigal cherished them as dearly as he did the prosthetic arm on his own body.

A peace-loving mechanical doll — an invention created by his lord, who loved peace more than anyone and cherished the people around her.

He couldn't count how many times he had thought about smashing them with his own hands.

But Neigal could not bring himself to hate these weapons, to condemn them as something truly evil.

Even as he listened to her casually recite the lives of the dead reduced to mere numbers —

*"See, a lot of people in the Black Flag died in the last war too, and Neigal and the others lost arms and legs left and right. But with soldiers made of stuff like this, even if their arms and legs fly off you can just fix them, and even if they break you can just rebuild them…… So everyone can relax, right?"*

He could never forget the way she had smiled as she said that.

He never would, for the rest of his life.

What was packed into this core was nothing less than the unmistakable love of his beloved lord.

"How about Krische reviews and expands the existing helper function a bit, things like cargo transport and such? That's what you're getting at, isn't it, Neigal?"

"Yes, Krische-sama. That is precisely what I meant to say."

Neigal gazed tenderly at the core in his hand.

"Someday, when they awaken in a peaceful world, I want the Jaleia Gashea that Krische-sama gave us to become something that works for people and is loved by them. ……Because it would be sad if something Krische-sama made for the sake of peace were unneeded in a peaceful world."

He hoped the love she had packed into these would someday reach someone.

Not as merciless, cruel killing dolls, but as things created to make people happy.

He hoped that even a little of the feelings of the lord Neigal loved from the bottom of his heart would be conveyed.

He noticed a glare-like gaze and hastily spoke.

"My apologies — Jaragasha, of course."

"……Honestly. No matter how long it's been, that accent of yours never goes away. Krische will have to make sure to teach the Jaragasha to watch out for incorrect pronunciations too."

At the sight of his lord folding her arms and nodding with a serious expression, Neigal smiled wryly.

"Well, never mind. As Krische said, this was meant to be the final adjustment, so once it's done, take some rest. From now on, Krische will focus on the gatan-goto. As for exterior maintenance and the like, Krische will leave that to the mages going forward."

"……Yes, ma'am. Thank you, Krische-sama."

"Krische will think about the helper function too. Rest well."

She said that with a serious face, raised a finger, and then pattered off at a run. Watching that retreating back, Neigal smiled too.

Always, always in a hurry, almost always at a trot no matter where she was going.

Her eagerness to finish tedious work as quickly as possible and return to the manor at the earliest moment was transparent.

Something so lovable had created these things.

There was no way he could ever hate them.

"……So she says. Good for you."

He spoke to the core as he gazed at it, then placed it on the shelf of the storage compartment as gently as if handling a jewel.

"Someday, may you awaken in a peaceful world."

A place where even the memory of what kind of weapon they had been was long forgotten.

——In the distant, distant future.

* * *

As things stood, Sena's existence was legally in the clear.

The current laws were not so unjust as that.

Even if an illegally created artificial intelligence were to exist, it was the developers who should be tried; the created being itself was recognized as having basic human rights, which were to be respected.

There was a criterion limiting this to those meeting the definition of "an artificial intelligence possessing a certain level of cognition and biological thought processes," but even the AI in an old industrial robot would meet it.

For Sena, there was no issue whatsoever.

In the first place, she was an artificial intelligence from an era when current law did not exist — in the sense that she had not violated any law, she was completely in the clear. So long as the fact that Doguru had been conducting AI research was set aside, there was no grounds for prosecution.

Still, being in the clear did not mean there were no problems at all.

The world was full of many ideologies and religions.

Life ethics were debated under every conceivable definition.

The propriety of putting machinery inside human bodies, the propriety of gene therapy and modification, the propriety of pharmacology and surgical treatment, the propriety of artificial intelligence and robots — the number of issues raised was overwhelming even to enumerate.

Doguru had his own views, and there was nothing wrong with that in itself.

He believed that a society where debate takes place is preferable to one where it does not, and that any assertion, whatever it may be, must be permitted as an assertion.

On that basis, people considered each other's positions, found common ground where they could, and through the tremendous effort of discovering compromises, today's society had been built.

But the world also contained people who believed their own views alone were absolutely correct, who turned hostility toward different views, who tried to resolve matters through violent means.

It was people like that who had taken Doguru's wife and daughter.

If he carelessly brought Sena out into the public eye, she would live in fear of their violence, and there was no way to prevent it.

Just as Doguru believed that verbal advocacy was right, there were people who considered violence a valid means of advocacy too — and in a sense, that was simply the way of things.

From Sena's era and long before, humanity had ultimately resolved matters through violence as a diplomatic tool, and even the intelligent Minalshi were currently waging war against a hostile species with whom dialogue was impossible.

Children who got into arguments hit each other, and there were adults like that too.

Human beings were not perfect, nor as rational as most believed.

If they were, there would have been no need for codified law to exist in the first place.

With all that in mind, the question was how to welcome Sena into this society as an individual.

The sticking point was her provenance.

The combat magical doll Jaleia Gashea, created by none other than Alberinea.

Hidden within it was an advanced artificial intelligence that could not be replicated even with modern technology — no matter how you looked at it, this would attract attention.

Scholars, celebrities, and the press would descend on this small rural town, and that alone would be bad enough, but for radical anti-AI activists, she would be a prime target.

They would see it as an opportunity to make their cause known to the world and would seek to harm her.

That said, he had no wish to take measures that would stain her record, such as forging an identity.

After extensive discussions with Ols and the others, they arrived at a somewhat forceful approach.

"It's been a while, Sena."

When he emerged from the prison where he had been incarcerated, there in the waiting car were Ols and Sena.

"……Doctor."

Sena's eyes widened at the sight of Doguru. She stopped in her tracks.

Ols approached with a beaming smile and embraced Doguru.

"Truly, well done, Doctor. ……Today, we've prepared a party at my place to celebrate your release. Everyone is waiting for you."

"Thank you, Ols-san. I'm sorry to have worried everyone."

He smiled while running a hand over his head, where the hair had grown out a little from its shaved state.

After that, he had spent another half year working with Sena to develop an artificial intelligence using ether crystals.

An AI possessing a certain degree of cognitive ability, closely resembling a human — they had crafted the story that Doguru, heartbroken and mentally unwell, had created an AI named Sena.

It was half true, except that Doguru had lacked the technology to build an AI himself.

When Ols heard the plan, he agreed to put on a performance: that, as a friend, he had advised Doguru to turn himself in.

The penalty for illegal AI development was severe.

However, the loss of Doguru's wife and daughter had been covered as a tragedy in news broadcasts around the world, and Ols had also held a major press conference on Doguru's behalf, explaining the circumstances in full — as a result, voices of sympathy outweighed the backlash.

Given the circumstances, mitigating factors were recognized and his sentence was reduced; the prison term came to only ten years.

More than Doguru, it must have been Ols who had the harder time.

While maintaining that the development of an AI was unforgivable, he said he could understand the feelings behind it.

His defense of Doguru as a dear friend even after he had committed a wrong subjected the company to a great deal of harassment.

"They'll get bored eventually," Ols had said, but when he came to visit, there were dark circles deep under his eyes, and he looked as though he had aged all at once — yet he never missed a visit and always wore a smile. Doguru could never thank him enough.

They separated, and both Ols and Doguru wiped away the tears that had inadvertently welled up, and laughed.

"Now then, to the car……"

"Yes. ……Is something the matter, Sena?"

Sena stood still, staring intently at Doguru.

Sena had been entrusted to Ols's family.

Doguru had told them not to bring her to visits, fearing what she might say, and Ols had explained to others that, ostensibly, keeping the two apart was a matter of discipline — part of atoning for his crime.

Apart from censored video letters, this was the first time Doguru had seen her in a long while.

Sena was staring at Doguru with a look of surprise.

And then, a hint of wavering in her gaze, as though she were uncertain.

Ols had told him that she had become considerably more human-like from living with his family.

She apparently got along well with Ols's daughter, who taught her all sorts of things, and she had naturally learned to smile at least a little.

Seeing her make such a face at this reunion, Doguru tensed, wondering if perhaps —

"……Sena?"

"Right now, I feel as though I understand many things. ……I cannot put it well into words, but."

Sena looked down, pressing a hand to her own chest as if confused.

At her words, Ols too looked at her in surprise.

Sena closed her eyes, raised her face, and fixed her gaze on Doguru.

"——How about 'Chestnut-Head'?"

To be precise, she was looking at the hair that had grown out a little from his shaved head.

"That is, um……"

"Your nickname, of course, Doctor."

*Of course about what?* Doguru thought.

But Sena wore a faint smile, her hand still pressed to her chest.

"Master wrote that a nickname is something that descends upon you in a sudden *pipin* flash…… I see now, so this is what that means. Right now, within me…… I do feel that something has descended in a *pipin* flash."

"I-I see, that's wonderful…… Sena."

"Yes. I had been thinking all along that I needed to give you a nickname in return for the one you gave me, and at last, I can offer mine."

Everything, for her sake.

That said, he hadn't had high hopes for this reunion after ten years, nor any particular expectations.

It was simply something Doguru wanted to do, nothing more.

But seeing her again now, the only thing that filled his entire being was a feeling he could not quite name: *"Ah, that's right, this is what this girl is like."*

"Chestnut-Head…… Chestnut-Head. Yes, it really is a very fine nickname."

She nodded again and again as if savoring her delight, and at her happy expression, he thought, *Well, all right then,* and smiled wryly, exchanging a glance with Ols.

Ols's residence was right next to Roktarna's headquarters.

When Ols and the others returned by car, his wife and daughter came out to greet them with smiles.

"Miri-chan, you've grown so much."

"Fufu, it has been ten years, after all…… Welcome home, Doctor."

"Ah, I'm home. I hear Sena was very much in your care. ……Thank you."

Golden hair, inherited from her mother.

The girl who wore that lovely smile released her embrace and shook her head.

"When Sena-san came to our house, I was still only eight, so really it's more that she took care of me……"

Miri smiled wryly.

"She helped me study, played with me — really, I should be the one saying thanks. Of course, she also made me cry plenty of times."

She said this while looking at Sena with evident amusement.

Since Sena would be staying at Ols's home until Doguru was released from prison, she had taken it upon herself to help around the house as a servant.

She had been eager about looking after Miri too, as if it were the most natural thing — but this was the inflexible Sena, after all.

*"Miri-sama, it should be time for your horizontal bar practice. Crying does not contribute to improvement; rather, it is a waste of time. Personally, I would like you to resume practice promptly."*

*"Uuu……"*

When the girl said she wanted to learn to do a pull-over on the bar, Sena responded flatly.

*"Miri-sama, perhaps you should revisit addition before studying equations. I cannot begin to imagine what sort of calculation is being performed inside Miri-sama's head that would cause 12 and 7 to produce the number 20."*

*"I-I just made a little mistake, you don't have to go that far……"*

*"By Miri-sama's answer, if one were to gather 12 and 7 rakura berries into a basket, a bizarre phenomenon would occur wherein one rakura berry of unknown origin would spontaneously materialize inside it. I believe this exceeds the scope of 'a little,' but is my definition incorrect?"*

*"Sh-shut up……"*

In studying, not even trivial mistakes were forgiven.

*"Miri-sama, you have improved. Your survival time has increased compared to before."*

*"……Okay, I get that Sena-san is strong? But couldn't you, I don't know, hold back a little for a beginner like me…… ? I've just been combo'd to death without being able to do anything."*

*"In terms of total play time, Miri-sama should have spent more hours on this game than I have, but…… Understood. I will restrict in-game tactical level to 'extremely silly' to match Miri-sama's capabilities, and endeavor to play with restraint going forward."*

*"Um, you're not actually doing all of this on purpose to mess with me……?"*

*"……? Mess with you?"*

*"N-never mind……"*

In games, she would steamroll her opponent utterly and without mercy.

In the beginning, Ols and his family had been troubled by the sight of Miri being reduced to tears almost daily, but fortunately she was a good-natured child and, rather than growing to dislike Sena, had accepted her as a difficult older sister.

"But it's a little sad that today is goodbye."

"We're right nearby. Come visit anytime. Sena will be happy too."

"……I will be happy?"

"……Sena-san, at times like this, you should say you'd be happy even if it's a lie."

At Miri's exasperated words, Doguru bowed his head apologetically.

Miri smiled wryly and shook her head.

"It's fine. I know that Sena-san is just this kind of person. By now, I know Sena-san better than you do, Doctor."

"You've spent ten years with this girl. I can't argue with that."

He laughed, and his eyes softened at the phrase "this kind of person."

To her, Sena was not merely a machine but a person.

That she could think of Sena that way — nothing made him happier.

About thirty people had gathered for the party.

Ols had conceived it not as a company event but as a small party among friends to welcome him home, yet most had apparently volunteered to attend.

The majority were researchers from the development division, people he had known for years.

Every one of them genuinely celebrated Doguru's return.

Most did not know the truth of Sena's origins; officially, Doguru had committed the crime of unauthorized AI development, yet they treated him with the same faces as before.

Several said that what mattered most was that he could smile again, and wept as they said it — and Doguru wept with them.

No one directed ill will toward Sena either; it seemed Ols had involved her in development division issues on several occasions, and they regarded her favorably.

Her "personality" was also understood, it seemed, and by now — just like Miri — she was accepted as "that kind of girl."

One of the reasons unauthorized AI development was illegal was humanitarian.

Human beings were not clean creatures.

There were those who took pleasure in hurting others, and those who sought to use others for their own gratification.

For the desires of such people, an artificial intelligence made an excellent tool.

There were always those who would toy with one, reasoning that it was merely a fabrication, not a real person.

To prevent such people from creating — from obtaining — an unfortunate AI, AI development was strictly regulated, and only work conforming to ethical standards was permitted.

Even without malice, the same held true.

An AI created as a replacement for a wife or daughter was, for instance, an unfortunate existence.

Despite being a different being entirely, to have one's identity twisted for selfish reasons and be treated as someone's substitute — that should never be permitted.

Doguru, even in the depths of his illness, had felt the same way, and while Sena served to fill the void left by his wife and daughter, he had never considered giving her their names.

Others had apparently been concerned about that point as well, but Sena, though she resembled his wife and daughter in appearance, was a completely separate being.

Nothing like the cheerful pair — serious, inflexible, never showing so much as a smile. When people understood that Doguru had properly recognized her individuality as an AI and created her on that basis, they were greatly relieved.

They accepted her as another daughter of Doguru's, and treated her accordingly.

Good, kind people.

These people, too, were Doguru's precious family.

When he thought of how he had forgotten even that during the depths of his illness, he felt ashamed.

It was not only Ols — many people had been deeply concerned for him.

When he conveyed his feelings to Ols,

"I thought you would probably say that, Doctor. Of course there are considerable risks, but like you, I too would like to believe in people's goodness. ……For Sena-kun's future, a great deal of goodwill will be necessary."

He called out to those gathered, asking that only those who could carry a secret to the grave come to the drawing room, and decided to tell them about Sena's origins.

A crystallization of Alberinea's wisdom, created over fourteen thousand years ago —

"——13,562 years ago, Doctor Chestnut-Head."

"……My apologies."

The story was interrupted several times by her corrections, but in a way, that too worked in their favor.

It seemed more than a few had harbored doubts about whether even someone of Doguru's caliber could have created something so extraordinarily capable on his own.

Still, Doguru's outlandish explanation that she was a product of lost technology created by Alberinea should have been hard to accept — but her reactions apparently convinced them.

There was no one involved in etheric prosthetics who did not know the name Alberinea, and there were staunch believers in the Alberinea mythology that Doguru himself had once dismissed.

And then —

"So Sena-san is actually a Jalei——"

"Jaragasha, Miri-sama."

"Ugh, could you at least not interrupt me when I'm trying to express my shock…… Anyway, now I understand why I was taught some bizarre pronunciation during history lessons…… Papa, you could have told me at least."

Miri glared at Ols and heaved a deep sigh, then looked to Sena and spoke.

"Hey, Sena-san. I have one favor to ask."

"……A favor?"

"Yeah, um——"

What intertwines with what, and leads to what kind of future — no one can know such things, including both the good and the bad.

A single meeting, a single word, can change something profoundly.

This day's exchange was surely one of those.

Doguru and those who had been listening laughed at the young girl's outlandish request, wondering whether she had even been paying attention to what they'd just heard.

But no one can know what lies ahead.

Perhaps, if what intertwines is love and goodness, it leads to a good future.

Most likely, that was exactly how this girl had drifted to this era.
