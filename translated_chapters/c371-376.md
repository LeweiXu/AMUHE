# Chapter 371: Alberan VII

The great tribe Aldos, then Garkha. And countless smaller tribes brought under her dominion in the blink of an eye — it had been a little over two years since the name Bazarishe of Enreine had begun to spread.

Just as those upheavals had settled and begun to fade from memory, a single proclamation was carried through the surrounding lands.

*— Children hiding in the shade of trees, gather beneath Alberan.*

Most took the words as a provocation. For those who revered the trees of the forest as sacred, especially, it was tantamount to trampling upon their faith.

Several tribes banded together. Weapons in hand, they marched into her territory, intending to make a public example of the impudent girl who presumed to call herself Alberan.

"……What is this?"

Eidenar, chieftain of the great tribe Rodett — a towering elder warrior with a long white beard woven into braids — stared in open-mouthed astonishment, his normally half-closed eyes thrown wide.

On horseback, leading four thousand warriors, they had passed through the forest and entered the domain of Alberan. What had been erected there was an enormous wall.

It was not particularly uncommon to line up crude logs and build a simple palisade. But the wall before his eyes was built of white stone, stacked in orderly rows to a height of twenty shaku, and atop its heights, archers gazed down at them in silence.

He had lived a hundred years, accumulating vast knowledge and experience. By now, he had thought, it was time to cede the chieftain's seat to a promising youngster, to support that successor and pass on the lessons he had learned, and to wait until the earth reclaimed him.

Then had come the insolent girl's words, piercing the ears of the man who had believed his work was done. He was not the only one enraged — he had called upon tribe after tribe, gathered them together, certain that this battle was the very purpose for which he had been born.

Surely there was no one else for the task. He was a great chieftain, and one who had lived long. Warriors paid especial respect to those who had reached old age — proof of having survived countless battles with a single life, all the more if unblemished by the dishonor of ever fleeing an enemy.

Eidenar — a valorous warrior and, at the same time, a sage rich in knowledge and experience. Even tribes that ordinarily opposed each other were willing to march shoulder to shoulder at his call, following him to strike down Bazarishe, their common enemy.

The great tribe Aldos, then Garkha. Given that these warriors, famed for their valor, had submitted to Bazarishe, everyone was prepared for a hard fight — but a battle of this scale came once in a lifetime, if at all. Even should they fall, there was no better place for a warrior to die.

The brave warriors laughed and rallied at that thought, and the raw youths among them were swept up in their fervor, morale soaring.

But at the sight of the enormous stone wall before them, every last man was struck speechless.

To hew logs and build a simple palisade around a settlement was a considerable labor in itself — something only a few tribes with surplus could manage. Since there were warriors capable of scaling such walls with ease, the palisade served less for practical defense than as a display of authority.

It was precisely because they understood the labor of building even a log wall that they could not believe what they were seeing — a wall built of quarried stone.

Cutting and dressing stone required labor on an entirely different order from timber. Moreover, no one had heard of any quality quarry in this vicinity. The sheer volume of stone used was so staggering it seemed conjured from nothing.

The blocks were dressed with astonishing uniformity, as though cut to order. Even a hundred craftsmen laboring for decades — could they truly have built anything on this scale?

It was precisely because Eidenar was a sage of such breadth — rich in knowledge, experience, and the wisdom to wield them — that the sight set off a trembling deep in his core that he could not suppress.

Before them was a great gate. Enormous — as though built to admit giants — and reinforced with iron, heavy and imposing. He did not imagine it could be easily broken by the axes they carried.

A glance told him the wall encircled the entire area. But of their four thousand warriors, fewer than a hundred could scale a wall of this height. The vast majority were powerless before twenty shaku of stone.

Naturally, the thought of raising ladders occurred to them — but it was obvious that arrows would rain down the entire time the ladders were being carried and climbed. Jutting from some sections of the wall were wooden platforms, positioned to shoot at the ladders from the side.

They had brought hunters of their own, of course. But even with equal numbers, the advantage of those shooting upward from below versus those raining arrows from above was self-evident. The crenellations along the wall made clear that an archery duel had been anticipated.

He was a superb sage. Even in his astonishment, his mind produced an endless stream of ideas for how to assault this wall. But the more he considered, the more he understood a single fact: *all of it had been anticipated when the wall was built*.

The great gate was set back slightly — beneath a stone arch, with gaps above and to the sides. Any attempt to breach the gate would be met with arrows, or worse, from those openings.

Given the ditch before the wall, the ladders would need to be immensely long. Building them sturdy enough would be no simple task, and preparing them in great numbers would require time — time they did not have the provisions to sustain. Even if they managed somehow, the morale of most warriors would not hold. No one would cheerfully help build ladders knowing they would be ordered to scale the wall under arrow-fire in a suicide charge.

Provisions aside, by tomorrow morning half of them might simply be gone.

And yet, to withdraw now would shatter this coalition. Tribes declaring submission to Bazarishe would inevitably appear. To lose this moment was to lose, permanently, the fighting strength needed to stand against Aldos and Garkha.

Even knowing all that, there was nothing to be done.

Wave after wave of newcomers emerged from the forest, struck speechless at the sight of the massive wall. Agitation rippled into murmuring. The words *This is beyond what mortals can do* reached his ears. And alongside them — the suggestion that she might truly be Alberan.

Some of those voices were born of fear, knowing there was no chance of victory. But for one who truly grasped how extraordinary the sight before him was, Eidenar could not bring himself to casually deny them.

Was Bazarishe truly, genuinely Alberan? The words dancing in his own heart were the same as theirs.

"……!"

What rang through the sky was the layered sound of many horns. Countless crimson banners — each bearing the arrogant design of a sword-tip thrust toward heaven — snapped and fluttered as a single girl appeared above the gate, directly before Eidenar and his host.

Backlit, her form was indistinct; only her beautiful silver hair shimmered in the sunlight. Draped in silk, wearing an elegant white dress, she simply stood — and looked down upon them in silence.

Understanding what was demanded, Eidenar stepped forward. That he did not dismount was his last act of pride as a chieftain.

Several followed — the chieftains who led each tribe.

Under the extraordinary tension, not one uttered a word as they advanced. Whatever their feelings, they bore the responsibility of leading their tribes. They surely understood the magnitude of what stood before them.

*The divine child used her power to build this wall.* Were someone to say so, they would have believed it. Such was the sight.

This entire region had come under Bazarishe's umbrella barely two years ago. If one were told this wall had been built in that span, it would be far easier to accept that it was a ruin from the age of the gods — the distant, primordial past.

When they reached the stone bridge leading to the gate, its craftsmanship stole their breath. The surface was dressed smooth, with not even the joints visible. As if a bridge-shaped rock had simply fallen from the sky.

Looking up to the top of the gate, they saw elegant golden hair in silver armor — presumably the Radiant, Bernaik, great chieftain of Aldos — offering a sword, and the girl drew it smoothly from its sheath.

"……Rodett great chieftain, Eidenar."

Her voice rang out like bells — sweet, and yet commanding. The sword's arc was smooth, and its point was leveled at Eidenar's face.

The tip wavered slightly, then indicated the ground.

From within a silhouette framed in silver, cool purple eyes cast an inorganic light that shone in Eidenar's own.

He could no longer think of this girl as an insolent fool usurping the name of Alberan.

"Lord Eidenar……!"

Eidenar dismounted, removed his helmet, dropped to one knee, and bowed his head.

"……Forgive our insolence, Alberan. This Eidenar, as great chieftain of Rodett, swears fealty to you — upon my sword and my name, and upon my ancestors and the gods."

A wave of agitation rippled through the warriors behind them. Yet not a single man hurled insults at Eidenar. Every one of them had been overawed by the situation.

The other chieftains exchanged glances, and one man roared.

"How contemptible, Eidenar! Surely you have not been cowed by a mere girl!?"

A hulking man with a beard as long and white as Eidenar's own.

"Boral……"

"It seems even your soul has grown old. To cower at a single wall and drop to your knees…… How wretched."

The great chieftain of the great tribe Selbas — a man who had crossed swords with Eidenar time and again. Eidenar's greatest enemy, and at the same time, his greatest friend.

Eidenar shot him a look that questioned his sanity, and then immediately understood. Boral did not believe there was any chance of victory, nor that this girl who had raised such walls was any ordinary person.

He had volunteered for the role of the one who barked — to test her true worth, and to show it to the warriors watching from behind. So that no warrior would feel that the chieftains had been frightened by a girl and surrendered without a fight.

Boral drew the longsword at his hip and leveled its point at the girl.

"Do not lump Boral, great chieftain of Selbas, with these gutless dogs! If you truly claim to be Alberan — descend to this earth and prove it!"

Boral laughed, then turned to the man beside the girl — the golden-haired warrior.

"That said, as much as she may be an impudent fool, drawing a sword on a woman or a child is not to my liking. ……I'd be just as happy to face you, Bernaik."

Bernaik narrowed his eyes and said something to Bazarishe. Bazarishe smiled, answered something in return, and then, catching the hem of her skirt, leapt lightly down to the bridge.

There was no other way to describe it — the landing was soundless.

Even the splendid landings of Bernaik and the other large man who followed her seemed crushingly heavy by comparison.

Eyes drawn to the brilliance of her landing, Boral nonetheless dismounted and shouldered his longsword.

"Great chieftain of Selbas, Boral. ……As Alberan, I accept your challenge."

Bazarishe advanced, longsword dangling at her side, a faint smile on her lips.

Seen up close, she was extraordinarily beautiful. Long, smooth hair like silver thread. A cleanly defined nose, and lips of cherry blossom that shaped a soft smile. Large purple eyes, edged in the same silver thread as her hair, shone like gemstones.

She was small, yet the slender lines of her limbs and the smallness of her head concealed that impression; combined with the soft feminine curves of her figure — she was as though sculpted by the gods.

Her mere presence captured the eye, and embodied the age of the divine.

Alberan — Bazarishe, simply by being there, drew everything around her into the world of legend.

"Whenever you please, from any direction."

And she stopped — one step inside Boral's striking range.

Strangely, neither Boral nor anyone watching took it as mockery.

"……!"

Boral sank his body — a body that belied his age — and drove forward with explosive force. A man who had slain three magical beasts single-handedly, who had devoted nearly a century to the art of war — he was one of the foremost warriors of the surrounding lands.

His lunge was beyond the ability of an ordinary man to follow. The body would be cleaved in two, and the victim would collapse without even comprehending what had happened.

Savage yet beautiful — the fastest sword. That single stroke embodied the name by which he was known: Boral the Body-Splitter.

But the girl slipped through as if threading a gap. Without the faintest show of fear, her fluid movement was no more than walking. The return stroke that sought her neck cleaved the air above her head. Only a handful saw her deflect the blade — gliding it off the spine of her own sword, turning it aside.

To most, it seemed the sword itself had flinched — had refused, of its own will, to cut her.

That a blade could not reach a god — it seemed entirely natural.

Even at a distance, Boral's mighty strokes were plain to see. What cut through the air was not merely wind — it was the atmosphere screaming.

Because the observers could grasp the ferocity of those movements, the girl before their eyes seemed something other than human. Swaying like a dance, her body wavering gently, they became convinced that she was shielded by an invisible divine power.

With a single step, she seemed to float backward, opening distance, and smiled with her hands behind her back, head tilted.

"Was that proof enough, Boral? ……Your sword is one worthy of being wielded in my service."

Panting, chest heaving, Boral looked at the girl — and laughed.

"……You are truly, genuinely Alberan. This Boral…… now I know that I lived to see this day."

His eyes softened, his mouth gentle.

"Those are gracious words. If so, then let me have a warrior's honor here. My young blades shall rally beneath the divine child's banner."

Words spoken softly enough for only those nearby to hear. Eidenar, catching them, closed his eyes for a moment and called the man's name.

"I see. ……What a shame."

With a laugh, she slipped past Boral's charge and came around behind him.

"……!"

With casual ease, the point of the sword held in reverse pierced his heart through his armor, and she withdrew it.

Boral smiled, sank to his knees, and toppled. Rolling onto his back, he gazed up at the sky.

Bazarishe knelt at his side, placed the sword he had dropped back into his hand, and smiled.

"Go forth — as a valorous warrior."

"……My thanks."

Boral smiled, content, and closed his eyes. After seeing that, Bazarishe rose quietly to her feet.

One glance, and Eidenar dropped to his knee again, head bowed. The other chieftains followed suit.

The warriors watching from afar — seeing the girl bestow her grace upon the great fallen warrior — also knelt and bowed their heads.

Catching it from the corner of her eye, she passed between the kneeling chieftains.

"A burial befitting a noble hero. ……Greetings can wait until after."

"……Yes."

Then she walked to the two men waiting before her — the great Mitskronia and the tall Bernaik.

"Mitskronia, if there are no issues, see to it that the warriors outside are given hospitality afterward."

"Yes. Splendidly done, Bazarishe-sama."

She smiled at Mitskronia's words, and then, passing through the gate, she saw Bernaik's sullen expression — and whispered:

"Well? That was properly Alberan-like Alberan, just as you wished. *Splendidly done, Bazarishe-sama* — you might say it too, for once."

"I might have considered it, had you maintained composure until we were in private…… This is exactly what I mean when I call you a fool."

"……Mitskronia, Bernaik is sulking because I took his dueling opponent. Please do something about him."

"That's my line. Give me back my sword, now."

Mitskronia chuckled at their exchange — *Dear me* — with a wry smile.

Then he turned and bellowed to the visibly tense soldiers around them.

"New warriors have rallied to Alberan's banner! Tonight, we celebrate with a feast! Raise your blades — let the heavens hear whose hand upholds us! Your voices shall reach the gods and bring yet greater blessings upon the divine child!!"

At those words, the men who finally showed relief answered with a thunderous roar.

# Chapter 372: Alberan VIII

Crush a certain type of rock, mix it with water and ash, and knead it — and over time, it hardens.

Bazarishe had first learned about what was called formed stone when she was still five years old. She had been examining old ruins with Mitskronia at the time.

"In ages past, there was apparently a time when many such stone-built temples existed…… Considering the labor involved, it's hard to believe."

"I wonder if they used up all the readily available stone in that era."

Being carried in Mitskronia's arms, Bazarishe said this between yawns.

There, the bases of enormous pillars and what appeared to be remnants of a roof remained. The tallest were around eight shaku, and two shaku across.

"Originally, there were stone roofs and splendid platforms as well, they say…… but those fragments were carted off and are now used in various temples around the region."

Mitskronia recounted to her the stories that had been passed down.

The divine child — Bazarishe was an exceptionally singular genius. By three she had mastered the manipulation of mana, could engrave mana crystal inscriptions with ease, and could recite the priests' words from memory after hearing them only once.

*Talented* was a word that fell hopelessly short.

Mitskronia's bedridden father — the previous chieftain — had strictly ordered the villagers never to let anyone outside know about Bazarishe, and appointed Mitskronia as her caretaker.

*Bazarishe is the divine child who will bring light to those who dwell in shadow.*

From that point on, Mitskronia had shown her everything, imparting every skill without reservation. The more knowledge and experience the divine child was given, the more truly divine she would become.

Like pouring water into a great jar with no visible bottom — no matter how much was poured in, she absorbed it all with casual ease.

That day was no different.

"……This isn't mere quarried stone."

"……?"

"There are no seams."

She hopped down from Mitskronia's arms, picked up a rock with surprising lightness, and hurled it with startling force at the upper portion of the pillar, cracking it open.

"B-Bazarishe-sama……!"

Mitskronia was stunned at her matter-of-fact destruction of a precious ruin, but she paid no heed. She picked up a piece of the broken section and examined it.

Her purple eyes took on an inorganic gleam as they studied it, and Mitskronia stopped his half-extended hand.

Bazarishe was childlike most of the time — but occasionally, she exhibited a transcendent demeanor. Those moments when her thoughts were circling at a speed beyond imagination.

In those instants, her usual childish expressions vanished, replaced by nothing.

The villagers called it divine rapture.

An appearance that made one hesitate to even speak to her. An unspoken understanding: unless she asked, one did not disturb Bazarishe in those moments.

She examined the pillar's cross-section, studied the surrounding rubble, looked this way and that. Then, without a word, she began to walk. Mitskronia followed.

What lay ahead was a cliff face.

"……Sword."

He offered his sword, and she drove it into the cliff, gouging out earth.

Then she tossed the sword aside and examined the extracted soil.

He had told her, many times, that swords were sacred and should be handled with care — but she had never quite taken it to heart, and while she was fine in ordinary circumstances, during moments like these her treatment of them was decidedly careless.

He picked up the sword. Her learning outweighed all else. Mitskronia had already half surrendered on the matter.

After toying with the soil for a while and examining the cliff face, Bazarishe spoke.

"That's closer to brick than stone."

"……Brick?"

"Something shaped and then hardened…… This cliff face has been artificially carved. The fine-grained clay here would naturally be one of the materials. Though, simply hardening this would just produce clay…… Either there's a special process, or a special ingredient."

Mitskronia furrowed his brow.

"If it were a process, one would expect it to have been passed down…… It's more reasonable to assume it's a question of materials. If we can identify what that is, we could make the same thing. Instead of quarrying, we'd simply mold it into the shape we need."

"You're saying such construction…… is possible even for us?"

"If we find the materials."

Bazarishe turned her eyes to Mitskronia, her elegant silver hair swaying.

"Cutting pillars like those back there to that quality is not impossible, per se — but there are no good quarries in this area. When you factor in the labor and effort, it's not realistic. That's what puzzled me."

"……I see."

"This has been most useful information. I trust a full week of unlimited honey is agreeable?"

"Three days, if you please."

"Muu……"

She narrowed her eyes and glared at Mitskronia.

"Five days."

"Very well — let us split the difference at four. Should you determine the actual method, I shall guarantee one full week of unlimited honey."

Bazarishe folded her arms in deep deliberation, then nodded — *I suppose I can put up with that*.

At her earnest manner, Mitskronia's eyes softened.

"Surely Bazarishe-sama would not resort to any underhanded tactic such as…… having already determined the method, yet parceling the information out piecemeal — but if you were to do so, I too would be forced to employ underhanded measures."

Bazarishe's shoulders gave a small jump, and her eyes darted sideways.

"I-I was just about to say it. I can make a rough estimate from the composition, at least……"

"……Composition?"

"There are small white stone fragments mixed in. I first thought they were simply mixed in, or that it was a difference in concentration during the combining process…… but it seems most reasonable to conclude that the stone was crushed and blended in."

She came closer and raised her arms for a carry; Mitskronia lifted her up.

"The white stone itself can be found in the composition of nearby rocks as well, but there's probably a solid mass composed entirely of the white portion somewhere. Given how few ruins like this exist, I doubt it's anywhere in this immediate area."

"……I see."

"In any case, a considerable quantity would be needed. Even if some were found, it would likely only suffice for experimental research. Whether this clay is truly an ingredient is still uncertain…… perhaps they simply exhausted some other resource."

Mitskronia nodded.

"I shall have a search conducted."

"Please do. ……In my estimation, everything in this world exists in a blended state."

"……Blended?"

Bazarishe nodded and indicated the sword at Mitskronia's side.

"Iron ore is refined to make iron. But I suspect the pebbles lying around here also contain something like iron elements. Gather enough and you could probably make the same iron. The impurities hammered out when iron ore is heated — they aren't iron, but they are elements that constitute something."

"Elements……"

"Yes. What matters is not the white stone itself, but its elements. The elements contained within this clay…… if they bond under the right conditions, it becomes something like that pillar."

She pointed at the ruin's pillar.

"Water freezes into ice when cooled. In heat, it becomes water. Gold is solid, but melts at high temperatures. Iron softens when heated — heat it enough and it would probably melt as well. ……Far, far back in time, all manner of elements were probably melted together like that."

"It is said that in the age of the gods, the earth was nothing but a smooth sphere."

That was the creation myth. The wrath of the supreme god Ordoris was said to have warped the smooth sphere, giving rise to the terrain of the world.

"I don't believe that, but never mind. ……In any event, heat is one condition that blends and separates elements. That's probably why fertile soil is faintly warm compared to barren ground, as well. Something invisible is blending and changing."

Bazarishe's words were not a child's idle fancy. No matter what she looked at, no one could peer so deeply, could read so much.

"The same is true for people…… Things in motion generate heat; things at rest grow cold. Bury a body and the flesh disappears into the soil, leaving only bone — yet a frozen corpse does not decay. Food does not spoil in winter either. Why do we start fire by rubbing wood together? To generate heat…… and heat transforms wood into flame, into ash and charcoal."

Bazarishe smiled.

"Everything changes. The human body is, in the end, the result of transforming what one eats…… growing, moving. And among the important factors in that process is heat — the right heat blends and separates elements."

*What do you think?* she said brightly.

"This is still in the realm of conjecture, but I don't think I'm far off. From that perspective, what matters is the concentration of the necessary elements…… If we can learn to identify soil and stone rich in the elements that turn earth into those pillars, we could make as much as we want."

"……A most fascinating discussion."

Everything she saw was different. A mere pillar, a single stone — yet Bazarishe extracted from it knowledge tens, hundreds, perhaps countless times beyond what Mitskronia could. And then she combined that vast knowledge inside her head with effortless ease, transmuting it into wisdom.

"This has been a most productive conversation, wouldn't you say? Combined with the earlier matter, perhaps a week would be appropriate."

"……So that's how you play it."

Mitskronia chuckled wryly, and thought for a moment.

"The human body, too, is the product of various things blending together?"

"That's what I think."

"……I see. Then allow me to pose a question. If one eats nothing but honey because it is sweet and delicious, would the balance of these elements in the body not become skewed and disordered?"

Bazarishe made a sour face and said *Urgh*, and Mitskronia shook his head with exaggerated solemnity.

"Believe me, nothing would please me more than to let Bazarishe-sama enjoy as much honey as she desires…… However, if it were to harm Bazarishe-sama's body, I could not look the other way. A body still growing…… surely bones and flesh cannot be formed from honey alone."

"Th-That's not necessarily true! Everyone says honey is full of nutrition!"

"And yet, relying on nothing but that is a serious concern…… Still, it would be cruel to offer nothing at all. ……Five days, then — shall we agree on that?"

Bazarishe groaned, and Mitskronia patted her head with a wry smile.

* * *

"I told you I don't want any. Stop putting honey in my tea without asking."

"I am attempting to educate you — a Bernaik who doesn't know the adult sophistication of honeyed tea — in the finer points of honeyed tea, because I feel sorry for you."

In one of the rooms of a manor within the walled citadel.

Having absorbed the great tribes with almost no casualties, exactly as planned, Bazarishe was in high spirits. *Let me brew you some tea personally*, she had declared, and proceeded to spoon honey into the cup — one heaping spoonful after another.

Bernaik, seated on the facing sofa, wore a look of pure exasperation. Mitskronia, watching from the side as Bazarishe loaded the tea with honey, smiled wryly.

Time was, Bazarishe had clutched a small pot of honey and refused to let go of it. After lengthy persuasion by Mitskronia, she now rarely ate it straight — but instead poured copious amounts into herbal tea.

Bazarishe stirred the honey-thickened tea and held it out to Bernaik with delight.

"Here you are, Bernaik. Surely a proud warrior like you wouldn't refuse tea offered by Alberan herself?"

"……Mitskronia, replace the tea."

"That's not allowed. You always make fun of me when I'm enjoying my honeyed tea! You should try it at least once before you say anything."

"Now, now, Lord Bernaik. I would ask you to humor us, for my sake……"

"See? Even Mitskronia says so."

Bernaik heaved a sigh. *Just one sip*, Bazarishe said, and reluctantly Bernaik brought the cup to his lips. The twitch of his eyebrow did not escape her, and her face lit up with delight.

"Hmph — just now, you completely made a 'that's surprisingly not bad' face. Your eyebrow does that little twitch at times like these, Bernaik. I know all about it."

"……I merely thought that a modest amount of honey was not objectionable. But regardless, it's far too sweet."

"If reducing it brings it to your taste, then you should acknowledge that honeyed tea itself is perfectly valid. If you promise not to complain anymore, I'll use less next time."

"I told you to stop putting it in without asking."

Bazarishe looked at Bernaik's disgruntled face and laughed with evident pleasure.

She had grown quite fond of him, it seemed — and seeing it always brought a sense of relief.

The divine child — she was a genius, and somewhat warped, but also thoroughly ingenuous. The villagers all thought well of her, yet there had always been a certain distance.

For a community that had been oppressed by the strong, cowering in fear — she was their symbol of hope. They drew a line born of reverence, and worshipped her with all their hearts. Bernaik's fame was well known, and many had not imagined so decisive a victory; yet even so, they had taken up their blades trembling, willing to stake their lives for this hope called Bazarishe.

Such devotion as to make them risk their lives — that was faith itself. And so no one aspired to stand beside her; they regarded her as one looks up to a god.

Bernaik, who talked back to her without a thought, mocked her on occasion, and treated her like a child — his existence meant more to her than anything.

She was Alberan in truth — yet the one person who believed it least, who wanted it least, was Bazarishe herself. She was so only because everyone around her wished it.

Perhaps that was why, in Bernaik's presence, Bazarishe became so much more talkative.

A knock sounded, and in came a muscular young man — Godevus, great chieftain of Garkha.

"My apologies — the situation was unclear, and I was delayed."

"That's quite all right. The discussion starts now."

He had been stationed outside the walls with his troops, held in reserve in case the newcomers chose to attack.

Even had they gone on the offensive, they surely would not have held out for long. Bazarishe was certain that a night raid, launched when their morale had utterly bottomed out, would be all it took to collapse them.

"So the wind does make it harder to see the smoke signals."

"It does…… I could tell it was the 'all clear' signal, more or less, but precisely because of that there was even less reason to hurry. I kept watch on their situation, just to be safe."

"That's fine. Caution is your virtue. Sit."

Godevus nodded and took a seat beside Bernaik.

"As it happens, Bazarishe just prepared a cup of tea for you."

And Bernaik slid the cup across to Godevus.

"Ah…… the tea I went to the trouble of brewing for you."

"Brew a new one."

"Don't give me orders."

Puffing her cheeks even as she poured a fresh cup of tea, Bazarishe looked on as Godevus stared at the cup and then at Bernaik, confused.

"Um…… isn't there some kind of strange thickness to this?"

"It is the tea of our Alberan. Drink it without concern."

"That's right — it's delicious."

"……Yes, sir."

Looking rather unconvinced, Godevus brought the cup to his lips and furrowed his brow. A faint herbal aroma drowned beneath the taste of honey.

Then he watched Bazarishe happily mixing honey — *this much should satisfy you* — and the stony-faced Bernaik, and spoke.

"Erm……"

"What, don't tell me you have complaints about Alberan's tea, Godevus."

"It's sweet and delicious, isn't it?"

"N-No…… y-yes, of course."

Silenced by the combined pressure of the two, Godevus could only agree.

Watching from the side, Mitskronia shook with quiet laughter.

# Chapter 373: Alberan IX

"— Construction of the citadel cities is proceeding well. It looks as though the key locations will be more or less completed this year."

The light conversation concluded, they turned to serious discussion. Cities enclosed by walls of formed stone were currently being built at strategic points throughout the territory.

Years of painstaking research from childhood had been brought to the practical stage by clay derived from volcanic ash and a white rock found in the southern mountains governed by Garkha. And in a few settlements near the quarry site, the method of production had been handed down in a small, quiet way.

The secret art of formed stone in that region had been an object of faith, used by priests when making sacred statues.

*This could prove quite useful*, Bazarishe had judged, and spread the method of making formed stone to each settlement — wrapped in a quasi-ritualistic ceremony. She performed a dance, pretended to cast a spell on the appropriate raw clay, had them form it into blocks, and months later displayed how the mixed earth had transformed into stone.

Those who had been bewildered at first came to believe that Bazarishe had used divine power to transmute earth into stone, and threw themselves enthusiastically into the construction of citadel cities, worshipping her all the more as the divine child.

A few of the sharper minds understood that the transformation was caused by mixing in special materials — but the technique was of no disadvantage to them. Accept Bazarishe as Alberan and submit, or refuse and lose everything. No one chose the latter.

Through peaceful negotiation, and pressed by the majority who genuinely believed Bazarishe to be Alberan, their submission was decided with ease.

Some tribes — chieftains and priests included — truly believed in the faith. Others used faith as a tool for consolidating their own authority and binding their people. For the latter, Bazarishe's existence was hardly unwelcome.

Offer to teach them the method in exchange for framing it as *a power bestowed by the divine child*, and everyone was happy. In any case, she could not personally inspect everything at all times.

Granting a degree of autonomy, and ensuring that each chieftain earned the respect of their people — that was important. Their prosperity and strength would, in the end, circle back to become Bazarishe's own.

"……That said, was it really wise to spread it this openly? It's good that they're growing stronger, but there'll be those with less wholesome ideas."

"In the end, it's just building material — if anything, we should spread it aggressively. Shields alone can't win a fight. Raising the cost of plundering and increasing the number of people who seriously till their fields — that's the goal."

Bazarishe smiled at Godevus.

"To be honest, I dislike warriors. They produce nothing."

"……That's blunt."

"It's the truth. They swing their swords and kill each other on their own…… Before drinking and feasting in the halls of Koreis, I wish they'd put the effort into making it so they could drink and feast right here to their hearts' content."

She spoke with open exasperation, and turned her glare on Bernaik.

"I'm saying this to you too, Bernaik. I'll make it so you can drink and feast as much as you want in my halls — so don't you dare go dying on your own."

"I spar with you regularly, don't I? If you want a closer match, I could always hold back a bit more."

Bernaik fixed his gaze on Bazarishe.

"Right now, you are the stronger one. That is a fact. ……But in time, I will surpass you — and challenge you to a proper duel. Remember that I will only listen to your nonsense until then."

Bazarishe giggled at his words, and answered with evident delight.

"Hehe, hehehe…… Whenever, however many years from now, however many times — I will accept every one. It seems you'll be stuck listening to my nonsense for life, Bernaik."

"Bark all you want."

Bernaik answered curtly, and Bazarishe swayed her silver hair with mounting amusement. *Now, now*, said Mitskronia, raising both hands in a conciliatory gesture.

"Let us refrain from quarreling, for now……"

"Quarreling? Bernaik started it by getting angry on his own. Please do something about him, Mitskronia."

"……It looks like you're the one picking the fight."

"M-Mitskronia-dono is quite right…… for now, let us return to the matter at hand."

Godevus, too, appealed to Bernaik for a ceasefire, following Mitskronia's lead. Seeing it, Bernaik sighed, drained his tea, and slid the empty cup to Bazarishe.

Bazarishe shook with laughter and poured fresh tea.

"With Rodett also pledging fealty, there are effectively no opposing forces left in the region. This entire area is now under Alberan's dominion. By next year, we'll have five cities…… Even those who haven't yet submitted will agree when we send envoys."

Far to the north — beyond the Dragon's Maw — the great tribe that ruled the entire northern expanse. A collective of warriors said to command a hundred or more subordinate tribes, known as the mightiest in all the surrounding lands.

Many of the tribes now living in this region were said to be descendants of those who had fled from them in terror.

Had it been mere strength, there would have been any number of ways to fight. The problem was their griffins.

Soaring at altitudes beyond the reach of arrows, crossing vast distances in half a day. The griffins themselves were ferocious beasts, capable of swatting aside several men with ease. Ridden by seasoned warriors, a force of several dozen descending at once was enough to ensure no tribe could resist. A single hurled spear and everyone would cower and surrender.

The Kreisharana themselves were said to be roughly comparable in size to Garkha. But whereas Garkha was simply Garkha, the Kreisharana — with similar numbers — dominated dozens of times as many tribes and a vast territory. Their power was on an entirely different order.

Yet if Alberan could unite every tribe in this region, opposing them was no longer an impossibility.

"No — next year, we march north and invade."

"What? With all due respect, that's reckless…… I don't mean to deny Bazarishe-sama's power, but —"

"They aren't fools, either. Even beyond their domain, once they learn that a major power has been built here, they'll move to break it apart before it can threaten them. They'll burn settlement after settlement."

Bernaik jerked his chin toward Bazarishe, who was mixing honey into her tea.

"The unity we have now holds because that fool over there —"

"I am no fool."

"— is maintaining the illusion that she is truly Alberan. If we don't keep showing the rest of them that illusion, our current unity will collapse overnight. You understand that much?"

Godevus furrowed his brow and nodded.

"If Alberan has truly been born upon this land, then Kreisharana's paltry griffins must not be allowed to strike first and burn countless settlements. We need to send a massive army toward the Dragon's Maw to draw their attention."

"A decoy…… to prevent the settlements from being burned?"

"Exactly. ……In that respect, she's quite convenient."

"You really are terribly rude."

She stood and set a cup of tea beside Bernaik, cheeks puffed.

Then she looked at Godevus.

"We push all the way to the Dragon's Maw at once, and I challenge them to single combat. Based on what I've heard from Mitskronia and Bernaik, they'll accept without question. Refusing to fight a mere girl would make them cowards."

When no one dares challenge her any longer, we proclaim that the Kreisharana have not a single hero left, and mock them for it. ……Even those who currently live in fear of the Kreisharana will be emboldened — convinced they can win under Alberan's banner."

"……How interesting. Turning their warrior's pride against them."

Godevus nodded readily. Accept the duel, and they are eliminated one by one. Refuse, and they are branded cowards. The very authority that made the Kreisharana the Kreisharana would be shaken.

"Exactly. We have no real means to defend against scattered settlement raids, but by that stage, the warriors' response will be fury, not fear. And any warrior who aspires to the name will carry the shame of having fled from a duel. ……Their morale will drop considerably."

*Interesting*, said Godevus, cheeks drawn taut.

"The main army is, for the time being, a prop to buy time. We build a fortress before the Dragon's Maw while simultaneously drilling the troops…… then, once everything is in order, we attack in full force. The Kreisharana — facing an Alberan that fights them on equal or better terms — will find the northern tribes, who currently cower under their rule, stirring and threatening their rear."

"You're reasonably quick on the uptake, Godevus. How helpful."

"……Thank you very much."

He bowed his head with a wry smile at Bazarishe, who was sprawled against the table beside Bernaik, practically lying across it.

He had thought next year impossible, but hearing the reasoning, it made perfect sense. Even if actual fighting broke out, their side would be full of fighting spirit — knowing they were going head-to-head with the dreaded Kreisharana and holding the advantage, even cowards would become heroes.

The glory everyone craved was right there for the taking.

"We'll want to think carefully about how we rally the troops, too. Rather than 'join and win glory,' the message should be 'anyone who doesn't join this once-in-an-age decisive battle is a coward.' ……We want to create that atmosphere."

Godevus continued.

"The battle with Lord Bernaik…… the ranks of warriors standing in perfect formation — I was overwhelmed at a single glance. To marshal that many men, to hold them in flawless order…… I knew at once that the opponent I was about to face was an even greater man than the rumors suggested."

"Those words are hard to take at face value."

"Well, the center's disarray couldn't be helped. Still, only someone who stood on the opposing side would understand that feeling. The reason the Garkha warriors so readily swore fealty and embraced the new way of fighting as soldiers was exactly that. ……Everyone felt they were witnessing the moment history changed."

What Godevus had yearned for, far beyond what his warriors could achieve, had taken shape before his eyes.

A collective functioning as a single living organism. A dream that should have remained nothing more than a fantasy, no matter how much he envisioned and desired it.

The *something* needed to bind people together was not a thing one could obtain simply by wanting it. It was bestowed, as if ordained by fate.

Godevus had never possessed that something. And that was precisely why the sight of a dream made real had overwhelmed him.

*This is someone who has what it takes* — the moment the enemy made him think that, the battle's outcome had already been decided.

"Being overwhelmed by a single look, face to face — imagine what happens when they see tens of thousands marching in perfect order from the heights of the sky. The effect on them will be far greater. And the larger the army grows, the more their nerve will crack. ……And when they watch champion after champion cut down in single combat, they'll be convinced they're facing the true Alberan."

Tens of thousands of warriors marching in disciplined formation. Just imagining it sent a shiver of exhilaration through him.

"Aside from the griffin cavalry guarding the Dragon's Maw, the bulk of their force will be warriors drafted from subordinate tribes. However many griffins they have, fearful warriors cannot hold the Maw. They won't come out to meet us — at worst, we'll face only sporadic griffin attacks. ……For the first impression alone, we want to gather as many as we can. Even the reluctant ones — once they see those duels, their fighting spirit will soar."

Godevus said that much, then rubbed his chin in thought.

"That said, is there actually a way to deal with the griffin cavalry head-on? In mountain warfare, the problem becomes somewhat manageable, but……"

"I have a few ideas. Human muscle has its limits, of course, and they're a nuisance no matter what. Using siege engines is one option."

"Siege engines…… you mean, like the ones from before?"

Bazarishe nodded.

Catapults made from bundled animal sinew, enormous fixed crossbows, pendulum-driven contraptions that hurled boulders — she had shown him all manner of things before. All of them boasted jaw-dropping destructive power.

She had said they were built *just in case*, against the possibility of rebellion — yet she had been having the various components mass-produced across different settlements, so that no single location could see the full picture.

"I did think the number of 'spares' seemed excessive for a contingency……"

"As you suspected — the main purpose was always this. I've heard over and over that arrows don't work on griffins, but if the problem is the downdraft from their wingbeats, then raw force can solve it — and it depends on what you're launching."

*Well, however*, Bazarishe continued.

"Fundamentally, conventional archery should be quite sufficient. If wind scatters the arrows, simply loose more — a properly directed arrow storm should bring down some, and if fear drives them to keep their distance, all they can do is blindly drop spears from on high. ……Against an army rather than a rabble, do you think such attacks amount to anything?"

"……They do not."

Spears raining from an altitude that arrows could not reach. Against tens of thousands, a few hundred at most. With reasonable spacing, the strikes were ineffective; some would be unlucky enough to be hit, but that was simply misfortune, nothing more. It would not cause a rout.

And even if they concentrated their spear drops on a massed formation, that would only be an issue during the approach to the Dragon's Maw.

If anything, if the enemy resorted to such tactics, everyone would laugh at the cowardice. If an atmosphere formed that the Kreisharana were spineless — such attacks would actually benefit Alberan.

Before the divine child, the warriors of Kreisharana can only drop spears from beyond arrow range. They target settlements' women, children, and the elderly. When Alberan's warriors draw near, they flee in panic.

At that point, the griffin cavalry was no longer the terrifying symbol of Kreisharana. It was the mount of cowards who could not fight face to face.

Battle was never a matter of body counts. The atmosphere that filled the field decided victory and defeat. And in a conflict of this scale, the mood beyond the battlefield — in the surrounding lands — would weigh heavily, tipping the balance to one side.

The other side controlled through fear. And this side united people through a hope called the divine child.

Once the fear that served as Kreisharana's foundation crumbled, they would inevitably lose to this girl.

Godevus was certain of it — and then he looked at Bazarishe, who had sidled up next to Bernaik, tilting the cup he had set down, sipping noisily.

"……Why are you drinking my tea?"

"You weren't touching it at all, so I was wondering whether I'd used too little honey…… Mitskronia, the honey pot, please. It's still bitter."

"Don't adjust it to your palate — give it back."

"No. Today is the day I educate you in the refined tastes of Alberan, and I've committed myself to it."

*Don't be ridiculous* — Bernaik reached for the cup; Bazarishe held it away, demanding honey from Mitskronia. *Now, now — on my behalf, if you please*, Mitskronia smiled wryly; Bernaik protested.

*Here we go again*, thought Godevus with weary resignation, and raised the cloyingly sweet honeyed tea to his lips.

On the cusp of what would be a turning point in history — Godevus peered at the honey pooled at the bottom of his cup, and sighed deeply, deeply.

# Chapter 374: Alberan X

The Kreisharana were those who had taken root at the foot of Albyagel, where the divine being slept.

In ancient times, they had been gatekeepers — tasked with preventing trespassers from disturbing the divine being's slumber.

The divine being was an absolute existence — a god given physical form.

Its roar of fury could pierce even the tallest mountains with ease, reshaping the very terrain. A single beat of its wings conjured a tempest, uprooting trees from their bases, and its mere landing set the earth to trembling.

When exactly they had become gatekeepers, no one knew for certain. Only the tale that had been passed down.

*'I tire of dealing with the likes of you, no matter how many I crush. If you wish to earn my tolerance, then do not disturb my contemplation. So long as that is honored, I shall permit your existence.'*

In the time when all trembled before the divine being, brave souls had prostrated themselves before it and begged for mercy. Many heroes lost their lives in the blink of an eye. But the words spoken to Almantis — the hero who would become the first chieftain of the Kreisharana — when he made his plea, were the beginning of everything.

From that day forward, they took on the role of gatekeepers.

In that era, there were many fools who believed they could slay the divine being — coming on a dare or a test of courage — and the Kreisharana, as gatekeepers, eliminated every last one.

To carry out that duty, they had to be strong. They trained themselves relentlessly, devoting their lives to standing guard at the sacred domain.

All of it was to protect countless innocent people from the divine being's wrath.

Yet not all who came were such fools. The first person permitted an audience with the divine being was the sage Zekulit, widely respected across the surrounding lands. He bowed before the Kreisharana, expressing his deep gratitude for their service, and asked to also convey his heartfelt thanks to the divine being who had permitted their existence.

They knew well that he had spent years traveling from tribe to tribe, preaching that none should provoke the divine being's anger — and that his character matched every word. Judging him worthy, they permitted the audience, and together conveyed their deep gratitude to the divine being.

*'My demand was made clear, and it has been honored — if only for a blink. I require nothing further. I have little interest or concern for the likes of you. So long as my words are kept, I shall turn a blind eye to you crawling before me.'*

A single glance of reddish-purple eyes, conveying a gulf in stature beyond measure.

At the words granted alongside that look, all present felt profound relief.

The name Kreisharana derived from a word meaning a blade-thin crescent moon. It was said to have been taken from the moon that hung directly above Albyagel on the night Almantis descended the mountain, having obtained the divine being's permission.

*— We shall become like that moon above — an unblemished blade.*

They would remember their oath to the divine being every time they gazed upon the moon.

Almantis, who died young — shielding a young warrior from a jade tiger — how overjoyed he would have been, they said to one another, and that night, all offered heartfelt prayers to the blade-moon hanging in the sky, thinking of him.

So that his noble oath and their reverence for the divine being would never be forgotten, they fashioned their beloved swords in the likeness of the crescent moon — bent in the middle — and in time, it took root as a cultural tradition.

When a warrior came of age and received a sword, they received with it their mission: to devote their life to guarding the gates of the sacred domain where the divine being dwelled.

For those who had heard the tales of the dragon age and its cataclysms, no role could have been a greater source of pride.

They devoted themselves solely to that role and asked nothing in return from the surrounding peoples. But the people around them were not so restrained.

It began as goodwill — settlements in the area brought their harvests as offerings. In those days, the people expressed genuine gratitude and a sincere desire to support their sacred duty.

The Kreisharana accepted these gifts with thanks. Living at the foot of Albyagel, teeming with magical beasts, was perilous in itself, and the people truly admired the warriors who shouldered the burden of guarding the gate on top of all that. The Kreisharana, in turn, were moved by the sincerity of those who wished to help however they could.

Once they mastered the taming of griffins — the great beasts that nested in Albyagel's peaks — as a means to safely patrol the divine being's surroundings, they began visiting settlements far and wide, spreading word of their duty and the oath to the divine being. They were warmly received everywhere by those who understood the divine being's fearsome power, and the Kreisharana village prospered.

They never accepted precious metals; they urged no one to give beyond their means. And precisely because of that restraint, respect and offerings flowed to them all the more. In return, the Kreisharana gladly took on dangerous work like hunting magical beasts.

It all began with goodwill.

But over the long years, goodwill became custom.

Imperceptibly, hierarchies formed between the Kreisharana and the surrounding settlements, and offerings became expected. The fracture came in a particularly harsh year.

Harvests failed across much of the region. People starved. Thanks to the divine being's great mana, some meager crops grew in the mountains of Albyagel — but with a growing population, it was barely enough to stave off hunger. Griffin feed had to be secured as well.

The Kreisharana demanded the usual tribute. As guardians of the sacred domain, neither griffins nor warriors could be allowed to starve. Should anyone slip through the gate and provoke the divine being's wrath, the consequences would be far worse than mere hunger.

They considered it a legitimate demand. But the settlements that had lost many lives because of it — their gratitude turned to hatred.

Years later, many settlements united and sent thousands of warriors against the Kreisharana. *Destroy the Kreisharana, who steal our harvests under the guise of the divine being's authority.*

There were those among the Kreisharana who genuinely grieved over the casualties, and efforts had been made to mend relations — but it was too late. And the battle was over in an instant.

Warriors whose lifetimes had been devoted purely to martial training, sustained by the offerings of others. Griffin cavalry of unmatched skill. And on the other side — the people from whom the Kreisharana had taken for years.

Against double their numbers, the Kreisharana suffered a mere dozen casualties. The disparity in strength was absolute.

The chieftains at the time raged that this was the result of forgetting their oath to the divine being, and sent warriors to every settlement to tear the chieftains and elders apart as a public example. If the people had forgotten gratitude and their oath, then they must be bound by fear, so that this never happened again. *In the divine being's stead, we shall govern them.*

And so, custom became domination.

They decreed that the warriors of Kreisharana — entrusted by the divine being to guard the gate — must be shown the utmost respect by every other tribe. Even a chieftain, when before even the lowest-ranking Kreisharana warrior, was required to press their forehead to the earth in the deepest obeisance.

Settlements that resisted were attacked by swarms of griffin cavalry and burned as an example.

Power gradually corrupted many warriors. In those early days, there were still principled warriors who punished the young ones who abused their authority — but as the years passed, even such transgressions became accepted as a matter of course.

Officially, they were taught to shun luxury and honor frugality. Within the Kreisharana village, an ascetic front was maintained. But one step outside, the rules did not apply. The freedom to wield power unchecked on patrol became the greatest pleasure of all.

And once word spread that becoming a griffin rider meant the ability to tour settlements and exercise that power, those who sought the position for exactly that reason began to multiply.

The moon that the noble tribe had raised on high was clouded by desire, and in time, thoroughly defiled.

Because they stood before the divine being, their awe of it alone never faded. But after long years of dealing with the divine being, they understood — truly — that it had no interest in human affairs, and even the duty became a mere pretense.

They trained their bodies to torment the weak. The sword existed not to recall the ancestors' oath, but to display and assert authority.

One glimpse of the curved sword hanging at their back, and everyone prostrated themselves in servile flattery, offering up even beloved wives and daughters. Disrespect toward a warrior meant annihilation for the village — everyone knew it.

Beating a chieftain for the offense of *looking at him the wrong way*, even killing him — for a warrior who rode a griffin, the punishment was a scolding at worst. Killing a child or two who threw stones was a laughing matter. Slaying a villager for being *insolent* was considered justified. Teaching them to never defy the Kreisharana through terror was treated as their duty.

* * *

— A single griffin rider descended upon a small forest settlement called Peerls.

The instant his form was sighted, every person outside — man, woman, old, young — dropped whatever was in their hands and prostrated themselves, grinding their foreheads into the earth.

The sharp-featured young man — Kutos — closed his eyes at the sight. *A wretched state of affairs.*

"You, old man — where might Great Warrior Fanaro be?"

"Y-Yes……! He should be at…… the chieftain's house, I believe."

"Understood."

He flew the griffin a short distance to the chieftain's house and dismounted. Once more, everyone in sight prostrated themselves. *Wait here*, he said, tying his griffin's reins to a fence post.

His mount Lev was well trained and gentle by nature, but this was to keep from frightening the people around them.

"Return to your work."

At his word, the one who came rushing out was the chieftain himself — a bearded, imposing man who dropped to his knees the moment he saw Kutos.

"At ease. Chieftain Dolus, I've come to summon Great Warrior Fanaro. Show me the way."

"……Yes, sir."

The man's expression showed relief. They had met before, several times. Dolus understood Kutos to be *one of the decent ones*, at least.

He rose quickly and led the way inside, to the bedroom.

— It was a married couple's bedroom.

Kutos understood at once, told Dolus he was dismissed, and knocked firmly on the door.

"This is Kutos! Great Warrior Fanaro — the great chieftain has summoned a council!"

*Ah……? Hold on a moment.*

"He said it's urgent."

A tongue-click came from inside, and then — *Move* — a voice. A scream from a woman — likely Dolus's wife.

Then the door swung open to reveal a tall, brown-haired man with a stubbled chin, completely naked.

"What the hell, I was having a good time."

"My apologies. But this is a critical discussion regarding the future. ……About the situation south of the Dragon's Maw — as the rumors say."

"I see. ……Can't skip out on that one. Get Roppera ready while I get dressed — give me a moment."

"Please keep in mind it's urgent."

"Yeah, yeah — as you command, Lord Kutos."

Fanaro laughed, shut the door, and Kutos sighed as he went outside. He fetched Fanaro's griffin from behind the house and brought it beside Lev.

Fanaro's mount and Lev were siblings, and quite fond of each other — they pressed their foreheads together.

After waiting considerably longer than *getting dressed* should have taken, Fanaro emerged looking thoroughly satisfied.

The chieftain appeared behind him, prostrating himself in farewell.

"Dolus — tell the other warriors your wife is my favorite. I don't want them touching her."

"……Yes, sir."

"And your daughter — I'll be claiming her for myself before long. Don't let anyone lay a hand on her."

The chieftain's hands trembled faintly — whether from humiliation, rage, or sorrow.

Feeling he could not bear to watch, Kutos called to Fanaro and took to the sky.

"Hey! Don't leave me behind!"

Fanaro launched immediately after, chasing Kutos into the air.

"Ha — keep frowning like that and those creases between your brows will be permanent, Kutos. You ought to let loose once in a while. It's our job to make them understand their place…… You know I'm one of the gentler ones."

The words were despicable — and the fact that they were true was what made it hopeless. A worthless man who casually had a chieftain's wife presented to him for his amusement — and even Fanaro was, by the standards of this place, quite moderate.

Men like that existed in droves, and not only among the Kreisharana. But the Kreisharana's problem was that they were too strong.

Other tribes might abuse their power, but they observed certain limits. Provoke too much resentment, and one day the people would unite and destroy them. But the Kreisharana were different.

An ordinary warrior was one thing — but among the Kreisharana's twelve supreme warriors, the Great Warriors, each one was practically a god. Even if one slaughtered an entire settlement on a whim, the punishment amounted to a scolding — such was the extent of the territory and the number of tribes they now ruled.

And there were quite a few such men among the Kreisharana.

The duty of guarding the sacred domain no longer existed in their minds. They lived solely to wield violence and power — for pleasure.

Only a handful grieved for what the Kreisharana had become. Promising young trainees sometimes grew into something better — but more often than not, they too were swallowed by desire and corrupted.

"A woman as fine as Dolus's wife is wasted on a spineless coward like him. I'm being restrained, you know — anyone else would've replaced Dolus as chieftain."

"……Quite so."

*That's all you can see in them*, Kutos thought, closing his eyes. Dolus and his wife were both admirable people. No matter how much degradation they suffered, they showed Fanaro every courtesy — all to keep the warriors' hands off their settlement.

As Fanaro said, Fanaro was one of the moderate ones. So long as Fanaro claimed a settlement, the other warriors would mostly leave it alone.

If their sacrifice could save the village — that was all they thought of. The love and respect the villagers showed them both was born from understanding the purity of that resolve.

Many settlements offered up their weakest as tribute. But in some, there were those who stepped forward willingly, for the sake of their community.

They were nothing like the warriors of Kreisharana. Unlike those warriors — including Kutos himself, who could only stand by and watch — wielding defiled swords, these men and women carried swords in their hearts.

And one day, those swords would probably destroy the Kreisharana. He almost wished they would.

He had thought about it the first time he stood before the divine being and beheld it.

How much courage must it have taken — standing before an opponent whose mere proximity made one's legs tremble — to beg for mercy?

Surely the first chieftain had accepted and embraced his own death before doing so. A selfless act — not only for family, friends, and acquaintances, but for complete strangers as well.

That Kutos had not become like the rest was because of his reverence for those great ancestors.

Yet the Kreisharana had lost their way. The shining moon was clouded by desire.

If change was to come, it would surely require great pain.

"Still, *Alberan* — that's an impudent name. Don't you think they're picking a fight with us, the ones who protect a being that might as well be a god?"

"I agree that the name is impudent."

Though not nearly as impudent, he thought, as the current Kreisharana daring to call themselves Kreisharana.

"We've never meddled south of the Dragon's Maw, after all. Gives me something to look forward to, getting a chance to stretch my legs down there. ……I hear this Alberan is quite a beauty, too."

"I've heard that rumor as well."

Only rumors — but apparently, a princess who bore that name had rapidly expanded her power over the past few years. He did not know whether she was a priestess, a seer, or what her standing was — but in the southern tribes, the tale was well known.

Alongside the claim that she was a peerless strategist.

"If that's the case, she belongs to the chieftain, but…… If she's going to be executed anyway, I wouldn't mind a taste."

He let the vulgar remark pass, and sighed.

Whatever manner of opponent they faced, the fact remained — the enemy had no griffins. The outcome would likely be something close to what those words implied.

*If only she really were the divine child.* Kutos wished it, silently, in his heart.

# Chapter 375: Alberan XI

"I had heard the rumors before, but a single minor tribe to the southeast, calling themselves *Alberan* — that blasphemous, presumptuous name against the divine being, a god in the flesh — I thought it would vanish on its own. But it seems they've pushed their way to the very threshold of the Dragon's Maw."

In the great council chamber where many warriors had gathered, the enormous man — Kreisharana great chieftain Sharril — sat cross-legged in the seat of honor and spoke.

In his youth, he had slain a jade tiger single-handedly. Tiger pelts were wrapped around his waist and shoulders, and he wore an ornately carved, elegant breastplate.

He was past eighty, yet even in his age he could not be called an old man. He had stopped visiting settlements to amuse himself when he became chieftain, but just last year he had taken yet another woman as a concubine.

If the Great Warriors were gods to the many settlements, then the god of gods, for the warriors themselves, was the divine being — and the great chieftain. If the divine being was a god by birth, then the great chieftain was a man born human who had wrested the seat from countless warriors — the warrior among warriors.

The Kreisharana had thoroughly lost their way as human beings — but the one thing they had never forgotten, the one thing they still held sacred, was their identity as warriors.

If a warrior was so pathetic as to be killed in some settlement, he was no warrior. Naturally, the settlement would receive *appropriate discipline* for having defied the Kreisharana — but the fallen warrior would not even be given a grave marker.

Worse — he would be reviled as a fool who had disgraced the Kreisharana by allowing himself to be killed by a mere civilian.

Even naked in bed, a Kreisharana warrior must be, at all times, a warrior of the Kreisharana. They still held at least that much pride.

Perhaps, no matter how deeply they drowned in pleasure and swaddled themselves in the fat of desire, they never forgot the reason the Kreisharana could be the Kreisharana.

Their dominion over countless settlements and vast territories was not made possible by the griffins. It was possible because the warriors who rode them were strong — and they knew it.

The memories of most warriors began with training.

Shortly after learning to walk, they learned pain before anything else. At five, boys entered communal training grounds and lived under the lash. Ample rest and food were provided, but they were subjected to grueling selection drills, sifted again and again.

Those who fell ill and weakened simply died. Only those who survived that process grew older and became Kreisharana warriors.

By fifteen, most children bore countless scars on their bodies — just like the battle-hardened veterans — and roughly half had perished along the way. The survivors became warriors.

No community anywhere produced warriors so relentlessly honed. Pit fifteen-year-olds against one another, and the Kreisharana would be unmatched in both name and fact — such was the singular pursuit of strength.

That half would die was taken as a given — and producing children was a warrior's duty. This, too, served as one more justification for rampaging through the settlements.

From the brutal competition and training of early childhood, they learned viscerally how unfair strength was. They knew, from bitter experience, that no number of them could defeat a single truly gifted individual. Because they knew this, they all paid reverence to the Great Warrior seats — won through competition among the elite few — and when only one was chosen from their ranks as great chieftain, that reverence became indistinguishable from dread.

For the many who had never laid eyes on the divine being, the great chieftain was a god.

And yet, to Kutos, he was simply a father. One more man swallowed by power, desire, and self-regard.

The young man who had survived the literally life-or-death training was pure of heart. Among those who oversaw the training, there were quite a few principled men who hoped for exactly that — who wished the young sprouts would steer the community toward a better path. The mentor Kutos most respected had been one such man.

*We are gatekeepers, sworn to guard the divine being.*

One among the Great Warriors, too, was such a man — and he stood guard at the gates of the sacred domain. He never appeared at gatherings like this, and Kutos had not seen him in years. With a handful of warriors, he lived in the beast-haunted land before the sacred domain, never visiting the village, and when he did, never staying long.

The one time Kutos had spoken with him, the Guardian Chief had said, with sadness:

*'The shining moon will never again rise over that land. ……If you truly wish to behold the radiance our ancestors once gazed upon, you will come here eventually.'*

He had once trained many warriors, Kutos had heard. But almost every young warrior, like white cloth that soils so easily, was consumed by desire and fell. Invited to depraved amusements as a *celebration* of becoming a warrior, their initial excuses of *I had no choice* gradually faded as they sank and degraded.

Perhaps those doing the inviting carried a sliver of guilt. Perhaps that was why, when they saw a pure youth succumb, they took comfort — *See? Everyone's the same*.

The more stubbornly one refused, the more persistently they pressed — and those like Kutos, who never yielded, were simply laughed off as eccentrics.

He wished he had the power to judge and lead them.

But talent was cruel. No matter how diligently he honed his spear, he could not match even someone like Fanaro — who spent his days gallivanting about.

However steeped in corruption, they were simply strong. That was the reality of it.

*Before going to gaze upon the shining moon alongside the Guardian Chief, I will win a Great Warrior's seat and do what I can here.* That ideal was being painted over, day by day, with a sense of helplessness.

"She who presumes to call herself Alberan — an insolent creature. Unchecked, she will eventually bare her fangs even at the divine being. Indeed, I hear she has been moving materials to the very threshold of the Dragon's Maw. ……The Dragon's Maw is the divine being's outer gate, and this cannot be overlooked."

Sharril surveyed the warriors with an imperious gaze.

"For the next hundred, two hundred years — we must demonstrate to those beyond our gate the righteousness of our cause, and the strength forged in its service. ……Bring me the girl who calls herself Alberan — this Bazarishe — alive. ……Dottrias."

"Sir. If you entrust the command to me, it would be my pleasure. ……Before the same moon rises again, I shall deliver her before the great chieftain without fail."

Sharril nodded.

Warrior Chief Dottrias — the great chieftain's right hand, who commanded all warriors. The kind of monster who would venture out alone on a lark and return with a jade tiger.

He was tall and supple of build, with a handsome face — but the depravity of his nature bled through in the crookedness of his smile.

"The south is vast. You'll need an escort to make the divine being's authority known. Take half the warriors if you wish. ……It is regrettable, but those who have not felt pain will not change. Teach them — until they understand in the depths of their souls how foolish they have been."

"Sir. And the Great Warriors?"

"Gladly — every one of them is positively itching to wield their spears against these fools who defy the divine being…… I can see it in their faces. Three should suffice to stay behind. Discuss amongst yourselves and decide."

At those words, Kutos could see the Great Warriors — Fanaro among them — exchanging grins.

"However — Bazarishe is the first priority. Secure her quickly, so we needn't go hunting later. Once that's done, do as you please with the rest."

It was a thoroughly rotten council.

Kutos listened with a leaden heart.

"Hmph — that makes it my victory again, Bernaik."

In the forest — the girl leveled a sword at the man's throat, and smiled.

Long silver hair; a slender yet beautifully curved figure wrapped in an elegant long robe. Large purple eyes softened beneath silver-fringed lids. A clean, straight nose; soft cherry-blossom lips — all of it shaped by the divine into beauty.

Her fair skin was flushed the faintest pink, silver strands clinging to her forehead and cheeks with perspiration, lending her a mortal reality.

The man who stared at the sword point with a sullen expression was beautiful in his own right. Golden, radiant hair; a face at once fierce and refined. A tall frame honed like sculpture, all excess stripped away — hard, yet supple as a beast.

He wore only plain trousers gathered at the ankles, and gauntlets — his physique bared to the air, and drenched in sweat even more than the girl.

"No — magnificently done…… To think anyone could press Bazarishe-sama this far."

"Being pressed is a loss regardless."

Mitskronia — the strong-jawed man with a neatly trimmed beard — applauded with a smile, and Bernaik glared at him sourly.

Godevus, who had watched the bout at Mitskronia's side, addressed Bernaik with a look of exasperation.

"……There is no one in all the land who could fight Bazarishe-sama as closely as you, Lord Bernaik. There is not another warrior like you under heaven."

Anyone who witnessed Bazarishe's swordsmanship could not doubt she was the divine child. She read every blade as though it were standing still, slipping through with a hair's breadth to spare; her smooth, walking-pace movement glided beneath even the fiercest strokes.

And the only one who made her break a sweat was Bernaik.

Five, six killing feints delivered in the span of a blink — sword strokes that froze the very air, terrifying even to watch. Bazarishe read every one with ease; feints meant only to draw a flinch did not even register. And yet she was forced to dodge, forced to commit her own blade — that was the nature of Bernaik's sword, pressing her with the minimum of movement through an endless stream of feints, leveraging his vastly superior reach to deny her his range while dominating from his.

It had looked one-sided. To be sure, Bazarishe killed every stroke and evaded, but there had been no gap to insert her own blade — she could only commit on the narrowest of openings.

"If she stopped worrying about her hair and clothes and just committed to the kill, it'd have been over in an instant."

"They are *not* unimportant. This is my favorite outfit. Besides — you're the one who said he liked it long."

"I said it was convenient for making you recognizable. Don't twist my words."

"Is that so? Then I'll cut it short. I've been holding back, enduring and enduring how *annoying* it is, growing it out for your sake!"

Her face flushed crimson with childlike fury, and Mitskronia intervened — *Now, now.*

"It is true that long hair is considered beautiful…… Lord Bernaik may say such things, but he does think longer is better."

"But he said he doesn't care! He makes me groom it every single day, and then he says he doesn't care!"

"I don't. Put up with it."

"L-Lord Bernaik, perhaps that's enough……"

Godevus hastily tried to mediate.

After witnessing that transcendent duel, what always followed was this — the same childish squabble.

"I go to the trouble of sparring with you, and I'm always being considerate, and yet — what more do you want from me? I'll have you know, I give you *extremely* special treatment."

"You're the one who said you wanted to loosen up before the main event."

"I offered to spar because you *looked* like you wanted to move! Do you think I *enjoy* swinging a sword? If I had the time, I'd brew tea and sleep on a sofa — I mastered the sword ages ago! Unlike you!"

The usual full-scale fury.

*Do something about this*, Bernaik's eyes said as he looked at Godevus, who shook his head.

*Now, now*, Mitskronia soothed the fuming Bazarishe.

"In any case, tomorrow is a critically important day for all of us. ……Quarreling over something like this beforehand is most unbecoming."

"Precisely. If Alberan is seen to be a fool, it damages our prestige. Calm her down."

"A fool? Did you just — of all the things to say — call *me* a fool?"

"I neither searched for the word nor settled for it. It is simply the only word, and its synonyms, that adequately describes you."

"L-Lord Bernaik, perhaps that's enough……"

Godevus could not fathom how this man was so gifted at pouring fuel on a fire.

Bazarishe was clearly very fond of Bernaik. She kept him close and gave him special treatment — that much was undeniable. She was somewhat immature in temperament, but beautiful enough to steal the breath of all who saw her, and possessed a talent that the word *genius* did not begin to describe.

The only one who could cross swords with her on anything approaching equal footing was Bernaik — and the only one who could treat her with such casual, almost dismissive irritation was also Bernaik.

Mitskronia sighed in exasperation, pondered a moment, and whispered something to Bazarishe. She nodded as though to say *I see*.

"Very well. But a loss is a loss regardless…… Three consecutive defeats leave no room for excuses. It is the warrior's code that the victor may do as she pleases with the vanquished…… I believe I would be remiss to take that lightly."

"Mitskronia — I'll kill you."

"I beg your forgiveness…… Tomorrow is of the utmost importance."

Mitskronia answered with a wry smile under Bernaik's glare.

Bazarishe tossed her sword aside, planted both hands on her hips — and grinned.

"You ought to understand just how tiresome hair maintenance is, Bernaik. I have decided that later, you will groom my hair — for as long as I wish, until I am fully satisfied. Taste a little of what I go through."

"Say whatever you like."

Bernaik turned on his heel, and Bazarishe chased after him.

"I am absolutely making you do it! If you won't, I'll go to bed with my hair soaking wet and march out tomorrow with it in a tangled mess!"

"Stop."

"I'll give Lina and the others strict orders, too — unless Bernaik grooms my hair, they are forbidden from touching it ever again. An Alberan with bed-head and a tangled mess, and you'll have to kneel before the enemy looking at *that* — so think carefully about whether you'd rather pick up a comb —"

And so on, merrily — angry or not, it was impossible to tell.

Mitskronia, whose beloved sword had once again been unceremoniously discarded, picked it up with the air of a man who had long since given up, wiped it with a cloth, and sheathed it.

"……It's always like this, every single time."

"Well, she is charming in her way. Since Lord Bernaik arrived, Bazarishe-sama has been having the time of her life…… She never had anyone who was her equal."

Mitskronia smiled wryly.

"……I want this war finished as soon as possible."

He began walking, and Godevus followed.

"You told me you want to build the divine child's nation — is that truly all you wish for?"

"That is a difficult question…… Especially when it comes to *wishes*. Everything we do is a process, a means to an end. And the true purpose lies beyond — in the heart."

Godevus furrowed his brow, and Mitskronia chuckled wryly.

"……What I want is to become a true warrior."

"If you're not a warrior, then most men alive aren't warriors. There aren't many who can match you."

Mitskronia shook his head.

"I am still nothing more than a man pretending to be a warrior. ……I am, in truth, a coward who could never deserve the name."

The self-deprecating words left Godevus puzzled, but he chose not to press further, and clapped the man on the shoulder.

"Then let us save the rest for a feast in the halls someday. I don't know whether I or you will arrive first…… but we'll meet there, I'm sure."

*Without a doubt*, Godevus laughed, and Mitskronia laughed with him.

"When that time comes, I shall tell you the tale over fine wine."

"I'll look forward to it…… until then."

He thrust his forearm out, and Mitskronia did the same. Gauntlet met gauntlet with a clear, ringing clash.
