# Chapter 281: Once, Upon the Seat of the Gods
 
In that basin, the roots of the World Tree spread in every direction, and its branches and leaves formed a canopy over the sky.
 
The mana that saturated the air made the atmosphere glow, and the endlessly drifting petals shimmered with rainbow light in the sun.
 
Falling, dissolving into blue particles before they reached the ground.
 
The season of the World Tree's blossoming was spring.
 
The world was suffused with fantastical color, and the wind sprites frolicked joyfully with the petals. Even in the earth where the World Tree's roots ran, earth sprites scurried busily through the gaps, tending to small flowers.
 
As though gathering the vast quantities of water the World Tree demanded, the water sprites poured a waterfall into the basin――there was no lake, no river, and yet a prodigious, impossible volume of water cascaded down from above the basin's rim, and the World Tree drank it in, its branches and leaves trembling.
 
The fire sprites gathered and danced, intent on undoing the water sprites' efforts, and steam rose from the waterfall's pool.
 
The air carried a faint mist, adding further mystery and complexity to those fantastical hues.
 
And there beside the trunk, four blue-gray forms made small hills.
 
Beasts that all things worshipped as gods――dragons, their enormous bodies curled. Before them sat a single girl.
 
Her long black hair loosely gathered, wearing only a wrap and hip cloth, she too seemed to dissolve into the landscape as she perched on a root.
 
With a wry smile at the mute, dancing spirits, she surveyed the hundreds of spheres of mana floating above her.
 
They appeared randomly arranged, yet followed a precise pattern.
 
Judging by the angle of the sun, it was about time. She turned to look toward the edge of the basin, where a door stood with no building attached, an impossible thing.
 
――After a moment, the door opened, and a beautiful red-haired servant peeked in.
 
As she entered, the mischievous wind sprites flew to greet her, and she responded with a mature smile on her somehow youthful face, wagging a finger.
 
The moisture in the air gathered, and what formed in midair was a cat made of water, as large as a small hut.
 
Two, four, eight, sixteen――each time the water-cat pounced it split, and in the blink of an eye over a hundred tiny water-cats raced around the basin and up into the sky.
 
The winged wind sprites shrieked with glee, chasing after the water-cats.
 
The servant, too, gave a light hop, carried by a gust as though pushed, and landed elegantly before the four dragons――beside Lila, the priestess of the divine being, offering a graceful bow.
 
Then she pointed a fingertip at one of the floating mana spheres, moved it just slightly, and that, apparently, was work enough.
 
She turned to Lila and stepped closer.
 
From the servant drifted an intoxicating, sweet fragrance――Lila's eyes wavered slightly.
 
"Lila-sama, today, at Elvena-sama's request, I tried making tarts with a variety of fruits."
 
"I-I see……"
 
"Yes. From what I tasted, they turned out very well."
 
A soft smile on rose-pink lips.
 
Neither thick nor assertive.
 
And yet, somehow, those lips were undeniably alluring.
 
Their sheen was surely from the sweet tart――likely drenched in honey――she had just tasted.
 
――This was a garden of temptation.
 
Life at the manor, somehow so decadent, knew no limits.
 
A world where one could fall to the very depths, if one wished.
 
A paradise where, lacking self-control, one would tumble ever downward――a prison of desire.
 
To maintain her reason as a Kreisharana priestess within these walls was no small feat.
 
She had resolved to sleep not in the manor but in a hut, and eat nothing but the forest's fruit, and had kept to that for the last five years――but that, too, had been broken a few days ago.
 
――*I've added just a small touch to it.*
 
Hearing such an invitation had been her undoing.
 
A dish using only forest ingredients. Not extravagant. Just a small touch, and it was delicious.
 
Lured by those words, she had tasted it――and before she knew it, Krische's coaxing (*it's from the forest, after all*) had extended even to meat.
 
After five years, the meat was so delicious she nearly drooled disgracefully.
 
At this rate, words like *abstinence* and *moderation* would vanish from her mind. That much was certain.
 
And once again, she would slide into the indulgent days whose count she had lost track of.
 
――She had to put a stop to this early.
 
So Lila steeled herself――but before she could speak, the servant's hand stroked her cheek.
 
Then a beguiling smile.
 
"……I'm planning a feast a little while from now. Krische-sama very much hopes it can also serve as a celebration for the end of Lila-sama's training……"
 
"A-a celebration……"
 
"Fufu, Lila-sama really does eat with such relish, it makes me happy as a cook. ……Of course, I do feel a bit sorry, knowing how much Lila-sama values her moderation."
 
Mischievously, gently, or perhaps with a look of innocent concern.
 
Many things mingled in her smile, and it drew the eye irresistibly.
 
Different from the dazzling brilliance of Kreschenta or Selene.
 
"……If you wouldn't mind, could you stay a little longer? Krische-sama has been putting in quite the effort."
 
When asked with such a smile, the words of refusal simply would not come.
 
And in truth, even if she tried, she would find herself committed before she knew it. That was always how it went.
 
Lila groaned quietly and nodded.
 
"Uuu……j-just a little longer, then……"
 
She was a kind person who always thought of Lila, but she was also a servant's servant who always fulfilled her master's wishes.
 
Regardless of whether Lila wanted it or not――the moment her master said *invite her*, there was no escape.
 
"Fufu. Yes. ……Just a little."
 
She smiled that mischievous smile, shoulders swaying, then looked Lila over.
 
The wrap and hip cloth――the barest minimum of fabric. She studied them, then traced her fingertips thoughtfully across her own lips.
 
"……Um, is something the matter?"
 
"Oh, I was just thinking it might be a bit cold."
 
"Cold……where is the feast going to be?"
 
She swayed her red hair, narrowed her eyes.
 
And then, Bery Argan smiled.
 
"This time――"
 
* * *
 
――An endless snowfield, as far as the eye could see.
 
One step onto it and the snow would swallow you to the waist; he plowed forward, packing the snow with his knees.
 
The blizzard had stopped, but snow still fell and fell.
 
All around, a world swathed entirely in white and indigo.
 
A sacred, untouched land, and he alone was leaving footprints.
 
Ground no one had ever touched. A frontier no one had reached.
 
This was the most sacred place in all the world.
 
Greybyalbe――the highest mountain in the world.
 
The domain of the gods, where no one had ever set foot.
 
And he was the first to leave his mark.
 
"……The snow is firming up."
 
The south face of Greybyalbe.
 
Relieved to finally feel solid ground underfoot, Borok Andorzea stroked his frozen beard and exhaled.
 
From head to toe he was covered in fur and fabric cold-weather gear, thick gloves, boots fitted with steel crampons.
 
Ten mana crystal rings on his fingers, and mana crystals in his boots as well.
 
The crystals, affixed to various parts of his body, carried inscribed formulae that maintained his body temperature, but that was far from the only thing barring the way to the heights of this mountain.
 
The vast, gentle snowfield that stretched on beyond ten thousand shaku.
 
Twenty paces and his breath was ragged; he had to stop, breathe deeply again and again, and press on.
 
The air was thin, but this was still only the beginning.
 
He had climbed mountains surpassing twenty thousand shaku before.
 
And this was a divine realm that exceeded thirty thousand.
 
This mountain's heights did not suffer any living thing.
 
He could easily imagine the hardships ahead, unlike any he had faced in his life.
 
From the northeasternmost mountains of the continent to the town at their base was a half-month journey one way.
 
He had made that trip many times over to transport supplies, and the preparations had taken years.
 
Financially, too, if this attempt failed, there would be no second chance.
 
He had never considered turning back partway.
 
He would reach the summit and return, or he would not return at all.
 
That was what this challenge was.
 
He simply pressed forward across the snowfield.
 
He scooped snow into his heated flask, warmed it, and drank.
 
Despite being surrounded by snow――by moisture――the mountain drained moisture from the body at a staggering rate.
 
Perhaps because even the moisture in the air froze.
 
He drank even when his throat didn't feel dry.
 
Again and again――no matter how much he drank, the deep thirst would not relent.
 
A full day of walking brought him to the great ice wall.
 
Up close, it was overwhelming. A colossal wall of rock and ice, its upper reaches overhanging dramatically, forming a kind of roof.
 
This was the first rest point.
 
The overhanging wall above deflected falling ice blocks and avalanches.
 
Here he could pitch a large tent without worry, and if he felt like it, even sleep spread-eagled.
 
A large cache of food was stored here, every manner of equipment kept ready, and he could spend an entire season here waiting for fair weather――but this was also the last place where such rest was permitted.
 
He crawled into the tent he had pitched in advance――fabric walls packed and piled thick with compacted snow――confirmed the air vents were clear, and lit a fire.
 
The firelight illuminated the room and brought warmth.
 
No matter how well insulated, cold was cold.
 
The best one could do was minimize the loss of heat the body generated.
 
But even so, Borok allowed his cheeks to relax.
 
He brought the food he'd stored in the snow outside into the tent and stuffed the pot with snow, setting it on the fire.
 
After eating in the morning, he had survived on trail food through the day.
 
Dried nilkana berries, high in nutritional value――they restored energy, but could not satisfy hunger itself.
 
Here, at least, he could pack his stomach full.
 
He threw meat and vegetables into the pot, then rock salt.
 
He broke frozen bread and tossed that in too.
 
Borok waited for the pot to boil, then went outside.
 
He lay spread-eagled on the snow and looked up at the sky.
 
A dark indigo sea, and a pale blue river formed by the stars.
 
And the glow of blue――the currents of mana drifting through the sky were clearly visible.
 
That, he had been told, was the result of a great magic performed by a glorious queen of ages past.
 
It was called the Great Rite of Heavenly Change, and the world had been filled with mana as a consequence――the reason the sky was still enveloped in vast mana, even now.
 
Climb a high mountain and look up at the night sky, and you could see it plainly with the naked eye.
 
The first time he had sensed its existence was at the age of eighteen.
 
When he climbed the World Tree.
 
The night sky he had looked up at then――the great currents of mana flowing through it.
 
Borok had simply been overwhelmed, and the pride he had felt at conquering the World Tree crumbled before the knowledge of his own smallness.
 
Fifteen hundred years ago, a glorious queen had cast a spell upon the world itself.
 
And here he was, boasting about a single small summit.
 
――After that, Borok traveled the world.
 
Summiting peaks, and visiting the World Trees in each region.
 
In some areas it caused problems and he had to give up, but in most places he was celebrated as an adventurer who had challenged great things.
 
But in the end, fame was merely a tool, a foothold for reaching the next height.
 
He documented his journeys in books, built his name to raise funds, and conquered many mountains.
 
All to leave his footprints on the summit of this mountain.
 
This was Borok's culmination, and every footprint he had left until now could be seen as part of this ascent.
 
Greybyalbe was the Corner of the Earth.
 
An isolated peak, rising far above the surrounding mountain ranges.
 
He had only climbed the gentle snowfield, yet even from this point at the entrance, he was already looking down on the peaks around him.
 
And from here, Greybyalbe thrust its horn of ice and rock far higher into the sky.
 
Many adventurers and ascetics had challenged it and never returned.
 
Avalanches and winds like storms, ice and air that did not permit human survival.
 
No matter how powerful a mana user, it was not something one could simply scramble up.
 
Release your body from the rock face, and if the wind blew at that instant, a human body would be dashed against the mountainside far below.
 
His vivid imagination, born of experience, carved the difficulty of this mountain into his body in the form of trembling and fear.
 
And only a human who could face that fear head-on and swallow it would be worthy of setting foot upon this mountain's summit.
 
Borok looked up at the sky, reached toward the moon, and clenched his fist around it.
 
He closed his eyes, then brushed off the snow and went back inside.
 
The boiling pot filled the tent with the rich aroma of rendered fat and vegetables.
 
The murky broth held dissolved nutrients and flavor.
 
Meat and vegetables――that alone made for a delicious soup.
 
He ladled some into a bowl and sipped the scalding broth; it burned from throat to stomach with rich flavor.
 
He was glad he had tossed in garlic.
 
He could feel his chilled body warming from the inside out.
 
Savoring the taste of the meat, he spread out the supplies he would carry upward.
 
Mana-thread rope, endlessly elastic; heavy, but capable of stretching a thousand shaku under light load.
 
The ice axe was crafted from artificial Kiuiris steel with enhanced mana affinity. The crampons were the same.
 
Both bore inscribed formulae for heat manipulation, so that when driven into the ice wall and infused with mana, they would melt the ice and instantly refreeze it, easily securing an anchor point.
 
For what lay ahead――the wall of ice and rock――no better tools existed.
 
These tools, refined over decades, were Borok's life itself.
 
Put into words, the task was simple.
 
Keep crampon-claws and axe always biting into the wall, crawl upward so the gale could never peel him free, and haul the supplies up by rope once an anchor was secured.
 
Barring problems with weather and avalanches, he was confident he could do it.
 
He had accumulated that much experience and that much ability.
 
Looking through all of history, he could declare without hesitation that no one surpassed him in mountaineering.
 
And yet even so, certainty eluded him.
 
Nature was not built to be something humans could stand against.
 
He had honed himself to the point where he was confident of surviving even a magical beast, but that did not mean he could survive an avalanche.
 
And even without that, a house-sized block of ice striking him dead-on meant instant death.
 
Whether he reached the summit came down to fate.
 
Do everything within your power, eliminate every possible cause of failure with obsessive care, hone yourself to the limit, and then leave the rest to luck.
 
That was what it meant to challenge a mountain.
 
He understood it was madness, and stepped forward anyway. That was human nature.
 
The soup was delicious.
 
But food eaten in the lowlands was far better.
 
There were no gold or jewels at the summit.
 
No paradise awaited beyond here.
 
*Why am I climbing?* he wondered.
 
When asked why he climbed mountains, he had given glib answers before, but none of them were true.
 
Borok climbed to end his challenge.
 
He climbed to make himself understand that there was nothing at the top.
 
He climbed to be freed from the thing that had possessed him that day.
 
――*I want to go to the highest place in the world someday.*
 
Because, at that moment, he had vaguely thought that.
 
He emptied the pot into his stomach, ate fruit, and brewed tea.
 
He stirred in plenty of honey, converting it to fuel.
 
The view from the top of the World Tree had been both a blessing and a curse.
 
The kind of emotion that determined the course of one's life――that was always how it was.
 
He waited five days for the weather to stabilize, then left the tent.
 
Clinging to the rock face, crawling ever higher.
 
When climbing, he thought of nothing but the climb.
 
From the shape of the wall, where was rockfall most likely?
 
If an avalanche occurred, which position was safest?
 
What he weighed was always risk against reward.
 
Even if dangerous, if it could be passed in an instant; even if safe, if it took too long――he managed the danger and chose his route.
 
There was no absolutely safe place on a mountain.
 
There was always some chance that ice or rock would fall from above and crush your skull.
 
In the end, climbing quickly reduced the total risk.
 
He had drilled the route he would take into his head beforehand.
 
For a wall that felt nearly vertical, he had mapped each foothold, each point to push off from.
 
All he had to do was trace the image.
 
Three days to conquer another ten thousand shaku of wall, and he arrived at the Great Jaw of Greybyalbe.
 
Beyond the countless rock spires jutting skyward, the slope eased again into a vast snowfield.
 
From here on was uncharted territory――thanks to the clouds and rock spires, even from neighboring mountains, one could not clearly see what lay beyond.
 
What he did know was that it was a nest of avalanches.
 
Straight ahead, quite far off, the horn-like summit was visible, and before it spread the snowfield.
 
Hidden beneath the snow, the tips of countless rock spires broke through here and there.
 
The true terrain must be brutally uneven.
 
The snow merely smoothed it into an apparent plain.
 
One wrong step and you would be imprisoned forever in a crevasse.
 
Folds in the snow surface were visible everywhere.
 
Those elegant pleats, like a noblewoman's skirt, were natural pitfalls formed by snow accumulating over crevasses.
 
He paused, considering whether to rest here.
 
It was not yet dusk.
 
A solid-looking rock was visible a short distance ahead, ideal for pitching a tent, and he could reach it before dark.
 
He should gain what distance he could.
 
So he thought, but could not bring himself to step forward.
 
*Why am I not going?* He puzzled over his own inaction, glancing at the rock ahead.
 
"――!?"
 
――In that instant, the snowfield split open.
 
A crack tore through the very ground he had been about to step onto, and it began to *slide*.
 
Borok threw himself against a nearby rock.
 
His eyes squeezed shut instinctively, and what he heard was a roar that no ordinary day could ever produce.
 
Enough snow to swallow a small town, sliding off from cloud height――an unfathomable sound.
 
His body, which had been heated from the climb, felt frozen to its very core.
 
The collapse went on and on, and from far below.
 
When its echoes finally subsided, he opened his eyes, steadying his shallow breathing.
 
It was probably only a surface layer sliding off.
 
And yet the landscape had been transformed.
 
The snow to his left had been largely swept away, and at the border with the snowfield to the right, a wall of snow some fifty shaku high had been born.
 
The snowfield he'd been about to step onto no longer existed, and had he taken that single step, he would certainly have been caught in it.
 
Which is to say: he would have died.
 
He sank to his haunches on the spot, deliberately forcing slow, steady breaths.
 
Thin air――excitement would inflict severe damage on the body.
 
He decided to pitch his tent right there for the day.
 
The sky, which had seemed stable, began to produce an ominous wind.
 
The moment when a journey that had seemed smooth was about to change――such things could be sensed instinctively, strangely enough.
 
An instinct held since the days when humans were no different from beasts.
 
That moment had been just such a thing.
 
He secured his small tent firmly, driving anchors into ice and rock in multiple places.
 
He packed and piled snow around the outside as a windbreak――and what had been a smooth journey took a sharp turn.
 
In the end, Borok spent two weeks inside that tent.
 
Inside, there was no light except the faint glow of a mana crystal used as a heat source.
 
Since then, the sky had raged, and the blizzard had begun.
 
Snow that stormed upward as though racing up the mountain in reverse.
 
The tent entrance was long since buried in snow; the air vent had been extended upward like a chimney.
 
Even peering through it, the sky was dim, telling Borok the blizzard continued.
 
His food supply totaled two weeks.
 
Even rationing, there would not be enough to summit and descend.
 
――It was a failure.
 
Still, he wondered: if he descended the next time the sky cleared, could he survive?
 
Thinking that, and yet, when the storm broke, Borok climbed without hesitation.
 
Following the ridgeline of exposed rock left by the avalanche, he scrambled up and down, crawling on all fours to keep from slipping.
 
This was a once-in-a-lifetime chance, and it was the whole of Borok's life.
 
It took a full day to cross the snowfield――the Jaw of Greybyalbe――and he reached the base of the horn that night.
 
He pitched the small tent, ate dried meat and dried nilkana, rested his body, and the next morning drove his ice axe into the wall.
 
Gale-force wind was the default.
 
A moment's carelessness and the wind would hurl his body away, and yet he pressed against the rock face, crawling upward.
 
Right, left, right, in steady rhythm, sometimes raising a leg high enough to split his hips.
 
Sometimes he caught an updraft, used the momentum to leap, and gained more height.
 
Each jump was a wager with his life as the stakes.
 
But he knew that without such risks, his stamina would not hold.
 
He no longer had food enough to climb with margin.
 
At times like these, he was grateful to his parents for being born a mana user.
 
What was impossible for an ordinary person, a mana user could push through. He could force his ice axe into rock instead of ice, and each leap covered significant altitude.
 
That he could still move in this extreme cold was also because he was a mana user.
 
With death so near, what welled up unbidden was gratitude for being born.
 
He thought of his father and mother.
 
He had not seen his parents in a long time. He had never thought he wanted to.
 
They, too, probably had no desire to see the son who had left home.
 
His name, as an adventurer, surely reached them, but not a single letter had come, nor had Borok sent one.
 
A strange thing.
 
In his youth, bitter about the strict upbringing and the demands placed on him, he had resented them.
 
That was why he had left home.
 
And yet here, in extremity, what came to mind was always his parents.
 
An old, name-only noble house.
 
A father who guarded his small domain as its lord.
 
And a mother who supported that father.
 
Two people who observed musty traditions.
 
There was no freedom, no openness, only suffocation――and still they went through their unchanging days to protect what their ancestors had left them.
 
Thinking of them, he wondered again: *why do I climb mountains?*
 
The truth was, there was nothing lofty about it.
 
He had simply wanted them to acknowledge him. That was all.
 
A difference in philosophy, nothing more.
 
And yet, even so――he wanted them to think that he was their son. That he was a son they could be proud of, to others and to their ancestors.
 
He thought of parents who had been strict with themselves and with others.
 
Practicing frugality, tolerating no waste, seeming almost like emotionless golems.
 
Their way of life had never looked happy, and yet in the small moments of daily life, the smiles that occasionally slipped through were genuine, and the people of their domain loved them deeply.
 
In times of famine they had emptied their private coffers without hesitation to protect those people, and as a child, he had always wondered why they could not direct that same love toward him.
 
*"We are stewards. The people entrust us with what they earn through sweat, and we return it to them as needed. This manor, this food, these clothes are all given to us by them. Never forget that. ……A lord must possess self-control beyond anyone."*
 
The children in the towns and villages had seemed so much happier.
 
From childhood, demanded to achieve beyond what was sufficient, his sleep cut short for training and study.
 
He envied other children his age who could cling to their parents, and worked solely to be praised, solely to be acknowledged.
 
And all he ever received was a single: *well done*.
 
His parents had always been busy for their people.
 
He clung to the rock, steadied his breathing, and swung his right hand.
 
Except in extreme circumstances, he always kept three of his four limbs anchored to the ice and rock.
 
Otherwise, the gale would tear him away in an instant.
 
Right hand, left hand, right foot, left foot.
 
Right hand, left foot, left hand, right foot.
 
Leaving scratches in rock and ice. As though proclaiming: *I am here.*
 
A tortoise-like pace, to accomplish what no one had ever done.
 
All the knowledge and wisdom, his parents had given him.
 
How to wield mana, his parents had given him.
 
How to work hard, everything――his parents had given him.
 
That he was here now was because of them.
 
No matter how far away he went, he was their son.
 
His breathing was shallow. He stopped and breathed, again and again.
 
The thin air never felt like enough no matter how much he inhaled, and he looked up at the sky.
 
The summit was not far now.
 
His mind grew hazy, but still he pushed upward.
 
Mountaineering was an ascetic trial.
 
Punishing the body to its limit, and at the end of the suffering, standing on the summit.
 
Sometimes, in a hazy state, the view from the top did not even remain in memory.
 
What awaited at the summit?
 
There was nothing there. He expected nothing.
 
There was only the result of having climbed.
 
And still, *I climb*, he thought.
 
Higher, higher.
 
To a place no one had ever reached, to engrave his name as the one who arrived.
 
To let that name ring out, to be heard.
 
A little more. A little more.
 
His mind was hazy.
 
His thoughts would not coalesce.
 
And yet the vast experience carved into his body guided Borok ever higher.
 
He drove the axe in, dug in his crampons, and crawled upward.
 
In the instant he swung the final blow, a tingling sensation ran through his body.
 
――The sensation of the summit.
 
The certainty that he had conquered this Greybyalbe.
 
He channeled the last of his strength into a surge of mana, hauling his body up――
 
"Um……what are you doing……?"
 
What met his eyes was a silver-haired girl, nibbling on a skewered piece of meat.
 
Her out-of-place beauty wore a look of puzzlement, and the goddess tilted her head.
 
Borok's feet slipped out from under him.
 
He cried out involuntarily, sliding down the steep slope, and then his body was enveloped in a floating sensation.
 
He was lifted into the air, encased in a sphere of mana.
 
Weightlessly, as though gravity were irrelevant, his body was carried back to the summit.
 
The violet eyes, skewer still in hand, met his again.
 
In all his years of arriving at summits, he had never been so astonished.
 
The temperature had risen sharply; the air that should have been bitterly cold now carried warmth, and there was no wind at all.
 
And what occupied the summit were beautiful young women.
 
Some kind of feast, it seemed.
 
They held skewered meat dripping with oil, and not one among them wore anything resembling Borok's cold-weather gear.
 
Most were in apron dresses, and only one girl with golden hair even wore trousers.
 
The girl in furs wore nothing besides the fur except a wrap across her chest and hip, her navel fully exposed.
 
A grill was set over a bonfire, sizzling away, and everyone was silent, looking at Borok with troubled expressions. The silver-haired goddess who had spoken (she, too, was in an apron dress) studied him with curiosity.
 
Between the cold and the shock, Borok stared at them in speechless amazement.
 
Beside them stood a large tent, flimsy-looking enough that any gust would carry it off.
 
For some reason, countless tiny snowmen lined the area.
 
At the highest point in the world, it looked for all the world like a winter roadside.
 
The place where storms should have been constant was, here alone, distortedly calm, so much so that he wondered if he had died.
 
Yet the sphere enclosing him was surely magic.
 
He knew that a great sorcerer could levitate.
 
He had connections to such great mages, but what stood before him was something far beyond a great sorcerer.
 
With one hand nibbling a skewer, casually.
 
The silver-haired girl had merely pointed a fingertip toward Borok as he slid away――it had taken but an instant.
 
The precision and transcendent speed of the formulae inscribed and drawn around Borok in that moment were nothing short of divine.
 
The silver-haired girl clapped her hands (skewer still in hand), then tottered over to the grill, picked up a skewer, and offered it to Borok.
 
At the same time, his feet touched snow.
 
"Um, would you like some?"
 
"N-no……"
 
The confusion was absolute, but he managed to drop to one knee.
 
In his muddled state, he understood at least this much about what was happening.
 
A feast of the gods.
 
He had, it seemed, stumbled upon it here at the summit.
 
"……M-my sincerest apologies. To defile……such a sacred feast."
 
"Sacred……?"
 
The beautiful girl cocked her head and thought about it.
 
The more he looked, the more her beauty seemed divine.
 
A girl made as though beauty itself had been sculpted. As though beauty were her entire purpose.
 
Silver hair gleaming in the moonlight and the firelight.
 
Large eyes framed by long lashes, like violet gems, and she was unmistakably a being made to be beauty itself.
 
*Right now, I stand before the gods.*
 
At that thought, his consciousness cut away――
 
"……?"
 
――When he woke, he was inside a tent.
 
He looked at his hands, still in their gloves, confirmed them, confirmed that he was alive.
 
He sat up. His body felt strangely well.
 
"Why am I inside a tent……?"
 
Had he pitched it unconsciously?
 
In that daze, after such a dream.
 
He furrowed his brow, then threw open the tent and stepped outside, and froze.
 
This was the summit.
 
And yet there was no wind, and the air was dense.
 
He gazed down at the clouds and the white mountain ranges below, and marveled at the stillness.
 
The small tent, viewed from outside, had not even been banked with snow for windproofing, yet it had survived the night intact.
 
From the east, the sun shone and illuminated the summit where Borok stood.
 
He simply gazed at it, and then, suddenly, tears fell.
 
An emotion beyond words swelled within his chest as though it would burst.
 
"This is……the goddesses'……"
 
He tried to recall the figures of the beautiful goddesses from his hazy memory, then shook his head.
 
Only that they had been beautiful.
 
That was all he could remember, and when he looked around, countless small snowmen remained.
 
No work of nature.
 
It had not been a dream. The goddesses truly had been here.
 
And they had saved Borok.
 
He inhaled deeply, closed his eyes.
 
Opened them again, and for a long while, burned the scene into his memory.
 
The world seemed upside down.
 
The clouds lay far below. The sky was filled with a blue so deep it could swallow him whole.
 
The white peaks were visible, and faintly in the distance, the world where humans lived.
 
On a clear day, the summit of Greybyalbe could be seen even from a nearby village.
 
And from Greybyalbe, that village could be seen as well.
 
――*Time to go home*, he thought vaguely.
 
He took two mana crystals from his rucksack.
 
One was for surveying. A commission from this expedition's sponsors, and also proof that Greybyalbe had been conquered.
 
At the highest point, he channeled mana into it, and the crystal responded and self-destructed.
 
A wave of shining light propagated through the atmosphere.
 
"……This is the land of the gods."
 
He looked at the second one, then put it back in the rucksack.
 
The first had been the transmitter.
 
The second was the receiver.
 
If he mounted it on a pole and planted it at the summit, the crystal would serve as a permanent receiver.
 
But he did not do that.

She leaned in and embraced her.
 
"……You're right — perhaps I do want to be comforted."
 
Bery showed a faint flicker of surprise, then slowly began to stroke her head.
 
It was soothing. There was relief.
 
She did not even know what she was relieved about.
 
"……I think I love Nee-san. Whether it's as a sister, or something more. ……I don't really know, even myself."
 
Bery stroked her head in silence for a while, as if turning something over in her mind.
 
Then, after a moment, with what sounded like a wry smile:
 
"……I still find it hard to say whether my love for Nee-sama was purely sisterly. ……If you ask me where the boundary lies, it's very difficult."
 
She spoke, and though Elvena could not see her face, she could picture the troubled, gentle smile.
 
"For instance……fufu, if Nee-sama had whispered of love, I would have nodded. I loved her that much."
 
As though confiding a secret, as though revealing something precious.
 
Elvena narrowed her eyes at the sound of her voice.
 
"With Krische-sama too — is it a desire to protect, or possessiveness, or a desire to control……or perhaps sexual love, or affection, or simple dependency? ……Surely each answer is correct, and each is also wrong."
 
"……Wrong?"
 
"Yes — if one were to choose only one."
 
She gave a small, rueful laugh.
 
"I simply give the name 'love' to that jumbled feeling. ……Sorting it into categories won't make any part of it disappear, so I see no point in defining it precisely."
 
"Is that how it is?" Elvena asked, and "I believe so," Bery answered.
 
"Ojou-sama once lectured me about the very same thing," she added.
 
"What's needed is surely only one's own acceptance. ……Love is probably not as complicated or difficult as one makes it by overthinking — it's something very simple."
 
Eyes closed under the soothing touch on her head, she listened to the comforting words.
 
There was no greater happiness than being affirmed by someone.
 
"……That may well be true."
 
Elvena, who had been welcomed into the manor as a stranger, had somehow been accepted as family, trusted, worried over, and comforted.
 
What she had been anxious about.
 
She began, dimly, to understand — and opened her eyes halfway.
 
Three lights in the deep darkness of night — a dim corridor stretched before her.
 
Warmth in the arms that held her, gentle fingertips tickling her hair.
 
She could see herself crawling into her sister's bed and gazing at the eternal magic lamp.
 
She had been afraid, all this time, that it would be taken away.
 
Experience had piled up, and all sorts of feelings had grown distant.
 
But even now, the child that Elvena had been was still here.
 
"I love Nee-san."
 
"Yes," Bery murmured, and held her just a little tighter.
 
Elvena let the tension leave her body, surrendering to the embrace, and closed her eyes.
 
"And so……I feel jealous, and lonely, that Mia-san took her."
 
Once she put it into words, it was easy — of course, that was simply how it was.
 
She wanted them to be happy, she wanted to celebrate, there was genuine joy — and yet such feelings crept in too.
 
No matter how many times she traced the outline, no matter how she sorted it — none of it would vanish.
 
"……May I stay like this, just a little longer?"
 
She scooped up the disordered tangle, with no boundary, no system — and gave the name 'love' to the vague, uncertain doze.
 
Not 'probably.' Not 'perhaps.'
 
She decided that was what it was, drew no distinctions, and simply, broadly, put it all into the box.
 
It did not feel like a perfect answer — and yet, strangely, it fit.
 
"……Yes, of course."
 
At the very least, it fit inside the box.
