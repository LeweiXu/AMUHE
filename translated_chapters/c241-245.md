# Chapter 241: A Maiden's Unwanted Heroic Epic

Red had brought fifty thousand soldiers.

Ten thousand on the left flank, from the north, in a flanking attack aimed at the main camp.

Fifteen thousand on the right flank, sweeping wide from south of the river to strike deep behind enemy lines.

If it was Alberinea, she would undoubtedly advance into the forest.

His movements were predicated on that assumption.

Alberan or not, there were limits to the speed of information relay.

With an offensive that prioritized speed above all, the enemy could not possibly be fully prepared.

At best, they could muster slightly more than a single corps.

He did not believe that a numerically inferior force would engage his in a straight fight within the forest, and by extension, concluded it would inevitably be a hit-and-run strike aimed at stalling their initial advance.

There was also the possibility that the hit-and-run might be aimed at Red's head — but if so, that aligned perfectly with Red's objectives.

What Red wanted was to create a situation where he and Alberinea faced each other directly.

Strike the main camp from the side, land a blow on the rear to disrupt their footing, and drag the enemy headquarters out in front of Red.

From everything he had gathered, Alberinea was an aggressively offensive commander who favored decapitation strikes.

Hemmed in on both flanks and with Red himself appearing in front, there was a high probability she would launch a counterattack.

The enemy's main thrust — the judgment of Kokys Agrand.

The high level of control and command exhibited by the enemy corps on the left.

There had been slight deviations from his predictions, but if Alberinea was here, things would go according to plan.

And so Red did not consider the situation particularly bad.

The miscalculation was that the force commanding Alberinea's army was not Alberinea, but the Kingdom Marshal, Selene Christand — but he would never realize that error.

"……What is she thinking?"

The place where Alberinea had once fought Aulgorn Hilkintos.

Flanked by forest to the north and river to the south, yet a wide meadow spread within the tree-sea.

There, arrayed in bold formation, stood roughly ten thousand troops in two corps — Alberinea's army.

And at their head, astride a jade tiger, cascading silver hair.

Red laid eyes on the figure for the first time — and yet he did not doubt for a moment that it was she.

Even from a distance, she was visibly small — wearing a billowing cloak that would be grabbed in a melee.

A presence utterly out of place on a battlefield.

No — the reason she looked so out of place went beyond that alone.

The moment he emerged from the forest path and stood here, an incomprehensible weight pressed down upon his entire body.

His trembling was not directed at those ten thousand — not at the battle line where warriors stood alongside Kokys Agrand — but at the lone girl who sat naturally at the very front.

"Form up!"

"Sir!"

It was not the trembling of eagerness for battle.

This was different from facing Felwors Keesliton, or standing opposite Kokys Agrand.

There was no boiling-blood fury at confronting his enemy.

Instead, it was as though ice water had been poured into blood that should have been seething.

Unwittingly, he found himself staring at his own trembling palms.

——*Am I… afraid?*

He asked himself, and looked around.

The soldiers around him were raising their voices, beginning to form the battle line.

But something was off. There was a sense of gears slipping.

Their voices were pitched slightly too high.

Or perhaps — wound too tight.

As though they were using sheer will to smother their fear.

Red was not the only one trembling.

Alberinea's behavior defied comprehension.

The northern corps was holding the enemy offensive well.

It would be some time yet before the flanking force from the south made contact with the enemy rear.

But even so, that was only a matter of a short while.

The proper action for Alberinea's main force should have been to withdraw during this window.

Even if she intended a counterattack or reversal, it needed to be disguised as retreat to be effective.

In a forest environment where numerical advantage counted for little, surprise was what gave such action meaning.

To engage in a bold head-on battle in open ground like this was absurd.

Had she held the numerical advantage, it might have made sense — but both locally and overall, the inferior force was decidedly hers.

Even if she intended to take this approach, she should have positioned herself at the exit to this clearing.

There was not a shred of tactical logic in forming such an absurd battle line facing them, with the bridge at her back.

By doing this, complete withdrawal of Alberinea's main force had become impossible.

It defied tactical and strategic understanding.

No — that was precisely why it was unnerving.

For Alberinea's main force to exit this forest safely now required the destruction of the twenty-five thousand under Red's command.

In other words, Alberinea intended to crush Red right here, with less than half the force.

It was insane. It was reckless.

He had flanking forces on both wings. There were no reports of ambushes.

None of the iron golems used in the wars against Galshan and Elsren were visible.

No sane mind could account for it.

And yet, what he felt when he looked at Alberinea standing at the front — simply watching as they formed their lines — was that same unidentifiable dread.

——*As though this entire space was the stomach of some vast, invisible creature.*

The advantage was his. The upper hand was his.

And yet, reason be damned, a leaden atmosphere clung to his body.

"What is Alberinea……?"

"I don't know. But stay on full alert. At the very least, she believes she has more than enough to win."

He had no other answer to give.

Had Red been in her position, he could think of no means to turn the tide.

Setting aside the outcome entirely, the fact was that her stance only further strengthened Red's already superior position — there were no advantages to it, anywhere.

And his soldiers felt the same.

The confusion was not limited to Red's side; it seemed to exist among Alberan's soldiers too.

In this situation, simply watching in silence as a superior enemy force formed its battle line.

This was a clear deviation from the logic of warfare.

Nothing in the original plan accounted for it.

The only information that could be conveyed to them in this short time was that Alberinea had a hidden strategy.

The soldiers of Alberan exchanged uneasy glances under the heavy air — and yet they obeyed without question, forming their battle line. This was the product of training and experience.

They knew full well what manner of existence Alberinea was.

This was not the first time they had experienced what appeared to be suicidal folly.

Opinions on her personal character might differ, but not a single person in this army doubted her overwhelming strength and ability.

Galshan, Elderant, Elsren — she had effortlessly shattered invasions that had filled every one of them with despair.

Her power was beyond question, and having challenged an ancient dragon of godlike might, exchanging true names and winning its friendship, she was nothing less than a living myth.

And so, with a faint tremor and eyes brimming with fierce expectation, they watched Alberinea.

Paying no mind to any of those gazes, Alberinea moved.

Having watched the Vezrea army's battle line take rough shape, she set the jade tiger walking.

A leisurely pace — both armies' soldiers exchanged glances, wondering if she intended some kind of opening salute — but after a short distance, the jade tiger stopped in the center, between the two forces.

There, Alberinea used her curved sword to cut loose two large bags that hung from the tiger's flank, opened them, and scattered their contents roughly across the ground.

What littered the meadow was a vast number of mana crystals.

The jade tiger turned its back on the scene as if its work were done, yawning as it headed toward the Alberan main camp.

The moment the tiger leaped over the battle line, the girl picked up one of the crystals — and crushed it.

The hard-edged mana crystal shattered with a high-pitched sound, transforming into a dense blue magical power visible even to those without mana sensitivity, and the energy from that first crystal flowed entirely into the girl's small hand.

In compressed time, simple curves of light intersected, diverged.

Three-dimensional blue geometric patterns danced upon her palm like flames.

It was, in a sense, a computing circuit.

A virtual mind to elevate the girl's thought — already transcending time itself — even higher.

What it signified was one singular law and logic.

To most who watched, it must have looked like a kind of dance.

As though a priestess were calling a god down into herself.

Or as though she were stepping, of her own will, into that realm.

She spun, dancer-like, and her cloak and long hair fluttered — and every mana crystal scattered across the meadow shattered.

——With a sharp, crystalline chain of sounds that shook the heavens, the girl was enveloped in blue flames and blue arcs.

"That's……"

Murmurs rose from both armies.

No one present could name what it was, or what she was doing.

It was precise and beautiful as a work of art, and dreadfully profuse — a vast aggregate of magical circles.

Her long silver hair, split into two tail-like streams, swayed left and right without wind, her cloak billowing.

Blue light flitted around her, and within it, Alberinea raised a hand above her head.

The blue light gathered into her small palm, changing shape, radiating a blinding brilliance.

The light extended far beyond her own height, and the sound of the atmosphere crackling radiated in all directions.

If one had to describe it, it was something like a spear.

——Or perhaps it was lightning itself.

It was the lance with which a god of heaven displayed wrath — the staff of storms that burned away all upon the earth below.

That which cleaved, absolutely, between divine and mortal, between heaven and earth.

The girl, having seized that tremendous power, ran lightly and simply threw the violence in her hand forward.

Not a single person could react to the oncoming girl and her spear.

Not the half-grown who had never killed.

Not the master who had devoted his life to the martial path.

Not even the hero whose name was known to all in that society.

Indiscriminate, uninvited, unreasonable.

"——————"

The flash that arrived before the thunder, that filled heaven and earth, reduced all of them to nothing in an instant.

The flash that pierced the left wing of the Vezrea battle line raised a pillar of fire that seemed to scorch the sky, and a beat later, the blast shook air and earth alike.

Everyone present stood dumbfounded, motionless.

When the flash faded and the smoke cleared, what remained was a gouged, molten stretch of earth.

Naturally, the several thousand soldiers who had formed the left wing of the line — their very existence had been erased.

No — in the rain of something falling from above, some residue of them might have remained.

With luck, fragments of bodies might have been permitted to keep their shape as they tumbled to the ground.

What was certain was that everyone at the blast's epicenter — from common soldiers to renowned heroes — had, in that single instant, reached the end of every life they had built.

The girl crafted the violence in her hands with effortless, flowing motion.

A second lightning spear struck the right wing of the line.

The same scene replayed itself — vaporizing bodily fluids formed a mist, and charred, liquefied flesh rained down upon the ground a moment later.

Alberinea's warriors remained frozen, staring at the spectacle in blank shock.

"Hi——Fall back! Retreat!!"

In that world where time had stopped, the first to move were the warriors of Vezrea.

That difference in reaction was only natural.

Clear death and violence were being directed at one side alone.

This place was ruled by one singular law and logic.

Everyone understood that to stand against it was futile.

Just as no one raises a sword against a storm, just as no one lifts a shield, it was simple natural law.

No matter what army one brought, no matter what formation one assembled, no matter how brutal the training, how desperately one vomited blood in the effort——

In the end, they were pitifully fragile creatures, powerless against the will of heaven.

Some stood stupefied, unable to process what had just happened.

Some had been hurled aside and barely clung to life, crouching and curling into themselves.

And most of the survivors, naturally, began to retreat — even brave commanders ordering withdrawal on their own authority, without waiting for the general's command.

——In this place, Alberinea was the one and only god.

No one present doubted it. Two spears had decided the outcome — and yet, unconcerned, Alberinea fashioned a third great spear of light and hurled it straight ahead.

It was the sort of thing one might do to a pebble in one's path — not particularly meaningful.

Had she merely walked forward, they would have cleared the way and fled.

She knew that, and it didn't matter.

She threw. A pillar of fire erupted. Another two or three thousand lives vanished.

It had struck her as 'somehow' being in the way, and that 'somehow' cost several thousand lives, taken without thought.

The pinnacle of logic that a genius, working for a hundred years, might barely manage to construct.

The spear, computed and woven in a single breath, was an art of theoretical perfection far beyond any dragon's breath — death, refined to its most efficient form.

The lightning spears that tore through battle lines were an act of violence far too great for any individual to wield — and yet the girl wielded it without a sliver of hesitation or fear.

Everything in her sight struck her as unpleasant, and they had, in fact, been in her way.

That alone made them worthy of killing in her eyes.

To her, this world was filled with things that did not matter.

She had no interest in their lives — no interest in what efforts had brought them here, what good they had done, what evil they had done.

Everything they had built was, to her, equally worthless.

Her scales would not stir, not if their lives were stacked by the thousand, the ten-thousand — even if, hypothetically, hundreds of millions were piled upon them.

She had been such a being from the moment of her birth, and that had not changed.

——The girl, born a princess of the kingdom, was an aberration from the very start.

The girl walked forward, paying no mind to anything she had killed.

Surrounded by countless blue magical circles, her cloak trailing — her long silver hair, tied with pale pink flower ornaments, swaying left and right.

She did not even run.

Blue arcs appeared at the girl's back.

Magical power formed wings and lifted her body into the air.

She drifted over the gap she herself had pointlessly torn in the line, and landed.

Before her was the main camp — and she noted that the supreme commander had not fled. She walked forward.

"……Are you the commanding officer?"

She addressed the young man standing dazed, gripping a greatsword, and continued walking without stopping.

She had not particularly expected an answer. She could simply kill everyone until there was no one left.

An answer would save some effort — that was the extent of the question.

"……Yes."

The young man — Red Lani — dragged his greatsword into a fighting stance and looked at the soldiers flanking him.

Even in this situation, the soldiers killed their fear and nodded.

Every one of them understood, without any room for doubt, that they would die alongside their commander.

They spread out and took positions to the left and right of the approaching girl.

Every one of them was moving now only to give meaning to their deaths.

"This is for Shelna. ……I'll take your life, Alberinea!!"

Red roared and lunged — and Krische stopped.

She glanced at the soldiers flanking her — and that was all.

The blue arcs that had been floating around her streaked like bolts of lightning, impossibly fast, toward them — into their chests, or into the mana crystals they clutched.

The last thing they saw was a pair of utterly expressionless violet eyes.

——In an instant, blue light engulfed them, and with the sound of detonation, their flesh burst apart and scattered.

Without accomplishing anything at all, their existence simply ceased.

If a reason must be given, it was this: they had defied the one singular law and logic.

There was nothing more to say about their deaths.

Red did not so much as flinch — not at the effortless deaths of the comrades who had fought beside him for years, not at the loss of every means to victory.

Pouring out everything he had built into this moment, he unleashed a rising diagonal slash.

The girl leaned her torso back, just slightly, and it missed.

The next strike missed too, and the one after that — the girl read each blow at a distance of a single sheet of paper.

Four, five, six, seven, eight — every last ounce of martial skill Red had accumulated in his life, and the girl simply kept dodging.

Everyone watching that spectacle — that thing which could not even be called a fight — saw it plainly.

The gulf between them was not that of child and adult, nor any such measure — it was a simple truth.

A blade that could never reach.

A gap between heaven and earth that no warrior, no matter how supreme, could ever cross.

It was not a matter of being able to match or unable to match — it was presumptuous even to compare. An absolute rift of power.

It was, after all, nothing more than natural law.

Frozen violet eyes watched the young man swinging his sword at her.

To Red, it felt like fighting a shadow that could never be cut.

He had survived hellish battlefields, honed his body — and he understood now that before this girl, it was all worthless.

"Excuse me."

The girl dodged his sword with perfect ease, and tilted her head, expressionless.

An eerie gesture, like that of a doll.

"Is that all, perhaps?"

He poured every drop of magical power in his body into the greatsword, harnessing every ounce of force it could generate.

He swung with violence enough to tear his own body apart.

His blade now moved at speeds that exceeded even his own perception.

And still the girl dodged by the thinnest margin, watched him — and drew her curved sword.

"——!?"

She slid its edge along his greatsword.

A diagonal strike against his blade, creating a tiny notch — and she followed through.

It was an eerily graceful, devastatingly precise sword-stroke.

The greatsword inherited from his father was bisected by the girl's small curved sword, and Red, his momentum suddenly gone, stumbled and fell to his knees.

*'——I commend you as a warrior. And for that very reason, I pity you. ……You will gain nothing.'*

Those words surfaced in his mind.

Before him stood an opponent that no amount of human effort could ever reach.

"Since you mentioned revenge, I assume this is retaliation for the previous battle?"

The girl's ironclad boot kicked the fallen Red in the stomach.

The force, impossible to imagine from her slender frame — a blow that broke through his abdominal muscles, shattered organs — and his muscular body gave a dull sound as it was lifted off the ground.

"……But you're so weak — why did you come to attack?"

Red coughed, spat blood, and clutched his left side as he staggered back to his feet.

And still tried to throw a bare fist at the girl.

"Gah——"

"Krische did this so this wouldn't happen……"

Her curved sword severed his arm at the root with effortless ease.

And in the same motion, she kicked his body and sent him tumbling.

"……You're so weak, and Bery is having such a hard time, and you're getting in Krische's way."

Not a single drop of blood touched her.

"And besides, you're the ones who attacked in the first place……"

The girl twisted her beautiful face, pressing her fingers to her forehead.

Then she stepped on Red's remaining left arm, pinning it down.

"……Aah, Krische is really, really irritated."

Red looked up at the girl who was about to kill him.

Shelna's body had been clean — not a single wound from a crossed blade.

Her fight must have been exactly like this. Completely one-sided.

——*This was the view she had seen, alone.*

"——She…… ru……"

"……That's enough."

Before he could finish, Krische severed his neck with casual ease.

She seized his head, impaled it on the tip of a nearby fallen banner, and drove it into the ground. Then she turned to face the Alberan army.

Selene, Kokys, Begil——

Even the commanding officers could not move at the sight, could not find their voices, and so neither could the soldiers.

Among them, the first to act was a single officer in black armor, who cried out the great Alberinea's victory.

He, too, had been among the frozen — and his shout was born only of a desperate desire to end the killing, to declare the victory final before his beloved Alberinea could slaughter further.

The warriors in black-painted armor answered, and the soldiers joined in.

What most of them felt toward Alberinea's power was something that should be called fear — awe and reverence born of terror.

Defeating an army single-handedly, in the most literal sense.

Many throughout history had been called war-gods and divine generals, but none before her had ever made those words real.

To speak of it was to speak of a kind of myth — a heroic epic.

The fairy tales one learned as a child to be mere fantasies dressed in embellishment — they were here, now, real and manifest before their eyes.

The child abandoned by the royal house was taken in by a hero, became a warrior, became a hero, and had reached even the realm of the gods — so the story went.

They needed a justification to accept the impossible reality unfolding before them.

And so, with trembling voices raised high, they praised the hero Alberinea to the heavens.

The divine child, guided by destiny.

The great hero, born as such.

To accept that overwhelming individual required faith — and so, as though crushing their fear of the aberration that she was, they raised their voices, on and on.

The girl watched the cheering with eyes that could not have been more dispassionate.

She had sought none of it — not the praise, not the glory, not the reverence.

She had been thinking only of a single woman, waiting for her return.

Needless to say, the battle concluded shortly after.

Leaving everything to her sister and her subordinates, the girl drove her jade tiger toward the capital.

From time to time, she clutched the small pouch hanging from her neck.

To her, it was a promise more precious than anything.

She had done everything she could, given her absolute best — and yet anxiety remained.

But once she returned this candy to her, as always, it would finally put her at ease.

*Just a little more, and my wish will come true.*

She cursed the interruption with a helpless bitterness, and did her best to arrange a smile so she could meet her with one. She tried not to think about it — pushed the battlefield from her mind and thought only of her.

A kiss, an apology.

Then reassurance, once more, that it was truly almost over.

Once everything was finished, things would go back to the way they'd always been.

They would stay that way, forever — the same as always, ordinary days.

That was all the girl wished for, and she wanted to do so many things to make her happy that all the suffering would be forgotten.

The girl believed that was what she existed for, and she did not doubt it.

Her place was not the battlefield — it was in the estate where she waited.

When the capital came into view, she pushed her mount harder, apologizing as she urged it on.

She tore through the capital like the wind, arriving before the familiar estate.

She took the pouch from around her neck — and without even waiting to enter through the front door, she leapt from the balcony into her own room.

——But no one lay on the bed. Only a single servant with black hair cut to her shoulders.

Seeing the girl appear, the servant stared blankly — then twisted her face, covered it with both hands, and sank to her knees.

"——————"

Trying not to think the thought that flickered at the edge of her mind, the girl asked, trembling.

Against the faintest thread of hope, the servant shook her head.

The small pouch slipped from her hand and struck the floor with a hard, crystalline sound.

She could not pick it up. She could only stand there, frozen.

As though time had stopped. As though all warmth had been drained away.

The world lost its color, and her mind refused to understand.

She swayed. Her eyes wandered.

She pressed her forehead and shook her head.

Unable to accept that the most precious, irreplaceable time had been stolen from her.

Inside her head, the high, ringing praise from the battlefield echoed like tinnitus.

As though afraid of the girl. As though shrinking from her.

——The voices that sang, loud and high, the heroic epic the girl had never wanted.

# Chapter 242: The Frog in the Great Sea

"Onee-sama, today Kreschenta made a pumpkin pie, your very favorite."

The girl who entered the room spoke in a bright voice.

Red-glinting golden hair — the beautiful girl in a white one-piece dress approached the bed, plate in hand.

Anne bowed to the entering Kreschenta and quietly stepped outside.

Kreschenta, well aware there would be no response, sat on the edge of the bed, set the plate of pumpkin pie on the bedside table, and stroked the cheek of her sister lying there.

She was not sleeping.

Yet it could not be said she was awake.

Her gaze was vacant, sparing Kreschenta only a glance.

Staring into some empty distance, Krische lay motionless, as though she had become nothing more than a doll.

When Kreschenta lifted her sister's body to a sitting position, it felt slightly thinner than before.

She deftly took up a fork with her right hand, broke off a bite of pumpkin pie, and brought it to those lips.

"Onee-sama, please eat. It's my pride and joy."

When pressed against her lips, Krische slowly opened her mouth, bit into the pumpkin pie, and swallowed it immediately.

For several days she had not so much as touched food or even water.

Having grown anxious as Krische's condition began to deteriorate, Kreschenta had pleaded desperately, and finally she had begun to eat again — but she never moved of her own accord.

She did not cry. She felt neither sorrow nor anger.

She had simply been like this ever since she came home.

"……Kreschenta, Krische isn't hungry."

"That simply cannot be true. Today as well, you will eat every last bite."

*You must get well soon, after all*, Kreschenta said, and Krische listened without a flicker of emotion.

Kreschenta pressed the next piece upon her regardless.

The meal was a mere task.

When she finished, as if fulfilling a duty, Krische lay back down.

Kreschenta started to say something, stopped, lowered her eyes.

Then, in a bright voice, she said she would go clean up, and left the room.

She leaned her back against the door and let out a quiet sigh.

She closed her eyes and asked.

"……What is it?"

Black hair swept and tied to the side.

Anne was watching Kreschenta with a worried expression, and lowered her eyes.

"……No, it's nothing."

Kreschenta glared at Anne with irritation, then looked away.

"Selene-sama should be coming home today. ……Perhaps that will change things for the better. While Kreschenta is away, please stay by Onee-sama's side and make sure she doesn't do anything rash."

Anne nodded — *yes* — and then moved her lips again, and stopped.

*What is it*, Kreschenta asked once more, and Anne bowed her head.

"……I am sorry. I have been harboring unworthy thoughts regarding Her Majesty the Queen."

She said it, and looked up.

Anne met Kreschenta's gaze squarely.

"But regardless of the truth of those thoughts, I revere Her Majesty the Queen as a lord worthy of my service and love. ……I simply wished to convey that."

Kreschenta looked back at Anne, and spoke as though exasperated.

"Unfortunately, Kreschenta does not place trust in words."

"Yet there are things that cannot be conveyed without words. That is why I wished to say it, just once. ……Please forgive my impertinence."

Kreschenta stared at the servant for a long moment, then looked away.

"……Nothing but incompetent servants. Do as you please."

Kreschenta said it and left.

Anne kept her head bowed until that figure disappeared around the corner of the hallway.

* * *

A bolt from the blue — that was the only way to describe it.

Krische had, quite literally, raced through the forest and hurled lightning spears.

To many soldiers, it must have seemed like a genuine natural disaster.

No — more than that. Krische's lightning spears effortlessly annihilated not dozens but over a thousand soldiers at a time.

Selene understood anew that even the Jaleia Gashea and the Baumje Ira had been considerably restrained by her standards.

Krische's absolute power, capable of bending the laws of the world itself if she wished — even stopping time.

It was easy to see why she had been able to fight that dragon.

*'——The principles of magic are actually quite simple once you get used to them, so I asked Krische-sama long ago to please keep it a secret and refrain as much as possible. It's a power that could become anything, after all.'*

When Selene had told her about Krische stopping time to save Bery, she had said it with a troubled look.

She always knew everything about Krische.

She understood the reasons and the intent behind what was hidden, and yet there was something, just a small something in her heart, and she swept it away with happiness.

It was always like that.

There were always two conflicting feelings inside Selene.

A clean emotion, and one that was somewhat dark, sticky.

Trivial. Small.

When it came to Bery, Krische was reckless, and for Bery she would do anything.

That was the Krische Selene knew, and having accepted that, Selene loved her — and loved Bery too.

So that was fine.

*'Selene-sama must be thinking it too, at least a little, no? That if Argan-sama weren't around——'*

Kreschenta's words, lashing out in frustration.

They could not have been heartfelt — and yet, the reason she had bristled for even a moment was that she could not say with certainty there was no sticky feeling stirred by those words.

*'I……I need a reason for being at her side. So at the very least, if I can't manage even the minimum of what I ought to do, I can't forgive myself.'*

She had wanted to prove it.

That she could literally stake her life for the two of them.

That she was prepared to do anything within her power.

No matter what feelings might be mixed into her emotions, she wanted to prove to herself that she loved them both with that much sincerity.

She could not do the things that Krische, Kreschenta, or Bery could — but to be of help to them.

Even Selene, hopelessly small and possessed of so little power compared to them — if she gave her absolute all, she could be just a little bit of help.

Selene had simply wanted them to believe that.

——And the result.

*'As Krische-sama said, it's already just a mopping-up operation. No trouble to speak of. ……Selene-sama, you may as well leave it to us and head back. General Remin should arrive before long.'*

*'I should——'*

*'That's a terrible face you're wearing. ……With all due disrespect as a soldier, Selene-sama, you're in a dangerous state right now. Don't push yourself.'*

Kokys said it as though looking at something painful, as though worried.

*'I do want to say this much — neither I nor anyone else saw any problem with Selene-sama's tactics. I don't mean to suggest any lack of ability. Even without Krische-sama, we would have gotten through this just fine. Please, don't blame yourself. You have done nothing wrong.'*

*'……Is that a kind consolation to a benefactor's daughter?'*

*'You're free to take it however you like. ……But I would like you to believe it's not merely lip service.'*

She had wanted to throw a tantrum like a child.

But Selene believed his words, at least somewhat, and understood the kindness behind them.

She did not want him to be disappointed, so she obeyed.

She had gone to the battlefield and accomplished nothing.

Without even managing the bare minimum of her duties, she had been sent back to the manor.

*'……Marshal? What about the battle——'*

*'Krische killed the enemy commander and ended it. The rest is a mopping-up operation; General Karnarius is in command. I'll brief you. General Remin, continue assisting as you are.'*

Passing Teknrea on the march, she delivered only the situation and brief orders to the puzzled woman, and rode on.

She was so pathetic, she wanted to die.

And when she returned to the manor——

* * *

——Whether she was awake or asleep, she could not say for certain herself.

# Chapter 244: True Name

Without the will to maintain herself, this body would readily transform into that sphere.

What had originally existed in that form had simply shaped itself to fit the vessel called a body.

It was natural law.

The soul was like a liquid — it had no inherent shape.

Poured into a bottle, it held that shape, but once the vessel was lost, it would quickly return to its original form.

The soul-substance had only a gentle cohesive property, and its shape was merely the container's shape; forming a sphere in weightlessness, as water would, was the natural state.

"I'm sorry, Alkirens-san. Krische was just so happy, Krische got a little carried away……that was thoughtless."

"If you understand, then that is enough. It is presumptuous of me before one such as Krische-sama, but consider it the advice of an old man, nothing more. ……Desire is inexhaustible. Even we sometimes find the thought crossing our minds — of devoting an eternity to our practice. The older we grow, the nearer death draws, the more so."

Vinsreel looked at Alkirens with faint surprise, but the chieftain merely smiled like an ordinary old man.

Then he indicated Vinsreel, and narrowed his eyes.

"Blessed with a fine successor, having passed on all that I have to pass on — and still. ……Yet humans are not individuals alone. We weave as a collective, build many connections, and live. I wish, as our ancestors did, to entrust myself to that flow. ……I find beauty in the common flower that withers and leaves its seed, rather than in the great blossom that blooms forever — and I wish to live out my life so."

His words were carefully chosen, almost supplicating.

Yet his voice was gentle — like an old man explaining things to a child.

"……Please, do not lead someone like me into temptation. I ask that you fully understand your own power, and from this point forward, give thought to every action, every word."

She understood she was being admonished, and Krische bowed her head sincerely.

Strangely, she felt that if Gallen or Elrouga had been here, they would have said much the same — and that thought only deepened her embarrassment at her own thoughtlessness.

"……I will be careful."

Alkirens nodded in satisfaction, and looked once more at the men.

The men nodded, excused themselves, and filed out.

"Lila, prepare a meal. And bring something easy to eat for Krische-sama."

"Yes."

Lila nodded and looked at Krische.

"……Whatever the case, I am glad you are safe. More than anything, that is what makes me happy."

Krische smiled in return, and Lila said:

"Personally, today's story was very……"

*——I wonder if Mother is also walking a new life by now.*

She gazed at the light within the crystal Krische held, murmured those words, and smiled.

"……Indeed. For her short span in this life, she will surely live long in the next."

Alkirens nodded at his daughter's words.

He was remembering his wife, who had died young.

* * *

Taking an extra night in the Kreisharana village as a precaution, Krische discussed the future with Kreschenta.

Kreschenta, who also seemed to have settled, offered lucid proposals to Krische's plans.

To release the vast mana filling the source, saturate heaven and earth, and create an environment where Krische and the others could manipulate the very world at will.

And then, using that mana, to bring forth a new dimension — one that weighted the soul more heavily than matter.

Creating a world they could freely control, where management and oversight would be simple, would make stabilizing the soul's vessel — the body — far easier.

They would simply need to assist Bery and the others until they adjusted to their new bodies and stabilized.

Their small garden — the eternal birdcage, where no enemy could exist.

Even having reached into the domain of the Creator God, what the girl sought was only safety and peace.

They refined plans spanning decades as casually as discussing tomorrow's schedule while lying down, and at sunrise they thanked Alkirens and the others, left the village, and returned to Rigaleiwe.

Krische expressed her thanks again, told the dragon she would likely be back soon, and performed the transference.

They chose sunrise to cause as little commotion as possible.

The two landed near the capital, slipped past the guards, and made their way to the royal estate.

Elvena, who had been feeding the jade tiger early that morning, noticed them and hurried to fetch Selene.

Before Krische could say a word, Selene wrapped her thin body in a tight embrace.

Krische apologized again and again to Selene, whose dark circles betrayed sleepless nights, and waited for her tears to subside.

Then Krische told her what had happened, little by little, and explained the plan for 'the eternal birdcage.'

The relief on Selene's face gradually stiffened, but Krische, still being held, did not notice.

"Ehehe, and there, everyone can be together foreve——"

"……Krische."

At Selene's voice, cutting in, Krische tilted her head.

"I'm glad you came back safely. And about Bery too. ……But that's a separate matter. I'll pass."

"Huh……?"

Selene pulled back, creating distance, wiped her tears, and looked at Krische.

The two servants, who had been listening to this sudden conversation, exchanged glances. Kreschenta narrowed her eyes.

"……Of course, I don't mean to interfere, and I'll support you. ……But, I don't want it."

"Why——"

"……I wonder if Krische can understand the reason."

Kreschenta said it as though exasperated.

"Are you sulking?"

"I'd prefer you not make light of it. ……It might be trivial to you, but I'm being serious."

Her voice did not rise.

Seeing this, Krische moved to close the distance.

But Selene gently held her back by the shoulder.

"Um, Selene, if you're angry——"

"I'm not angry. I love you the same as ever, and I understand how you feel. ……I believe I understand you."

Krische was wearing the mana crystal on a cord around her neck.

Selene watched her squeeze it tightly with both hands — and gazed at the light shining within.

It was a brilliance that was endlessly beautiful, endlessly radiant.

Bery's soul was hopelessly, achingly beautiful.

*What about me?* she wondered.

*How would I look to her?*

A heavy, muddy feeling pooling inside her.

If she had a soul, it must be something unbearably ugly.

"……It's not your fault. It's my problem. ……Please. Let me be alone today."

"Selene——"

"Please. Let me be alone. ……I'm tired."

If something so blindingly beautiful was worth staking a life for, she could understand why.

"……Tired of everything."

There was a story here, cruelly beautiful.

Beautiful characters, a tale of beauty worth dying for.

But Selene had neither the heart nor the right to keep watching.

"I'm……not like you."

——Unlike her, who had been born with everything, Selene was not a special being.
