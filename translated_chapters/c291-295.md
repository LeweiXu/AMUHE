# Chapter 291: The Priestess and the Cage of Desire I

In the cave at night, there was a single bonfire and a single tent.

A red-haired girl had draped a blanket over her apron dress and wrapped it further around two other girls.

Curled up inside the cloak were silver hair and gold hair glimmering with red.

"Onee-sama, I am finished……"

"You mustn't, Kreschenta. ……If you fall asleep in the snow mountains, you'll die."

"Uuu……"

The silver-haired girl pinched and kneaded the cheeks of her sister, who was trying to drift off to sleep, while clinging to the red-haired girl's arm.

She, too, narrowed her eyes sleepily, yet somehow relaxed her cheeks into a look of happiness.

The red-haired girl being clung to by both of them — the servant Bery — settled Kreschenta onto her lap, giggled softly, and nuzzled her cheek against her head.

Kreschenta, too, drowning in the bliss of warmth in the space between waking and dozing, fluttered her eyelids open and shut.

Across from them, the enormous jade tiger yawned, and the golden-haired girl leaning against it — Selene — let out a yawn of her own, saying, "You three never get tired of that."

It was, on the surface, a grueling trip: spending a full two weeks or so enjoying sledding and boarding down the snowy mountain using the tallest peak in the world, living in tents and caves the whole while.

That said, while they were playing, Krische kept the wind lifted off the mountainside, so although it looked like a blizzard, the conditions were mild.

The northern face of this mountain was prone to avalanches, but each one carved out a fresh course, so there was never any shortage of variety.

If she could have had her preference, Selene would rather have gone to the sea, but the snow mountains weren't bad in their own right.

The thrill of riding an avalanche down the mountain was something beyond words, and Selene had grown rather fond of these descents.

At night they would choose a suitable spot like this to eat and sleep.

Krische and Kreschenta apparently preferred this part even more, and were thoroughly enjoying their 'stranded game.'

The two of them hated the cold, yet they loved having the excuse of being cold to cling tightly and bask in warmth.

Once they latched on, neither would budge an inch — their capacity for spoiled dependence was truly limitless.

Selene usually shoved them off onto Bery by the third day, but Bery showed no sign of minding the two little cling-ons in the slightest — her head was, as ever, considerably out of order.

"……Lila, just watching you makes *me* cold. Are you really all right?"

"I-I am a bit cold, but……f-fufu, it really is nice to come to a place like this every now and then. Snow mountains are quite enjoyable……"

*It tightens my resolve*, she said, shivering as she answered.

She had at least put on boots, but otherwise she had simply wrapped some furs over her usual outfit.

Lila was diligently mass-producing snowmen, while Elvena watched with evident amusement and brought in more snow from outside.

Even accounting for some temperature adjustment, it was still rather cold, yet she was dressed in her usual midriff-baring look.

Selene knew their bodies could not fall ill (though a certain fool occasionally pretended to have a fever), but it was still cold just to look at.

Apparently Lila intended to build a thousand snowmen to discipline her wayward self — her thought process was sometimes quite baffling.

Anne, gazing with a serious expression at the scenery outside while jotting something into a stack of papers, at least *looked* more sensible by comparison.

What she was writing was some manner of unwholesome tale, but that was surely still more reasonable than mass-producing snowmen in the name of training.

She, too, was a touch odd in the head.

Nothing but strange people — surely, no matter how many years passed, she would never understand them.

Selene yawned again and gazed outside.

Beyond the cave, the roaring wind sent white snow dancing, filling the entire world.

*The 57th Christand Snow Mountain Survival Tour.*

* * *

At the fantastical paradise Krisneit — in the manor.

It was the meeting room that had been established in the manor since their arrival here.

Shelves lined the walls, displaying horse sculptures, vases, swords, and suits of armor — and in the center stood a large table.

A single-piece table, enormous, carved from a branch of the World Tree.

At the head of the rectangular table's short side sat one person.

Selene Christand, mistress of the manor.

The Christand princess who had left her mark on history — her golden hair shining, her arms folded — cut a figure of commanding dignity.

Though she appeared as a girl, she possessed an aura no ordinary person could project.

To her right sat the very same continental unification queen, Kreschenta Alberan, arms likewise folded, and beside her the hero without equal in all of history, Alberinea — Krische Christand — who sat with her hands resting on her belly, watching the proceedings with an earnest expression.

Beside her sat the priestess of the divine being, Lila Sharana, with an indescribable expression, and across from them all, three servants including the head servant, Bery Argan, had taken their seats.

Before Selene Christand and Bery Argan lay ten face-down cards each. When Selene pushed one card forward, Bery responded in kind.

Without a word, both flipped their cards — and it was Selene whose brow furrowed.

The card Selene had played was cavalry.

And the card Bery had played was long spear.

Inevitably, Selene's cavalry was defeated.

Selene's card went straight to the graveyard, while Bery pulled her own card back, face-up.

On Bery Argan's youthful, beautiful face, a thin smile had formed at her lips.

"We've only just started, Bery."

"My apologies. ……I was thinking about what to bring on the mountain trip, and it slipped out."

"……What are you talking about? This time, we're going to the sea."

What was being played was a game called Kessen-fuda.

It was essentially an advanced form of rock-paper-scissors using picture cards composed of three types of infantry, cavalry, archers, siege weapons, and generals — with victory determined purely by card matchups.

Drawn or lost cards went to the graveyard, while winning cards remained in hand.

The more one lost, the more disadvantageous it became — so this single loss had significant impact. However, it was still only the first bout.

This situation could easily be turned around.

No need to panic yet, Selene told herself, sipping her tea and glaring at Bery.

"Hurry up and play."

"Very well, then — two cards."

"—!?"

Bery pushed forward two face-down cards.

Supremely aggressive. Bery intended to decide the match.

A two-card play — this was a kind of gamble.

It could only be done by combining cards of the same type, or by pairing a card with the general. If the opponent countered with a single card and won, those two cards went to the graveyard.

Even in the case of a draw, Bery would have to send both cards to the graveyard, giving Selene a one-card advantage.

However, if Selene lost, she would not only lose the card she played, but would also have to send two additional cards of Bery's choosing to the graveyard.

Losing two of eight remaining cards would be painful enough, but the greater problem was that if the general happened to end up in the graveyard, it meant an instant loss — meaning there was a one-in-four chance of Selene losing outright.

Responding with a two-card play of her own would avoid that risk — but even so, what should she do?

Her opponent had opened with spear.

It was hard to imagine anyone would include three spears in their deck, but this was Bery.

There was every possibility she was anticipating that Selene would aggressively play cavalry again.

A two-card play on only the second bout was a reckless gamble — but given Bery's character, it was better viewed as a carefully laid trap.

The old Selene would have judged that three long spears were unlikely and played cavalry.

She glanced at the ornaments along the wall.

For some reason, the horse sculpture Selene had made was oddly eye-catching — it was subliminal conditioning.

Bery Argan was a woman who would stop at nothing to achieve her ends. Using furnishings to manipulate Selene's thinking was merely her opening move. She was a wretched woman, overflowing with malice and cunning.

Selene's first choice of cavalry had been intuitive — but in all likelihood, even that had been guided.

Caught in Bery's manipulation, Selene had unconsciously included four cavalry cards in her deck.

Without a doubt, the horse sculptures lining the wall were there to make Selene think of horses.

No — come to think of it, the very fact that Selene had suggested playing Kessen-fuda after their argument was because she had seen the swords and armor placed in this room.

The unusual furnishings — from the very beginning, Selene had been playing into this servant's hands.

Disguising it as coincidence, Bery had engineered the battlefield to her advantage, leading Selene to choose Kessen-fuda and then to play cavalry first.

And then, capitalizing on the panic from that opening defeat, a two-card play to press the attack.

Selene had nearly been swept away by Bery's deception.

Countless defeats at the hands of Bery Argan.

The cause could be attributed to a pure difference in thinking ability, but it was certainly not just that.

In sheer hunger for victory, Selene had been outmatched.

Despite possessing rare talent, Bery never neglected effort, and on top of that, she possessed the brazen audacity to calmly justify even what might be called underhanded means in pursuit of victory.

Bery was strong. The number of times Selene had been brought to her knees before her was beyond counting.

But the day she would surpass her — that day was today.

"You meticulously prepare the battlefield and even guide my subconscious. I truly admire your hunger for victory. ……Playing cavalry here is what the old me would have done."

Cavalry was a strong card on offense, with advantage against heavy infantry, siege weapons, and archers — but on defense, it lost that advantage and became merely ordinary, with only a disadvantage against long spear.

That said, forcing a draw would mean trading one card for two — the old Selene would have judged the opponent unlikely to triple-stack long spears and played the practical choice, using the cavalry that had just lost to bring the stolen advantage back to even.

A one-for-two trade isn't bad, she would have thought — biting down on Bery's cunning bait.

"But today is different."

She pushed a single card forward, flipped it, and declared:

"What you played is long spear, isn't it?"

The red-haired servant stared at that card for a long moment, then sighed.

"Haah……as expected, Ojou-sama."

The card she had played was archers. Strong against long spear and light infantry, weak against cavalry.

It lost to light infantry on offense, but on defense it was an excellent card with two favorable matchups and only one unfavorable.

"……You've grown compared to before."

However, upon seeing it, Bery smiled.

"I'm glad you've grown."

"……What?"

Selene's eyes widened at the revealed cavalry and general.

The general was a card that could not fight alone, but could be combined with any other card.

It turned draws into victories, and when winning with an advantaged card, it allowed sending one additional card to the graveyard.

"Then please send that archer and……the third card from your right hand to the graveyard, if you would."

By deliberately giving Selene the information of the general's position, what Bery gained in return was an overwhelming advantage.

Whether Selene had chosen cavalry or the back-reading archers — no matter which, Bery had made a gamble designed to win either way.

Selene's general barely survived, but what remained was ten cards against five.

— In just two bouts, the outcome was already decided.

"……Wow."

Watching Selene freeze up as usual, Lila looked at Bery with an expression of exasperation.

Bery had told Lila things like *this time we'll hold the banquet in the snow mountains*, as though the destination had already been decided — and in fact, it had apparently only been decided in Bery's own mind.

Selene had apparently wanted to go to the sea, and this 'Christand council' had started, as usual, with an argument between Bery — who tried to proceed on the assumption of a mountain trip — and Selene.

Having been told they were going to the snow mountains, Lila had been left confused.

The outcome was all but decided.

In the end, things would proceed to the snow mountains just as Bery had declared — but Lila wondered where on earth her confidence came from.

Even in games that appeared to come down to luck in the end, she inexplicably won by a landslide more often than not.

Of course, it wasn't as though she won without fail every single time — but in these thousand-odd years, Lila had never once seen her lose a battle that seemed important (though personally, the stakes seemed inconsequential).

By the time she had declared they would go to the snow mountains, victory was already a foregone conclusion in her mind.

Selene, looking frustrated, and Bery, wearing a cheerful smile — in Bery's head, this entire situation had probably been calculated from the start.

A contest that appeared fair and equal, yet was engineered from the ground up with victory as a given.

A servant who calmly lured her own mistress into a trap.

The power dynamics in this manor were labyrinthine — a mystery even after a thousand years.

The day she understood them would surely never come.

*Perhaps divine beings are lonely creatures*, she thought, idly.

The divine being looked so happy whenever it spoke with Krische, who visited from time to time — the air would shift, just slightly.

Since Krische's arrival, the divine being seemed to have changed a little; it had also begun asking Lila things more often, rippling its mana in what resembled laughter.

It was surely because of what she had seen.

The Kreisharana people spoke of the divine being — silent, merely sleeping — as the ultimate existence.

Occasionally it would turn its red-purple gaze upon them, but very few had ever heard it speak.

Seeing the divine being exist as a solitary individual, wanting nothing from others, the Kreisharana had revered it as the 'strong way of being' that all should strive for.

Humans are born as incomplete, helpless infants.

Because of that, people are tossed about by countless emotions and desires.

Everyone was once an infant born from a mother into society, and no matter how harsh their life, no one grows to adulthood without any protection at all.

Seeking connection with others could be called a human instinct, and emotions and desires are nurtured within that bond.

No matter how much one trained, it was impossible to cast off all emotion and desire — no matter how loftily one postured as a solitary figure, one could never truly sever those bonds entirely.

What the Kreisharana — those who polished themselves as individuals, who forged themselves as solitary beings — had once felt toward the divine being was surely admiration born of knowing a realm they could never reach.

Discarding desire, enduring solitude, punishing body and mind — yet knowing there existed a place they could never attain — that was why they had gazed upon the divine being with longing.

And perhaps someday they might reach it, they had thought — taking the divine being as their model, generation after generation.

Lila, too, had learned these Kreisharana teachings as a matter of course and grown up without particularly questioning them.

The divine being was a complete existence from birth.

Unlike incomplete humans, it was free from all emotion and desire — without waste, and beautiful.

All humans should emulate that way of being, strive for it, and make the effort to draw nearer to it.

If that could be achieved, then all seeds of conflict would vanish from the world, they said.

Lila had indeed considered it a fine teaching — but when she heard the divine being's unexpectedly gentle words, when she saw it ripple its mana in what resembled laughter, a vague doubt had taken root.

Why had the divine being spent all that time sleeping?

Perhaps they had been fundamentally mistaken.

The claim that the divine being was complete — that, too, was merely human reasoning.

Looking at the prosperous nation of Alberan, seeing the 'friend of the divine being' laugh innocently and without guile among people, Lila had been further confused.

That figure had been far removed from a 'fine person' by Kreisharana standards — yet it had seemed, at the very least, a deeply happy sight, and the girl's effortless naturalness resembled the divine being more than anyone.

There was nothing wrong with holding up the divine being as a model to aspire to.

However, that was not the same as declaring the divine being to be a perfect and flawless existence.

Being spoiled by someone felt pleasant, and exchanging words was enjoyable.

These were simply obvious truths, and none of them were bad things in themselves.

It was hardly strange for the divine being to enjoy exchanging words, and the claim that it was a perfect existence had always been nothing more than a human assumption.

She had begun to wonder whether admiration had, at some point, pushed the truth further away.

What they had been seeing all along seemed to be an 'idol of what they wished the divine being to be,' fabricated inside human minds — and with each word exchanged, new aspects became visible.

*'Rigaleiwe-san just doesn't understand what loneliness is. Even talking with Krische like this — that's surely because Rigaleiwe-san is lonely.'*

Ah, she had thought.

If the divine being had truly been a complete existence from birth — if it had no need for others — then it would never have spoken at all.

*'……If I might be permitted……would you not take me along as well?'*

She had made that request of Krische hoping to ease even a little of the divine being's long solitude.

Her father and brother had opposed it, and she had agonized over it in her own way before reaching that decision — but since coming here, other divine beings had begun visiting frequently, and the surroundings had become quite lively.

For a decision wrung from such anguish — all that awaited was games and idle chat, the divine beings thoroughly enjoying themselves.

*— Had she been making a terribly foolish, terribly presumptuous worry?*

Every time she recalled her past agonies, she felt an indescribable sensation — but she tried not to think too deeply about it.

Remembering that solemn resolve of the past was embarrassing, to be sure, but considering that an infinite number of similarly embarrassing memories would continue to pile up from here on, willful ignorance was the correct approach for now.

Eternity appeared harsh but was, in truth, kind.

There was no need to rush to conclusions, and if one wished, one could postpone them forever.

And at the very least, the divine being did not rush to conclusions.

That was one of the things Lila had learned since coming here.

She had been somewhat braced for what eternity might entail — but there had been nothing dramatic.

Or rather, perhaps it was *because* there was nothing dramatic that it deserved to be called eternity.

Waking each morning, living out the day, sleeping at night — nothing in particular changed.

She had vaguely understood that an unfathomable number of such days would repeat, and that was, in a sense, a frightening thought — but that didn't mean anything came of it.

Whether eternal or not, what one lived through was, in the end, today — this single day, this present moment.

The past simply accumulated behind it, and whether one lived a hundred years or a thousand, one could not know even something as trivial as what tomorrow's meal might be.

No enlightenment descended upon her, and at the end of the day, Lila Sharana was still Lila Sharana.

One might imagine that a being who had lived a thousand years would possess sage-like insight rivaling divine revelation and a spirit unshakeable as a great tree — yet Lila had no real sense of having changed.

*Never again*, she would think, only to periodically lapse, and the weak-willed woman who writhed at her own past memories was still the same person Lila lived with every day.

That would likely remain unchanged a hundred years from now, a thousand years from now.

*Today's toil bears tomorrow's fruit* — her father and brother's favorite saying. But in Alberan, there was apparently a saying: *'The same root bears the same flower.'*

It meant that one's nature never truly changes, and between the two, this one felt more fitting to her.

With each passing day, her lack of progress — no, that very thought was what she must reject, and she shook her head and shaped the snow.

She stacked two more balls of snow and, shivering, added another snowman.

When she lifted her face, the view was nothing but magnificent blue and white.

It was the scenery of Greybyalbe, the tallest place in the world.

Tall, rugged mountain ranges stretched below as though encircling this peak, and in the far distance one could perhaps see the sea. The blue of the sky and the blue of the sea merged and dissolved into each other, their boundary shimmering faintly with light.

Looking straight ahead, it was a landscape of pure silence — but below, massive avalanches repeated one after another, filling the air with tremendous roaring and tremors.

A spectacularly bold form of mountain descent was taking place: standing on boards and riding the avalanches down the snow face.

Happy voices carried between the rumbles; Lila watched Anne get swallowed by an avalanche, then averted her eyes and focused on her snowman-building.

She had come this time to train her body and mind, to discipline a self that had repeatedly fallen into indulgence.

Even with the temperature somewhat regulated, building snowmen in this cold was quite an ordeal — but precisely because of that, it was good for re-forging herself.

For several days before the trip, she had stayed at the manor preparing — and had let herself be carried along, savoring exquisite food, getting scrubbed in the bath, then sleeping soundly, snug in a fluffy bed.

Just this morning, she had been unable to escape her blankets in the haze of drowsiness and had ended up being dressed by Elvena.

Her fall from grace had reached its zenith.

Her father and brother would surely lament greatly if they saw Lila's current state.

*'It was not fear of death that moved me, nor was I captured by the temptation of eternal life. ……I simply wish to serve at the side of the divine being, as the last priestess of Kreisharana.'*

*'But——'*

*'……From now on, I shall live alone in the forest, meeting no one, accepting no one's help. Until I reach the end of my natural life. Will you accept that as proof of my resolve?'*

Before coming here, she had spent decades alone in the forest to demonstrate her resolve to her opposing father and brother.

Looking at prosperous Alberan, it had seemed certain that Kreisharana would eventually change through its interactions — and that this was the natural course.

The Kreisharana way of rigorously forging body and mind had been a beautiful thing, but whether it constituted a happy life for everyone was another matter.

The physically frail were looked down upon, and those who didn't fit the 'beauty of Kreisharana' fled to the lowlands or lived quietly on the outskirts of the settlement.

The role of priestess of the divine being was called an honor, yet no woman who actually desired it existed — at least not in Lila's generation.

She remembered even her brother worrying repeatedly about whether she was truly all right with it, and though the previous priestess had been a fine woman, she too had not chosen the role from the heart, or so Lila had heard.

*Like the divine being, unadorned.*

Those born in Kreisharana were taught 'the proper way of being.'

Lila had thought such a way of being was beautiful — but she also understood that even within Kreisharana, she had probably been a peculiar person.

She had known they considered her a strange one for wanting to become the priestess of the divine being, and though everyone had spoken of the Kreisharana teachings as proper and fine things, what was proper and fine was not necessarily what made one happy.

Being proper and fine was a difficult thing, and suffering was the greater part.

Precisely because it was hard, precisely because humans were incomplete — that was the reason for the teaching.

The reason she had wanted to end the priestess role with herself was that she had felt it was no longer suited to the coming age.

There would not always be someone as peculiar as her.

As life grew more prosperous, there would surely be even fewer.

It would be a sad thing if someone were forced into the life of a priestess without desiring it, if they had no other choice — and no matter how proper and fine such a life might be, it would not be a happy one for that person. ……Such had been her noble ideals, imposing frugality and asceticism upon herself — but that was a story from long ago.

Looking at the state of herself, sliding ever further into indulgence, the past transformed swiftly into mortification.

Patting and shaping snow, she recalled the distant past——

"Hyah—"

Something warm was pressed against her cheek.

She toppled backward onto her behind, and there was Elvena, shoulders shaking with amusement.

Her hair, trimmed at the shoulders, swayed as she wore a mischievous smile, and in both hands she held two steaming cups.

"Fufu, it must be cold out here, so I brought tea."

"Th-thank you very much……"

Elvena handed her the tea, then wrapped Lila inside her own thick cloak.

Then she nestled close and sat down beside her. Very close.

Lila's body, which had been shivering in the name of training, was warm in no time.

"You've made quite a lot."

"……It's not nearly enough. Lately I've been on a one-way path to indulgence, so this much isn't……"

Lila said this while watching Elvena begin to comb through her hair.

Fundamentally close in proximity, with what might be called excessive skinship — that was the manor style, and Lila had grown accustomed enough that it no longer felt strange. But in Elvena's case, her manner was less innocent and more somehow calculated.

The atmosphere was subtly different from the others — there was something hard to put into words about it.

Elvena, seeming to notice the gaze, tilted her beautiful face in puzzlement and smiled.

This woman, too, was a clever person, perceptive of others' feelings — but precisely because of that, these gestures of hers sometimes felt deliberate, making Lila wary.

*She's going to interfere with my training again* — no, that was an unkind thought.

It was reprehensible to harbor suspicion toward someone who had been so kind.

Elvena certainly had a touch of playfulness — a mischievous streak, yes — but it was wrong to interpret everything in that light.

Rather, Lila should be grateful that despite having witnessed an embarrassingly countless number of compromising moments, Elvena still treated her the same as before.

Just this morning — who exactly was it that had been dragged out of bed while displaying a witless expression, then dressed like a child?

"Um, this morning, I……"

"Please don't worry about it. I did push quite a bit of wine on you last night, after all……I knew you weren't very strong with it, yet I couldn't resist."

"No, no……I'm just weak, but the wine itself is……"

The wine Elvena had brought to her room the previous night had been truly delicious.

Honestly, back in Kreisharana, Lila had never even found alcohol pleasant — but the tastes of Krische and Kreschenta played a large role, no doubt.

Setting aside the proper vintage wines made for sale, the wines made for the manor's own use were light and fruity — even Lila, with her childish palate, could enjoy them.

At some point she had been offered a cocktail mixed with fruit, and while happily drinking it down, her memory had grown hazy — she couldn't clearly remember what they had talked about.

"But, um, l-last night too, I have a feeling I said something rude while drunk……"

"My, rude? ……But you did seem quite tired. All that asceticism and self-denial seems to have built up quite a bit of frustration……"

"Um, did I……say something strange……?"

Within that haze, what dimly remained in memory was Lila leaning on Elvena, lecturing her.

Remembering it, she felt her ears grow hot.

"Let me see……You said the people in this manor should learn more self-restraint. That it was unbearable, like having food dangled in front of you, and could they please consider the feelings of someone who was trying to hold back, and so on……"

"Ugh……"

"It was, well, fufu, quite reminiscent of Kreschenta-sama when she's sulking……I found it very entertaining, personally."

What pathetic things she had said.

Recalling Kreschenta when sulking (her three basic modes being: sulking, spoiled, and imperious), a double wave of shame surged through her.

The great queen, freed from the burden of her former responsibilities, was — just like her sister — exactly as she appeared.

Lila could not remember seeing Kreschenta speak seriously about anything (not *pretending* to speak seriously, but actually doing so) for at least the past thousand years.

An embodiment of desire, faithful to the pleasure of each moment — she was, in essence, a child.

"L-like sulking Kreschenta-sama……"

Kreschenta — the existence furthest removed from the words *abstinence* and *self-restraint.*

They say alcohol reveals one's true nature, and if someone were to tell Lila that the same nature dwelt within her, she could not deny it. She froze.

Elvena, a cheerful smile on her face, continued:

"Perhaps it's time for you to let loose a little, Lila-sama? Going out of your way to train in a place like this……"

"N-no, if I let myself be carried along like that, I'm the type who'll turn into a hopeless person before I even notice……no, I can hardly call my present state respectable, but I've already let loose too much, so to speak……"

"You're so serious, Lila-sama. I do think you could afford to relax a bit more, like Krische-sama and Kreschenta-sama……"

No one will laugh, Elvena said, pointing a finger at the sky.

"Everyone has desires. Even if you bare them openly, there is no one here who would laugh at you……that's simply normal. And even if you called it decadence — well, eternity itself is the ultimate pinnacle of decadence, so it's a trifle."

"Th-the ultimate pinnacle of decadence……"

"Yes."

Pressing even closer, Elvena brought her face near enough that Lila could feel her breath.

Her dark brown eyes held a quality that was almost — drawing.

"What will be will be. Even if you drown for a time, your time is infinite. I personally think that surrendering to it completely, just once, could be a worthwhile experience……"

"E-experience……"

"Don't worry — humans are creatures that naturally find balance. Just as rules and society and discipline have naturally formed in this manor, where there were none to begin with."

*You might call it a cycle*, Elvena said, and this time she indicated Krische and the others, who were enjoying the mountain descent.

Selene was boarding down the snow face together with an avalanche, scattering brilliant white spray, and far below, Bery and the others were in the middle of rescuing Anne, who had just been buried — right at the moment Anne's body was floating up out of the snow.

How very peaceful. How fun it all looked.

"With the power to permit anything at their disposal, what they do is play in the snow mountains. Quite wholesome, really. Indulgence and luxury become tiresome if you simply repeat them endlessly……and so, naturally, there come times when they feel like playing like children. Selene-sama is a good example."

"Selene-sama……?"

"Yes. As you know, she's a serious person — but once every thirty years or so, she gets tired and spends a while being thoroughly idle……that kind of rest is important too. Including the part where she recovers and declares it's time to reform the manor's discipline — it's all become one of the manor's cycles."

*Even someone like Selene-sama is like that*, Elvena said, and stroked Lila's cheek.

"No one can maintain body and mind in an ideal state forever. There are times when relaxing your shoulders is necessary. ……Especially someone like you, Lila-sama — if you rested your wings for a while and surrendered to a little indolence, you'd return to your former self when the time came."

"……I have, um, no confidence whatsoever in that……"

In that case, she had the strong feeling she would wallow in self-indulgence for eternity.

"Not at all. Likely, Lila-sama, you're simply anxious……once you've indulged to your heart's content, you'd be surprised how quickly — a year or two, perhaps — you'd grow bored of idleness and turn your interest elsewhere. Everything is experience."

"Um……what exactly do you want to do with me, Elvena-sama……?"

"What I want to do, you ask……that's a difficult question……"

Elvena appeared to consider the matter with every show of genuine perplexity.

And then she smiled, prettily.

"I suppose I'd simply like you to spend your days a little more at ease. ……Both as a member of this household, and as a servant attending a guest."

Then, looking slightly embarrassed, she continued:

# Chapter 294: The Priestess and the Cage of Desire — End

The peak of the world's tallest mountain, Greybyalbe.

"W-well……I suppose you could say the meaning lies in the meaninglessness of it……"

She was simple.

A bag containing a small tent.

He had forged his body and mind, pushed himself to his absolute limit, and challenged this mountain with nothing but himself.

Strip away what was unnecessary, refine, and only then did you reach the bare minimum.

Selene had tried to mimic the appearance of that artistry without growing first——that was the source of her plateau.

The key insight had come from two people.

What made Krische and Kreschenta outstanding above all else was their power of observation.

When they looked at something, they could read clearly what its purpose was, what it was for, what it prioritized.

And then, from imitation, they rationally stripped away only what was unnecessary——with precision.

She didn't expect to do it as effortlessly as they could, but she had all the time in the world.

If ten years weren't enough, a hundred; if not a hundred, a thousand.

Adding value could come after.

She had been demanding too much extra too soon.

Like chasing a bird and stepping off a cliff.

The important thing was to proceed one step at a time.

*——When this is done, I'll try again to forge a knife that surpasses that one.*

With that resolve, she brought the mallet down.

*Tap, tok*——satisfying sounds, and she focused on those too.

Soft spots and hard spots; the sound told her how to adjust the angle and force.

She had spent the last thirty years or so on stone sculpture, and compared to all the other crafts she had tried, she felt she was making progress.

She was aware of her own lack of dexterity and her rough edges, but building houses and carving statues——larger-scale projects——seemed to suit her better.

What emerged from the great block of stone was a pair of young angels nestled close together.

Each bearing only one wing, the image was Krische and Kreschenta.

Into this work she poured everything she had learned about craftsmanship from those two, carefully.

Consciousness extending to the very tip of the mallet, she carved.

At this rate, it might be finished in another week.

She paused for a breath, raised the mallet again, and——

"——Selene-sama! Listen to this!"

*Crack*——an ugly sound rang from the stone.
