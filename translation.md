She knew of the theory that this earth was a great, round sphere.

*――What manner of world might this truly be?*

It was Grabareine, the Queen of Alberan, who had put an end to the many competing theories.

Astrology — divining the future from the movements of the stars. In the lands surrounding old Alberan, it had long been believed that the positions and movements of celestial bodies signaled auspicious and inauspicious omens, influencing all manner of things in the world.

At the time, such astrology had been valued as one factor in political decision-making, and in order to silence it, she had demonstrated through theoretical calculation of orbital and physical laws that this world was merely one star adrift in a vast expanse — the universe.

Gravity and centrifugal force binding together a vast cosmos of stars.

None could refute Grabareine, who had unraveled the divine mystery into physics. Astrology lost its mystique, and her theories remained in use as the foundations of astronomy to this day.

Grabareine had likely held no interest in the cosmos itself.

She had unraveled it merely because it was necessary.

Though she had laid the perfect foundation, she had never written anything beyond it, and astronomical research had been treated more as a dilettante's pastime.

Bery had felt much the same, and had never been driven by strong enough curiosity to pursue the research herself — but looking at the rough diagram of the cosmos drawn on the parchment upon the desk, she could not help but feel her heart stir.

"I see…… it really is an immense scale, isn't it."

In the room sat Selene and Bery on the sofas, and Krische and Kreschenta upon their laps respectively.

The usual scene — including Elvena standing attendant beside them, all eyes were directed to the desk.

"That you feel that way only proves the low caliber of your mind."

"……Yes, you're quite right——"

"Honestly, it's unbearable that you can't even manage basic arithmetic. Must I teach you from a child's four operations? Despite going to all the trouble of explaining it myself, and the best you can manage is 'it really is an immense scale, isn't it' — there truly are limits to how foolish——"

"……Woof."

"Wh-what was that……?"

Bery maintained her smile as she pressed a cookie into Kreschenta's mouth to silence her.

"Mgh……"

"Woof, if you would, Kreschenta-sama."

Then she deepened her smile, ruffling Kreschenta's head roughly, as one would a dog.

Ignoring Selene's exasperated gaze, Bery lowered her eyes to the parchment.

Beautiful circles that seemed impossible to have been drawn freehand were inscribed there in countless number, conceptually depicting this star's surroundings and the world beyond.

Great celestial bodies and small ones, innumerable objects tracing spirals, drifting through the endless void.

Noticing that Elvena had also been peering at the parchment, she asked.

"Are you interested in this sort of thing too, Elvena-sama?"

"Yes, it's quite fascinating. I know nothing at all about astronomy, but……"

Elvena answered, curiosity alight in her eyes.

"Fufu, I'm not particularly knowledgeable myself either, but…… it's fun to let the imagination wander, isn't it? With such an unfathomable number of stars, I wonder if there really might be aliens out there."

"……Aliens?"

"Yes — just as we came into existence by chance, perhaps others have been born by chance somewhere else…… given the number of stars and the span of time, it doesn't seem entirely impossible."

"I see, that's true. Insects and fish, lizards and snakes…… perhaps even clever plants."

While giggling together and feeding Kreschenta cookies, Krische, who had been drowsy on Selene's lap, asked.

"Does Bery want to see aliens?"

"Well…… rather than wanting to see them, it's more that it's fun to imagine, I suppose."

"I see…… ehehe, it would be nice if they existed. Alien-san——"

*It would be nice if they existed, alien-san* —— the voice of a small creature on a small planet.

A sound that would normally vanish in an instant across the unfathomable void.

In the course of conversation, that small creature noticed a gap in her 'safety net,' rewrote the network she had inscribed into the source realm, and set it to propagate.

The Fuyopita network — the world-law that would later be called the Ether Limit — closed the pathways leading to the source, imposing limits on magical inscription through mana.

It restricted the rapid advancement of magical power, introducing distortions in formulae that exceeded a certain scale of inscription, suppressing them.

It was a 'rule' etched into the deepest place in the world to prevent world-destruction through overwhelming magic.

And so the voice of the girl who was connected to the source passed, unintentionally, into the network.

*——It would be nice if the servant she loved could enjoy herself.*

That unconscious wish echoed from the source outward, into the unfathomably vast cosmos of stars.

*It would be nice if they existed, alien-san* ——

From source to source.

The voice twisted space-time alongside the propagating network, rippling through the world of nothingness.

Star to star, as if to fill every last corner of that endless void.

*It would be nice if they existed, alien-san* ——

A journey through the source, transcending space-time. The network merely traveled onward, filling the discrepancy of distance with time.

Riding the undulations of space-time as though traveling backward through the cosmos's ages, it proceeded through the void of an era before life had yet spread across their star, carrying out the instructions it had been given.

*——It would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——it would be nice if they existed, alien-san——* from the warped source, the voice reached into the unfathomably distant past.

"Karararara."

On the planet that would later be designated the seventh planet of the Minalshi System — Minardria.

The lizard-like creatures inhabiting it sensed something pass through their bodies like a wave, and raised their heads.

"Karararararara?"

"Karara."

Communicating through their cries, they quickly forgot about the wave.

And yet, something fundamentally different had been inscribed deep within them.

* * *

——Adjusting and firing generated tachyon particles with negative mass.

Having pinpointed the enemy's precise current position through reflection, compensating for temporal discrepancy, a wormhole was formed and entered immediately.

What erupted from the fleet simultaneously with warp-out was a torrent of energy powerful enough to reduce a planet to a dead world in days.

The enormous energy supplied from the degenerate reactor was channeled through a massive particle accelerator, producing instantaneous destruction and light, sweeping away and annihilating the enemy before them.

Hit-and-run via warp.

In battles staged across the vast cosmos, such tactics were the mainstay.

Warping out directly in front of an enemy light-years away, delivering a simultaneous barrage while engaging thrust propulsion.

After breaking through to the enemy's rear, forming a wormhole again, warping out to create distance.

Energy shields, wormhole formation, anti-close-range defensive fire.

All of this was processed simultaneously across the enormous bridge manned by five hundred, with countless voices relaying reports.

Those raising their voices were creatures resembling reptiles.

Lizard-like faces, two proud horns at the temporal region.

They had no scales, but possessed dull, lustrous gray skin, and their bodies were sheathed to the tip of their tails in suits of a rubbery purple texture.

Apart from silver metal protectors on the chest, groin, feet, and gauntlets, the design was relatively simple. Their four-fingered hands, wrapped in rubber, moved freely, each manipulating a spatially projected holographic display floating in the air.

The engineering-form fleet was cutting through a swarm of dangerous cosmic organisms — a colony of space amoebae.

The horde of Arnia — grotesque monsters that consumed organic matter and proliferated.

If even a single one slipped through, it could easily devour an entire planet.

Whether they had gained wisdom through the long war despite their low intelligence, they were beginning to mimic the shape of the fleet's own vessels.

They fused and split freely, and when enlarged, they far exceeded the size of their own combat vessels, which housed crews of ten thousand.

Through unknown means, they formed particle accelerator cannons internally, and once they breached a ship's shield and attached themselves to the hull, they easily chewed through the nano-coated high-density armor and infiltrated inside, proliferating.

Not a single one, not a single fragment, could be allowed to approach.

The main cannon at the prow incinerated the majority deployed ahead, and the fleet passed through the center while scattering secondary fire in every direction to maintain the breakthrough.

"98.7% of the Arnia have been destroyed! Remaining individuals: 18,375!"

"Good. Cancel the warp-out, move to mopping up. All vessels, come about. Launch the Fortelus."

The one raising his voice from the especially large central chair was Milganga, commander of the 237th Expeditionary Fleet.

The horns on his brow were adorned with gold and jewel decorations, and instead of a suit he wore cloth.

A cloak draped from both shoulders, its interior emitting a faint, warm glow.

——It was a heater.

The Minalshi, who proclaimed themselves rulers of the galaxy, were a sapient species possessing robust physiques and rich intellect — yet they had a notable weakness: extreme sensitivity to temperature changes.

They fell into a dormant state when cold.

For this reason, the suits they wore were always designed to maintain a constant body temperature, but that was merely the minimum preservation of warmth, far from comfortable.

They were extremely averse to cold, but due to their racial characteristics, warmth in turn brought about a state of mental laxity — in other words, they also possessed the concurrent flaw of becoming self-indulgent.

That Milganga could overcome such racial traits, warming his body in direct, cozy comfort while still maintaining discipline and a commanding presence, was worthy of being called a great hero.

Everyone regarded him with gazes of respect for that fortitude.

The fleet came about — at the command, a vast number of carrier-borne craft launched, proceeding to mop up the remnants.

The cutting-edge Fortelus fighters, directly linked via brainwave interface, scattered particle accelerator fire from both arm units while executing inertia-defying, irregular maneuvers to annihilate the remaining small Arnia.

Designed also for planetary subjugation, the three-legged configuration of feet and tail was dead weight, but thanks in part to the brainwave link device, their performance was not bad.

The confusion among the Arnia was clearly visible from the environment sensors.

"I'd wondered what sort of toy they were, but not bad at all. Technical officer, send me the combat data afterwards."

"Yes, sir!"

"However……"

Milganga turned his head to the side.

There stood one Minalshi, small for their kind — gray skin patchy and balding in places, indicating the nearness of death.

"That was a close call. To think they had spread even to such a place. Had the authorization come any later, the jewel you spoke of may well have been swallowed by them."

"Indeed, you are absolutely right, sir. ……But what magnificent command — a flawless victory. Against a swarm of that size…… had it been any fleet but yours, Milganga-sama, who knows what would have become of us."

"If you're astonished by this, your tail would snap off just watching a Hive assault operation. In the old Kudaras assault, there were over 100,000 times as many——"

『Data cross-reference: 135,347 times, sir.』

"Details are unnecessary. Well, there were that many Arnia swarming about."

Smiling wryly at the AI, Milganga continued.

"I would rather not imagine. A Hive that has swallowed an entire planet…… in that assault, my grandson and great-grandchild — ten of my kin — gave their lives for Minalshi."

"Many died. It would be harder to find someone who did not lose family in that battle. Including those who had lived on Kudaras."

Milganga closed his eyes, reaching out as if to touch his right horn, and the old Minalshi, Giryurya, likewise closed his eyes.

For them, this was the gesture of prayer.

"After the mopping up is complete, we'll conduct another ultra-long-range search. If there are no issues, wormhole entry is in thirty-two Minalshi standard time units. Giryurya, get some rest and compile the data on Benua. I want to review it once more before arrival."

"Sir. As you command, Milganga-sama."

Habitation zones enclosing entire stars, artificial celestial habitation zones.

The Trans-Galactic Minalshi Empire had established many celestial structures beyond planets and had developed through them.

But no matter how far they went, these were artificial ground.

Many Minalshi harbored a longing for real ground — a naturally habitable planet.

Crashing ice asteroids and icy bodies to adjust orbital paths and rotation speeds.

Moving stars into habitable zones, providing abundant water, planting vegetation — sequential terraforming was underway on many planets.

That said, even with a technological level capable of remaking entire star systems, it took many long years to make a single planet habitable.

Currently, ninety percent lived in artificial habitation zones — most of them ended their lives without ever setting foot on real ground.

There was no doubt that the cosmos still held many undiscovered habitable planets, but the universe was unfathomably vast.

Even having obtained means of travel far exceeding lightspeed, no matter how many survey ships they dispatched, they remained minuscule beings.

Even with their numbers reaching the hundreds of billions, they were but a speck of dust in the cosmos.

Naturally habitable planets were not so easily found.

"Milganga-sama. Warp-out near planet Benua complete. Coordinates identified, spatial stabilization finished. No anomalies across all vessels."

"……Very well. I want to see it with my own eyes. Remove the shielding."

"Sir!"

The frontal shielding that had been closed for warp navigation.

The bridge's protective barrier opened left and right, revealing a small, jewel-like celestial body.

At the sight, Milganga's eyes widened, and his tail swung vigorously from side to side.

"This is…… Benua. How beautiful……"

It was a celestial body confirmed not long ago.

Abundant water resources. Covered in green, with many mineral resources likely sleeping beneath its surface — a cosmic jewel, planet Benua.

Like Milganga, many who laid eyes on it wagged their tails.

They lacked facial expressions; instead, their emotions manifested in their tails.

Many were overwhelmed and overjoyed by the beauty of planet Benua.

——It was long ago that the Minalshi had taken to the cosmos and gained the technology to traverse the stars.

At times they had waged wars with comparable spacefaring civilizations — wars that consumed entire star systems over a single habitable planet.

Even so, when one turned one's eyes outward, the cosmos was truly vast.

Before the beauty of a star such as this, it seemed almost comical how small a world they had been fighting over.

The cosmos truly overflowed with what could only be called an infinite number of stars.

Blue ether covered Benua like a veil, casting light and shadow across its verdant surface.

Despite having encountered many astonishing adversaries, gazing upon what could be called the ultimate beauty of a celestial body, one still felt the reverence toward divinity in one's horns.

Calling to mind something that ought to be called a god — something that had faded with scientific advancement — he closed his eyes and touched his right horn.

"……Seeing it in person is indeed different from mere data."

"Truly. That I could bring you to this star during my own lifetime, Milganga-sama…… it is an honor beyond words, the pride of my life."

The small lizard standing beside him — Giryurya — trembled his tail, eyes glistening.

"Giryurya. I will personally convey your service to His Majesty. ……Surely a gold horn will be permitted."

"Sir. I am deeply grateful for your words."

All of Minalshi was ruled by the Trans-Galactic King, Minashea.

Permission to adorn one's horns was generally limited to royalty like Milganga, or to those few who had been recognized for extraordinary service.

Though gold horns ranked below the jeweled horns of Milganga's kind, for a commoner it was the highest honor — bestowed upon those who deserved to be recorded in Minalshi history.

And planet Benua was well worth it.

Abundant water resources, a stable climate, a gravity ratio nearly identical to their homeworld Minardria, and extremely similar oxygen concentrations.

Planets that required no terraforming at all — where Minalshi could set foot without even protective suits — numbered only a handful in all their long history.

Giryurya was a renowned survey technician to begin with.

When word of his discovery of planet Benua spread, no one would object to bestowing the gold horn upon him.

"As planned, we'll send the advance party down tomorrow. Giryurya, this is the star you found. You shall ride in the first boat."

"……Are you certain, sir?"

"Yes. The inhabitants are pre-spacefaring — there should be no danger, but…… heh, don't get too excited and wander about."

"Sir……!"

Eyes closing as if overcome with emotion, tail swishing happily.

Watching Giryurya, Milganga laughed.

Planet Benua was inhabited by various organisms, and among them, bipedal intelligent life forms called Benuans had been confirmed.

Organic life forms, a species of endotherm.

They reproduced through copulation via reproductive organs, bearing live young.

——From the Minalshi perspective, they were closely related.

Considering silicon-based life, gas-based life, celestial-scale cosmic organisms, and amoebae capable of solo space travel — compared to such incomprehensible life forms, beings like these inspired a sense of kinship.

The ecological survey via drones had been largely completed.

Their vision relied on light.

They possessed hearing and smell, and their sensory organs were broadly similar to Minalshi.

They were capable of fairly advanced conversation through sound exchange via vocal organs, and while they could not perceive heat through sensation as Minalshi could, they possessed the astonishing ability — in no small proportion — to sense and manipulate psychokinetic particles: ether.

Despite having developed a reasonably advanced society, their technological stagnation was likely due to this.

Ether appeared useful at first glance, but due to fluctuations in large-scale operations — the existence of the Ether Limit — its scalability was low, and it would not lead to the kind of technological development that could carry one across galaxies.

Being able to wield it in daily life was certainly convenient, but that very convenience was presumably what slowed the development of their other scientific technologies.

And low technological level narrowed the world.

Currently there was no unified government, and multiple nation-states were engaged in territorial disputes.

It was a scene reminiscent of when Minalshi themselves had still been crawling across their homeworld.

High-rise buildings lined the streets, and on the battlefield, low-level kinetic weapons using ether-powered launch mechanisms were in use. Their space travel technology was similarly primitive.

Debris-like 'toys' had been confirmed orbiting in satellite trajectories, but at the very least, it would be a long time before they could colonize other planets.

They likely believed this single planet was the entirety of the world.

Establishing a coexistence relationship with beings who had not yet achieved planetary unification would have its difficulties, but this was their duty as the vanguard of civilization, as their predecessors.

Having thus elevated, protected, and managed many intelligent species was of course in part for the Minalshi's own benefit — economic reasons existed — but it was not only that.

The cosmos held many dangerous life forms and species with which communication was not possible.

Though lacking in intellect, the instinct-driven, organic-consuming, endlessly-dividing amoebae called Arnia — if even a fragment reached this world, it could easily destroy and swallow a civilization like this.

To address such threats, technological enlightenment of these beings was indispensable.

And the cosmos still held many problems that even the Minalshi could not solve.

If these beings, who could intuitively sense and manipulate psychokinetic particles, could be brought to a comparable level and an alliance formed, it was possible that breakthroughs could be found for several cosmos-scale challenges that currently existed.

The richness of the planet, the species dwelling upon it.

They had to bring them under their protection before other hands could reach them.

Not only alien life forms, but even among closely related species there was no shortage of barbarians who sought to enslave others.

It was fortunate that their hand had reached this world before such people could.

Now then, the means — currently, Benua possessed no effective weapons capable of breaching their ship shields.

A simultaneous landing at key planetary locations as a show of force would be sufficient to bring them to the negotiating table.

Military force kept to the minimum as intimidation, with as little cultural destruction as possible.

As he was considering the path ahead, an alarm sounded.

"……!?"

『High-density ether entity intrusion on primary bridge. Repeat: high-density ether entity intrusion on primary bridge——』

Before him, at some point, stood a Benuan — a small female intelligent life form with long silver hair (on her head).

No larger than Milganga's waist. A tiny creature.

She showed no sign of fear, tilting her head curiously, pressing the tip of her right finger to her lips, gazing up at him with violet eyes.

Then she clapped her hands together, leaking sounds in an incomprehensible language — something like *oh, it's an alien*.

How had she appeared here?

Preparations for warp-based close-quarters combat were more than adequate.

The internal defenses were robust, and breaching the spatial barrier powered by energy from the degenerate reactor required an equivalent-scale vessel or installation.

Yet no such installation had been detected within this star system, and judging from the current technological level, it would be thousands of cycles before they could even grasp the rudiments of such technology.

Naturally, concealment of it should have been impossible.

——The academic questions could wait.

He immediately recovered from his confusion and aimed his left hand at the silver-haired female in her black-and-white garment.

A compact particle accelerator pistol deployed instantly, and he thrust out his right hand in a halt gesture.

Simultaneously, he accessed the ship's internal network through his neural implant device and activated the defensive systems.

"Halt!"

The portable particle accelerator's safety was disengaged, battery cartridge connected.

A pull of the trigger would discharge energy sufficient to easily vaporize a single life form.

He caught the peripheral movement of defense drones beginning to deploy and, with that, finally steadied his breathing.

The silver-haired female turned those peculiar violet eyes left and right.

Even without understanding the language, she seemed to grasp the situation.

"That's right, good——?"

He stopped mid-sentence not because he had forgotten to activate the translator.

It was because another alarm was sounding.

『Ether alert. Cracking has been performed——access granted. Welcome, Administrator.』

One of the defense drones floated over to the female and was casually grabbed.

The female patted and examined the armed defense drone with evident curiosity, and Milganga looked about in bewilderment.

He could see one of the technicians touching the holographic console and being denied access — and realized a moment later that the ship's controls had been hijacked.

"You……!"

It was a reaction born of primal fear.

He pulled the trigger aimed at the female, and a particle accelerator blast was discharged.

An excessive dose of energy was fired directly at the life form.

"……!?"

But the light did not incinerate the female.

Before her, it vanished as though swallowed by the void.

The female watched it with no apparent surprise, then walked calmly toward him and swept her hand.

——In that instant, every Minalshi on the bridge was crushed to the floor.

As if every cell in their bodies was being pressed against the deck.

"GA——!?"

Milganga was no exception, flattened against the floor, barely managing to keep his eyes on that single creature.

The female crouched, drone still in her arms, and looked down at Milganga with a softening of her lips.

『First meeting. Greeting. Space primitive Minalshi. I servant name Krische.』

Alarms warning of powerful ether interference echoed.

A bizarre sensation of meaning being transmitted directly into his mind.

『Star. What business?』

Innocently undulating psychokinetic particles.

The unfathomable pressure they carried was more terrifying than any enemy he had ever encountered.

For the Minalshi, it struck at an ancient, abhorred instinct.

『Question.』

All around, countless sounds of *pichi-pichi* — snapping.

The tails of many Minalshi on the bridge had been severed at their instincts' command, and were bouncing across the deck.

Milganga felt the ache at the base of his own tail — and wrestled down the instinct that was trying to sever and flee with an act of will.

The silver-haired female watched this, uttered a mysterious sound — *hoeee* — and tilted her head.

Only then did she apparently remember to activate the translator.

From the data Giryurya had previously collected and analyzed, it informed: 『"Hoeee": exclamation, or vocalization of similar nature.』

『Greeting. Attack. Bad.』

Again, meaning transmitted directly into his mind, and the pressure lifted from Milganga's body.

He rose to find those around him in a similar state — still confused, staring at him.

『……What are you?』

"Oh, you can speak in this language."

『I am using a translation device. ……Answer my question. How did you intrude?』

"Mm…… how, you ask — Krische just sort of hopped over……"

Reviewing the translator's linguistic analysis — 『"hopped": repetition of "hop" for emphasis; onomatopoeia for jumping』 — he simultaneously scanned the female before him.

Externally, she appeared no different from the intelligent life forms inhabiting Benua, but the critical difference was the extraordinarily high-density ether observed emanating from this individual.

The ship's AI classified her as a high-density ether entity — at the very least, not a simple biological organism.

Several subspecies of Benuans had been documented, but no such entity had been confirmed.

Giryurya, who had finally gotten to his feet beside him, shook his head.

Indeed, an unidentified species.

What she called "hopping" was likely a form of specialized warp.

At the very least, it was an unknown method of travel.

"Let Krische try again…… Nice to meet you, space-lizard-san. Krische is a servant. If you have no hostile intentions, Krische won't do anything terrible either, so please don't worry."

*What brings you to this star?* — the female, Krische, tilted her head.

He glanced at Giryurya, who nodded silently.

It was best to answer honestly — that seemed to be the shared assessment.

With an ether wielder of this caliber, there was a possibility of her reading their thoughts.

This individual had subdued the fleet flagship — packed with the latest technology — as easily as twisting a baby's tail.

And terrifyingly, this one was a servant.

Literally — if her earlier telepathy and Giryurya's translation analysis were correct, there existed a master above her.

What manner of being could possibly surpass this trans-dimensional ether entity?

『I am Milganga, Supreme Commander of the Minalshi Expeditionary Fleet, in service to His Majesty the Trans-Galactic King Minashea. Allow me to apologize for the earlier discourtesy. ……We came to establish exchange with the intelligent life forms of this planet, for mutual prosperity, and to bring technological enlightenment. We are also considering colonization after deepening relations.』

"Enlightenment, colonization……"

『We intended to share technology with intelligent life forms who have yet to achieve space migration, and to provide the strength to confront the many unknown threats that still lurk.』

That she showed no hostility was fortunate — or rather, she simply had no need for it.

The other party had instantly seized control of this state-of-the-art large combat vessel and casually stopped a particle accelerator blast that could penetrate an infantryman's shield.

Paying closest attention to the behavior of this fearsome unknown entity, Krische, he continued.

『To be honest, we had considered that some degree of military force might be necessary in the process of forming a cooperative relationship. This fleet was intended as intimidation…… however, I swear by this horn that we harbor no intention or malice to conquer and destroy this star.』

"Hmm, I see…… so that's how it is. That was quite a large amount of energy, so Krische might have jumped to conclusions a little. Umm……"

As she pressed her finger to her lips, the ether alarm sounded again.

What manifested instantly was a large, old-fashioned double door.

The Minalshi stared wide-eyed, exchanging glances — and when the door opened, a different individual with red hair (on her head) peered through.

"Ehehe, Bery, they're space lizard-sans."

"Krische-sama, you mustn't. The aliens probably have business with the people over there, and Ojou-sama is absolutely furious that you should bring them back quickly…… um, hello……?"

The red-haired female also bowed her head, looking confused at the many tails snapping and bouncing about.

Then she beckoned to Krische.

"Yes. The reason for their visit seemed peaceful, anyway."

"That's good to hear. Well then, shall we head back?"

*Please excuse us,* the one called Bery said, leading Krische through the door and closing it.

The door vanished without a trace, like a passing storm — and Milganga stared blankly at Giryurya.

"Giryurya, that individual……"

"I don't know. In appearance, she looked like a Benuan, but…… a being composed of ether…… perhaps? It was a sight beyond belief, and yet……"

The hijacked ship AI had returned to its original state, but the tails of the Minalshi bouncing across the floor — *bichi-bichi* — were very much real.

"……No, it truly is beyond belief. To breach the ship's protective field and then so easily seize control of our cutting-edge ship AI……"

"……Yes."

"The former is at least comprehensible as an unknown ether-based method of travel, but the latter is not something ether alone could accomplish. The AI itself was not destroyed — it means she instantly understood the complex structure and rewrote it…… even if that entity itself functioned as a computational device, it exceeds any physically possible processing speed."

Giryurya displayed Benuan data on his holographic window.

The average intelligence level was slightly lower than Minalshi.

Perhaps due to the limited data, the variance seemed large, but even at the high end, it was within realistic biological levels.

Nowhere near sufficient to make what had just occurred possible.

At the very least, she was an anomaly even among Benuans.

"She was beyond anything we've encountered…… but the word 'jumped to conclusions.' At the very least, she came to investigate our intentions. We possess combat vessels more than sufficient to destroy this star. She was likely wary of that."

"And the other one spoke of 'the people over there,' which suggests…… that there are beings like this, distinct from Benuans, lurking on this star?"

"That would be the reasonable conclusion. ……On this star, there exist transcendent beings, as if the gods we had forgotten truly walked the earth."

Gods — yes, that was the only way to make sense of it.

At the very least, they were higher-dimensional entities, fundamentally different from themselves.

"Shall we…… withdraw?"

Giryurya asked in a voice that seemed to suppress something anguished.

An overwhelming anomalous existence against which their technology was entirely useless — the danger level was extreme, and abandoning interference with this star was one possible course.

"No. We continue."

"But……"

"If she had meant us harm, we could have been killed at that very moment. She merely came to observe us — that was the atmosphere. Withdrawing now would only increase our concerns…… rather, we should take the initiative in deepening relations with them before some hardliner, hearing what happened, decides to come here."

Milganga declared this with authority and looked at Giryurya.

"But we proceed with the utmost caution. We will conduct a thorough new round of observations, and the landing and negotiations will follow after. Giryurya, I leave the command of that operation to you."

"Sir!"

* * *

The embassy established for the Minalshi was quite grand, proportioned to their size.

Ceiling heights, corridors — everything on the scale of an art museum, and the conference rooms were the size of party halls.

Why such dimensions were necessary was immediately apparent upon seeing a Minalshi in person.

『I must say, the more I learn, the more fascinating this place is. The various equipment and tools utilizing ether…… you are literally a gathering of mages.』

"Ha ha, we were indeed called that in the old days, sir. Ether use has advanced considerably, but in the pre-historic era it was not as widespread as it is now, and terms similar to what you describe were commonly used."

Standing twice the height of an adult man — the Minalshi were extremely large.

The first time he had seen one, the sheer intimidating presence had stiffened his body, but after fifty years of association, there was no longer any fear.

As frozen water cannot flow, stagnation became a binding.

Orburina stood in a realm beyond the reflection of the moon in water — her stance alone was enough to make Fujisai sense defeat.

He had assumed she would be a respectable swordsman, while resigning himself that she would fall far short of his own conception of a heavenly sword.

The heavens were beyond human reach — that was their nature.

# Chapter 341: The Ojou-sama and the Servant — A Duel in the Garden

The fantastical radiance that filled sky and sun alike had long since become part of the everyday.

Though it had been given the name Krisneit, they hardly ever called it that.

Usually it was "over there and over here," "that side and this side" — the realization that the name served no purpose had come only afterward.

It had been named to prevent it from becoming something ridiculous like *Fuyofuyo-Fuwan*, but the word "Krisneit" had not been heard once in a thousand years.

The manor, and everything else.

For Selene and the others, that was about as far as distinctions went.

"Fufu, you can't lay a finger on me, can you. As expected of Ojou-sama."

"……How about you try putting in a little more effort?"

"Effort or no effort, I am doing my best, as it happens……"

Smiling wryly, her red hair swaying, she was slightly shorter than Selene.

Her figure, wrapped in a pure white apron dress, never changed; her youthful features and relaxed expression never changed either — at the very least, Selene had never once seen her face twisted in genuine frustration.

A thousand, two thousand years since Selene had been born.

When one spoke of Bery Argan, the image that invariably came to mind was this infuriating smile of composure.

In her hand was Krische's favored curved sword.

Her stance was formless — dangling loosely.

Like Krische, she was small, and this curved sword suited her in terms of weight.

Whether that was actually true was another matter.

Long ago — when she had trained the young Selene — she had handled a longsword with ease. Whatever weapon she used, that would not change.

That she was a genius was something Selene knew to an aggravating degree.

She was the kind of person who soared over obstacles that others strained to leap, with a single flap of her wings. A pathologically perfectionist creature who refined her skills as naturally as breathing.

Even just looking at how she handled mana — ever since the old world, the flow of mana around her, the way it clung to her standing form, had possessed the same stripped-to-essentials beauty that Krische's did.

She was not the kind of opponent whose next move could be read from the flow of her mana.

And on top of all that, she was an extreme sore loser, and her malice and spite were first-rate.

Not "she won't fight a losing battle" — Bery Argan *only fought battles she could win*.

The reason she had picked up a sword now was because this was merely a *game*.

She drew a sharp line between games and real contests, never gave her all in a game, and for her, games were laying the groundwork for the real contest to come.

In a board game with no element of luck, a 100-to-0 shutout was entirely possible.

Selene had long conceded that she could never win in that kind of raw intellect — and yet, Bery was undefeated even in games like rock-paper-scissors, where luck and chance played a role.

Even if a *game* existed in which she could only win one or two out of ten, she would invariably make those one or two wins happen during the *real contest*. That was the kind of woman Bery Argan was.

For the sake of victory in the *real contest*, she would calmly lay invisible groundwork on a scale of centuries.

Her every gesture, her every word, was suspect; you could tie her to a stick, wrap her in a rock, and sink her in water, and still you could not rest easy around this woman.

"Again. Here I come."

"Yes, go ahead."

A Zain-stance before her sword — Selene's step, bursting with mana.

Against a lunge like an arrow loosed from a bow, Bery stepped back half a pace to create distance.

This had been the deciding move a moment ago.

The extended tip had been pressed to Selene's throat, ending the bout.

But this time, with a half-step retreat — minimum distance to evade — she simultaneously ducked under the longsword, stepped in, and swept her curved blade.

A single flash aimed at the right arm holding the sword, with neither tension nor fighting spirit.

That absence of intent was so like Krische's that the sensation of a delayed reaction returned.

Selene pulled her arm back and leaned away to dodge — and instinctively leapt backward, just as a sweeping kick timed to the curved blade's slash cut through the space where she had been.

A chill ran through her, but she immediately exploited Bery's wide opening, stepping back in and pressing her sword to her throat.

"I thought that would work……"

"Listen — don't underestimate me."

For instance, had Selene dodged with only her arm and torso, that sweep kick would have been the finishing blow.

Had she grown careless from the gap in skill, she might have been toppled easily — but against Bery, carelessness was unthinkable.

A single use of the Zain blade, and she had read the timing, judged the distance.

Selene had a feeling this would happen.

When the tip had been pressed to her throat a moment ago, it had not been truly out of the blue.

She had not been entirely unable to react.

Selene had been practicing the Zain blade in training, and Bery had watched from time to time.

Its speed was within her predictions — in fact, even with the tip at her throat, there had been no surprise on Bery's face. She seemed to have been coolly confirming Selene's stance and foot placement.

As if gauging just how far Selene's thrust could reach.

*Well done*, she praised with a smile, all the while gathering Selene's data with those eyes, coolly constructing her counter-strategy.

*That won't work; then how about this?*

She gathered information, methodically eliminating Selene's options one by one.

A technique shown once would not work against her.

She had the eyes and the instinct for it, and her mana control was endlessly refined, unrestricted.

Just as Selene had honed her sword over these years, Bery's virtual musculature had been polished further still across these unfathomable ages.

Selene, of course, had reached a level where physical enhancement was something she forgot she was even using.

That said, Bery's mana work — the delicacy and precision with which she operated while handling advanced magic without the slightest disruption — was still several steps ahead of Selene's.

In raw explosive power, Selene far exceeded her, but that naturalness — the slight lag in reaction it caused — would be fatal if allowed even once.

The gap in ability was still considerable.

And despite that, Bery threw in attacks that made Selene's blood run cold.

*What happens if she hones it further?* — she was the perfect rival.

An opponent like this was needed after all.

Swinging a sword was fun on its own, but more fun with an opponent.

"Let's end with five more bouts today. I need to prepare dinner."

"All right. We'll continue another time…… but don't think you'll win easily. I intend to enjoy a good thirty more years of beating you handily."

"My. As if I would think it easy. This is merely an occasional diversion for me, after all……"

"What kind of person can fight this well as a *diversion*—— Hah!"

Against Selene's thrust, Bery twisted instantly to dodge — and Selene immediately moved left.

From beneath the fluttering skirt, a reverse roundhouse kick closed in.

Selene spun her own body to dodge, and swung a horizontal slash.

No contact — Bery had opened the distance, murmuring *that didn't work either* with a wry smile.

Against the ambush, no carelessness, no surprise.

Bery truly was an impossible woman.

*Why don't you try it sometime?* — Selene had made the invitation, and the fact that Bery had taken up a sword even once made the conclusion foregone.

From the moment she had, the future contest was all but decided — someday, this woman would inevitably declare she wanted to settle things with the sword.

To smile through a hundred or a thousand defeats in the *game*, and then come for the win in contest number one thousand and one — that was Bery Argan.

What Selene needed to think about was how, when that inevitable one-thousand-and-first time arrived, to turn the tables and make her howl.

* * *

After that, Bery took up the sword only a few times a year.

Selene won every bout, but Bery was steadily improving.

In the realm of mana use, swordsmanship was largely a matter of mental image.

Given that the body was essentially moved through mana, if one could control mana with near-perfect precision, it was theoretically possible to move the body exactly as imagined.

If one could imagine the correct principles in one's mind, training could be done even without holding a sword.

Of course, body control reliant purely on mana was not as easy as it sounded.

Selene herself had only truly mastered it in this body, after coming here.

Breaking the habit of moving the body through muscle tension had taken a very long time.

In the old world, the only one who had perfectly embodied it was probably Aleha.

It was the kind of technique that a talented person would spend a lifetime acquiring.

But Bery's mana control, taken on its own, had always been world-class.

She had been using this body naturally from the very first day here — a thoroughly infuriating woman.

The foundation for swordsmanship had been in place since the old world, and all that remained was knowledge and experience — and yet, despite not gripping a sword every day, within a few decades she had polished her blade skill to the point where Selene felt genuinely threatened.

Likely, even while watching Selene spar with Krische, or instruct Lila and Elvena, Bery was picking up subtle habits and fighting imaginary battles in her mind.

The proof was that Bery's swordsmanship was nothing but a blade dripping with sheer malice.

Whenever they crossed swords, Selene would find herself maneuvered into positions and stances where swinging her sword was difficult, without knowing how it had happened.

Striking at the opponent's weakness was the essence of combat.

But few could commit to it as thoroughly as this woman.

She gathered information from watching Selene swing her sword, read her habits and preferred lines of attack, and devoted herself entirely to never letting Selene swing comfortably.

For now, Selene had won every bout, but whether each of those victories had truly been her own was doubtful.

Unless the skill gap was enormous, going 10-to-0 in swordsmanship was difficult.

Carelessness too subtle to notice, fatigue — the peaks and valleys of form — produced selection errors too minor to call mistakes.

And if those accumulated, even by chance, defeat was inevitable.

There had, in fact, been several narrow victories that might have gone the other way.

She had won them all, somehow — but those were likely traps.

*——Bery was deliberately not taking the win, in order to lull Selene into complacency.*

She would not reveal a trump card meant for the *real contest* in a mere *game*.

Given Selene's suspicions, it was almost certain that Bery was holding a winning hand in reserve.

Bery was a woman who would literally do anything to win — a woman of fundamentally twisted character.

Fair play, enjoying a close contest, confirming and praising each other's progress — Selene suspected she was entirely devoid of such wholesome, healthy concepts of joy.

The clean sportsmanship that had characterized the bouts between Kokys and Aleha was utterly absent.

Bery Argan was a woman who could smile while kicking a bound and helpless opponent.

Her interest in nothing but winning went beyond sore loser — it was a sickness.

And then, the day came — about fifty years after Bery had first taken up the sword.

"Very well. Fine. ……Let us settle this with the sword, shall we, Ojou-sama?"

"Oh?"

In the conference room of the manor, after a heated debate — it was this statement that put an end to it.

The previous round had been Selene's loss, resulting in Bery's preferred deserted-island-castaway tour.

This time, the priority should have gone to Selene's previously deferred proposal: a natural cave exploration tour.

But, reneging on that agreement, Bery had suddenly proposed a deep-sea-submersion tour.

Changing plans on a whim, ignoring prior agreements.

It was tantamount to picking a fight.

*'We decided the next trip would be the Kriishian magic crystal caves! And why is it sea after sea!'*

Selene's objections were met with sophistry.

*'Ojou-sama, shallow waters and the deep sea are entirely different things. That's like calling a mountain and a meadow the same because they're both land. Besides, I never said we wouldn't visit the magic crystal caves. According to Krische-sama, there are similar caves deep in the ocean as well, so while exploring the deep sea, we could also visit those magic crystal caves——'*

*'That's not the point! Do you understand? You are breaking the promise you made with me the other day, selfishly changing the destination on a whim?'*

*'If I may remind you, even at the time of that promise, the Kriishian magic crystal caves were merely one candidate. It was agreed that we would visit magic crystal caves, but…… I find it quite unfair to say I broke my promise. The question of which magic crystal caves to visit was to be decided going forward. Ojou-sama, isn't it rather selfish to insist it was decided for Kriishian simply because that is where you wish to go……?'*

*'Listen — I don't care who else in the world says it, I refuse to be called selfish by you!'*

And so on, until finally:

Last time, the result of the contest had meant the trip went Bery's way.

This time, the priority was Selene's, and if the matter came to a contest over a difference of opinion, the right to choose the contest's terms was naturally Selene's.

No further sophistry would be tolerated.

*If you insist on being willful, shall we settle it by the sword?* — when Selene put it that way, Bery considered briefly, then agreed.

With an expression that was infuriatingly suggestive of *oh, very well, if I must*.

"This too is the duty of an educator…… though it pains me to embarrass Ojou-sama, when reprimanding willfulness, it cannot be helped."

"Who's being willful? ……You agreed awfully quickly. Does that mean you've had a plan to beat me ready for ages?"

"A plan to beat you — Ojou-sama, have you forgotten? Who it was that first taught you the sword?"

"How many years ago was that!"

It was, by now, over two thousand years ago.

Biting back her irritation at the usual condescension, she steadied her breathing and fixed her gaze on Bery.

"Fine — if you're that confident, then no take-backs. You said it, didn't you? That you'd fight me with swords. The contest is right now, in the garden — first to score a clean hit wins…… sound fair?"

"My, you won't give a handicap, despite the sword being your specialty?"

"I've decided long ago — against you, and only you, I will never drop my guard. ……Today's demands were simply too outrageous. I'm entitled to take a slight advantage to make it even."

"……Well, fine. Let's get this over with."

"……Keep talking."

When Selene stood, Bery rose to match her.

It was just past noon.

"U-um…… Bery? What about lunch……"

"I'm sorry — I got carried away. Please wait just a little longer."

"Uuu…… okay……"

Krische, whose thoughts were occupied solely by lunch, picked up the sleeping Kreschenta from the next chair and followed Bery out of the room——

"——Just so we're clear, this is one-on-one. No pulling that idiot in for a two-on-one, understood?"

"I don't think you need to be quite that suspicious……"

"If I don't say it in advance, you'll trample that kind of basic decency without a second thought."

"Hah…… where did I go wrong raising you……"

"……You, honestly."

As the three left, Selene sighed and looked at the three remaining.

"As always, Bery-sama's confidence is impressive…… Under these conditions, it looks quite difficult for her to beat you, Selene-sama……"

"That kind of provocation is just another one of Bery's tricks. I've gotten used to it by now."

Telling the wide-eyed Lila as much, the other woman tilted her head and smiled softly.

"You seem to be enjoying yourself more than usual today."

"Well, I had been wanting to fight Bery with swords…… It's infuriating, but played straight, she'd probably take one or two out of ten. There were close calls when we sparred, and given how confident she just looked, the fact that she wasn't going for the win was obviously deliberate."

"……True, her rate of improvement has been remarkable……"

Lila nodded thoughtfully.

"If only I had a bit more talent with the sword, I could be a proper sparring partner…… I'm sorry."

"You're talented enough, I'd say…… and there's no need to apologize. Training with you is plenty enjoyable, and with more time you'll keep improving."

As one would expect from someone raised among the Kreisharana, Lila's movements had never been those of a beginner.

Regular practice sessions with Selene had brought her far, and she was now quite formidable — nothing to belittle. She was diligent and had good instincts.

Her unarmed technique, in particular, was outstanding — but inevitably, compared to Bery, she felt inferior.

*Bery is the most amazing person in the world!* — the fact that even Krische blindly believed it said everything. Where ordinary people staggered, gasping and clawing their way to a destination, Bery simply pulled up in her carriage of talent, serene and unbothered.

If she were like Kokys or Aleha — seekers who devoted themselves to a single path — Selene could respect her unreservedly. But everything for Bery was a side project.

Like someone leading a caravan through the desert alongside travelers dying of thirst, bathing in water and splashing it everywhere because *it's hot* — that was how she flaunted her overflowing talent, day in and day out.

Even granting, generously, that she meant no harm — having a daily feast next door would make even the most oblivious person feel some irritation.

For Selene, defeating Bery was payback for all of that accumulated grievance.

She wanted to soundly beat Bery — *Bery who had gotten serious within her side project* — and tell her frustrated face:

*That's what happens when you only put in half the effort. For once in your life, try giving it everything you've got.*

This was not a contest between Bery and Selene.

It was a battle between talent and effort — Selene's entire life, pitted against Bery, who had lived in comfort atop her carriage of talent.

If she could defeat Bery when Bery was truly going for the win, the joy would be beyond description.

"It's a different kind of enjoyment from the usual, a contest with Bery. I mean, of course the swordfight is exciting, but it's more like a goal from childhood…… a wall I have to overcome someday."

"Fufu, you always say that."

Elvena, who had been listening, smiled wryly.

Beside her, Anne was scratching away with a fountain pen on parchment — apparently recording the saga of Selene and Bery's battles. After two thousand years of recording losses, a certain unfortunate familiarity had set in.

*I wonder how Argan-sama will ensnare Selene-sama this time* — Selene spotted such a line written there, and glared at Anne.

"……Anne. You're assuming I'll lose again, aren't you?"

"N-no…… not at all."

"Fine. Just watch. Today is the day I show everyone I'm not the same as before."

So Selene declared, her expression full of confidence, and rose.

Anne recorded those words — uttered without fail for two thousand years — with practiced fluency.

* * *

And so the two faced each other in the manor garden.

Selene narrowed her eyes at the sword Bery held.

Not Krische's curved blade, which she had used all this time because it suited her — but a longsword that Selene herself had forged.

A straight blade, one sun longer even than Selene's own considerable weapon.

Thin-bladed and slender, light for its length — but its most notable feature was the inscription.

It was no ordinary sword — it was an inscribed blade of artificial Kiuiris steel.

Etched upon it was a simple yet potent extension formula.

When mana was channeled through it, this already-long longsword deployed a blade of mana, extending its reach by another full shaku.

An inscribed blade that Selene, still unfamiliar with formulaic inscription at the time, had completed for the first time on her own — a sword of deep personal significance.

"Ojou-sama — surely you won't say that's not allowed?"

"No. An inscribed sword is still a sword…… I won't call it unfair."

It was her own masterwork, forged to perfection.

She could not deny it, and could only concede the point.

Fighting with a plain sword against an inscribed blade was a clear disadvantage.

A mana blade could selectively cut its target, and in the hands of someone as skilled with mana as Bery, defense was impossible — even attempting to parry would be useless, as the mana blade could simply phase through the physical sword.

It was possible to block the physical portion, of course, but if the blade was pulled back and the block missed, that was the end.

Selene's signature technique — deflecting an opponent's sword to break their balance and end the fight — was all but nullified.

And beyond that, the difference in feel was enormous.

Until now, Bery had stubbornly used only Krische's sword.

Selene had anticipated the possibility of a longsword at the decisive moment, but they had been sparring for decades with the other weapon.

A small curved blade with an edge barely a shaku long, to a weapon nearly four shaku long including the mana blade.

The change in reach was immense.

Until now, Bery had incorporated hand-to-hand combat suited to Krische's curved blade, pressing aggressively in close quarters — but her fighting style would certainly change completely now.

Whether Selene could shake off that impression quickly was, honestly, doubtful.

"You're really going for the win against me with the sword, aren't you, Bery. But I've planned for this level of surprise…… don't think two thousand years of effort can be beaten so easily. And fighting in an apron dress…… what exactly are you thinking?"

"It was you who said 'right now, in the garden,' Ojou-sama…… If I'm late, you'll only complain about that too, won't you?"

"I wouldn't complain about waiting for you to change. Not that you had any intention of doing so."

An apron dress — decidedly not easy to move in.

Deliberately fighting in such attire to anger Selene and rob her of composure was certainly one reason.

But a skirt was not entirely disadvantageous.

A long skirt that concealed the entire lower body made leg movements harder to read, unlike trousers.

The warriors of the island nation east of the continent wore clothing designed with this in mind — trousers with notably wide hems.

On a battlefield, such flowing garments posed the risk of being grabbed (Selene had never seen anyone but Krische fight in them), but in a one-on-one situation, a skirt was not without its merits.

"I won't hold back. We don't need to pull our strikes since injuries don't matter with these bodies — I'm going all-out for a clean hit. Don't complain later if your clothes get cut."

"The same applies, naturally…… though, the thought of slashing at Ojou-sama is rather painful, so I'll try to avoid actually hitting you."

"……You really are something."

In pure sword technique, Selene was clearly superior.

A bout predicated on pulled strikes would prevent full commitment, shrinking the gap — but in these bodies, a live blade was no concern.

Selene had no intention of holding back, nor was this an opponent she could afford to.

A plain blade against an inscribed one.

In a fair fight, a conservative estimate said she would lose three or four out of ten.

And Bery would certainly have those three or four made real with some hidden hand.

She steadied her breathing, looked up at the sky, and told herself: *don't rush.*

Don't rush for the win until Bery shows her trump card.

Don't lose composure.

She had been on the verge of tunnel vision — she opened her awareness, and called out to Krische.

"Krische, the signal——……?"

Krische, holding the sleeping Kreschenta, was looking up at the sky.

The roof of the manor — there was nothing there, and Selene tilted her head.

"Krische-sama, is something the matter?"

"Oh, no…… it's nothing……"

When Bery called to her, Krische shook her head.

Then she turned to face them and formed a small mana sphere at her fingertip.

It floated gently, settling precisely between Selene and Bery.

Drifting slowly toward the ground.

*Was there something on the roof?*

Selene had expected traps — pitfalls wouldn't have surprised her — but the roof was an odd place for one.

It could be nothing more than a sprite flying past.

She looked at Bery's face. Perfectly composed.

A woman who could deceive with a straight face — discerning anything was difficult.

Something was certainly afoot, but whether Krische's gaze was related was unclear.

No — this kind of thinking was also counterproductive.

She needed to focus. Even without these worries, defeat was a real possibility.

She brought her sword to a middle guard, settling her body.

Six ken of distance — the maximum range for a single-step strike.

Maximum range meant maximum commitment, maximum inertia, the greatest vulnerability to a counter.

Bery would not miss that opening.

And Bery's inscribed-blade counter would surely find its mark.

Even if dodged, the initiative would be Bery's.

From there, pressed and overwhelmed, the odds of defeat were high.

She watched the mana sphere drift toward the ground, and shifted from middle guard to a one-handed grip.

The blue light touched the ground — burst — and Selene took the Zain stance.

"……"

Bery's reaction was nearly simultaneous.

A slight withdrawal of the foot — a retreat. She had read it completely.

Not a tepid half-step retreat — a minimal retreat that precisely captured the point of maximum reach, containing the smallest possible evasion.

A shift of weight designed to ensure her return stroke would end things.

It was breathtakingly superb.

Had Selene followed through with the thrust, she would have lost.

As expected of Bery Argan — the woman who had inflicted two thousand years of humiliation.

But the Zain blade was also a feint.

Holding the explosive mana she had built up, Selene took a light step.

What appeared to be a thrust had instead closed three ken of distance.

Bery's body was set for the counter.

Her front foot was weighted slightly forward.

*——From here, the Zain blade cannot be dodged.*

A gliding step across the turf, and at the instant of landing, the stored mana constructed virtual musculature.

Selene's own killing blow — never once shown before Bery.

The deception would not work on Krische, who could freeze time in her perception — but genius or not, Bery was still within the realm of the human.

Her kinetic vision exceeded the ordinary, but she could not fully track the Zain blade.

She always dodged by reading.

But from this distance — three ken — and from that stance, physical evasion was impossible.

With the certainty of *got her*, Selene stepped in, thrusting with everything she had.

"——!?"

What she saw in that instant was a flash of mana.

What she felt was a violent gust.

A wall of air rushed at her from the front, and Bery's leaping body sailed through the wind to Selene's right.

Selene instantly threw herself into a roll, dodging the slash that raked the air above her, and lashed out with a heel kick.

She rose immediately and furrowed her brow.

"……Excuse me."

"What is it?"

"I said *swords*, didn't I?"

"You did. But you never said magic was off-limits."

Smiling, utterly unrepentant.

*This woman* — she felt the blood rush to her head but forced herself calm.

Bery Argan exploited every gap in the rules. That was the kind of woman she was.

If Selene could call it carelessness, it was — she had gotten swept up in the excitement of a sword contest.

She should have insisted on physical enhancement and swords only, with hand-to-hand.

"Of course, feel free to use magic yourself, Ojou-sama. Fair and square — the conditions are equal. I won't complain either."

"Y-you……"

"If you really insist…… well, I suppose we could change the rules even now? The world of competition is cruel, and I admit I may have been unsporting. It is not beyond me, as a servant, to defer to my mistress and accommodate her weakness in magic, but……"

Blatant provocation. A woman of truly twisted character.

Selene lacked the dexterity to use magic while swinging a sword.

But banning all magic now and winning under those terms would still be a loss in the contest.

Defeating Bery when she was going all-out was the only thing that mattered.

"……The contest is still about the sword. If you run away to a distance and bombard me with magic, there's no match at all. Neither of us may open more than ten ken of distance. Fair enough?"

"Ten ken…… very well."

"Anything else goes. Bind me with magic, do whatever you want — I'll still beat you."

"As expected of Ojou-sama. Most admirable."

"Laugh while you can. ……I'll wipe that smirk off your face."

She picked up a pebble from the ground and flicked it.

The contest had tilted sharply against her.

Even so, *fair and square* was exactly right.

Considered as a real contest, the true test was defeating Bery when she held nothing back — including magic.

And now that Bery was using magic, she had no excuses left when she lost.

There was still a chance.

Bery could wield magic effortlessly, but magic required concentration.

Even Bery could only manage simple spells while simultaneously swinging or dodging a sword.

Earlier, she had manipulated the wind to obstruct Selene and circled to her right — but her follow-up against Selene's roll had been a fraction slow.

Perhaps, had she fully lifted Selene off the ground, she could have finished her.

But it was not that she chose not to — she *couldn't*.

Deploying magic in that brief instant to block a killing blow was impressive enough — it must have been near Bery's own limit.

Press the attack — stay close, keep her in sword range — and there was a real chance.

She watched the pebble fall.

Slowly — time was gentle.

As always, Bery wore her composed smile, and Selene took in every detail — every gesture, every flicker of mana, missing nothing.

To use magic, one had to rewrite the surrounding mana to one's own color.

And already, without Selene noticing, the majority of the ambient mana had been brought under Bery's control.

*How had she not noticed?*

It was all her own carelessness.

To use magic, one first had to seize control of the surrounding mana.

Like painting — before one could draw, one had to prepare a blank canvas, laying and stabilizing the groundwork for inscription.

Had she been on guard, she could have detected the mana seizure — the telltale sign of magic preparation.

Bery's inscriptions were lightning-fast, but if the mana seizure itself had been detected, it would have been enough warning.

This was not a question of rules.

It was her own carelessness — and Selene accepted it.

A rare, near-pathological capacity for self-discipline.

Selene Christand did not forgive her own immaturity.

At the same time, the woman she was now had the strength to accept it.

She had faced many hardships, accumulated regrets, struggled desperately and overcome them each time — those experiences had strengthened her and forged her core.

Reflect, but do not be trapped by it.

Reassess the situation, and devote every faculty to winning the fight before her.

Facing her with that composed smile was Bery Argan.

A woman who would stop at nothing to win — vile, villainous.

One look at her face told the story.

A simple trick like magic was not the trump card.

This magic was itself another layer of misdirection, positioning for the killing blow.

Selene would not miss the slightest flinch, not a hair's-breadth of movement.

And in that moment, Selene's concentration was sharpened enough to make it possible.

Which is why she did not notice Krische's gaze directed at her back.

"——!!"

The instant the pebble landed, she crushed the six-ken gap.

Maximum range — but that was the old limit.

She could not defeat this red-haired woman without surpassing her limits.

In the extreme focus beyond her limits, even at maximum acceleration, she could still perceive everything around her.

Bery Argan would dodge this strike without fail.

But Selene would not yield the initiative.

A momentary, arrow-like Zain lunge — the mana she had just built and released, she would build again in that same instant.

Against Bery who dodged, an immediate second Zain blade.

Not a feint this time.

Two consecutive thrusts at theoretical maximum speed.

Not even lightning-fast inscription could reach before this edge.

In the expanded, stretched-out moment of acceleration, she saw the infuriating composed smile.

Like a sister, like a mother, like a best friend, and like the object of admiration — the wall she must someday surpass.

On the threshold, the necessary mana was built.

This strike would be dodged, and then once more — dodged again——

"……Huh?"

——but it was not dodged. The Zain blade had pierced straight through Bery's chest.

Not dodged — she had not even moved — and why was the blade passing through her?

Bery, still smiling, dissolved — *bashaaan* — into water, and the inscribed sword she had been holding clattered to the ground.

"——Hyah!"

From behind — Krische's curved blade pressed to Selene's throat.

"Fufu, it seems this contest is my victory, Ojou-sama."

As Selene turned — like a rusted mechanical doll — she saw a red-haired servant beaming with delight.

Bery Argan.

"I was worried when Krische-sama's gaze nearly gave me away…… It was a fine bout, truly nail-biting."

"……Bery, what is this?"

"I thought you would probably propose a sword contest today, so I prepared a water doll last night in secret. I set it up to be controlled through the other side of the dimensional fold, and I was hiding on the roof until partway through, but……"

*A close contest,* Bery nodded.

"Really — Krische went 'shhh' and everything, but…… Fufu, well, let me go make lunch."

"Ehehe…… yes!"

She petted Krische, in utterly high spirits.

The concentration taken beyond its limit — the finest swordsmanship.

What was surely the highest plane she had ever reached.

The dense, drawn-out moment — and everything that had led to it — slowly passed through Selene's mind.

Fifty years of training, the inscribed sword brought specifically for this, the fight-picking magic — all of it, every bit, was nothing but a smokescreen for a backstab.

A wave of dizziness struck. Selene pressed her forehead, froze for a moment, and let her beloved sword slip from her fingers.

"Well, it was an unfortunate result for Ojou-sama, but contests are a matter of timing. For now, shall we agree on the deep-sea submersion tour——"

"N-n……"

"……?"

"——As if I'd agree to that!! How underhanded can you possibly be, you coward!!"

Face bright red, she erupted in fury.
